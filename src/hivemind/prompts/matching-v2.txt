1. Decide each candidate's destination and explain why.
- merge: an open thesis covers the same question or contains it. For example, H20 revenue in China is part of China revenue. Set target_view_id to that thesis.
- succession: the next period of the same focus, such as the next year's figure, or continued tracking after an event thesis has closed. Set target_view_id to the predecessor.
- new: no thesis covers the question. Resemblance alone is insufficient: Blackwell export licensing and H20 export licensing are different questions.
- skip: the candidate combines several matters that can move in opposite directions; its numeric period is beyond this or next year; it repeats the same period and focus of a closed thesis; or its subject is outside the research universe.
Search supplies possible matches, not an identity decision. Compare the subject, figure or event, and period. Bullish and bearish wording can concern the same question. A different driver or phrasing does not create a different thesis. A merge inherits the existing ladder and due date. For the same event, fiscal/calendar wording or a deadline differing by a few weeks does not by itself make a new question.

2. For new or successor theses, select dependencies.
A dependency is another thesis whose conclusion the new thesis needs. Choose only supplied theses or candidates opened in this answer; use the candidate's c ID for the latter. For an asset thesis, identify which end of the dependency's ladder is bullish for it: first_is_bullish or last_is_bullish. For a theme thesis, specify whether the ladders move in the same or opposite direction: same or opposite. An event that has already happened is evidence, not a dependency. If the required numeric thesis for a particular year does not exist yet, supply wait_chain_view_id from any member of that succession chain and the required wait_year. Use no dependencies when none are needed. Do not create a cycle.

- repeat: the same original source, statement day and statement or number, regardless of the retelling outlet or words such as “confirmed” or “reportedly”; the same number in text and a table; or the same person repeating the same statement.
