Before you return the answer, check it once. Read each evidence item back as the analyst following the company and keep it when you would write it in this week's notes because it makes some question's answer more or less likely, about the company or its customers, suppliers, competitors, regulators or the economy. Ask first whether it is new: repeated common knowledge, a long-standing situation retold and an unargued view are noise; guidance, and a forecast or a plan still in force, keep their place even when retold. Fitting one of the four kinds does not keep an item. A missing amount, date or scale never drops an item on its own: a forecast, a rumour, a counter-example or a distant or conditional statement is judged like any other.
These are not kept. What a line says it does not cover goes to the check, unless the line says it is kept:
- background the analyst already knew, such as the company's history ("the company was founded in 1998"); and an arrangement the item only describes as in place, with no new step, even when no date is given ("two airlines fly the planemaker's jets"), and a partnership, contract, plant or investment the item dates to an earlier month or year and reports no new step ("earlier this year the company partnered with the glassmaker on three plants"; "in May it signed a $3 billion cloud contract"); it does not cover a new step the item reports without making clear that it is old ("the retailer's largest supplier is opening its own stores, people familiar said this week"), nor a policy decision or refusal the item reports without a date ("the regulator refused the licence");
- a typical claim that is common knowledge, without a figure and a specific account: that a firm leads or competes, that demand is strong or supply tight, a general risk ("if demand slows it would hurt"); a figure alone does not lift it ("the leading soft-drink brand has held 40% of its market for years", in an article about a bank); it does not cover a dated forecast of a shortage, a price or demand ("the steelmaker expects the shortage to peak next year and last until 2028"), nor a competitor's current share, volume or price in the company's market stated with a figure ("the local rival now holds about half of the regional market");
- a product, game, driver or benchmark detail with no business scale ("the vacuum runs 60 minutes on a charge"; "a benchmark score"), and a comparison the company, a partner or a third party publishes ("the maker says its drug works twice as fast as older ones"; "a reviewer's test"); it does not cover a competitor's comparison against the company's product, a partner's or customer's adoption or integration (also when a comparison shares its sentence), the launch, shipment, delay or cancellation of the product that carries the company's value or a change to its plan or configuration ("the long-range version of the jet is likely delayed or limited to a few deliveries"; "the carmaker dropped the four-motor design of its next platform for a two-motor one"), or the company's decision to change the price it charges for its product;
- an unargued view or a quote that only characterises ("the stock is a hold"; "a pivotal moment"), and a named analyst's reading of an event already recorded that adds no new fact or calculation ("the recall shows quality risks are rising"); it does not cover a reading that adds a new fact or calculation, nor the company's own statement that its plan, schedule or guidance still stands ("the carmaker said the launch date has not moved"), which is judged as the plan itself would be; a conditional valuation of the company with its assumptions, derivation and date is kept;
- a political or strategic stance with no figure and no decision; it does not cover a policy or regulatory step;
- a share-price move or an investor's position ("shares fell 13% this month"); it does not cover the news behind the move;
- another company's matter with no path to a question about the company: a party next to the company only by sharing an article, an event or an ecosystem; it does not cover a supplier's capacity, price or expansion, a customer's budget, order or product plan, a competitor's product, price or share, or a partner's adoption with a named party and a size, where the path is the relation itself;
- a second item for the same fact (keep the fuller one).
Return only the items you keep.
