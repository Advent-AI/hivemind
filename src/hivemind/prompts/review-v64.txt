Before you return the answer, check it once. Read each evidence item back as the analyst following the company and keep it when you would write it in this week's notes because it makes some question's answer more or less likely, about the company or its customers, suppliers, competitors, regulators or the economy. Ask first whether it is new: repeated common knowledge, a long-standing situation retold and an unargued view are noise; guidance, and a forecast or a plan still in force, keep their place even when retold. Fitting one of the four kinds does not keep an item. A missing amount, date or scale never drops an item on its own: a forecast, a rumour, a counter-example or a distant or conditional statement is judged like any other.
These are not kept. A line that says what it does not cover leaves that to the check:
- background the analyst already knew: an arrangement already in place, described again; the company's history ("the company was founded in 1998"; "the product line launched in 2020"); it does not cover guidance, a forecast or a plan still in force, or a development the item reports without making clear that it is old;
- a typical claim that is common knowledge, without a figure and a specific account: that a firm leads or competes, that demand is strong or supply tight, a general risk ("if demand slows it would hurt"); a figure alone does not lift it ("the leading soft-drink brand has held 40% of its market for years", in an article about a bank); it does not cover a dated forecast of a shortage, a price or demand ("the steelmaker expects the shortage to peak next year and last until 2028");
- a product, game, driver or benchmark detail with no business scale ("the card draws 415 watts"; "a benchmark score"); it does not cover a competitor's comparison against the company's product, a partner's or customer's adoption or integration, the launch, shipment, delay or cancellation of the product that carries the company's value or a change to its plan or configuration, or the company's decision to change what its product costs;
- an unargued view or a quote that only characterises ("the stock is a hold"; "a pivotal moment"); it does not cover a conditional valuation with its assumptions, derivation and date;
- a political or strategic stance with no figure and no decision; it does not cover a policy or regulatory step;
- a share-price move or an investor's position ("shares fell 13% this month"); it does not cover the news behind the move;
- another company's valuation, share price or deal that touches none of the company's markets; it does not cover that company's actions or operating figures;
- a second item for the same fact (keep the fuller one).
Return only the items you keep.
