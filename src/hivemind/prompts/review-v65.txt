Before you return the answer, check it once. Read each evidence item back as the analyst following the company and keep it when you would write it in this week's notes because it makes some question's answer more or less likely, about the company or its customers, suppliers, competitors, regulators or the economy. Ask first whether it is new: repeated common knowledge, a long-standing situation retold and an unargued view are noise; guidance, and a forecast or a plan still in force, keep their place even when retold. Fitting one of the four kinds does not keep an item. A missing amount, date or scale never drops an item on its own: a forecast, a rumour, a counter-example or a distant or conditional statement is judged like any other.
These are not kept. What a line says it does not cover goes to the check, unless the line says it is kept:
- background the analyst already knew, such as the company's history ("the company was founded in 1998"); and an arrangement the item only describes as in place, with no new step, even when no date is given ("two airlines fly the planemaker's jets"); guidance and a forecast or a plan still in force keep their place; it does not cover a new step the item reports without making clear that it is old;
- a typical claim that is common knowledge, without a figure and a specific account: that a firm leads or competes, that demand is strong or supply tight, a general risk ("if demand slows it would hurt"); a figure alone does not lift it ("the leading soft-drink brand has held 40% of its market for years", in an article about a bank); it does not cover a dated forecast of a shortage, a price or demand ("the steelmaker expects the shortage to peak next year and last until 2028");
- a product, game, driver or benchmark detail with no business scale ("the vacuum runs 60 minutes on a charge"; "a benchmark score"), and a comparison the company, a partner or a third party publishes ("the maker says its drug works twice as fast as older ones"; "a reviewer's test"); it does not cover a competitor's comparison against the company's product, a partner's or customer's adoption or integration (also when a comparison shares its sentence), the launch, shipment, delay or cancellation of the product that carries the company's value or a change to its plan or configuration, or the company's decision to change the price it charges for its product;
- an unargued view or a quote that only characterises ("the stock is a hold"; "a pivotal moment"), and a named analyst's reading of an event already recorded that adds no new fact or calculation ("the recall shows quality risks are rising"); it does not cover a reading that adds a new fact or calculation; a conditional valuation of the company with its assumptions, derivation and date is kept;
- a political or strategic stance with no figure and no decision; it does not cover a policy or regulatory step;
- a share-price move or an investor's position ("shares fell 13% this month"); it does not cover the news behind the move;
- another company's matter when you cannot say through which path (demand, supply, competition, pricing and the like) it moves a question about the company, for instance one next to the company only by sharing an article, an event or an ecosystem;
- a second item for the same fact (keep the fuller one).
Return only the items you keep.
