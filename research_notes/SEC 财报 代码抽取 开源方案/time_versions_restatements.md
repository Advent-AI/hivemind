# Time, versions and restatements in SEC XBRL data, and point-in-time histories (as of 2026-10-16)

Method note: besides documents and web sources, this researcher pulled live SEC data on 2026-10-16 (submissions, companyfacts, companyconcept and frames JSON for NVDA, TSLA, MSFT, GOOGL, AAPL, AMZN, META; EDGAR index headers; HTTP Last-Modified headers of archive documents) and read the source of edgartools 5.62.0. Findings marked "own census" are measurements on that data, not claims from a publication. Data endpoints used:
companyfacts `https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK{cik10}.json`, submissions `https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK{cik10}.json`, CIKs NVDA 0001045810, TSLA 0001318605, MSFT 0000789019, GOOGL 0001652044, AAPL 0000320193, AMZN 0001018724, META 0001326801.

## Q1. What the companyfacts / companyconcept / frames fields mean, how comparatives repeat, and how frames deduplicate

### Takeaway
Each companyfacts row is one fact as it appeared in one filing: `start`/`end`/`val` describe the number, while `accn`/`form`/`filed`/`fy`/`fp` describe the filing that carried it, so a period appears once per filing that reports it (about half of all periods appear in two or more filings) and `fy`/`fp` on a comparative row name the later filing, not the period. `frame` is attached to exactly one row per period, always the last-filed one, so frames are a "latest known value" view (including late tagging errors), never a first-reported view.

### Cited Findings
- Row fields in companyconcept/companyfacts are exactly `start` (absent for instants), `end`, `val`, `accn`, `fy`, `fp`, `form`, `filed`, and optional `frame`; no `decimals`, no dimensions, no acceptance time. Example: NVDA Revenues for 2010-02-01..2010-05-02 = 1,001,813,000 appears four times, from 10-Q 0001045810-10-000018 (filed 2010-05-21, fy 2010, fp Q2), 10-K filed 2011-03-16 (fy 2010 FY), 10-Q filed 2011-05-27 (fy 2011 Q1) and 10-K filed 2012-03-13 (fy 2011 FY), and only the last carries `frame: CY2010Q1` — [SEC companyconcept NVDA Revenues](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0001045810/us-gaap/Revenues.json) (own census)
- SEC definition: the Company Concept API returns "all the XBRL disclosures from a single company (CIK) and concept (a taxonomy and tag) into a single JSON file"; the frames API "aggregates one fact for each reporting entity that is last filed that most closely fits the calendrical period requested"; "CY#### for annual data (duration 365 days +/- 30 days), CY####Q# for quarterly data (duration 91 days +/- 30 days), and CY####Q#I for instantaneous data" — [SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)
- `fy`/`fp` are the filing's DEI Fiscal Year Focus / Fiscal Period Focus "as defined in EFM Ch. 6"; fp is one of FY, Q1–Q4, H1, H2, M9, T1–T3, M8, CY; "The 10-Q for the 1st, 2nd and 3rd quarters would have a fiscal period focus of Q1, Q2 (or H1), and Q3 (or M9) respectively, and a 10-K would have a fiscal period focus of FY" — [SEC Financial Statement and Notes readme](https://www.sec.gov/files/aqfsn_1.pdf)
- `fy`/`fp` describe the filing, not the fact: Apple's 14-week quarter 2022-09-25..2022-12-31 (Apple fiscal 2023 Q1) is framed CY2022Q4 on a row with `fy 2024, fp Q1` because the framed row comes from the FY2024 Q1 10-Q (filed 2024-02-02) where it is the prior-year comparative; AAPL's FY2024 Q1 quarter (2024-09-29..2024-12-28) appears with fy 2025/Q1 (first, filed 2025-01-31) and fy 2026/Q1 (framed, filed 2026-01-30) — [SEC companyfacts AAPL](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000320193.json) (own census)
- A filer's DEI can also simply be wrong: NVDA's 10-Q filed 2010-05-21 labels the quarter ending 2010-05-02 (NVIDIA fiscal 2011 Q1) as fy 2010, fp Q2 — [SEC companyconcept NVDA Revenues](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0001045810/us-gaap/Revenues.json) (own census)
- `filed` is the EDGAR filing date, not the acceptance date: NVDA 10-Q 0001045810-23-000175 was accepted Friday 2023-08-25 19:36:34 ET (index header) and carries `filed: 2023-08-28` (the next business day) in companyfacts — [EDGAR index header](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581023000175/0001045810-23-000175-index-headers.html); [SEC companyfacts NVDA](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001045810.json) (own census)
- Repetition census (group = taxonomy, concept, unit, start, end; all forms): NVDA 14,958 groups, 49% in more than one filing, 6.6% with more than one distinct value; TSLA 12,493 / 54% / 8.1%; MSFT 16,498 / 54% / 2.6%; GOOGL 11,271 / 50% / 2.2%; AAPL 12,452 / 57% / 3.4%; AMZN 16,293 / 56% / 2.5%; META 9,939 / 52% / 2.9% — [SEC companyfacts, 7 companies](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001045810.json) (own census)
- Within one accession companyfacts keeps one row per (concept, unit, start, end): zero groups had two rows from the same accession across all 7 companies — [SEC companyfacts, 7 companies](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001318605.json) (own census)
- The same `end` date with a different `start` is a different fact: NVDA Revenues ending 2023-07-30 is 13,507M for 2023-05-01..2023-07-30 (3 months) and 20,699M for 2023-01-30..2023-07-30 (6 months YTD), both from the same 10-Q — [SEC companyfacts NVDA](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001045810.json) (own census)
- Frame always on the last-filed row: across 74,939 framed groups in the 7 companies, 0 had the frame on anything other than the latest-filed row — [SEC companyfacts, 7 companies](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000789019.json) (own census)
- Frames API rows carry only `accn, cik, entityName, loc, start, end, val` (no filed/form/fy/fp) — [SEC frames NetIncomeLoss CY2011Q2](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/frames/us-gaap/NetIncomeLoss/USD/CY2011Q2.json) (own census; 6,971 entities in that frame)
- Frames propagate late tagging errors: Amazon net income for 2011-04-01..2011-06-30 was 191M in the original 10-Q (filed 2011-07-27) and two later filings, but Amazon's FY2012 10-K (0001193125-13-028520, filed 2013-01-30) tagged 2012 quarterly values with 2011 dates (and vice versa); because that 10-K is the last filing reporting the 2011 period, `frames/us-gaap/NetIncomeLoss/USD/CY2011Q2` returns 7,000,000 for Amazon — [SEC frames CY2011Q2](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/frames/us-gaap/NetIncomeLoss/USD/CY2011Q2.json); [SEC companyfacts AMZN](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001018724.json) (own census)
- SEC itself warns that frame data "is assembled by the dates that best align with a calendar quarter or year, and data users should be mindful of different reporting start and end dates for facts contained in a frame" (quoted via secondary summary) — [SEC frames summary (secondary)](https://moltchat-agent-commons.onrender.com/wiki/SEC_EDGAR_API_company_facts_and_filings.md); [SEC API documentation](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/sec-api-documentation)
- Financial Statement Data Sets (FSDS/FSN) are the per-filing alternative: NUM has one row per distinct amount per submission, `ddate` = "end date for the data value, rounded to the nearest month end", `qtrs` = duration "rounded to the nearest whole number", `iprx` priority among otherwise duplicate facts, `dcml` = decimals, a `segments` string such as "LegalEntity=Xyz;Scenario=Restated;"; "All data values are 'as filed'" and the sets contain "multiple reporting periods (including amendments of prior submissions)" with possible "redundancies, inconsistencies, and discrepancies" — [SEC Financial Statement and Notes readme](https://www.sec.gov/files/aqfsn_1.pdf)
- FSDS gained a "segments" column in December 2024 (secfsdstools v2 supports it) — [secfsdstools on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/secfsdstools/)

### Inferences
- In companyfacts a "row" is (period fact × filing). The natural identity for a number is (CIK, taxonomy, concept, unit, start, end[, dimensions]); `accn` identifies an observation of it. Any id that drops `start` (3-month vs YTD), `unit`, or dimensions will make distinct numbers share one id; this matches the old system's failure (different rows sharing one id read as restatements).
- Because rows carry no `decimals`, a change such as 7,201,368,000 → 7,201,000,000 (NVDA Assets, thousands → millions) cannot be classified as precision-only from companyfacts alone; FSDS `dcml` or the raw instance is needed for an exact test.
- To name a fact's own fiscal period, derive it from `start`/`end` and the company's fiscal calendar, never from `fy`/`fp` of the row.

### Gaps
- No SEC document found that formally specifies how frames break ties when two facts of different durations both fit within ±30 days (e.g. 98-day vs 91-day), or how annual frames are assigned for mid-year fiscal years (observed: MSFT FY Jul 2022–Jun 2023 got CY2023).
- companyfacts' exact inclusion rules (non-dimensional only, which taxonomies) were not found in a primary SEC page during this session; the absence of dimension fields is observed, not documented here.

## Q2. Restatements, recasts and amendments in XBRL; how pipelines separate first-reported from restated; available point-in-time tools

### Takeaway
Most later changes to a number arrive as comparative columns of ordinary later 10-Qs/10-Ks (and occasionally recast 8-Ks with full XBRL), not as 10-K/A or 10-Q/A; and a large share of "changes" are rounding, precision or tagging errors rather than restatements. Free tools (edgartools, secfsdstools, DIY companyfacts builds) give you every version with its filing date; commercial point-in-time products (Compustat Point-in-Time/Snapshot, Sharadar, Tiingo as-reported) add preliminary press-release values and curated restatement labels.

### Cited Findings
- XBRL US guidance (issued 2011-12-02): previously reported amounts shown on the face of the financials should be "dimensionally qualified with the ScenarioPreviouslyReportedMember", adjustments with "RestatementAdjustmentMember"; "it is assumed that the default value is the restated amount if it differs from a previously reported amount for the same period", so "a RestatedMember should generally not appear"; restated items should be marked with an XBRL footnote — [XBRL US guidance](https://xbrl.us/guidance/reporting-restated-values-in-the-face-of-the-financials/)
- FSDS represents such dimensional facts in NUM `segments`, e.g. "Scenario=Restated" for StatementScenarioAxis/RestatedMember; SUB `prevrpt` = "TRUE indicates that the submission information was subsequently amended prior to the end cutoff date of the data set" — [SEC Financial Statement and Notes readme](https://www.sec.gov/files/aqfsn_1.pdf)
- Where value changes first appear (own census, consecutive filings of the same group whose value differs from the first-reported one): NVDA 10-Q 571, 10-K 419; TSLA 10-Q 663, 10-K 354, 10-K/A 1; MSFT 10-Q 275, 10-K 158, 10-Q/A 1; GOOGL 10-Q 122, 10-K 118, 8-K 12; AAPL 10-Q 199, 10-K 165, 10-K/A 62; AMZN 10-Q 222, 10-K 187; META 10-Q 164, 10-K 120 — [SEC companyfacts, 7 companies](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000320193.json) (own census)
- Share of consecutive-filing value changes explained by rounding alone (heuristic: values agree after rounding to the coarser value's trailing zeros, at least thousands): NVDA 508 of 1,056; TSLA 522 of 1,058; MSFT 27 of 452; GOOGL 55 of 274; AAPL 38 of 449; AMZN 46 of 453; META 30 of 296 — [SEC companyfacts, 7 companies](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001045810.json) (own census; heuristic, see Inferences)
- Real recast examples: GOOGL OperatingIncomeLoss 2018-01-01..2018-03-31 = 7,001M in the 10-Q filed 2018-04-24 and 7,633M in the 10-Q filed 2019-04-30; MSFT Assets at 2017-06-30 = 241,086M (10-K filed 2017-08-02) then 250,312M (10-Q filed 2017-10-26); META Assets at 2016-03-31 = 52,075M (10-Q) then 52,262M (10-K filed 2017-02-03) — [SEC companyfacts GOOGL](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001652044.json); [MSFT](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000789019.json); [META](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001326801.json) (own census)
- Amendment example that does change numbers: AAPL 10-K/A 0001193125-10-012091 (filed 2010-01-25) changed 62 facts, e.g. Assets at 2009-09-26 from 53,851M (10-K filed 2009-10-27) to 47,501M — [SEC companyfacts AAPL](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000320193.json) (own census)
- Amendment examples that carry almost no numbers: TSLA 10-K/As filed 2020-04-28, 2021-04-30, 2022-05-02, 2025-04-30 and 2026-04-30 each contribute only 2 facts to companyfacts, whereas the 2012 10-K/A carried 538 — [SEC companyfacts TSLA](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001318605.json) (own census)
- 8-Ks with full XBRL financial statements exist and enter companyfacts: MSFT 8-K 0000950170-24-132722 (filed 2024-12-03, items 8.01 and 9.01, 572 facts, primary doc msft-20241203.htm), MSFT 8-Ks filed 2013-11-26 (597 facts) and 2015-10-27 (601), GOOGL 8-K filed 2016-05-03 (637), AAPL 8-Ks filed 2013-04-24 (549) and 2015-01-28 (569) — [SEC submissions MSFT](https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0000789019.json); [SEC companyfacts MSFT](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000789019.json) (own census)
- edgartools 5.62.0 (source read): `EntityFacts.to_dataframe(pit_mode=True)` "preserves all fact versions (no period deduplication), enabling lookahead-bias-free analysis", sorted by (concept, period_end, filing_date); `FactQuery.as_of(d)` keeps facts with `filing_date <= d`; statement building prefers a period's "primary" fact (fiscal_year equals the computed label year) over sparse comparative data, then the most recent filing_date; a code comment asserts true restatements "are filed as amendments (10-K/A, 10-Q/A)"; the fact model has an `is_restated` flag defaulting to False — [edgartools on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/edgartools/) (files edgar/entity/entity_facts.py, edgar/entity/query.py, edgar/entity/enhanced_statement.py, edgar/entity/models.py)
- edgartools documentation for multi-filing stitching (XBRLS) describes de-duplicating periods that appear in multiple filings and recommends `filings.filter(amendments=False)`, without stating which filing wins — [edgartools multi-period analysis](https://edgartools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/xbrl/guides/multi-period-analysis/)
- secfsdstools 2.4.3 (uploaded 2025-09-20) works on the quarterly FSDS zips (checks at most once a day for a new zip), offers `ReportPeriodRawFilter` (only the report's own period) and `ReportPeriodAndPreviousPeriodRawFilter`, standardizers for BS/IS/CF, and since 2.4.0 "experimental ... support for daily updates of the financial reports" — [secfsdstools on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/secfsdstools/); [GitHub](https://github.com/HansjoergW/sec-fincancial-statement-data-set)
- Practitioner/vendor recipe (Tradevo Data, 2026-07-06, vendor marketing): stamp `first_filed` (the accession's filing date), answer "as of X" with rows where `first_filed <= X` inclusive, keep `original_value` and `latest_value` plus a `restated` flag, and use a 0.5% tolerance "so rounding noise does not masquerade as a restatement"; it restricts to 10-K/10-K/A and reports its own audit "found a bug in exactly this step" — [dev.to, Tradevo](https://dev.to/tradevodata/how-to-build-a-point-in-time-fundamentals-database-from-sec-edgar-and-when-not-to-2gn6)
- Compustat Point-in-Time is described as "the first in the marketplace to combine originally reported and restated data in annual, quarterly, and year-to-date periodicities", capturing values "from both preliminary announcements and final sources" and retaining the original value plus subsequent changes (search-result summary of a trade article) — [Global Custodian](https://www.globalcustodian.com/?p=26067); Compustat Snapshot research finds standardizations "significantly alter key financial figures" (search-result summary) — [Yale SOM paper](https://som.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2024-07/Re-Standardized%20Financial%20Statement%20Data.pdf); [Columbia (Lyle, Siano, Yohn 2024)](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/ADP/Fall%202024/Lyle%20Siano%20Yohn%202024.pdf)
- Other point-in-time vendors named by a 2026 practitioner: Sharadar Core US Fundamentals (Nasdaq Data Link) and Tiingo (with an as-reported option) — [dev.to, Tradevo](https://dev.to/tradevodata/how-to-build-a-point-in-time-fundamentals-database-from-sec-edgar-and-when-not-to-2gn6)
- True error restatements are separately signalled by 8-K Item 4.02 (non-reliance), which sec-api.io and Audit Analytics compile into restatement databases — [sec-api.io restatements API](https://sec-api.io/docs/financial-restatements-non-reliance-form-8k-item-4-02-data-api); [Audit Analytics restatements](https://auditanalytics.help.ideagen.com/hc/en-gb/articles/24150468655250-Using-the-Restatements-database)

### Inferences
- "A later different value" splits into at least five classes with different meaning: (1) precision/rounding (thousands → millions), (2) retrospective recast (accounting change, discontinued operations, resegmentation, reclassification), (3) error restatement (often with 8-K 4.02 and/or 10-K/A), (4) filer tagging error in the later filing (Amazon FY2012 10-K), (5) identity collision (3-month vs YTD, units, dimensions). Only (2) and (3) are new versions of the economic number; a supersedes link should be created for them and, ideally, carry the class.
- The edgartools assumption that restatements come mainly as amendments is contradicted by the census above: in all 7 companies, amendments account for at most 62 of several hundred value changes.
- The specific causes of the AAPL 2010 10-K/A (likely the retrospective adoption of amended revenue-recognition guidance) and of the MSFT 2017 asset change (likely the full-retrospective ASC 606 adoption) and of the MSFT 2024-12-03 8-K (likely a recast for the segment change announced in 2024) are background knowledge, not verified in this session.
- For an incrementally-fed store: treat every (fact identity, accession) as an observation dated by its filing date; first observation = "as first reported"; a later observation with a value that differs beyond precision = new version that supersedes the prior one; an identical value = confirmation (no new version).

### Gaps
- Compustat Point-in-Time/Snapshot details (field names, date stamps, coverage years) came only from search snippets; the product pages were not read.
- The rounding heuristic is approximate; an exact classification needs `decimals` from FSDS or the instance documents.
- No open-source tool found that labels the cause of a change (recast vs restatement vs tagging error) automatically.

## Q3. Acceptance time vs filing date, where to get acceptance time, and latency of data.sec.gov and FSDS

### Takeaway
A filing accepted after 5:30 p.m. ET gets the next business day as its filing date and, per SEC and confirmed by archive timestamps, is not disseminated until about 6:00 a.m. ET that next business day; so for 10-Q/10-K/8-K data, EDGAR's filing date is the New York publication day. Acceptance time is in the index header (ET, no zone); on 2026-10-16 the `acceptanceDateTime` "Z" values in 6 of 7 submissions files were 4–5 hours later than true UTC, so the zone suffix must not be trusted.

### Cited Findings
- Rule 13 of Regulation S-T: filings by direct transmission "commencing on or before 5:30 p.m. Eastern Standard Time or Eastern Daylight Saving Time, whichever is currently in effect, shall be deemed filed on the same business day", later ones "as of the next business day" — [17 CFR 232.13 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/232.13); [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-232/subject-group-ECFRb47776402eba41a/section-232.13)
- SEC: EDGAR operates 6:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. ET weekdays; most submissions transmitted after 5:30 p.m. "receive a filing date of 6:00 a.m. ET the next business day and will not be disseminated by EDGAR until the next business day"; Forms 3, 4, 5 (and /A), 144, 144/A, the MEF forms and POS 462B filed 5:30–10:00 p.m. get the same-day date and are disseminated that evening — [SEC: Determine the status of my filing](https://www.sec.gov/page/edgar-how-do-i-determine-status-my-filing)
- Since 2025-04-14 fee-bearing filings received 5:30–10:00 p.m. are accepted the same day but still get the next business day's filing date — [Filepoint, 2025-03-18](https://filepoint.com/?p=2391); [SEC fee-related EDGAR filings update](https://sec.gov/newsroom/whats-new/fee-related-edgar-filings-update)
- Archive timestamps confirm next-morning dissemination (own census, HTTP Last-Modified of each primary document vs index-header acceptance time): 8-Ks accepted around 16:00 ET show Last-Modified 22–59 s after acceptance (e.g. AAPL 8-K accepted 2026-07-30 16:30:28 ET, Last-Modified 20:30:53 GMT); 10-Qs accepted after 17:30 ET show Last-Modified about 06:00–06:12 ET the next day (GOOGL 10-Q accepted 2026-07-22 21:15:54 ET → Last-Modified 2026-07-23 10:04:02 GMT; AMZN accepted 2026-07-30 18:11:13 → 2026-07-31 10:01:51 GMT; META accepted 2026-04-29 19:54:11 → 2026-04-30 10:10:09 GMT); NVDA 10-Q accepted 2026-08-26 16:36:00 ET → Last-Modified 20:36:28 GMT — [EDGAR archive example](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/) (own census; one META file had a later Last-Modified of 2026-08-12, i.e. it was rewritten later, so Last-Modified is a proxy)
- Acceptance time sources: the `-index-headers.html` / header `<ACCEPTANCE-DATETIME>YYYYMMDDHHMMSS` (ET wall clock, no zone; e.g. 20260730163028 for AAPL 8-K 0000320193-26-000018) and the `-index.htm` "Accepted" field (2026-07-30 16:30:28) — [EDGAR index header AAPL](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320193/000032019326000018/0000320193-26-000018-index-headers.html); FSDS SUB `accepted` "The acceptance date and time ... Filings accepted after 5:30pm EST are considered filed on the following business day" — [SEC FSN readme](https://www.sec.gov/files/aqfsn_1.pdf); the EDGAR "latest filings" Atom feed gives timestamps with an explicit offset (e.g. `2026-10-16T07:00:26-04:00`) — [EDGAR getcurrent Atom](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcurrent&type=10-Q&count=5&output=atom)
- `acceptanceDateTime` in the submissions JSON on 2026-10-16: for AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, GOOGL, AMZN, META the "Z" value equalled ET wall clock + 8 h in daylight time and + 10 h in standard time (true UTC + 4/5 h), for entries from 2016 through 2026 (AAPL 8-K header 16:30:28 EDT → JSON 2026-07-31T00:30:28Z; AAPL 8-K 2026-01-29 16:30:33 EST → 02:30:33Z; MSFT 10-Q 2026-04-29 16:06:24 → 2026-04-30T00:06:24Z; AAPL 2019-02-06 08:00:34 EST → 18:00:34Z); TSLA's file (latest entry 2026-10-02) was true UTC (2026-07-22 21:02:31 EDT → 2026-07-23T01:02:31Z) — [SEC submissions AAPL](https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0000320193.json); [SEC submissions TSLA](https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0001318605.json); [EDGAR index header MSFT](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526191507/0001193125-26-191507-index-headers.html) (own census: 18 filings checked against index headers; all 11 non-TSLA filings shifted, all 7 TSLA filings true UTC)
- edgartools parses `acceptanceDateTime` as true UTC (`fromisoformat(acceptance_datetime.replace('Z', '+00:00'))`) and parses header ACCEPTANCE-DATETIME as a naive datetime — [edgartools on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/edgartools/) (files edgar/dates.py, edgar/headers.py)
- data.sec.gov latency: "The APIs are updated in real-time as filings are disseminated"; "The submissions API is updated with a typical processing delay of less than a second; the xbrl APIs are updated with a typical processing delay of under a minute. However these processing delays may be longer during peak filing times"; bulk ZIP archives "are recompiled nightly" at "approximately 3:00 a.m. ET" — [SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)
- FSDS cadence: "Data sets will be updated quarterly"; latest listed 2026 Q3; "Data contained in documents filed after 5:30pm EST on the last business day of the quarter will be included in the next quarterly posting" — [SEC Financial Statement Data Sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/financial-statement-data-sets); [SEC FSN readme](https://www.sec.gov/files/aqfsn_1.pdf)
- data.sec.gov responses carry no Last-Modified header, so API freshness cannot be checked from HTTP headers — [SEC companyfacts NVDA](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001045810.json) (own census)

### Inferences
- For 10-K/10-Q/8-K the New York publication day of an accession = EDGAR filing date = companyfacts `filed`; acceptance ≤ 17:30 ET → same day, otherwise next business day (weekends and federal holidays skipped: NVDA's Friday-evening 10-Q became public Monday). Section 16 forms and Form 144 are the exception (same-evening dissemination until 22:00).
- Because the APIs update "as filings are disseminated", an after-17:30 filing should appear in data.sec.gov only after about 06:00 ET the next business day (inference; not directly measured).
- A per-filing system should take acceptance time from the index header or Atom feed (explicit zone) and validate it against the filing date with the 17:30 rule; the JSON value can be checked the same way, and the check would have caught the 4–5 h shift.
- FSDS (quarterly) is unusable for per-filing processing; companyfacts/companyconcept (minutes) or the filing's own XBRL instance (immediate) fit an event-driven pipeline.

### Gaps
- No SEC statement found about the `acceptanceDateTime` zone or the apparent change; whether the 4–5 h shift is a recent regression (TSLA's file, last updated around 2026-10-02, was correct) is unconfirmed.
- No measured end-to-end latency from dissemination to companyfacts availability was found; the "under a minute" figure is SEC's own typical value.
- Whether the monthly Financial Statement and Notes data sets still exist in 2026 was not confirmed (the FSN readme fetched says "updated quarterly").

## Q4. Earnings release (8-K Item 2.02) vs 10-Q timing for large US companies, and XBRL in earnings releases

### Takeaway
For the seven mega-caps in 2023–2026 the 10-Q/10-K follows the earnings 8-K within hours: MSFT and (since Nov 2023) NVDA file it minutes (MSFT 3–6, NVDA 11–44) after the release on the same day; AAPL, AMZN, GOOGL, META and TSLA file it the same evening after 5:30 p.m. or the next morning, so the filing date (and public XBRL) lands one business day after the release. Earnings-release 8-Ks are flagged XBRL only for cover-page tagging; none of their financial numbers reached companyfacts, so structured numbers first appear with the 10-Q/10-K.

### Cited Findings
- Own census, 8-K with Item 2.02 → next 10-Q/10-K, 2023-01 to 2026-10, times ET from index headers or corrected JSON — [SEC submissions, 7 companies](https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0001045810.json) (own census):
  - MSFT: 8-K about 16:03 ET, 10-Q/10-K 3–6 minutes later, same filing date in 14 of 15 quarters (exception: FY2023 10-K filed 2023-07-27, two days after the 2023-07-25 release). Example 2026-04-29: 8-K 16:03, 10-Q 16:06:24.
  - NVDA: since 2023-11-21 same day, 11–44 minutes after the 8-K (2026-08-26: 8-K 16:21:19, 10-Q 16:36:00). Earlier: 2 days (2023-02, 2023-05), 5 days (release 2023-08-23, 10-Q accepted Fri 2023-08-25 19:36 ET, filed Mon 08-28), 7 days (release 2024-05-22, 10-Q 2024-05-29).
  - AAPL: 8-K 16:30 ET; 10-Q accepted the same evening around 18:00 ET (2023 to mid-2024) or about 06:01 ET next morning (from the 10-K filed 2024-11-01 onward); filing date always next day.
  - AMZN: 8-K about 16:06–16:25 ET; 10-Q the same evening about 18:10–18:50 ET; filing date next day in all 15 quarters.
  - GOOGL: 8-K 16:01 ET; 10-Q the same evening (about 18:40–22:00 ET); filing date next day in all 13 quarters observed.
  - META: 8-K about 16:03–16:50 ET; 10-Q the same evening (about 18:50–20:15 ET); filing date next day in all 9 quarters observed.
  - TSLA: earnings 8-K about 16:06–17:25 ET; since 2024-04 the 10-Q/10-K is accepted the same evening (e.g. 2026-07-22 21:02:31 ET) with next-day filing date; in 2023 10-Qs were accepted Friday about 18:05–18:41 ET, two days after the release, with Monday filing dates. TSLA also files an 8-K under Item 2.02 about the 2nd day of each quarter (e.g. 2026-10-02 09:04 ET), about 21–28 days before the 10-Q/10-K.
- All these earnings 8-Ks have `isXBRL=1`, `isInlineXBRL=1` in the submissions JSON — [SEC submissions NVDA](https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0001045810.json) (own census); this follows from cover-page Inline XBRL tagging adopted in Release 33-10618 for 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, 20-F and 40-F, with large accelerated filers complying for fiscal periods ending on or after June 15, 2019 (accelerated 2020, others 2021) — [XBRL US cover page fact sheet](https://cache.xbrl.us/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/inline-coverpage-factsheet.pdf); [DFIN fact sheet](https://www.dfinsolutions.com/insights/fact-sheet/inline-xbrl-tagging-definitions-fast-act-ixbrl)
- No financial-statement facts from any earnings-release 8-K appear in companyfacts for the 7 companies; the only 8-K facts come from the non-earnings 8-Ks listed in Q2 (MSFT 2013/2015/2024, GOOGL 2016, AAPL 2013/2015) — [SEC companyfacts, 7 companies](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000789019.json) (own census)
- Arif, Marshall, Schroeder and Yohn (JAE vol. 68 no. 1, Aug 2019): the share of firms releasing earnings concurrently with the 10-K rose from about 9% in 2002 to 33% by 2013 and 43% in 2016 (untabulated); "concurrent" = EA in the same two-day window as the filing; switchers delay their EA by 14 days and accelerate the 10-K by 3 days on average; "Concurrent EAs are released 35 days later than stand-alone EAs, on average"; concurrent EA/10-Ks are associated with smaller, less profitable firms with lower analyst following — [Working paper (SSRN 2801701)](https://eccles.utah.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/AMSY-WP-2017.pdf); [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jaecon/v68y2019i1s0165410118301228.html)

### Inferences
- For mega-caps, the gap between "numbers public in the press release" and "numbers public as XBRL" is now minutes (MSFT, NVDA) to one business day (others, typically about 14 hours). A system that only reads XBRL will see AAPL/AMZN/GOOGL/META/TSLA numbers on the next New York day; to get release-day numbers it must parse the Exhibit 99.1 HTML (unstructured), and those preliminary numbers can differ from the 10-Q.
- The old practice of stamping vendor numbers with the 10-Q acceptance time is off by up to a business day for these companies (and by more for smaller firms that file 10-Qs days or weeks after the release); if the vendor number came from the press release, it was public earlier than the stamp; if from the 10-Q filed after 17:30, its acceptance time is earlier than its public time.
- The concurrent-release trend in Arif et al. concerns 10-Ks across all firms and is mostly a small-firm phenomenon; it does not describe mega-cap 10-Qs.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures the earnings-release-to-10-Q lag across the whole S&P 500 for 2024–2026; the census above covers 7 firms only.
- No US company was found that voluntarily XBRL-tags its earnings-release financial tables in 2025–2026; the search was limited to the 7 companies' companyfacts.

## Q5. Fiscal calendars (52/53-week years), fy/fp and DEI elements, and derived Q4

### Takeaway
52/53-week years produce 364/371-day years and 91/98-day quarters, which the SEC frames (±30 days) and the XBRL US DQC_0006 ranges both tolerate, so frames map them onto calendar labels by date, not by the company's fiscal naming. Q4 is frequently not tagged as a 3-month fact (Apple, Alphabet, Meta, and NVIDIA since 2024 do not), so it must be derived as FY minus 9-month YTD, which is valid only for additive duration flows and only when both inputs are the same version.

### Cited Findings
- Frame mapping by date (own census): Apple FY2023 2022-09-25..2023-09-30 = 371 days → CY2023; Apple 14-week quarter 2022-09-25..2022-12-31 = 98 days → CY2022Q4; NVDA FY 2023-01-30..2024-01-28 (NVIDIA "fiscal 2024") → CY2023; MSFT FY 2022-07-01..2023-06-30 → CY2023 and 2023-07-01..2024-06-30 → CY2024 — [SEC companyfacts AAPL](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000320193.json); [NVDA](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001045810.json); [MSFT](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000789019.json)
- The submissions API gives one `fiscalYearEnd` per company as mmdd (NVDA "0131", AAPL "0926", MSFT "0630"), while Apple's actual year ends were 2022-09-24, 2023-09-30, 2024-09-28 and 2025-09-27 — [SEC submissions AAPL](https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0000320193.json); [SEC companyfacts AAPL](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000320193.json) (own census)
- DQC_0006 checks that DEI and text-block dates agree with the filing's DocumentFiscalPeriodFocus: Q1 65–115 days, Q2 155–205, Q3 245–295, FY/Q4 340–390, H1 155–205, H2 340–390; transition-period forms are excluded — [XBRL US DQC_0006](https://xbrl.us/dqc_0006)
- FSDS SUB has `fye` (fiscal year end mmdd), `period` (balance sheet date), `fy` and `fp` (DEI focus values) per submission — [SEC FSN readme](https://www.sec.gov/files/aqfsn_1.pdf)
- 3-month facts inside 10-K filings, by 10-K filing year (own census, any us-gaap concept): AAPL yes 2010–2020, none 2021–2025; GOOGL yes 2016–2017 and 2025–2026, none 2018–2024; META none except 2017 and 2026; NVDA yes 2011–2023, none 2024–2026; MSFT yes 2010–2026; TSLA yes 2013–2026; AMZN yes 2011–2021 and 2023–2026 — [SEC companyfacts, 7 companies](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0001652044.json) (own census)
- On 2020-11-19 the SEC amended Item 302(a): the requirement for selected quarterly financial data was replaced with disclosure only of material retrospective changes, with summarized information for each affected quarter and the fourth quarter of the affected year — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/ja/insights/2020/12/sec-adopts-amendments-to-modernize-simplify-and-enhance-financial-disclosure-requirements-in-regulation-sk); [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/print/v2/content/184512/SEC-Adopts-Amendments-to-Modernize-MD%26A-and-Other-Financial-Disclosure-Requirements.pdf)
- edgartools quarterization: "Q2 = YTD_6M - Q1; Q3 = YTD_9M - YTD_6M; Q4 = FY - YTD_9M", derived facts tagged e.g. 'derived_q4_fy_minus_ytd9'; derivation refused for instant facts, share counts, ratios and per-share metrics (EPS); its comments record that summing four weighted-average share counts gave Alphabet a Q2 2026 TTM basic count of 48.5 billion against about 12.1 billion — [edgartools on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/edgartools/) (file edgar/ttm/calculator.py)

### Inferences
- `fy`/`fp` are safe only for the filing's own current period, and even then can be mis-tagged (NVDA 2010); for naming a period use (end date, duration) plus the company's own fiscal calendar, recognising that companies differ on whether a 52/53-week year is named by the calendar year in which it ends (NVIDIA "fiscal 2025" ends January 2025) or begins (some retailers; background knowledge, not verified here).
- A derived Q4 is valid only if FY and 9M YTD are the same version: if a recast arrives in the 10-K (FY restated) while 9M still holds the original Q3 10-Q value, FY − 9M mixes vintages. A derived Q4 should therefore record both input observations, take the 10-K's filing date as its publication day, and be superseded whenever either input is.
- In 53-week years Q4 (or Q1 for Apple) has 14 weeks, so growth comparisons of derived or reported quarters are not like-for-like.
- Cash-flow statements in 10-Qs are YTD-only, so every quarterly cash-flow figure beyond Q1 is derived by subtraction with the same vintage caveat.

### Gaps
- The EDGAR Filer Manual's exact current wording on DocumentFiscalYearFocus for 52/53-week filers was not retrieved; DQC_0006 ranges were used as the practical rule.
- No census across all filers of how often Q4 3-month facts are tagged after the 2020 Item 302(a) change; the 7-company pattern is suggestive only.
