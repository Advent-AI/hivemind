## Key question 1: What explicit tests do practitioners apply to decide a question is a thesis-worthy debate, not "just a financial line" or "too vague to settle"?

### Takeaway
The practitioner tests found are all variants of one rule: a question is a debate only if (a) resetting your answer to consensus changes the recommendation, (b) a dated event can settle it in both directions, (c) it is where the house view diverges from what the price assumes, and (d) you can write the sentence "the thesis is wrong if ...". Sell-side formats (BofA "top 5 debates", Morgan Stanley "Big Debates") show the practical ceiling of 3–5 live debates per name.

### Cited Findings
- **Materiality test (reset-to-consensus):** "Reset that single input to consensus in your model. If your conclusion does not change, the disagreement does not matter." — [Financial-Modeling.com, Variant Perception in a Stock Pitch: A Worked Example (dated 2026-10-11)](https://www.financial-modeling.com/variant-perception/)
- **Three layers of "what is priced":** sell-side estimates, price-implied expectations (reverse DCF), buy-side positioning (13F, short interest, earnings reaction patterns). "A forecast above consensus can still be below what the share price already assumes." Worked example: forecast beats consensus by 3 points of growth yet implies only +3.1% upside vs −16.4% downside to the price-implied case. — [same source](https://www.financial-modeling.com/variant-perception/)
- **Catalyst test (settleable, dated):** "A catalyst is an event or evidence path that can change market expectations within your time horizon. General long-term growth does not qualify." Template requires a catalyst "by [date]" with defined outcomes in both directions. — [same source](https://www.financial-modeling.com/variant-perception/)
- **Kill criterion test:** the template contains the line "The thesis is wrong if [kill criterion]" — "the observable result that would make you exit." — [same source](https://www.financial-modeling.com/variant-perception/)
- **Hedge-fund case-study interview rubric (older, standing):** "What is the consensus opinion? What is your variant perception ... How do we get paid? How much do you expect to make if you are right?" — [Street of Walls, Hedge Fund Case Study](https://www.streetofwalls.com/finance-training-courses/hedge-fund-training/hedge-fund-case-study/) (excerpt)
- **Sell-side "Big Debates" selection rule (Morgan Stanley, 2026-01-21):** analysts "start with conversations with leading investors about which debates matter, combined with proprietary data and analytic tools to provide a sense of 'what's in the price' and what should be, but isn't yet"; the series covers issues "where the firm's views point in different directions than the current market consensus" over "the next 12 months". Each debate is phrased as a yes/no question (e.g., "Airlines - Is the Low-Cost-Carrier Model Broken?", "Transportation - Is 2026 the Year of AI in Freight?"). — [Morgan Stanley, Contrarian Views That Could Shape Markets in 2026](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/contrarian-investing-views-shaping-markets-2026) (excerpt; page timed out on full fetch)
- **Sell-side "beat is not enough" test (Morgan Stanley on NVDA, 2026-08-18):** "Nvidia's stock has closed lower the day after earnings for four consecutive quarters," meaning "simply delivering numbers above market expectations is no longer sufficient"; attention has shifted to "longer-term structural issues, including gross margin trajectory, circular financing risks in AI infrastructure, and whether market share can be defended against competition from ASICs and AMD." — [BigGo Finance summary of Morgan Stanley note](https://finance.biggo.com/news/e5cb8cda-c737-4bf3-9187-7e79e527bf0d)
- **Reaction-sizing test (TradingKey summary of Morgan Stanley, Aug 2026):** stock reaction depends on whether "results beat expectations, guidance is raised, and demand for Rubin is confirmed"; "if revenue falls short of expectations or gross margin guidance is revised downward, the drop could exceed 10%." — [TradingKey, Nvidia Q2 Earnings Preview](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262125952-nvidia-stock-q2-earnings-guidance-gross-margin-rubin-blackwell-china-tradingkey)
- **Buy-side "what would change my mind" test (Melius, Ben Reitzes, 2026-06-25):** on hyperscalers, "Call me when they figure it out. I don't want to invest in that stuff while they're figuring out the consumption versus subscription." He names the evidence that would change the view: July earnings "with CapEx guidance and AI revenue disclosures." — [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/06/25/melius-research-buy-the-chip-dip-skip-hyperscalers-until-ai-payoff-becomes-clear/)
- **Fundamental Edge (pod-shop trainer Brett Caughran) framework:** graduates should be able to "grasp a stock's key drivers, understand what is discounted in stock, develop a differentiated view ... dispassionately monitor and adjust a position"; selection framework "FEV (fundamentals, expectations, valuation)". — [Fundamental Edge, Analyst Academy](https://www.fundamentedge.com/analyst-academy) (excerpt)
- **Reference only (already covered by team):** Steinhardt's variant perception (No Bull), Mauboussin's price-implied expectations, Morgan Stanley Counterpoint "Who Is On the Other Side?" — [Morgan Stanley IM PDF](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/publication/insights/articles/article_whoisontheotherside.pdf) (older).

### Inferences
- Written as checkable rules, the practitioner tests reduce to five: (1) **Swing test** — set the question's answer to consensus; if the rating/target does not change, it is a line item, not a debate. (2) **Price-gap test** — the debate must sit where your number differs from the price-implied number, not merely from sell-side consensus. (3) **Settlement test** — name a print/KPI and a date on which the question resolves in both directions; "long-term growth" is not a catalyst. (4) **Kill test** — the sentence "wrong if X" must be writable with an observable X. (5) **Cap test** — sell-side previews hold 3–5 debates per name per quarter (BofA: five; Morgan Stanley: three structural); more than that is a checklist, not a thesis.
- The Morgan Stanley four-quarter observation is the operational form of "too fine": a revenue beat vs consensus stopped moving the stock, so the beat itself is no longer a debate; the debates migrated to margin trajectory, financing circularity and share.

### Gaps
- No primary pod-shop (Citadel/Millennium/Point72) analyst statement of an explicit numeric materiality threshold (e.g., "x% of EPS") was found; the only numeric rule is the reset-to-consensus test above. The Brett Caughran podcast on pod shops ([Yet Another Value Podcast episode](https://creators.spotify.com/pod/profile/andrew-walker260/episodes/Fundamental-Edges-Brett-Caughran-on-setting-up-for-success-in-the-pod-shop-world-e2eg9f2)) was not transcribed in search.
- Morgan Stanley "Big Debates 2026" full page timed out; only the method description and example debate titles were captured.

### Inferences
- The NVDA debate list is written in a fixed grammar: *driver + numeric bogey + quarter*. Examples: "Rubin ~150k units / ~$9B in Q3 FY27"; "GM 75% vs 72–73%"; "$884B DC CY26–27 vs Street $785B"; "China: $0 in guide vs $5–30B potential". A debate without one of the three parts (e.g., "competition from TPUs") is carried as a qualitative risk, not a modelled line, until a number appears (Goldman's "$635B even if ASICs take share").
- Houses differ on the number, not on the question; that is what makes the debate tradable (Morgan Stanley $884B vs Street $785B; Bernstein 72–73% vs BofA 75%).

## Key question 3: How is the hyperscaler capex debate phrased in 2026 (aggregate, per company, ratio, depreciation) and what thresholds are named?

### Takeaway
The 2026 debate is phrased in four nested forms, each with its own bogey: aggregate ~$700B (2026 guidance sum), per-company guides ($220B / $195–205B / $175B / $130–145B), ratios (capex/OCF >100% at Amazon; debt from 9% to 32% of capex; FCF sign), and depreciation life (6 years vs Burry's 2–3). Since Q2 2026 the debate moved from "will they keep spending" to "when does ROIC show", with Alphabet's first negative FCF (−$5.9B) as the trigger print.

- **Aggregate and per-company bogeys:** "Company guidance for calendar 2026 sums to roughly $700B of intended spend, with Amazon (~$220B), Alphabet/Google ($195–205B), Meta ($130–145B), and Microsoft (~$175B)". — [TMT Finance](https://www.tmtfinance.com/intel/2026-hyperscaler-capex-tops-us700bn-analysis) (excerpt)
- **FactSet (2026-07-23), ratios and sign changes:** five hyperscalers' capex "expected to exceed $690bn" (">80% YoY"), "close to $800bn (including finance leases and customer pre-payments)"; FY26 free cash flows "move close to zero or turn negative for all except Alphabet and Microsoft"; incremental debt "from 9% of capex in FY24 to 32% LTM by mid-2026"; lease commitments "~$820bn", total debt "~$700bn"; "Investors have grown more cautious on capex, keeping the group's share prices suppressed since late 2025." — [FactSet Insight](https://insight.factset.com/hyperscalers-tap-external-financing-as-ai-capex-outruns-cash-flow)

### Inferences
- The capex debate is **not** held at the aggregate $700B level by stock analysts; the aggregate is the supplier-side bogey (NVDA/TSMC demand). For each hyperscaler the debate is held as a *ratio with a sign*: FCF sign (Alphabet negative), capex/OCF (>100% at Amazon), debt share of capex (32%), and depreciation life (a disclosed accounting policy, read as a leading indicator). Those are the settling prints; "AI ROI" is the narrative above them.
- The demand-side bogey is now explicit and dated: Gerstner's "$180B combined AI-lab run-rate by year-end 2026"; Sohn pitches "$200B Anthropic+OpenAI by year-end 2026". That is the one number that converts the "is it a bubble" narrative into a settleable question.

### Gaps
- Oracle-specific 2026 debate wording (RPO, FY27 capex) was not retrieved. Named per-company 2026 capex/revenue-ratio thresholds from sell-side (e.g., "capex above x% of revenue is a downgrade") were not found; the ratios above come from FactSet and Silicon Analysts, not from a rating rule.

### Inferences
- Read side by side, the houses disagree on *where the debate is held*, not on the list: Goldman and JPM hold it at the hyperscaler's income statement (revenue required, OCF vs capex, debt share); Morgan Stanley holds it at the NVDA print (Rubin units, GM %, a >10% reaction band); SemiAnalysis holds it one layer below any company (wafers, HBM, tokens/MW) and asks who captures the rental-price gap; Citrini holds it above every company (phases, a market-level drawdown) and so is judged by the others as unfalsifiable within a quarter.
- Only Cembalest and Goldman publish an explicit *conversion* from debate to EPS: Cembalest's 3-year-life table (−6% to −8% EPS; Oracle −17%) and Goldman's 15% ROIC → $1T revenue. These are the two models of a "settle-able" debate in this set: a disclosed accounting assumption with a sensitivity, and a required revenue with a return hurdle.
- JPM's stock format (Thesis / Valuation / Risks) does not name "debates" at all; its debates appear as the risks the target "absorbs" (China at zero, $119B commitments, rental-price declines). JPM's thematic page names them as "What to Watch For", i.e. KPIs, and names the single most salient one ("Revenue from cloud service providers").
