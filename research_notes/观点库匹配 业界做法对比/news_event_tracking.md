# News and event tracking: how streams decide "assign to an existing story/event, or start a new one" (as a model for matching new investment views against the views database)

Scope note: notes are in English; the final report is in Chinese. Every number below carries its dataset and metric. "Vendor claim" marks a statement a company makes about its own product with no independent measurement. Older results are dated; where newer work changes them, that is said. Not repeated here (already established upstream): embeddings cannot separate direction, numbers or periods; choosing among about 4 candidates beats pairwise judging.

## 1. Topic Detection and Tracking (TDT), first story detection (FSD) / new event detection (NED): formulation, the new-vs-old decision, evaluation, why thresholds were hard

### Takeaway
In classic TDT the new-vs-old decision is a single scalar: the distance from the incoming story to its nearest earlier story (or centroid), compared with a threshold. It is scored by a cost-weighted mix of misses and false alarms, usually at the best threshold found afterwards over the test set. That evaluation hides how hard it is to pick the threshold ahead of time, and Allan et al. (2000) showed that FSD accuracy is bounded by tracking accuracy. Later work gained by adding category- and entity-aware evidence, but the gains did not carry from one corpus to the next. Streaming work (Petrović 2010) solved cost (constant time per item through LSH), not the decision itself.

### Cited Findings
- **Classic decision rule.** "Each new document is then compared to the previous ones, and if its similarity to the closest document (or centroid) is below a certain threshold, the new document is declared to be a first story." This is how the UMass (Allan et al., 2000) and CMU (Yang et al., 1998) systems worked. The novelty score is the minimum distance to any earlier document. — [Petrović, Osborne, Lavrenko 2010, NAACL](https://aclanthology.org/N10-1021.pdf)
- **Evaluation hides the threshold.** The official TDT evaluation asks each system for a confidence score per story, assigned the moment the story arrives in the streaming setting. Performance is reported as DET curves and the *minimal* normalized cost: stories are sorted by score, every threshold is tried, and the best one is kept. C_det = C_miss·P_miss·P_target + C_FA·P_FA·P_non-target, and C_min is the minimum of C_det over all thresholds. — [Petrović et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/N10-1021.pdf)
- **"FSD in TDT is hard" (2000, still cited as the reference result).** Allan, Lavrenko and Jin give a method that predicts FSD error rates from tracking performance. Observed FSD performance matched the prediction, and they concluded that with the approaches of the time, FSD performance had likely reached the limits of its effectiveness: high-quality FSD would need tracking to improve to a degree considered unlikely. (The PDF's text is font-encoded and could not be extracted; this paraphrase comes from the abstract as given in the index listing. Exact wording not verified.) — [Allan, Lavrenko, Jin, CIKM 2000](https://ciir-publications.cs.umass.edu/pub/web/getpdf.php?id=206)
- **Misses cost more than false alarms, and what drives each.** "False alarms are caused when an old story is assigned a low score. Misses, which are more costly than false alarms, are caused when a new story is assigned a high score." An in-depth look at misses showed that named entities had to be isolated and treated preferentially. Generic topic terms (e.g., in health care: drugs, cost, coverage, plan, prescription) dominated the similarity between two stories about *different* events on the same issue. — [Kumaran & Allan, SIGIR 2004](https://ciir-publications.cs.umass.edu/pub/web/getpdf.php?id=485)
- **Topic-conditioned comparison: hard category partitions hurt.** Grouping stories by their LDC category and comparing only inside a category (as in Yang et al. 2002) "was a failure", because "it is possible for a story to be on multiple topics at the same time. Mistakes made by the classifier can also throw such a system off-track". It "would work well only in situations where the granularity of the categories was much finer". What worked: per-category stop lists (the top 300 terms per category), then comparison against *all* past stories whatever their category, plus per-category rules for when to trust the named-entity score versus the non-entity score (e.g., β for Elections and Legal/Criminal cases, γ for Natural Disasters and Violence/War). — [Kumaran & Allan 2004](https://ciir-publications.cs.umass.edu/pub/web/getpdf.php?id=485)
- **Gains that did not transfer.** Topic-weighted minimum cost on TDT3 went from 0.5723 (baseline) to 0.5578 (category stop lists) to 0.5229 (full multi-stage system), against other systems' range of 0.5783–0.9785. On TDT4 the same systems scored 0.5144, 0.5293 and 0.5510: **the full system was worse than the baseline**. The authors attribute this to TDT4's topic mix. — [Kumaran & Allan 2004](https://ciir-publications.cs.umass.edu/pub/web/getpdf.php?id=485)
- **Streaming FSD (Petrović 2010).** LSH is used for the nearest-neighbour search. Pure LSH "yields poor results with a lot of variance", because LSH only finds a true neighbour that is already close, and a genuinely new document has none. The fix: when LSH declares a document new, the system also compares it with a fixed number (2000; values from 1000 to 3000 gave "very similar results") of the *most recent* documents through an inverted index. Bounded memory: a cap on documents per bucket (the oldest is evicted) and at most 3L comparisons per document. Parameters: k=13 hyperplanes, with L set so that the chance of missing a neighbour within distance 0.2 is below 2.5%. Twitter threading: tweet a joins the thread of its nearest neighbour b if 1−cos(a,b) < t, with t=0.5; results were "very much the same" for t in [0.5, 0.6]. — [Petrović et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/N10-1021.pdf)
- **Measured on TDT5 (English, 221,306 docs, Apr–Sep 2003).** C_min was 0.69 for UMass and 0.71 for the LSH system with the recent-documents fallback. Pure LSH scored 0.88 mean over 10 runs (sd 0.046); with variance reduction 0.70 (sd 0.004). Bounded-space variants scored 0.73–0.76. Runtime was 28 hours for UMass and 2 hours for LSH. — [Petrović et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/N10-1021.pdf)
- **Twitter (160M tweets), ranking detected threads.** Average precision for events vs. the rest: random baseline 16.5, thread size 24.1, unique users 24.5, entropy filter (<3.5) + users 34.0. Kappa between the two annotators was 0.65 on the 1000 fastest-growing threads. The FSD decision itself was *not* evaluated on Twitter ("prohibitively expensive"). — [Petrović et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/N10-1021.pdf)

### Inferences
- The classic formulation has the same shape as our "new view vs. existing view" step: one nearest-candidate score and a threshold. The literature's experience is that this scalar mixes "same topic" with "same event". Entities and time were what separated them, and only in some categories.
- Minimal-cost evaluation (an oracle threshold) gives no evidence that a threshold set beforehand will work. Any acceptance of our "is it new?" decision must fix the operating point *before* scoring, on stored data, and report misses (a duplicate view created) and false alarms (evidence attached to the wrong view) separately, with explicit costs.
- Kumaran & Allan's failure with hard category partitions maps directly onto our 16 upstream document categories. A single investment view collects evidence from 10-Ks, transcripts, news and sell-side research, so the document category should be a feature (what kind of evidence this is), never a partition of the matching space.
- The fallback in Petrović 2010 ("if nothing was found, look harder among recent items") is a reminder that a retrieval miss reads as "new". Our draft (shared affected object + top-20 by embedding) has the same failure: a new view is created whenever retrieval misses the true existing view. Retrieval recall must therefore be measured separately from the LLM's choice.

### Gaps
- The exact TDT cost parameters (C_miss, C_FA, P_target) could not be verified from a primary source in this pass: the Allan 2000 PDF text was not extractable, and Petrović 2010 gives the formula without values.
- The Yang et al. 2002 topic-conditioned novelty detection paper (KDD) was not fetched; it appears here only through Kumaran & Allan's description.

## 2. Production event and story clustering: what is publicly documented

### Takeaway
Documented production systems all follow the same pattern:
- Each item is compared against **recent** clusters only (time window or cluster pruning), using a centroid of sparse keyword/TF-IDF vectors enriched with entities and locations.
- It is assigned by a threshold (or a learned classifier) and immutably.
- Errors are repaired by a **separate, coarser pass**: periodic full re-clustering, cluster-to-cluster linking, or merge passes.

The "same event reported by many outlets counts once" problem is handled by keeping one event record with many mention rows (GDELT) or by a novelty score that discounts later similar stories within a window (RavenPack). Measured quality is published rarely: EMM, Event Registry and Reuters Tracer give numbers. Google gives a description only, and Dataminr, Bloomberg, Feedly and AlphaSense have no public pipeline documentation that this search found.

### Cited Findings
- **Event Registry (JSI, multilingual), per-language online clustering.** Articles are TF-IDF vectors, with IDF over a 10-day window. An article joins a cluster if its cosine similarity to the cluster centroid exceeds 0.4, otherwise it starts a new cluster. The centroid is updated on every assignment. Clusters are pruned when their oldest article is more than 4 days old. A cluster becomes an "event" once its size passes a language-dependent threshold. — [Rupnik et al., JAIR 2016 (arXiv 1512.07046)](https://arxiv.org/html/1512.07046)
- **Event Registry cross-lingual cluster linking.** Candidates come from the 10 most similar articles in each language (cross-lingual similarity); their parent clusters become candidate pairs. A linear SVM then judges each pair on: linkCount and avgSimScore; concept (entity/keyword) cosine and Jaccard; hasSameLocation; timeDiff (hours); sharedDates. Overall: accuracy 78.2%, P 76.3%, R 81.6%, F1 78.9%; en–de accuracy 91.8% (F1 92.5%), en–es 87.7% (F1 87.6%). **Concept (entity) features alone reached 88.5% accuracy, against 78.3% for the cross-lingual text-similarity features alone.** Small clusters (<20 articles combined) scored 77.8% accuracy, large ones 81.2%. Scale: about 200,000 articles/day, 24 languages, about 2 million similarity computations/second. — [Rupnik et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/html/1512.07046)
- **Europe Media Monitor (EMM) / NewsExplorer (EU JRC, 2008 paper; the system has run since 2002).** Every day and for each language it builds group-average agglomerative clusters over log-likelihood keyword vectors enriched with countries, people, organisations and locations. A cluster is represented by its keywords plus the title of its **medoid** (the article closest to the centroid). Each new daily cluster is compared with every cluster of the **past 7 days**, and a link is made when keyword cosine exceeds **0.5** ("empirically derived"). The score against a story accumulates incrementally: score_i = score_{i−1} + (1−score_{i−1})·sim(c, cluster_i), over the story's clusters from the last 7 days only. If the final score exceeds 0.5 the cluster joins the story, otherwise it stays unlinked. Base rates: of an average 970 new clusters/day (9 languages, one month), 281 (29%) joined an existing story, 90 (9%) started a new story and 599 (62%) remained singletons. — [Pouliquen, Steinberger, Deguernel, Coling 2008 MMIES workshop](https://aclanthology.org/W08-1408.pdf)
- **EMM measured quality and failure modes.** Precision of story membership (an expert judged 330 random stories): German 0.94, English 0.86, Spanish 0.86, Italian 0.80, overall 0.86 (1,056/1,225 components correct). Recall could not be evaluated meaningfully. Cross-lingual story links: 59% accurate overall, 87% for the 10 largest stories (96.5% complete). Of the 40 top stories, 2 should have been merged (two English stories on Israel), and in each language one should have been split ("all China-related news merges into one story"). Stated limits: the algorithm "is rather sensitive to the starting date": different starts give different stories, sometimes "two separate parallel stories" on closely related subjects. A story "dies" after a 7-day gap and a new one starts on day 8. Story split and merge are "not currently dealt with … a non-trivial issue". Volume: about 50,000 articles/day in more than 40 languages. — [Pouliquen et al. 2008](https://aclanthology.org/W08-1408.pdf)
- **Yahoo News incremental clustering (US patent US8832105B2, filed 2011, granted 2014).** Articles younger than a set age (about 4 hours) are "transient" and may be reassigned; older ones are "fixed" and keep their cluster. Nearest neighbours come from LSH over randomly selected phrases. A transient article joins an existing cluster when the ratio of fixed to transient neighbours passes a threshold. **The whole corpus is re-clustered every set period (e.g., every 4 hours)** using LSH and correlation clustering. (Patent claims describe intended design, not measured production behaviour.) — [Google Patents, US8832105](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8832105)
- **Google News / Search "Top stories" (2019 blog; vendor description, no metrics).** Google built "story-understanding technology to map the people, places and things involved in a news story, then draw connections between them", using machine learning including BERT "to examine related articles and determine where one story ends and another begins". — [Google blog, Smarter organization of top stories in Search](https://blog.google/products/search/smarter-organization-top-stories-search/). A Google News story averaged "around 40 news articles" in a 2023 sample of 400 stories. — [Huang et al., NAACL 2024 (DiverseSumm)](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.32.pdf)
- **GDELT 2.0 (events coded in CAMEO from global news, 15-minute updates).** There are two tables. The Event table holds one row per event. The Mentions table "records all mentions of each event": "As an event is mentioned across multiple news reports, each of those mentions is recorded in the Mentions table", with the position of each mention in the article and an extraction "Confidence". NumMentions counts mentions "during the 15 minute update in which it was first seen". It and IsRootEvent describe only the *first* report and are "included for legacy purposes"; users are told to rely on the Mentions table. The event date (Day/SQLDATE) is kept apart from DATEADDED (when GDELT first saw the event). Comparing the two fields picks out "events being mentioned for the first time … or … events of a particular vintage being mentioned now (such as … events at least one week old)". The exact deduplication rule (which fields must match) is **not** stated in the codebook. — [GDELT Event Codebook V2.0](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf)
- **Reuters Tracer (Twitter → news, in production at Reuters; papers 2016–2017).** Tweets are TF-IDF vectors. Clustering has two phases: "unit clusters of 3 tweets with similar content" are formed and then merged into the pool of existing clusters. A unit cluster that does not merge "is called out as an event". The search is full rather than LSH, run on big-data infrastructure. Novelty checks drop events older than 12 hours and compare against previously reported events. Funnel: 12M tweets/day; more than 78% filtered as noise (<1% false negatives); 624,586 clustered; 16,261 clusters; 6,695 newsworthy events per day. Coverage: about 70% of news stories with a 2% Twitter sample, 95% with 10%. Lead time: on average 8–60 minutes ahead of Reuters, AP and CNN across 66 stories. — [Liu et al., Reuters Tracer, arXiv 1711.04068](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04068)
- **RavenPack (financial news analytics), novelty scoring.** The Event Novelty Score (ENS) runs from 0 to 100 and gives "the novelty of a news story within a 24-hour time window". A score of 100 means the story "was not already covered by earlier announced news". Later stories on a related event are discounted below 100. Academic users keep ENS=100 rows to take only the first story about an event. (Secondary description in an academic paper. RavenPack's own similarity-key and decay documentation is behind a licence.) — [arXiv 2307.02673 (uses RavenPack data)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.02673); vendor overview: [RavenPack News Analytics, Univ. St. Gallen library](https://www.unisg.ch/en/university/library/search-and-use/databases/ravenpack-news-analytics/)
- **Thomson Reuters / Refinitiv News Analytics novelty.** The API has novelty fields per time window (e.g., "Novelty7Day"); the exact definition was not retrieved. — [Refinitiv developer community thread](https://community.developers.refinitiv.com/discussion/comment/41438)

### Inferences
- In every documented production system, the expensive, accurate step runs **at cluster level** (EMM story linking, Event Registry cross-lingual linking, Yahoo's periodic full re-cluster, Tracer's merge phase). The cheap step runs per item. Our "duplicates among existing views → reversible merge" is the cluster-level step. It runs over a much smaller population (views, not documents) and can afford an LLM.
- The strongest single discriminator in production was entity/concept overlap (Event Registry: 88.5% from concept features alone). For us the closest structured analog is the set of affected objects. But in our domain one object (e.g., NVDA) carries many distinct drivers, so object overlap works for *blocking* and not for the decision. (Compare GVC in §3, where every event is a shooting.)
- GDELT's split into one event record plus many mention rows, keeping the event's own date apart from the date it was added, is the documented pattern for "the same event reported by many outlets counts once" and for "an old event mentioned now". For views, the analog is an evidence record keyed to the underlying fact, with many source rows, and the fact date kept apart from the ingest date. RavenPack's 24h first-story rule is a cruder version for a single entity.
- EMM's stated failure modes are exactly the risks of an immutable, order-dependent assign-or-create loop: dependence on the starting date, parallel duplicate stories, stories that die after a quiet gap, and no split or merge. Our design already allows merge. It also needs a split (or "detach evidence") operation and a way to revive a long-quiet view, which matters because investment views can be quiet for weeks between earnings.

### Gaps
- **Dataminr**: no primary public description of its event clustering or deduplication pipeline was found (searches returned unrelated patents and generic alert-dedup pages).
- **Bloomberg** news story clustering, **Feedly** (Leo deduplication), **AlphaSense** story grouping: nothing primary found in this pass. Any figures about them would be vendor marketing.
- Google News internal clustering beyond the 2019 blog: no public metrics.
- GDELT's exact event-deduplication rule is not in the codebook.
- RavenPack's Event Similarity Key and decay schedule: proprietary, not verified.

## 3. LLM-era approaches (2023–2026): retrieve-then-LLM, LLM summaries as the comparison target, periodic re-clustering

### Takeaway
Measured LLM-era work keeps the classic online loop (embed/TF-IDF, compare with recent clusters, threshold or learned create-decision) and puts the LLM at **cluster level**:
- writing a summary that becomes the cluster's representation;
- checking whether a cluster is coherent;
- merging clusters by comparing summaries;
- periodically renaming, merging or splitting.

Direct "LLM, cluster these documents" performs very badly at scale (GPT-4 few-shot CoNLL F1 10.2 on GVC). LLMs used as pairwise judges are close to trained annotators, but they are overconfident and force decisions when the information is insufficient. On standard stream-clustering benchmarks, non-LLM learned pipelines (TF-IDF + entity-aware embeddings + time + a learned create-classifier) still hold the best published numbers. **No published, measured benchmark of the specific "retrieve top-k candidates, LLM picks one or none" assign-or-create loop on a news-stream dataset was found.**

### Cited Findings
- **Learned create-decision beats a tuned threshold (pre-LLM, still the reference).** Miranda et al. (Priberam, EMNLP 2018) compare each document with all live cluster centroids using a weighted sum of cosines over TF-IDF sub-vectors (tokens, lemmas, entities × title, body, both) plus three Gaussian time features: distance to the newest, mean and oldest timestamp in the cluster, σ=72h. Weights are learned with SVM-rank (20 hard negatives per document). English test set, pairwise F1: τ grid-search 82.8 (P 96.5, R 72.4) vs. **SVM-merge** 94.1 (P 98.2, R 90.3). SVM-merge is a separate binary classifier on the *max* similarity per feature between the document and the cluster pool. Adding timestamp features raised ranker accuracy from 86.5% to 96.9% but "interferes with our optimization of τ": "high accuracy in the reranking problem does not necessarily help with identifying when new clusters need to be opened". — [Miranda et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00540)
- **Miranda et al., other measured details.** A global cross-lingual τ "could not find a reasonable τ which worked well for every possible language pair" (F1 72.7 global vs 84.0 with a pivot language). Using dense cross-lingual embeddings for monolingual clustering gave F1 74.8 (English), below the bag-of-words baseline. Time features alone: F1 61.1 (P 44.5, R 97.6). Dataset (derived from Event Registry / Rupnik et al.): English train 12,233 docs / 593 clusters, test 8,726 / 222. Document-to-cluster assignments are **immutable**. Only cluster-to-cluster (cross-lingual) assignments can be revised ("domino-toppling"), and only 1% of cross-lingual updates triggered topples. — [Miranda et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00540)
- **Entity-aware embeddings + learned creation (Saravanakumar et al., Columbia/Amazon, EACL 2021).** The architecture separates the two decisions: (1) a weighted similarity model picks the best cluster c* (an SVM trained with an adapted triplet loss over 11 document–cluster similarities: 9 TF-IDF, 1 dense, plus time); (2) a **cluster creation model** (a shallow neural network on the document–c* similarity vector) decides whether to open a new cluster. New-cluster cases were only 5% of training samples and were oversampled with SVMSMOTE. B-cubed F1 on the Miranda English test set: Laban & Hearst 2017 89.76; Miranda 2018 92.36; Staykovski 2019 94.41; Linger & Hajaiej 2020 93.86; TF-IDF only 86.36; TF-IDF+time 91.72; entity-aware S-BERT alone 68.98; **TF-IDF + entity-aware S-BERT + time 94.76**. Cluster count against a true 222: theirs 276, Staykovski 484, Miranda 326, Laban & Hearst 873 (a 79.4% reduction in erroneous extra clusters). CEAF-e (weights every cluster equally) 76.93 vs Miranda 69.57. Dense embeddings alone "end up clustering together topically related events (for instance, two different events related to soccer)". Dropping ORDINAL/CARDINAL entity types helped by about 1 point. — [Saravanakumar et al. 2021](https://cdn.amazon.science/99/5b/19d4cf374b64a130b50975856446/event-driven-news-stream-clustering-using-entity-aware-contextual-embeddings.pdf)
- **Metric caution.** B-cubed "gives more importance to large clusters" and does not catch fragmentation of small, recent events. This is why CEAF-e and the cluster count are reported. — [Saravanakumar et al. 2021](https://cdn.amazon.science/99/5b/19d4cf374b64a130b50975856446/event-driven-news-stream-clustering-using-entity-aware-contextual-embeddings.pdf). (Note: Miranda's own table reports *pairwise* F1, 94.1; Saravanakumar's reproduction of Miranda reports *B-cubed* F1, 92.36. Different metrics, not a conflict.)
- **LLM summary as cluster representation (Nakshatri et al., EMNLP Findings 2023).** Peaks in the article time series are detected first. Clusters are formed within each peak. GPT-3.5 writes a two-shot event summary from the 2 documents closest to each centroid. A cluster is discarded as incoherent if its top-5 documents do not match the summary embedding (threshold). Clusters are merged by a greedy **pairwise LLM check over summaries** (at most 2 iterations). Articles are then mapped to events by **cosine similarity to the summary embedding with a threshold**, within ±1 day of the peak. NELA data: 335k articles retrieved, 90k after temporal filtering, 11 issues. Results: entity purity 82.69 vs BERTopic 66.00 and HDBSCAN 62.55; coverage 44.29% (vs 84.04 for BERTopic, i.e., more than half the articles left unassigned); human-judged mapping precision 72.19 vs 69.87 (BERTopic) and 62.27 (HDBSCAN). **Cost: "Since all GPT calls are made on the cluster-level", the total experimental cost was under $5.** — [Nakshatri et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.274.pdf)
- **Thematic, time-weighted story representation (USTORY, Yoon et al., SIGIR 2023).** Stories are represented by a time-decayed pooling of article embeddings that are themselves re-embedded around the story's theme (keywords). Assignment confidence is a softmax over theme similarity to the stories in the current window. An article is assigned if confidence exceeds γ = 1−(1−1/|C_W|)^T ("an adjusted random assignment probability"). Unassigned articles are held and used to seed new stories in later windows. Window 7 days, slide 1 day. B³-F1 / AMI: Newsfeed (16,136 articles / 788 stories) 0.789 / 0.812; WCEP18 (47,038 / 828) 0.810 / 0.871; WCEP19 (29,931 / 519) 0.825 / 0.880, reported as +41–52% B³-F1 over baselines including Miranda et al. and Staykovski et al. — [USTORY, arXiv 2304.04099](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.04099). Follow-up: SCStory (self-supervised continual) — [arXiv 2312.03725](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.03725) (not read in detail).
- **LLM in the streaming loop vs. embeddings (Continuous Prompts, Brown, Dec 2025).** A semantic group-by over streams compares: (1) the LLM assigns each tuple to an existing group or a new one, keeping group names and descriptions; (2) the same plus **periodic refinement every 10 tuples to merge, split or rename** groups; (3) incremental embedding clustering with periodic LLM naming of sampled members. Reported (MiDe22; F1/ARI/purity): embedding-based is fastest but "over-segmented"; LLM with refinement gives the "cleanest clusters with best global consistency" at a "modest throughput cost". (Exact numbers were not extracted.) — [Chen, Raghavan, Çetintemel, arXiv 2512.03389](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03389v1)
- **Real-time story identification in production-style settings (Slovene media monitor, 2025).** BGE-M3 embeddings concatenated with NER features feed DBSTREAM/TextClust micro-clusters, with a 10-day fading window. LLM-style article summarisation was tried and "abandoned due to performance loss". A daily post-clustering merge of micro-clusters that share named entities or topic terms raised AMI from about 0.16–0.17 (online only) to **0.569**. Data: 6,400 articles over 10 days, 4,028 gold stories, 3,199 of them single-article. — [Škvorc et al., arXiv 2508.08272](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08272v1)
- **LLMs on cross-document event coreference (the formal version of "same event?").** Zhao, Xue & Min (CoNLL 2023): GPT-4 zero-shot pairwise CDEC "outperformed crowd-workers by a large margin" and was "comparable to trained annotators", but showed "tendencies of being overly confident, and force annotation decisions even when such decisions are not warranted due to insufficient information." — [Zhao, Xue, Min 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.conll-1.38/)
- **Direct LLM clustering vs. fine-tuned small model vs. both (Min et al., ACL 2024).** CoNLL F1, GPT-4 few-shot / fine-tuned small LM / LLM-summaries + small LM: ECB+ 76.8 / 85.2 / 86.7; GVC 10.2 / 84.7 / 87.4; FCC 6.1 / 71.7 / 78.7. The GPT-4 failures were partly from truncation; it also "struggles to differentiate between similar but non-coreferential events based on arguments" and clusters "based on the literal meaning of mentions". The gain came from using the LLM to *write event summaries* that a trained small model consumes. — [Min et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02148v1)
- **Cheap blocking first, then a model (Ahmed et al., ACL Findings 2023).** Only 5–10% of mention pairs are coreferent. A lemma/synonym heuristic discards most non-coreferent pairs (making the task linear rather than quadratic), and a cross-encoder judges the rest. CoNLL F1, heuristic alone / heuristic + Longformer: ECB+ 76.4 / 81.7; **GVC 51.8 / 75.0** (SOTA 83.7). GVC (all gun-violence events, with similar wording) defeats surface heuristics. — [Ahmed et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.05672)
- **LLMs on massive document streams (StreamBench, 2026).** 605 events and 15,354 documents (2016 and 2025 news). Seven LLMs (1B–123B) were tested on topic clustering, temporal QA and summarisation. "Structural cues" that organise key facts by event improved clustering by up to +4.37% and temporal QA by up to +9.63%. "Temporal reasoning remains an open challenge inherent to current LLMs." — [Lee et al., arXiv 2603.19250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19250)
- **Other LLM-era news clustering.** LLM embeddings + clustering on GDELT, with LLM-written event summaries and labels after clustering; evaluated only on the authors' own stability index (CSAI), with no baseline comparison on standard data. — [Tarekegn, arXiv 2406.10552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10552). Hierarchical multilingual Matryoshka-embedding clustering (ACL 2025): batch, not streaming. — [arXiv 2506.00277](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00277)

### Inferences
- The field consistently separates **"which existing cluster is best?"** (ranking) from **"is the best one good enough, or is this new?"** (creation), and finds that the second needs its own evidence and calibration (Miranda: 82.8 → 94.1 F1 just by replacing τ with a classifier). Our draft folds both into a single LLM call ("which, if any, has the same driver"). That is defensible, but the "none of these" branch should be measured on its own (miss vs. false-alarm counts), with "none" and "insufficient information" offered as first-class answers. GPT-4 is documented to force a choice when it should abstain.
- Where LLMs measurably help is **cluster-level text**: summaries that serve as the comparison target, coherence checks, merges. Our views already carry a fixed one-sentence claim plus structured fields, which act like the LLM summary/medoid title but never drift. That is a sound choice: the summary-as-target pattern (Nakshatri; Min et al.) is the one with measured gains.
- Per-document LLM calls are the expensive pattern. Nakshatri stayed under $5 by calling the LLM per cluster. With thousands of documents per day and several candidate views per document, the design should keep code-side blocking strict (object + driver key + recency) so the LLM sees only ambiguous cases, as Ahmed et al. do with heuristic-then-model.
- GVC (CoNLL 51.8 for surface heuristics) is the warning case for us. Many views share the same driver *type* (margin, demand, rates) across many objects. Lexical or embedding similarity will merge "same kind of driver" with "same driver", just as dense embeddings merged "two different soccer events". The discriminating fields have to be structured (object, driver identity, period/horizon) and compared in code.

### Gaps
- No published measurement was found of a "retrieve top-k candidate clusters → LLM chooses one or none" assign-or-create loop on a standard stream benchmark (Miranda/WCEP/TDT), with accuracy, miss/false-alarm rates and cost per item. Our draft therefore has no external number to aim at and must be measured on our own data.
- Continuous Prompts' exact F1/ARI numbers and its LLM call counts were not extracted.
- No industrial (vendor) LLM-in-the-loop news clustering system with published accuracy was found.

## 4. How clusters are represented, and how drift, splits, merges and late or out-of-order items are handled

### Takeaway
Representations range from a running centroid (TF-IDF or embedding mean) to a keyword+entity set with a medoid title, to a time-decayed thematic embedding, to an LLM-written summary. Centroids drift as a story evolves; fixed text (medoid title, summary) does not, but it goes stale. Drift is usually handled by **time windows** (compare only with recent members) rather than by rewriting the representation. Splits are almost never handled. Merges are handled by a separate periodic pass. Out-of-order arrival is tolerated within a time-feature bandwidth (about 3 days in Miranda) and kept apart from "old event mentioned now" (GDELT date fields).

### Cited Findings
- **Centroid, mean-updated, immutable assignment**: Miranda (per-language mean of TF-IDF sub-vectors; three time features: newest, mean and oldest member). — [Miranda et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00540). Event Registry (TF-IDF centroid updated on each assignment; cluster dropped once its oldest article passes 4 days). — [Rupnik et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/html/1512.07046). Saravanakumar (mean-pooled TF-IDF and dense vectors plus min/max/mean timestamps). — [Saravanakumar et al. 2021](https://cdn.amazon.science/99/5b/19d4cf374b64a130b50975856446/event-driven-news-stream-clustering-using-entity-aware-contextual-embeddings.pdf)
- **Keywords + entities + medoid title, compared over a sliding 7-day window**: EMM compares a new cluster only with the story's clusters from the last 7 days, "as stories change over time and the purpose is to link the newest events to existing stories". — [Pouliquen et al. 2008](https://aclanthology.org/W08-1408.pdf)
- **Time-decayed thematic embedding**: USTORY weights members by exp(−|t_a − t_i|/δ) relative to the incoming article, so the story's representation is time-local to each query. — [USTORY](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.04099)
- **LLM summary as the target**: Nakshatri (summary embedding used both for the coherence check and for assignment). — [Nakshatri et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.274.pdf). Min et al. (LLM event summaries fed to a trained comparer). — [Min et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02148v1)
- **Time is a strong, partial signal**: Miranda time-only F1 61.1 (R 97.6), but it adds a lot to recall in the ensemble. — [Miranda et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00540). Saravanakumar: without time, random-order streaming merged distinct events ("clusters that are incorrectly merged in the absence of temporal information"). — [Saravanakumar et al. 2021](https://cdn.amazon.science/99/5b/19d4cf374b64a130b50975856446/event-driven-news-stream-clustering-using-entity-aware-contextual-embeddings.pdf)
- **Out-of-order tolerance**: Miranda's σ = 72h "shows a relative robustness to reordering of the articles – articles within 3 days of each other could appear anywhere in that window". — [Miranda et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00540)
- **Late correction designs**: Yahoo, transient (<4h) vs fixed articles plus a full re-cluster every period. — [US8832105](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8832105). Miranda, cluster-level domino-toppling (1% of updates). — [Miranda et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00540). Continuous Prompts, refinement every 10 tuples (merge/split/rename). — [arXiv 2512.03389](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03389v1). Slovene monitor, daily entity-based merge (AMI 0.17 → 0.57). — [arXiv 2508.08272](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08272v1). Nakshatri, LLM pairwise summary merge. — [Nakshatri et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.274.pdf)
- **Splits are rarely solved**: EMM, "stories can split or merge (an issue not currently dealt with), but this is a non-trivial issue"; the China case is a story that should have been split. — [Pouliquen et al. 2008](https://aclanthology.org/W08-1408.pdf)
- **Old events mentioned now**: GDELT keeps the event date apart from DATEADDED and records every later mention against the original event, so "first mention" can be told from "mention of an event at least one week old". — [GDELT Codebook V2.0](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf)

### Inferences
- Our views have a never-reworded claim, i.e., a fixed representation like EMM's medoid title or an LLM summary. Unlike news stories, views are *meant* not to drift. When the underlying argument changes, the right move is a new view (plus a merge or supersede link), not a moving centroid. This avoids the centroid-drift class entirely. In exchange, the "new vs. same" decision must allow "related but different driver", which news systems handle only by time windows.
- News systems lean on short windows (4h to 10 days) because events are short. Views live for months, and their deadlines come from confirm/falsify conditions, so recency windows cannot be the main blocking key. The usable analog is each view's own horizon and deadlines (the evidence date must fall inside the view's open period), which code can check.
- Late-arriving or out-of-order documents (e.g., a filing covering a prior period, a transcript ingested late) call for GDELT's two dates: the evidence's fact/period date vs. its ingest date. A document that restates an already-counted fact should attach as a mention, not as new evidence.
- A split operation ("detach these evidence items into a new view") is the gap nobody fills well. With reversible merges and evidence-level provenance, our design can support it, and it should, since EMM documents the "everything about China in one story" failure.

### Gaps
- No measured comparison of centroid vs. medoid vs. LLM-summary representations on the same streaming benchmark was found.
- No published production numbers on how often merge or split corrections fire (beyond Miranda's 1% topple rate and EMM's 2-of-40 merge and 1-per-language split counts).

## 5. Lessons that transfer to the views database: what to compare against, what to compute in code, when to use an LLM, how to recover from errors

### Takeaway
The field's settled architecture is:
1. **code-side blocking** on entities and time;
2. **ranking** of a few candidates;
3. a **separately calibrated create-decision**;
4. **immutable per-item assignment**;
5. a **periodic cluster-level correction pass** (merge, and rarely split), which is where expensive judgment (now LLMs) pays off.

Mapped to our views: block in code by affected object + driver key + view open period; let the LLM judge "same driver?" only over the few candidates and only where code cannot decide; give "none" and "insufficient" equal standing; keep evidence-level provenance so merges are reversible and splits are possible; and measure misses and false alarms separately against stored cases.

### Cited Findings
- Blocking before judging: Event Registry links only candidates found among the top-10 most similar articles per language. — [Rupnik et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/html/1512.07046). Ahmed et al. use a linear-time heuristic before the model (5–10% of pairs are positives). — [Ahmed et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.05672). Petrović bounds candidates to 3L LSH collisions plus 2000 recent documents. — [Petrović et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/N10-1021.pdf)
- Structured attributes compared in code as classifier features: hasSameLocation, timeDiff, sharedDates, entity Jaccard (Event Registry). — [Rupnik et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/html/1512.07046). Time features (Miranda; Saravanakumar). — [Miranda et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00540)
- The create-decision gets its own model and evaluation: Miranda's SVM-merge. — [Miranda et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.00540). Saravanakumar's cluster-creation network, with a 5% class imbalance oversampled. — [Saravanakumar et al. 2021](https://cdn.amazon.science/99/5b/19d4cf374b64a130b50975856446/event-driven-news-stream-clustering-using-entity-aware-contextual-embeddings.pdf)
- LLM judgment is strong but overconfident, forces decisions without enough information, and breaks on similar-but-distinct events. — [Zhao, Xue, Min 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.conll-1.38/); [Min et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02148v1)
- Cluster-level LLM use is cheap (<$5 for a whole study). — [Nakshatri et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.274.pdf)
- Base rates: in EMM, 29% of new daily clusters joined an existing story, 9% started one and 62% stayed singletons. — [Pouliquen et al. 2008](https://aclanthology.org/W08-1408.pdf). In Saravanakumar's training stream, only about 5% of documents opened a new cluster. — [Saravanakumar et al. 2021](https://cdn.amazon.science/99/5b/19d4cf374b64a130b50975856446/event-driven-news-stream-clustering-using-entity-aware-contextual-embeddings.pdf)
- Repeat coverage counted once: GDELT keeps one event row with many mention rows. — [GDELT Codebook](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf). RavenPack's ENS gives the first story in 24h 100 and discounts later ones. — [arXiv 2307.02673](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.02673)
- Order dependence and immutability cause parallel duplicates and stories that die after a gap; split and merge are needed but hard. — [Pouliquen et al. 2008](https://aclanthology.org/W08-1408.pdf)

### Inferences
These are mappings to our design, not measured results.
- **What to compare a new candidate view against:** the existing view's fixed claim plus its structured fields (driver, objects with signs, direction, horizon, open deadlines), not a centroid of its evidence. This follows the summary-as-target pattern, which has measured gains, and avoids drift.
- **What code should decide:**
  - blocking (shared affected object; driver key if one is extracted; view status active; evidence date inside the view's open period);
  - direction and horizon comparison (already in the draft, consistent with the field's use of structured features);
  - evidence de-duplication of the same fact across outlets (fact key + fact date vs. ingest date, as in GDELT's mentions);
  - the merge bookkeeping.
- **What the LLM should decide:** only "is this the same driver as one of these ≤4 views, or none, or cannot tell". Ask it once per candidate view, not per document pair, and record the abstentions.
- **Measure the two halves separately on stored data:**
  - retrieval recall (is the true existing view inside the top 4?): a miss there becomes a false "new" whatever the LLM does;
  - the create-decision: misses (duplicate view) vs. false alarms (evidence on the wrong view). Weight them by product cost. A wrongly attached piece of evidence silently corrupts a thesis; a duplicate view is visible and fixable by merge. That argues for leaning toward "new + later merge" when the LLM is unsure, the same asymmetry news systems accept with a periodic merge pass.
- **Recovery:** keep per-evidence provenance and make every attach, merge and split an event in an append-only log (Miranda's immutable item assignments, plus reversible cluster-level operations), so that a periodic cluster-level pass over active views (cheap, since there are far fewer views than documents) can merge duplicates and detach mis-attached evidence. Also needed: a revival path for views that went quiet (EMM's "story dies after 7 days" failure).
- **Do not partition matching by document category** (Kumaran & Allan's negative result). Use the category as a feature of the evidence.
- **Do not trust a single global threshold** (Miranda's cross-lingual τ; Kumaran's TDT3 → TDT4 reversal). Whatever decides "new" is accepted at an operating point fixed before scoring, on held-out stored data.

### Gaps
- None of the surveyed systems handles *directional claims*: news clustering and event coreference judge "same event", not "same argued view with a direction". The "opposite direction → weakening evidence" step has no direct analog in this literature; it is closer to stance detection and was outside this search.
- No source gives a measured cost per item for an LLM-in-the-loop assign-or-create stream at thousands of items per day.
