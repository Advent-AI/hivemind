# Limits of text embeddings for deciding sameness, and retrieval designs that work around them

Scope note: these notes cover where single-vector text embeddings fail at "is this the same view?" (opposite direction, numbers, dates, entities), and which retrieval designs have published evidence behind them. Current as of 2026-10-17. Each finding is marked by date. "Vendor claim" means the number comes from the company selling the product. "Preprint" means not peer-reviewed. Our internal context (from the brief, not from these sources): the same sentence about two quarters scored cosine 0.988, and only 34% of pairs at cosine ≥ 0.95 were true repeats.

## 1. Negation and opposite direction: do embeddings score "X will rise" and "X will fall" as near-identical, and do 2025–2026 models fix it?

### Takeaway
Yes, they do, and no, newer models have not fixed it. Every bi-encoder (single-vector) model measured in 2025–2026 stays near or below random on negation-contrast retrieval. Even the best 7B LLM-based embedder reaches only 39% pairwise accuracy on NevIR, where random is 25%. Cross-encoders and listwise LLM rerankers do much better (65–77%), but they are still not reliable. Cosine mostly measures how much the wording changed, so a one-word reversal ("tighten" → "ease", "withhold" → "administer") scores above a genuine paraphrase.
中文要点：向量相似度衡量的是"措辞改了多少"，而不是"意思是否相同"；方向相反的一句话往往比同义改写得分更高。2025–2026 年的 LLM 底座向量模型没有解决这个问题，只有 cross-encoder 或 LLM 重排能部分区分。

### Cited Findings
- **NevIR re-run on 2024–2025 models (Feb 2025, SIGIR 2025 reproducibility paper).** In the NevIR pairwise task (two documents and two queries differing only by a negation; random baseline = 25%), the bi-encoders scored: GritLM-7B 39.0%, OpenAI text-embedding-3-large 22.6%, Promptriever-mistral-7B 19.7%, gte-Qwen2-7B-instruct 19.0%, RepLlama-7B 13.0%, all-mpnet-base-v2 8.1%. Late-interaction models scored ColBERTv1 19.8% and ColBERTv2 12.8%. Sparse models scored SPLADEv3 9.9% and TF-IDF 2.0%. Cross-encoders scored Jina-reranker-v2 65.2%, MonoT5-3B 50.6%, bge-reranker-v2-m3 43.5%. Listwise LLM rerankers scored o3-mini 77.3% (best), RankGPT-4o 70.1%, RankGPT-4o-mini 64.1%, Qwen2-7B-Instruct 36.9% — [Reproducing NevIR (arXiv 2502.13506)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13506)
- Fine-tuning on NevIR lifts cross-encoders to about 75% and bi-encoders or late-interaction models to about 50%. It also overfits. Selected on NevIR alone, multi-qa-mpnet reached 50.1% NevIR but MS MARCO MRR@10 fell to 0.06. Under a trade-off selection it scored 48.9% NevIR at MRR@10 0.20. Only the cross-encoder category transferred between NevIR and ExcluIR. Bi-encoders and listwise LLM rerankers showed "limited to no generalisability" — [Reproducing NevIR](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13506)
- **Negation taxonomy (Petcu, Bhargav, de Rijke, Kanoulas; EMNLP Findings 2025).** NevIR covers few negation types: only 79 sentential, 54 affixal and 44 implicit instances. More than 81% of ExcluIR is exclusionary. In their synthetic benchmarks, cross-encoders and LLM rerankers were best, exclusion was "significantly more difficult to encode", and fine-tuning ColBERT took it from 0.21 to 0.48 pairwise accuracy. Fine-tuning on synthetic negation data "further harms generalisation on MSMarco". Architecture and training objective mattered more than model size — [A Comprehensive Taxonomy of Negation for NLP and Neural Retrievers](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22337v3) / [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.839/)
- **Implicit and lexical negation is our case.** The taxonomy has a "lexical negation" class. It covers implicit verbs (refuse, deny, avoid, fail) and contrasting antonyms (polar pairs). "Approved vs blocked" and "tighten vs ease" fall in this class, not under the word "not" — [Petcu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22337v3)
- **SentenceSmith (Univ. Zurich, EMNLP 2025).** Controlled AMR-graph edits (polarity flip, role swap, hypernym substitution) were used to build hard negatives that break a paraphrase. On the harder GPTP set the scores (TACC) were: best model sentence-t5-large 0.820, worst 0.326. E5-Mistral-7B scored 0.416 and GTE-Qwen-7B 0.540, which puts them in the "lower-middle rankings". Both 7B models "get more than 50% of [polarity] cases wrong". Role swap was the hardest edit on average. The ranking correlated only 0.54 (Spearman) with MTEB. cde-small-v1, the best MTEB model under 400M parameters in Oct 2024, ranked 20th here — [Sentence Smith: Controllable Edits for Evaluating Text Embeddings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1343.pdf)
- **Similarity-gate audit (Frias, Aug 2026; single-author preprint).** The paper tested shipped agent "quality gates" that threshold cosine: dedup filters, semantic caches and drift guards. A production drift guard (nomic-embed-text-v1.5 at MRL-256) caught 0 of 56 meaning-breaking mutations. The edit "withhold the study drug" → "administer the study drug" scored cosine 0.9608 against a firing line of 0.60. Across 90 configuration × threshold × task cells, balanced accuracy never exceeded 0.700 (median 0.525). The lexical-overlap contrast was recovered at stratified AUROC 0.975–1.000 for all nine encoder configurations, while decision content scored only 0.440–0.815. In the authors' words, cosine measures "how much did the wording change". When overlap was matched, the best encoders separated reversal from paraphrase: bge AUROC 0.900, mxbai 0.895, e5 variants 0.85–0.87, gte 0.815. The obvious repairs failed on held-out data. A logistic gate on (cosine, token-Jaccard) scored 0.750 in-sample but 0.533 held-out, and an NLI cross-encoder drop-in scored 0.831 → 0.533 held-out — [Similarity Gates Approve Reversals (arXiv 2608.10216)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10216)
- The same paper cites older work. Blagec et al. (2019) found negation and antonym subsets scoring *above* genuinely similar pairs on every model tested, at average cosines of 0.93–0.999. Anschütz et al. (2023) found that evaluation metrics learned from such models are insensitive to negation. This is an older, pre-LLM-embedder result, cited second-hand — [Frias 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10216)
- **Why cosine cannot express contradiction (ICML 2025).** Cosine is transitive. If A is similar to B and B to C, then A is similar to C. A paraphrase pair can both contradict a third sentence without contradicting each other, so "contradicts" cannot be a cosine neighbourhood. Measured zero-shot cosine baselines for contradiction retrieval (NDCG@10) were Arguana GTE-large 0.725, UAE 0.683 and BGE-base 0.658, and synthetic MSMARCO/HotpotQA contradiction sets about 0.60. SparseCL adds a Hoyer-sparsity score to cosine after special training. It raised Arguana GTE to 0.797 (+9.9%), synthetic MSMARCO to 0.953 and HotpotQA to 0.977 — [SparseCL (arXiv 2406.10746)](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10746v1) / [ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/xu25s.html)
- **Negation adapter (Cao, Amadeus; 2025).** The task was to pick the true paraphrase over the negated sentence (STSB-derived). Universal embedders averaged 77.25% accuracy (mxbai-embed-large 83.64%, e5-base-v2 72.55%). Parameter-free dimension re-weighting raised the average to 81.93%. On the harder SemAntoNeg benchmark (negation + antonym + double negation), universal models averaged 58.64% (re-weighted 61.02%) and older contextual models 33.32%. Gains were small on complex negation — [Semantic Adapter for Universal Text Embeddings (arXiv 2504.00584)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.00584)
- **HedgeMPNet (Melbourne/MBZUAI, Jul 2025).** all-mpnet-base-v2 was fine-tuned on 248k contrastive triples distilled from GPT-3.5, covering negation variants and hedged variants. NevIR rose from 8.10 to 40.56 and ExcluIR from 69.29 to 73.09. MTEB average fell from 58.79 to 57.14. Targeted contrastive data roughly matches a 7B embedder on NevIR, but still sits near 40% — [Learning Robust Negation Text Representations (arXiv 2507.12782)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12782v1)
- **"This is not a Dataset" (EMNLP 2023; older).** The benchmark has about 400k commonsense sentences, roughly two-thirds negated. LLMs of 2023 classified affirmative sentences well but struggled with negated ones, "often relying on superficial cues". Fine-tuning helped, but generalisation stayed poor — [García-Ferrero et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.531)
- **The failure persists in 2026 multimodal embedders.** Audio-language embedding models map affirmative and negated captions to nearly identical representations. The failure persists for a recent multimodal LLM-based embedding model — [The Sound of Absence (arXiv 2607.12290)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12290)

### Inferences
- For our identity rule, embeddings cannot decide direction. Measured evidence through Aug 2026 shows that "controls will tighten" vs "controls will ease" will usually sit closer than a fresh paraphrase of the same view. Direction has to be an explicit field (an enum), compared by code. This follows the project rule "Code over prose".
- This weakness is useful at candidate generation. A document arguing "controls will ease" will retrieve the existing "will tighten" view, which is exactly what we need in order to route it as evidence against that view. So embeddings should be used to find same-driver candidates regardless of direction, and never to judge direction.
- Our 34%-precision-at-≥0.95 result matches this literature. A high cosine marks wording overlap. Among high-overlap pairs, true repeats are mixed with reversals and with other periods (section 2).
- If a model is used to judge direction, the evidence ranks the choices in this order: listwise or pointwise LLM judge (≈64–77% on NevIR) > cross-encoder (43–65%) > bi-encoder (≤39%). None is reliable alone. The LLM judge should be given the two structured records, not two free-text sentences.

### Gaps
- I found no published NevIR, ExcluIR or SentenceSmith numbers for the newest commercial or open embedders: Qwen3-Embedding, Gemini embedding, voyage-3.5/4, Cohere embed-v4. The latest bi-encoder evidence is GritLM, gte-Qwen2 and text-embedding-3-large (Reproducing NevIR, Feb 2025), plus E5-Mistral and GTE-Qwen (SentenceSmith, 2025).
- I found no benchmark of directional or stance reversal on financial or forecast statements ("will rise/fall", "approved/blocked"). The closest are lexical-negation subsets in the taxonomy paper and the instruction reversals in the 2026 audit.
- The 2026 similarity-gate audit is a single-author preprint on instruction text, not financial claims. Its NLI-fails-held-out result rests on a small held-out set (CI [0.36, 0.71]).

## 2. Numbers, time and entities: are embeddings insensitive to numbers, units, dates, fiscal periods and which company did it?

### Takeaway
Largely yes. On a financial numeracy benchmark, 13 embedders (including Qwen3-Embedding-8B, NV-Embed-v2, text-embedding-3-small and the finance model Fin-E5) averaged 54% against 50% chance. Four-digit integers, the shape of years and many reported figures, scored at chance. Dense retrievers are "not trained to make temporal distinctions". On entity-centric questions, older dense retrievers lost to BM25 by about 22 points on average. Our 0.988 cosine for one sentence about two quarters is the expected behaviour, not an anomaly.
中文要点：数字、年份/季度、主体（哪家公司）在向量里几乎不被区分；"同一句话、不同季度"得 0.988 是已知的系统性行为。期间、主体、数值必须作为结构化字段由代码比较。

### Cited Findings
- **Numeracy gap (HKUST; EACL Findings 2026).** EmbedNum-1K has 1,000 synthetic financial triples (Q, A+, A−) where the two answers differ only in a number. Example: Q "Who owns over 15% of the company?", A+ "Alice owns a 20% stake", A− "Alice owns a 5% stake". Mean accuracy over 13 models was 0.54 against 0.5 chance. LLM-based models scored 0.56 on average and encoder models 0.51. Original-format scores: Qwen3-Embedding-8B 53.7%, finance-embedding 53.4%, text-embedding-3-small 52.7%, NV-Embed-v2 52.1%, Fin-E5 51.7%, e5-mistral-7b 51.5%. Accuracy varied by up to 12 points with number format. It was best on 0.x numbers (0.62) and "struggling most with 4-digit integers… at a random guessing level". Values such as 8% and 0.08 are treated differently, and better text skills do not imply better numeracy — [Revealing the Numeracy Gap (arXiv 2509.05691)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.05691) / [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.289/)
  - Caveat: this task asks for a comparison against a threshold ("over 15%"), so it measures numeric reasoning, not only whether two numbers are distinguished. It is still the closest published measurement on financial sentences.
- **Temporal blindness (TEMPS, Sep 2026; preprint).** "Dense retrievers inherit their representations from encoders such as BERT, ModernBERT and ELECTRA, which are not trained to make temporal distinctions". They match on topic but miss temporal fit. A temporal adapter improved TS-Retriever R@1 from 19.92 to 25.39 over the prior temporal state of the art. E5-base TimeQA MRR rose 60.16%, and RAG exact match rose from 0.2915 to 0.3845. Long-document QA systems reach 46% on TimeQA against 87% for humans. The paper gives no cosine numbers for same-sentence-different-date pairs — [TEMPS (arXiv 2609.28048)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28048v1)
- **Time-specifier retrieval (KAIST, Jul 2025).** Plain fine-tuning for time breaks other retrieval. Contriever fine-tuned on TimeQA improved TimeQA R@20 from 64.07 to 71.12, but Natural Questions R@20 collapsed from 53.13 to 22.69. Merging per-time-specifier models raised TimeQA from 64.07 to 83.49 and Nobel Prize from 51.40 to 75.58, with NQ roughly flat (+0.13) and MS MARCO −1.71 — [Temporal IR via Time-Specifier Model Merging (arXiv 2507.06782)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06782)
- TEMPO, a 2026 temporal-reasoning retrieval benchmark, reports a best NDCG@10 of 32.0 and dense retrievers at 21.4–30.4. This is a search-result summary; I did not fetch the full page — [TEMPO benchmark](https://tempo-bench.github.io/)
- **Entities (EMNLP 2021; older).** On EntityQuestions (simple Wikidata-fact questions), DPR scored 49.7% against BM25 72.0% on average, a gap of up to 60 points absolute on some patterns. Dense retrievers "can only generalize to common entities unless the question pattern is explicitly observed during training" — [Simple Entity-Centric Questions Challenge Dense Retrievers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08535)
- **2025 confirmation that lexical matching still wins on attribute combinations.** In LIMIT (50k documents, "who likes X?", two relevant documents per query), the single-vector state of the art scored Recall@100 as follows: Gemini Embeddings 10.0, Qwen3 Embeddings 4.5, GritLM 10.4, E5-Mistral 8.3, Promptriever 18.9. BM25 scored 93.6 and GTE-ModernColBERT (multi-vector) 54.8. The authors prove that the number of distinct top-k sets a query can return is bounded by the embedding dimension — [On the Theoretical Limitations of Embedding-Based Retrieval (Google DeepMind, ICLR 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21038v1)
- **Who did what to whom.** In SentenceSmith, a role swap ("A bites B" → "B bites A") was the hardest edit on average across the 29 main-table embedders. This is the same failure as "acquirer vs target" or "exporter vs importer" — [Sentence Smith (EMNLP 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1343.pdf)
- **Finance-domain mismatch (FinMTEB, EMNLP 2025).** The benchmark has 64 financial datasets (news, annual reports, ESG, filings, earnings-call transcripts). General models' MTEB scores are "not correlated" with their FinMTEB scores. A bag-of-words baseline beat dense embeddings on financial semantic textual similarity — [FinMTEB (arXiv 2502.10990)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10990v2)

### Inferences
- The internal 0.988 case (same sentence, two quarters) is what this literature predicts. "Q2 FY2025" vs "Q3 FY2025" changes one token, and years are 4-digit integers, which scored at chance. No re-weighting or model swap found in this research fixes this reliably, so the fiscal period must be a structured field matched by code.
- The same applies to issuer identity. Two companies reporting the same kind of event, such as "export licence revoked", will embed close together. Blocking by entity identifier (CIK, ticker, LEI) before any similarity step removes this class by construction. A fuzzy entity match inside the embedding is not needed.
- Numbers in a view's confirm or falsify conditions (thresholds, percentages) should be parsed into typed fields, with units and scale normalised (8% vs 0.08) by code. The embedding should not be expected to preserve them.

### Gaps
- No published study measures cosine for "identical sentence, different fiscal quarter" across current models. Our internal 0.988 may be the best measurement available. It should be checked across the candidate models on our own stored pairs.
- No published measurement of entity-swap sensitivity in 2025–2026 LLM-based embedders was found. EntityQuestions (2021) and LIMIT (2025) are indirect.

## 3. Mitigations: which designs have evidence behind them (metadata blocking, hybrid BM25 + dense with RRF, cross-encoder or LLM re-ranking, a structured canonical form, multi-vector, contrastive fine-tuning)?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced pattern is a cascade. A cheap, high-recall candidate stage (lexical plus dense, fused by rank) feeds a precise judge (a cross-encoder or LLM) on a few dozen candidates. Lexical retrieval contributes most where exact tokens matter (IDs, entities, codes). Entity resolution, the closest analogue to dedup, has converged on "blocking, then an LLM pairwise matcher", where the matcher is near its ceiling and the open errors sit in blocking. Fine-tuning on negation or time pairs helps the target skill but has repeatedly damaged general retrieval. I found no published evidence on embedding a structured canonical form; it is a design hypothesis.
中文要点：有证据的做法是"粗召回（BM25+向量，RRF 融合，按主体/时间/状态预过滤）→ 精判（cross-encoder 或 LLM）"。微调能提升特定能力，但多次被测到损害通用检索。把"驱动|方向|期限|对象"结构化后再嵌入，没有找到公开实证。

### Cited Findings
- **Hybrid and lexical baselines.** BEIR (NeurIPS 2021; older) found that "BM25 is a robust baseline and re-ranking and late-interaction-based models on average achieve the best zero-shot performances, however, at high computational costs". Dense models "often underperform" out of domain — [BEIR (arXiv 2104.08663)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08663v1)
- Reciprocal rank fusion scores a document as Σ 1/(k + rank), with k = 60 in the original paper. It needs no score calibration and beat Condorcet fusion and individual learned rankers in the original study (SIGIR 2009; older) — [Cormack, Clarke & Büttcher 2009](https://plg.uwaterloo.ca/~gvcormac/cormacksigir09-rrf.pdf)
- **Contextual Retrieval (vendor measurement, Anthropic, Sep 2024).** Top-20 retrieval failure rates, averaged over codebases, fiction, arXiv and science papers: embeddings only 5.7%; contextual embeddings (a 50–100-token LLM-written context prepended to each chunk) 3.7%, −35%; plus contextual BM25 2.9%, −49%; plus the Cohere reranker 1.9%, −67%. One-time context cost was about $1.02 per million document tokens with prompt caching. BM25 is credited with exact-string matches such as an error code ("TS-999") — [Anthropic: Introducing Contextual Retrieval](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)
- Elastic reports (vendor claim) that RRF raised average BEIR nDCG@10 by 1.4% over its learned sparse encoder (ELSER) alone and by 18% over BM25 alone. This figure comes from a search-result snippet; the page failed to load with a TLS certificate error, so the number is unverified — [Elastic Search Labs: hybrid retrieval](https://www.elastic.co/search-labs/blog/improving-information-retrieval-elastic-stack-hybrid)
- LIMIT (2025) is the strongest recent evidence that lexical recall complements dense retrieval on attribute-combination queries: BM25 scored 93.6 Recall@100 against at most 18.9 for single-vector models. A Gemini 2.5 Pro long-context reranker solved all 1,000 small-LIMIT queries — [Weller et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21038v1)
- **Re-rankers.** On negation, cross-encoders and listwise LLMs beat bi-encoders by 25–40 points (see section 1) — [Reproducing NevIR](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13506)
- An empirical study of 22 reranking methods (40 variants) across TREC DL19/20, BEIR and a new FutureQueryEval set (queries absent from LLM training until May 2025) found a 5–15% drop on novel queries in every category. The average degradation was 8% for listwise, 12% for pointwise and 15% for pairwise methods. Listwise LLMs (Zephyr-7B, Vicuna-7B) were most effective "but at significant computational cost". Fine-tuned pointwise models (MonoT5-3B, Twolar-XL) were "scalable, high-performing alternatives" (EMNLP Findings 2025) — [How Good are LLM-based Rerankers?](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.16757) / [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.305.pdf)
- **The LLM-judge stage in entity resolution.** GPT-4 without task-specific training outperformed fine-tuned pre-trained language models on 3 of 4 e-commerce entity-matching datasets and matched them on bibliographic data (Peeters et al., 2023) — [Using ChatGPT for Entity Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11244v1)
- On OpenSanctions Pairs (2026), off-the-shelf LLMs reached up to 98.95% F1 (GPT-4o) against a production rule-based baseline at 91.33%. The authors conclude that pairwise matching is "approaching a practical ceiling" and that effort should shift to "blocking, clustering, and uncertainty-aware review" — [OpenSanctions Pairs (arXiv 2603.11051)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11051)
- **Multi-vector.** Late interaction helps attribute combinations (LIMIT: GTE-ModernColBERT 54.8 vs single-vector ≤ 18.9 Recall@100) but not negation (NevIR: ColBERTv1 19.8%, v2 12.8%) — [LIMIT](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21038v1); [Reproducing NevIR](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13506)
- **Contrastive fine-tuning on targeted pairs works on the target skill, with collateral damage measured each time.**
  - HedgeMPNet: NevIR rose from 8.1 to 40.6 while MTEB fell from 58.79 to 57.14 — [arXiv 2507.12782](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12782v1)
  - NevIR fine-tuning: MS MARCO MRR@10 fell to 0.06 under NevIR-only selection — [Reproducing NevIR](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13506)
  - Time fine-tuning: NQ R@20 fell from 53.13 to 22.69 — [TSM](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06782)
  - Taxonomy-data fine-tuning "further harms generalisation" — [Petcu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22337v3)
  - SparseCL avoids retraining the base model's similarity by adding a separate sparsity score — [SparseCL](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10746v1)
- **Domain models (vendor claims).** Voyage reported voyage-finance-2 at 0.831 average NDCG@10 over 11 financial retrieval datasets (FinanceBench, TAT-QA, ConvFinQA, FinQA, Apple-10K, financial news and others). The comparison scores were OpenAI text-embedding-3-large 0.762, Mistral-embed 0.740 and Cohere English v3 0.713 (Jun 2024) — [Voyage AI blog](https://blog.voyageai.com/2024/06/03/domain-specific-embeddings-finance-edition-voyage-finance-2/)
  - These are retrieval-relevance scores, not sameness or direction tests. Fin-E5, which ranks first on FinMTEB, still scored at chance on the numeracy test (51.7%) — [Numeracy gap](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.05691)
- **Structured or atomic representations (indirect evidence only).** Retrieving propositions (atomic, self-contained factoids written by an LLM) instead of passages improved Recall@20 by +10.1 for unsupervised and +2.2 for supervised dense retrievers. The largest gains came at a 100–200-word budget (EMNLP 2024) — [Dense X Retrieval (arXiv 2312.06648)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06648)
- Contextual Retrieval (above) shows that rewriting the unit before embedding (adding who, what and when context) cuts retrieval failures — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)

### Inferences
- **Blocking by structured keys is the cheapest and most complete fix for the entity and period classes.** Candidate views are restricted to the same issuer or affected entity, and the live statuses are filtered in, before any similarity step. This removes those failure classes by construction rather than by a score. This rests on the entity-resolution analogy plus the measured entity and period blindness in section 2, not on a finance-specific study. Two cases must stay outside the hard filter, or retrieval will miss them: macro or sector drivers that affect many entities (export controls affect NVIDIA, AMD, TSMC and others), and views whose deadline window overlaps the document date.
- **Hybrid matters for our data.** Tickers, CIKs, product names (H20, "Foundry Direct"), rule names (ECCN 3A090) and fiscal-period tokens are exactly the exact-token cases where BM25 adds recall over dense retrieval.
- **What to embed for the "same driver" test.** One plausible design embeds only the driver and objects text, without direction or period, so that both directions of the same driver land together. Direction and horizon would then be compared as fields, and an LLM judge would rule on candidates. This is an inference and is not measured. Per the project's diagnosis rules, it should be tested on stored pairs before it is built.
- **Fine-tuning our own embedder for direction is the weakest bet.** Every published case improved the target skill and damaged general retrieval, and none reached reliability. Contrastive pairs are better spent on calibrating a judge or reranker.

### Gaps
- I found no published experiment comparing embeddings of a templated canonical form ("driver | direction | horizon | objects") against free-text claims for dedup or claim matching.
- I found no study of investment-thesis or forecast dedup specifically. The nearest analogues are entity resolution, claim retrieval and news-event clustering. I searched news-event clustering with LLM summaries plus time windows, but fetched no measured results (several 2024–2025 ACL papers exist, e.g. [LLM-enhanced clustering for news event detection](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.10552)).
- The Elastic hybrid numbers could not be verified (fetch failed).

## 4. Choosing top-k and thresholds: recall-oriented candidates versus precision-oriented judgment; pitfalls of fixed cosine thresholds

### Takeaway
Cosine values are not comparable across models, across prompts or instruction prefixes, or across text lengths. A threshold tuned for one model is meaningless for another; one reported migration took a threshold from 0.7 to 0.2. A single fixed cutoff used as a sameness decision has been measured near chance (median balanced accuracy 0.525). Practice that holds up uses the threshold only as a recall floor. It is calibrated per model on labelled pairs from the stored data, re-measured whenever the model, prompt or text shape changes, and the decision is left to a judge.
中文要点：余弦阈值不能跨模型、跨提示词、跨文本长度通用；把固定阈值当作"相同"的判决，实测接近随机。阈值只能当召回下限，按模型在自有标注对上校准，换模型/提示词/文本形态就重测。

### Cited Findings
- **Score ranges differ by model (practitioner measurement, May 2025).** For text-embedding-ada-002, similarity values sit "in a very tight range, between 0.75 and 0.88". For text-embedding-3-small they peak at about 0.68, with most between 0.2 and 0.4. The author's advice is to calibrate on "relative similarity values, not absolute" — [Pamela Fox: A visual exploration of vector embeddings](https://blog.pamelafox.org/2025/05/a-visual-exploration-of-vector.html)
- A developer forum reports that a 0.7 relevance threshold on ada-002 had to become 0.2 after moving to text-embedding-3-small. This is an anecdote seen in a search snippet, not verified — [OpenAI community thread](https://community.openai.com/t/why-are-similarity-scores-lower-with-text-embedding-3-small/1377685)
- **Optimal thresholds are model-specific (MeanCache, 2024).** A validation sweep found the best threshold for MPNet at 0.83 (F1 0.89, precision 0.92, accuracy 0.90) and for Albert at 0.78 (F1 0.88). Llama-2 embeddings peaked at F1 0.75 at any threshold. GPTCache's fixed 0.7 produced 233 false hits on 700 unique queries against 89 for the tuned system — [MeanCache (arXiv 2403.02694)](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02694v4)
- **Fixed gates measured near chance (Aug 2026 preprint).** Five shipped operating points were observed. Across 90 configuration × threshold × task cells, balanced accuracy was at most 0.700 (median 0.525). A naively built evaluation corpus made every encoder look inverted: AUROC was exactly 0.000 in 13 of 18 cells. The authors' advice is that an evaluation corpus must balance lexical overlap against meaning (a 2 × 2 design), or it will validate the wrong threshold — [Frias 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10216)
- **Cosine can be arbitrary for some learned embeddings (Netflix, 2024).** Learned embeddings can have a degree of freedom (rescaling or rotation) that leaves the training objective unchanged but changes cosine values. A 2026 response argues that unit-normalised training removes the issue — [Steck, Ekanadham & Kallus 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.05440v1); [Response (arXiv 2602.19393)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19393v1)
- **Length shifts the scale.** Embeddings of longer texts cluster together and have higher cosine with each other ("length collapse"), which creates distributional inconsistency between short and long texts. A fix (TempScale) gained +0.94% on MTEB and +1.10% on LongEmbed (ACL 2025) — [Length-Induced Embedding Collapse (arXiv 2410.24200)](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24200v2)
- **Prompts shift the scale.** A study of 6 embedders and 11 datasets found that "the default prompt can both systematically understate or overstate performance", and that "under adversarial prompt selection any model can be promoted to first place" (May–Aug 2026) — [One prompt is not enough (arXiv 2605.22544)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22544)
- **Recall-then-precision has the strongest measured support.** Contextual Retrieval: rank fusion plus a reranker cut top-20 failures by 67% (vendor) — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval). Entity resolution: the LLM pairwise stage is near its ceiling and the errors now sit in blocking — [OpenSanctions Pairs](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11051)

### Inferences
- The decision structure that matches the evidence:
  - **Candidate stage.** Blocking by entity, status and deadline window, then BM25 + dense fused by RRF. Take a fixed top-k (tens, not a threshold) with a deliberately low floor, sized so that recall on stored true repeats is near 100%.
  - **Code checks.** Structured direction, horizon and period are compared by code.
  - **LLM judge.** It sees only the surviving candidates.
  This mirrors the project rule "Build where code decides".
- Any threshold or top-k must be recalibrated, from stored labelled pairs, when any of these change: the embedding model, its version or dimensions (MRL truncation), the instruction prefix, or what is embedded (full sentence vs canonical driver text). Our 34% precision at ≥ 0.95 is a measurement for one model and one text shape only.
- The labelled set must include matched-overlap negatives: reversals and other-period copies with the same wording. Otherwise, per the 2026 audit, the calibration will look good and measure the wrong thing.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous published guidance on choosing top-k for dedup candidate generation. Practice is to measure recall@k on one's own labelled set.
- The practitioner figures on semantic-cache thresholds (e.g. "0.95 gives low single-digit false positives") found during search had no reproducible data, so they are excluded.

## 5. Asymmetric matching: a long document (10-K, news article) against short view statements; what to embed, and do instruction prefixes matter?

### Takeaway
Embedding a whole long document against a one-sentence view is the worst-supported option. Long-text embeddings collapse toward each other, and a 10-K holds dozens of unrelated claims. The evidence favours smaller self-contained units: propositions or extracted claims (+10.1 Recall@20 unsupervised), or chunks with an LLM-written context prefix (−35% failures, vendor-measured). Instruction prefixes (query vs passage) are part of how asymmetric models were trained. Leaving them out degrades results, but models follow detailed instructions unreliably and scores move with the prompt wording.
中文要点：不要整篇嵌入长文档去匹配一句话的观点；证据支持抽取成"原子主张/命题"或"带上下文前缀的分块"再匹配。查询/文档前缀要按模型训练方式加，但不要指望复杂指令被可靠执行。

### Cited Findings
- **Granularity.** Propositions (atomic, self-contained factoids) beat passages and sentences as retrieval units: Recall@20 +10.1 for unsupervised and +2.2 for supervised dense retrievers. The biggest gain came at a 100–200-word budget, about 10 propositions (EMNLP 2024) — [Dense X Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06648)
- **Context added to chunks.** An LLM-written 50–100-token context prefix per chunk cut top-20 failures from 5.7% to 3.7%. Adding contextual BM25 brought it to 2.9%, and a reranker to 1.9% (vendor measurement) — [Anthropic Contextual Retrieval](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)
- **Long inputs.** Longer texts' embeddings cluster and have higher mutual cosine (length collapse), which makes long documents less separable from each other and shifts their score scale against short texts (ACL 2025) — [arXiv 2410.24200](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24200v2)
- **Prefixes.** E5's authors state that the "query: " and "passage: " prefixes must be used because "this is how the model is trained, otherwise you will see a performance degradation". They recommend "query:"/"passage:" for asymmetric tasks, and "query:" on both sides for symmetric tasks — [intfloat/e5-large-v2 model card](https://huggingface.co/intfloat/e5-large-v2)
- **Instruction-following is fragile (Oct 2026).** Embedding models "can fail to follow even simple task instructions when query-side distractors are included". Fine-tuning with query-side distractors gave "substantial improvements, with minimal effect on other tasks" — [Your Prompt Should Do More (Myntti et al.)](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.10508)
- Scores and rankings shift with prompt wording ("any model can be promoted to first place" under adversarial prompt selection) — [Kostiuk & Enevoldsen 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22544)
- **Generated text as the retrieval key.** HyDE (Dec 2022; older) has an LLM write a hypothetical document for the query and retrieves with that document's embedding. It significantly outperformed the unsupervised Contriever and was comparable to fine-tuned retrievers zero-shot — [HyDE (arXiv 2212.10496)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10496v1)
- **Dimension bounds instruction-style matching.** A single vector cannot represent every top-k combination at a given dimension. For instruction-based retrieval over many attribute combinations, the authors point to cross-encoders or multi-vector models — [LIMIT](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21038v1)

### Inferences
- The asymmetry disappears if both sides are made the same kind of object. Extract claims from each incoming document (driver, direction, entities, period, numbers) with the same schema the views use. Then match claim against view: symmetric, short, similar length. Also keep the claim's source span for evidence. This combines the proposition evidence with the structured-field design from sections 1–2, but the combination has not been measured.
- An LLM-written document summary is a weaker key than extracted claims. A summary of a 10-K mixes many drivers into one vector and inherits length and "average topic" effects. That is an inference from Dense X and length collapse, not a direct comparison.
- If raw chunks are also indexed (a fallback for claims the extractor missed), give each chunk a context prefix (issuer, form type, fiscal period, section) before embedding, per Contextual Retrieval. Then the period and entity tokens are at least visible to BM25.
- Use each model's documented prefix convention consistently on both index and query side. Treat the prefix as part of the model version for threshold calibration (section 4).

### Gaps
- I found no published head-to-head of "embed whole document vs chunks vs extracted claims vs LLM summary" for matching documents to short claims or theses in finance.
- The exact size of the E5 no-prefix degradation was not retrieved (only the authors' statement that it degrades).
- I found no measurement of how claim-extraction misses (recall of the extractor) bound end-to-end view retrieval. It would need to be measured on our stored documents.
