# Independent challenge: no-growth revision criterion

Original (275 characters): `a later version of the stored piece's main statement, changed: the same speaker revises that guidance, forecast or event-progress statement, an official confirms or denies that reported claim, or the original source revises it; another statement on the same topic is not this`.

The first 271-character compression ended with `Same-topic statements are not this`. That is not equivalent: a legitimate revision or denial of a stored claim normally concerns the same topic. The original excludes **another statement** on that topic, not all statements about it. Reject that wording. Moving `later` from a condition of a version to an illustrative later change would also be risky; preserve it in the criterion itself. Official confirmation and denial, the same speaker, the original source, the stored **main** statement and the other-statement exclusion are all part of D168 / the current definition; they cannot be silently removed as mere bloat.

The parent's revised candidate (274 characters) is: `identify the incoming statement's target; a later change to the stored main statement: the same speaker revises guidance, forecast or event progress, an official confirms or denies the claim, or its original source revises it; another statement on the same topic is not this`. It preserves the stated boundaries far better. However, semantic safety is not *demonstrated*: `target` may be read as the person or company, rather than the claim. A new forecast or changed situation may not explicitly name its previous statement at all. Do not add a requirement for literal naming or reject indirect but supported targets. A single incoming piece may itself contain more than one statement. The more compact `stored main statement` and `its original source` retain intended referents to a reader but should be reviewed against actual source reports; `later change` versus `later version ... changed` should not harden unavailable dates or exclude confirmation. A no-growth character count by itself does not establish equivalent behavior.

An alternative 274-character exploratory wording is: `Identify the statement the incoming piece addresses; a later change to the stored main statement: the same speaker revises guidance, forecast or event progress; an official confirms or denies it; or its original source revises it. Another statement on that topic is not this`. It says a *statement*, reducing person/company ambiguity, and keeps the other-statement exclusion, but `addresses` can also be read too narrowly for ordinary reports of changed event progress, and `that topic` is an abbreviated reference. It too is a hypothesis and its behavioral equivalence cannot be proved by reading. The experiment must preserve C073's source-reported later policy change, C035's specific response and q0 direct denial, q1 legitimate repeat, and q9 different backup claim. C021's old source already mentions the denial and C059 is disputed, so neither proves a clean new-response time boundary.

All proposed choices still lack a separately visible intermediate answer, and any observed change could be due to wording generally rather than a demonstrated internal process. Compare full identical-candidate original requests with the proposed one, on the permitted small probe and full schema checks; inspect all outcomes and do not claim a global accuracy improvement or causal reason. No model call or production edit was performed in this review.
