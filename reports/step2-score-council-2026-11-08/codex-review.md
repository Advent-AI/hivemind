The blocking issues are:

1. **The shuffled arm makes a factual claim that is deliberately false.** The proposed text calls the numbers “Retrieval cosine similarities (query-to-stored-text),” while the placebo permutes them between candidates. It also retains an “origin query.” A score transferred from candidate A to B is no longer B’s similarity; its claimed origin or top-ten retrieval status can conflict with the number. [Design]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/reports/step2-score-council-2026-11-08/EXPERIMENT-DESIGN.md:7). The whole request otherwise asks Jev to “Use only the supplied text” to classify repeat, revision, or different, using words, source, speaker and time. The new numbers can encode thesis and period information Jev does not see, and can distract from that factual comparison. [Comparison request]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step2/compare.py:40), [criteria]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step2/compare.py:54), [contract]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/PRD.md:155).

   **Recommendation:** define the shuffled arm explicitly as a *counterfactual with intentionally incorrect metadata*, and do not treat it as a neutral formatting control. Register precisely which origin and retrieval labels remain, how contradictions are detected, and what T versus P can establish. The main accuracy contrast remains real scores versus no scores, assessed against sources.

2. **The multi-query mapping and historical candidate reconstruction are specified but not demonstrated.** Search constructs one query per associated thesis, sometimes followed by an entity-wide query; it unions each top-ten list in query and rank order. [Search code]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step2/compare.py:45), [candidate construction]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step2/compare.py:100). The design promises a score for every query, including queries that did not retrieve a candidate, and an origin query, but does not define how to represent a candidate retrieved by **multiple** queries, nor freeze the actual per-query mapping for the ten cases. Its cited census reconstructs archived request text using the *saved candidate list* and checks answers; it does not reconstruct the historical top-ten searches, verify origin membership, or verify every score. [Census code]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/reports/step2-score-council-2026-11-08/inspect_saved.py:10).

   **Recommendation:** before sending, replay each selected article’s historical pre-article library and calculate each query’s complete ranked top ten, union order, all retrieval origins and every displayed score from exact cached vectors. Require equality with the saved candidate IDs and archived request. Freeze a per-case manifest and show a multi-query example, including a candidate retrieved by more than one query and a non-retrieving query.

3. **Blind review cannot support the proposed accuracy claims as written.** The design asks readers to inspect changed outcomes, selected known repeats and at least two unchanged controls. [Design]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/reports/step2-score-council-2026-11-08/EXPERIMENT-DESIGN.md:47). That can find examples of improvement or harm, but it can miss a repeat that *all* arms leave unmerged, or a wrong unchanged decision outside the controls. It also does not say how readers identify the correct group when several candidates are plausible without seeing the model’s choice.

   **Recommendation:** have independent readers classify the incoming statement against the relevant candidate set and sources without scores, rank, arm or model answer; then compare those classifications with each arm’s complete outcome, including the selected group and revisions. Record unresolved cases and actual review coverage. If only a subset is reviewed, report observed errors in that subset rather than accuracy or missed-repeat totals for all ten.

4. **The decision and stopping rule are not yet executable.** The design says to “predeclare a compact decision before send,” but also leaves “material” harm and “balancing demonstrated gain” undefined. It predicts a better X22 result and no worsening of accepted repeats, yet permits balancing harm against gain. [Design]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/reports/step2-score-council-2026-11-08/EXPERIMENT-DESIGN.md:50). Three stochastic draws do not have a natural one-to-one pairing across arms; a difference in individual draws alone does not establish a score effect.

   **Recommendation:** register how the three outcomes per case are compared, what counts as improvement or regression, how uncertainty and conflicting reader verdicts stop the probe, and the precise conditions for **signal, stop, or inconclusive**. Treat a selected-case signal as a reason for later validation, never adoption.

5. **The new path’s decisive tests remain promises.** The design lists replay and refusal controls but gives no passing test evidence for score mapping, mutation detection, archive isolation or three genuinely fresh draws. [Design]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/reports/step2-score-council-2026-11-08/EXPERIMENT-DESIGN.md:32). This matters because `jev.ask` returns an archived answer for an identical request, and the archive keeps one row per request identity. [Jev archive lookup]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/src/hivemind/jev.py:87), [archive behavior]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/src/hivemind/model.py:74).

   **Recommendation:** run a no-cost replay and negative controls that deliberately swap a score, query, candidate or origin and must fail; verify a known multi-query case and outcome tie handling; prove three separate archive misses with a fake transport; and test interruption, malformed answers and resume without silent replacement. Compare the complete serialized control request to the saved request. The existing Jev transport sorts the request before sending, so that comparison should use its actual serialization. [Transport]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/src/hivemind/jev.py:28).
