最新修复：新题匹配与证据关联

每篇文章先读取刚刚写入的最新题库。新观点进入匹配时，相关不等于已经覆盖。

流程：文章提出新题 → 查最新题库 → 匹配模型给出可能的旧题 → 分析员模型复核这些旧题是否真正覆盖新题 → 已覆盖则合并；需要单独追踪则开新题 → 文章证据写入相应问题。

错误例子：「FY2028 全公司收入」曾被错误并进「FY2027 数据中心收入」。修复后，它们被识别为不同问题；如果下一篇文章再提 FY2028 全公司收入，会找到新开的题并合并。

为什么以前错：旧匹配器把双向的“包含关系”直接当成新题属于旧题；而实验的新题缺少结构化指标时，原本的分析员复核没有介入。现在即使指标暂时缺失，也会复核，并明确区分包含方向。

运行中发现的待核对问题：已有题追踪一笔收购是否签约；新文章又提出是否完成收购。匹配模型给了“部分关系”0.44，低于 0.5，复核没有介入，于是新开了题。复核模型在相关例子中给过不同答案：有时视为同一交易，有时认为签约与完成可以分开。这个颗粒度问题还没解决；没有靠调低阈值强行合并。

另一个接线修复：给已有问题找证据时，模型输出必须使用这篇文章看到的真实题目 ID，不会把主题 ID 当成题目 ID。

# System overview

> Status: foundation frozen; pull mode and closing at the due day decided and built (D54, D356). Universe, classification, Config, dispatch, extraction and human-review intake froze on October 18, 2026; the library and matching labels on October 20; the full review completed October 22. > Design page: `docs/system-design.html` → New → Open system PRD. Recorded decisions take precedence over this document.

## Purpose

Maintain a thesis library: create theses, update their judgements and close them when due. Upstream continuously supplies classified information. Extraction reads each whole item as a senior hedge-fund analyst, alongside existing theses, and returns linked evidence and candidates with research-group associations, declaring new groups where needed. The library preserves those associations and compares identity: merge, succession, new thesis or skip; repeat, replace or new evidence. Each thesis has a question, ladder and due date. Evidence moves its state, which freezes when due.

D149 requires organisation during full-text reading, with associations included in extraction output. See [the library's grouping contract](06-views.md#research-groups-associate-during-extraction) and [extraction's responsibility](05-extraction.md#determine-associations-during-extraction). Rubin shipments and SK factory deployment are separate questions in one continuing business process. D165 integrates grouping into the default pipeline; the [improvement plan](../poc/grouping-improvement-plan.md) records implementation gaps, D155 identity changes and validation work.

## Why rebuild

The old system pulled dated batches. Category definitions existed in nine modules, configuration used five overriding layers, and time had several clocks. Explaining an item's path required reading multiple implementations. The rebuild gives each decision one owner.

## System boundary

- **Upstream, outside this system:** acquire material, classify it, identify related subjects and publication time, and deliver it. Acquisition details are deferred.
- **This system:** dispatch through the library and pull mode, which closes due questions.
- **Tests:** stream stored items in publication order through the product's path, with replay time supplied to pull mode.

```text
Upstream → Dispatch → Extraction → Thesis library ↔ Matching labels
                         │               ↑
                  read-only; groups   Pull mode: at day end, freeze
                                      and close due questions
```

## Principles

1. Start from theses. Their needs determine what to read and retain; do not build a financial-number warehouse first.
2. A thesis is a judgement; evidence records who said what in which item. Only incoming information creates evidence. Theses cannot prove each other.
3. Retain sources for every thesis, evidence item and information-triggered change, including the location in the original.
4. The stream is driven by incoming information, not the calendar. No input means no stream work; pull mode owns time.
5. Dispatch sequence determines order. Read earlier sequences plus the item's own writes (extraction reads the theses open at the start of the item's day, D251), with results equivalent to serial processing. Append knowledge without rewriting history. Store a late older statement as one more piece with its actual date, never as revising a newer one (D202); accept newly learned matters with their actual dates.
6. The research universe alone chooses subjects and categories. Upstream supplies only that scope; process every supplied item.
7. At matching time, open one thesis per subject and focus, with separate current/next-year numeric periods; manual reopening is excluded. D155 governs the POC's bounded code-owned identity. Search finds comparisons, not final identity. Resolve an evidence item's thesis before searching within it.
8. Fix questions and ladders in advance, and freeze the state when due. Rungs are verifiable outcomes. Append records and retain closed theses; the writer retires no evidence (D202).
9. Do not guess unresolved identifiers or rules; use human review.
10. Do not copy whole source material into the library. Retrieve it from the team's source store by ID.

## Decision order

<!-- generated: routing -->
1. First validate: missing required metadata, malformed subject identifiers, unreadable text or the system's own output → invalid. Previously received identical content without an explicit replaced version → merge with the original.
2. Table data: register each dataset's category once at onboarding rather than judging every record. Split registration by field when an interface returns multiple kinds of content. All later records use that category. Unregistered datasets are invalid and go to human review.
3. Text filings (SEC, exchange, court): use the filing-system code table regardless of filer. Unknown codes are invalid and go to human review.
4. Other text with a registered publication series: use the series table (T8, T10 or T11), regardless of author type. An unregistered series name goes to human review while classification continues normally.
5. Remaining text: resolve author type from the author table valid on the publication day, falling back to the channel default; then use section or genre. Official authors without a registered series → T10. Unresolved author type → invalid and human review.

## Related subjects

<!-- generated: objects -->
1. Four subject kinds, matching the research universe: company, cryptocurrency, commodity and theme. An item may concern several subjects or none.
2. Use only channel-supplied identifiers and their stated reasons, plus the author when it is a company: the issuing company for a release or the company speaking at its meeting. Record why each subject is attached. An empty or unsupported identifier kind or reason invalidates the whole item.
3. Keep identifier kinds separate because equal strings can mean different subjects: companies use CIK, ticker or company name; cryptocurrencies and commodities use their own symbols; themes use theme IDs.
4. Themes have only two origins: upstream delivery for that theme or explicit identification at upload. Do not infer them from the body text.
5. Read identifiers, not body text or URLs. A filing agent's identifier can appear in a URL; an old Amazon 8-K URL contains agent ID 1104659.
6. Resolve company, cryptocurrency and commodity identifiers through reference data. Pad CIK to 10 digits; resolve tickers as of publication day (FB identified Meta before June 9, 2022); accept only registered company aliases. Resolve theme IDs through the theme library. Preserve unresolved identifiers as received, mark them unresolved and continue processing; fill them after registration.
7. A non-company author (media, analyst, official or individual) is not itself a subject. Preserve both filer and filing subject with their roles: in a 13D, the activist is the filer and the held company is the subject.
8. Do not label incidentally mentioned companies or themes here. Extraction associates them with specific theses and evidence.

## Time rules

<!-- generated: time -->
1. Use the New York publication day. Time of day matters only for SEC filing-day calculation (rule 4). Processing order follows dispatch sequence, not publication time.
2. Each item uses one date: publication day. Its described fiscal or observation period is an attribute. The stream has no concept of today's date; only pull mode acts on time.
3. Do not separately record a channel delay of several days. No rule uses the computer clock; receipt time is for audit only.
4. SEC publication day is the legal filing day. Acceptance before 5:30 p.m. uses that day; after 5:30 p.m., SEC publication is next business morning, so use the next business day.
5. Merged duplicates use the earliest publication day: knowledge begins when the market could first see it.
6. Corrections, updates, restatements, edits and category changes create new versions without overwriting old ones.
7. An item without publication time cannot be placed on the timeline and is invalid.

## Classification rules

<!-- generated: rules -->
1. Category describes what the information is. Text/table form, length, platform and channel are attributes. A category can have both forms; form only determines whether classification uses the dataset table or text rules.
2. Each item has exactly one result: a category, merge with the original, not source material, or invalid with a reason. Use fixed rules and lookup tables, not a model.

5. Author-table entries have validity periods evaluated on publication day. Status follows tenure, not influence: an account is official during office and individual during campaigning, before inauguration or after leaving office.
6. Registered publication series take precedence over author type: ISM PMI → T11; Reuters economist survey → T8; CBO Budget Outlook → T8; CBO monthly deficit data → T11.

15. Attribute quoted statements to their original speaker at the evidence level. The containing item's category does not change.
16. Keep incidental differences as attributes: channel, platform, length, summary/full text, venue, annual/quarterly, holding status, executive status and registration. The host of an oral event does determine transcript authorship (rule 7).

# Dispatch

> Status: first version frozen, October 18, 2026. English edition: October 25, 2026 (D163).
> Design page: `docs/system-design.html` → New → Dispatch. Recorded decisions take precedence over this PRD.

## Purpose

The system's only entry point asks three questions in sequence to determine each item's destination. Items published before coverage start are not dispatched; the separate coverage-start report reading is retired (D370; [extraction](05-extraction.md#separate-earnings-report-import-removed-d370)).

The old system spread routing across daily steps, channel-specific text/table rules, filename-based model routing and category lists in nine modules. This design gives that decision one tree.

## Scope

Check completeness, previous processing and classification. Give categorised items a sequence number and send them to extraction. Send issues to human review.

Do not inspect subjects, filter categories already selected by the universe, choose research scope or perform date-based work.

## Decision tree

<!-- generated: tree -->
- An item arrives → **Question: Is the item complete?** (Check author, publication day, classification result and upstream ID.)
  - Incomplete → **Return**: This is an upstream problem; also place it in the human review pool.
  - Complete → Check processing records, **Question: Has it been processed?** (Use the upstream ID. An updated version is a new item with its own ID.)
    - Already processed → **Skip**: Do not pay again when upstream resends an item or a test reruns.
    - Not processed → Read classification, **Question: What is the classification result?**
      - Invalid → **Do not process**: Record the reason; unresolved lookup entries go to the human review pool.
      - Not source material → **Do not process**: For example, derived indicators we can calculate ourselves, or a file listing.
      - Duplicate item → **Merge with the original item**: Record another source for the original item; do not process it again.
      - Assigned to a category → **Send to extraction**: Include its category and sequence number; extraction reads each category differently.
<!-- /generated: tree -->

## Notes

<!-- generated: notes -->
- Grey boxes stop processing. The black box sends the item to extraction.
- Do not filter by subject here. Incoming items are already within the research universe, so dispatch does not decide which categories to process.
- Every category goes to extraction with its category-specific reading method; no manual selection. Extraction defines what each category yields.
- An unregistered publication series does not stop processing; also record it in the human review pool.
- Assign sequence numbers in arrival order, without using the computer clock. All later steps use that order.
- Processed means completed. Resume unfinished work from its recorded step rather than treating it as completed.

# Extraction

> Status: foundation frozen, October 18, 2026; the production prompt is frozen at extraction v74i (`poc/config/poc-v108.json`, D274) and the code builds no other version (D359). D149 requires extraction-time grouping; D165 integrates it into the default pipeline (poc-v20), with a company's own financial lines carrying no group. English edition: October 25, 2026 (D163); corrected to D207, D209, D211, D251, D259, D264, D265, D273, D274 and D361 on 2026-11-05 (D367).
> Design page: `docs/system-design.html` → New → Extraction → Details. Recorded decisions take precedence over this PRD.
> The step's standard (what is kept, how much it weighs, which questions open, the golden set and how a prompt change is measured) is the signed page `05-extraction-standard.html` (D243), which replaces `docs/research/2026-10-29-analyst-standard/standard.md` (D229). Where this PRD differs from it on what is kept, its weight, which questions open or how a change is measured, the page governs; on the request's shape and the pipeline, the decisions in force govern (the page is frozen, D261; its statements later decisions changed are recorded in [05-extraction-standard-corrections.md](05-extraction-standard-corrections.md), D368).

## Purpose

Read each whole item with its category prompt as a senior hedge-fund analyst. Return evidence linked to shown or candidate theses, and candidates for important uncovered questions. Each candidate also carries structured group associations and any necessary new group definitions. Every output retains its source. Hand everything to the library, which owns the [grouping contract](06-views.md#research-groups-associate-during-extraction). Personal memos follow the prompt rule below.

## Rationale

- Extraction reads; the library compares and stores. Those responsibilities remain separate.
- Read the complete file with embedded tables, or an entire table release, without separate text/table pipelines.
- Categories require different reading: news for events and attribution, research for views and ratings, official decisions for actions and effective dates. They share one prompt structure.
- A thesis is the analyst's judgement expressed as a question, ladder and due date. Evidence is an objective record of who said what in which item. Most items add evidence; fewer create new questions.
- Evidence matters in relation to a question. Block 5 shows the theses open at the start of the item's day (the item's subjects' own and every theme thesis), each with its question, subject and due date and a line code writes from its key and research group saying what it collects (its takes line); it shows no states, no evidence and no separate list of research groups (D211, D251, D264). The model files each statement under those theses itself, with no separate placement call, so a company statement about assured export licences is filed directly under the licence thesis.
- Important uncovered matters, including another named company, a new material issue or a macro/policy event, become candidates with initiating evidence. The library later determines whether they are existing or new questions.
- Sources make both theses and evidence traceable to the original text.

## One item, step by step

The model performs step 2; code performs the others.

<!-- generated: steps -->
```
An item arrives with category and sequence number
  ↓ 1 Build the prompt (Code): Use the category's content in the shared template. The library supplies block 5 once per day: the item's subjects' theses and every theme thesis open at the start of the item's day, each with its takes line (D251, D264); no dependency links and no list of stored groups.
  ↓ 2 Read (Model): Read the whole item. Return evidence, each piece filed under the narrowest block-5 thesis it bears on and the answer's candidates it supports (D207, D264), and candidates for important uncovered questions with their initiating evidence. Personal memos follow their special rule. Decide each candidate's group association and return references under the library contract. Retain sources.
  ↓ 3 Check sources and group references (Code): Check that the quote or cell exists in the item and that cited numbers appear there. Evidence must link to block-5 theses or current candidates. Reject source mismatches with reasons. Validate association references under the library's complete handoff contract.
  ↓ 4 Hand off to the library (Code): Hand off evidence, candidates, associations and necessary group definitions with the sequence number. Matching decides identity; writing preserves membership.
```
<!-- /generated: steps -->

## Two outputs: evidence and candidates

One reading returns evidence, candidates, group associations and necessary new group definitions. Each statement is filed under one home: the narrowest open thesis it bears on, as that thesis's own figure, step or estimate or through the demand, supply, competition or pricing behind its answer, never also a broader thesis beside it, plus the answer's own candidates it supports (D207, D259, D264). When no specific thesis is connected, a statement that moves the demand, supply, competition or pricing behind the company's results goes to the company-wide revenue thesis of its period (D273, kept by D361); a statement nothing takes is dropped as "no home" (D209). Code keeps the narrower of a thesis and one it depends on when a statement is linked to both (D265). Candidates are reserved for important uncovered questions, subject to the personal-memo rule. ## Purpose

A thesis has an immutable question, ladder and due date. Evidence moves its state, which freezes when due. Search then compare candidates with existing theses to merge, continue, create or skip; compare evidence within its thesis to repeat, replace or add. Extra source references alone do not trigger rejudgement. Extraction determines group associations; the writer preserves them after resolving final thesis IDs under the [grouping contract](#research-groups-associate-during-extraction).

## Rationale

- Clear structures connect theses, ladders and evidence to source items.
- A thesis is a question with a state, not an assertion. Opposing views move the same state's direction rather than creating opposite duplicate theses.
- A fixed, verifiable ladder states in advance what would disprove the view, analogous to explicit analyst scenarios or predefined investment exit conditions. Do not change it after observing the outcome.
- The model judges from the evidence in view (the arrivals since its fifth most recent judgement and the basis its last ten judgements cited; the rest stays in force, D198) and dependency conclusions, at low temperature, rather than adjusting its previous score. A revision follows statement date, so a late older statement is stored as one more piece and never recorded as revising a newer one (D202).
- A theme's outcome can help one asset and harm another. Theme ladders describe outcomes; asset dependencies specify their own direction.
- A thesis contains one period and one due date, generally 6–12 months; intermediate quarterly reports are evidence. A thesis on a binding obligation a universe company takes on (a guarantee, financing, an equity stake, a purchase commitment) may set its own check day up to 18 months after the item, because the signing can be checked long before what it finances is built (D185).
- Search finds plausible matches and the model compares their meaning. A match inherits the existing ladder and due date; a merely similar question is new. D155's current POC additionally gives code ownership of fully determined duplicate identity, with its limits in the improvement plan.
- Evidence needs the same search-then-compare discipline. Otherwise February's 12% capex guidance and April's 25% revision sit side by side unrelated, forcing every judgement to rediscover which is current; the comparison records the revision, and both stay in force (D202).
- Record each repeat and revision once, as a relation between two stored pieces that both stay in force (D202, D204). The judge reads the relation, so a retelling counts as one claim and moves nothing (D260).
- Use two rungs for yes/no instead of forcing five.
- The model chooses dependencies like an analyst; code rejects cycles. Apparently mutual dependencies indicate poorly defined questions.
- Rejudge when inputs change. A changed conclusion includes a new rung or materially changed supporting facts/numbers. A repeated Fed statement should not trigger hundreds of dependants; AMZN capex moving from 12% to 25% can trigger NVDA even within the same rung.
- Match with Jev, and with the collision judge where a candidate meets a thesis of its own subject and measure (D169, D190); retain records, obtain human labels and later train a faster smaller model.

## Ladder

<!-- generated: ladder -->
```
Two kinds of ladder (Illustrative ranges)
├─ Asset thesis: numeric → NVDA FY2027 China revenue versus FY2026 | Five rungs describing NVDA's business impact (Due on the FY2027 annual earnings date)
│   ├─ Strongly bullish → Up at least 50%
│   ├─ Bullish → Up at least 10% but under 50%
```

1. A ladder has five rungs. Asset theses run from strongly bullish to strongly bearish, describing business impact; theme theses name five ordered outcomes without good/bad labels. Use two rungs for yes/no rather than forcing five: bullish/bearish for assets; named outcomes for themes, such as signed by the deadline/not signed. Decided for the next extraction version (D186, D368): asset rungs state the business outcome against guidance (above, near, below) and each thesis names its favourable end.
2. Each rung is verifiable: a numeric interval or event stage. Rungs must be exhaustive and non-overlapping, so exactly one applies at expiry. For example: up at least 50%; up 10% to under 50%; change under 10% either way; down 10% to under 80%; down at least 80%. Every boundary belongs to one rung. “Up a lot/a little” cannot be checked. Avoid vague partial outcomes; specify the partial state, such as conditional licences, as a distinct rung. State the comparison underlying neutral.
3. One ladder concerns one thing. Separate numeric and event questions and connect them with dependencies. Likewise, separate components that can move in opposite directions, such as H20 and Blackwell chip classes.
4. The extraction model writes the candidate's ladder. A new thesis adopts it unchanged until expiry. A merged candidate uses the existing thesis's ladder and due date.
5. Numeric theses name a year. Non-company quantities use calendar years: the publication year is this year and the following year is next year, each ending December 31. Company quantities use the fiscal year containing publication day and its successor. Never use the computer clock. For a given quantity, open at most one thesis for each of this year and next year; do not open later years. The due date is the quantity's publication date.
6. Set the due date when the debate can be resolved, usually 6–12 months away. Intermediate quarterly reports are evidence, not separate theses.

- Theme theses do not judge outcomes as good or bad. Each dependent asset thesis records its direction: rate cuts are bullish for Ethereum and bearish for JPM.
- A dependency has one direction. An apparent “good first, bad later” relationship needs an intermediate thesis: steel tariffs raising domestic steel prices and weakening downstream steel demand are separate paths.
- Dependencies and the theme block are the two ways theses influence each other; succession only orders periods. B depending on A means B reads A's conclusion. The theme block (D191, D195) shows every open, judged theme thesis to every asset thesis's judgement, one to all, and the judgement states which way each theme it weighed pushes it; no edge is written for it. Keep B's direct evidence separate from dependency states and reasons. A's conclusion can affect B like evidence but is not stored as evidence: external events and statements are evidence, whereas A is our own judgement. Treating it as evidence would let the system convince itself.
- Count a statement once. A statement about A is A's evidence, including when two candidates arise from one item. B learns it through A's conclusion. Daily filing gives a statement one home: the narrowest open thesis it bears on, as that thesis's own figure, step or estimate or through the demand, supply, competition or pricing behind its answer, plus the same answer's candidates (D207, D259, D264, D265); the separate coverage-start reading was retired (D370). Dependencies are chosen only when B is created; a later item adding a dependency to an existing thesis is decided (D191 point 2) and to be built (D368).
- Rejudge an open thesis when its inputs change: new evidence, replacement, a new link to existing evidence, a dependency declaring a changed conclusion, or (for an asset thesis) a theme thesis declaring one. Additional source references alone do not count. Binding an awaited A or moving to its successor also changes B's inputs. Closed theses do not update. A memo-derived thesis is first judged when evidence first links to it; before that, it and its dependants see “not yet judged”.
- The matching call chooses a new thesis's dependencies, stating which conclusions it relies on and each direction (D51). Choose only among searched theses, extraction block-5 theses and candidates opened from the same item; no extra search. Jev, the default matcher, is not asked for them, so new theses do not receive dependencies through the default matcher; building the choice on the Jev path is decided (D368). The historical seed questions are retired (D370). A historical seed's dependency arrow means "supports" (D368, by D208): for filing, the supporting (driver) thesis is the narrower one; for judging, the supported (root) thesis reads the supporting theses' conclusions, so drivers and themes are judged before their roots. The judging code reads the stored edge the other way today; the fix is to be built and measured (D368). Completed events are evidence, not dependencies: today's oil price influencing OPEC output is evidence for the OPEC-output thesis.
- Before adding an edge or following it to a successor, code checks for cycles. Refuse a cycle and ask the model to correct it; persistent cycles follow normal failure handling. Apparently mutually dependent questions indicate poorly defined theses.

## Ordering, concurrency and traceability

<!-- generated: order -->
- Only incoming information creates evidence. Theses send notifications, not evidence, so they cannot prove themselves.
- One queue writes each thesis: the theme's queue for theme theses; the company, cryptocurrency or commodity queue for asset theses. Within an item, write themes before assets. Rejudgement order follows dependencies under the round rule. Other queues send requests, such as dependency changes or replacement links, to the owning queue with the item's sequence number.
- Order by sequence, never the computer clock. An item reads only records from earlier sequences plus its own writes, except that extraction reads the theses open at the start of the item's day (D251). The result must match serial processing. If concurrent work changes anything it read, including search lists, before commit, redo the affected work.
- A day's items form one round: each item's evidence links, thesis creation, succession and dependencies are written in sequence order, and after the day's last item the round rejudges in dependency order (D63). Dependants read conclusions freshly judged in that round. Judge each thesis once. A widely depended-on thesis such as the Fed rate triggers each dependant once only if it declares its conclusion changed; otherwise propagation stops.
- Append-only records: questions, ladders and due dates are immutable. Matching, rejudgement, succession, closure, reopening and labels append records with sequence numbers.
- The timeline only appends, never rewrites past knowledge. A statement older than one already stored on the same matter is stored and linked as one more piece with its true date, and no revision is recorded (D202); previously unknown information counts normally with its true date.
- A revised input changes only its own source reference. Revising a retelling does not change original evidence. For an original-source revision, a changed statement is stored as a revision of the old one, both in force; nothing is retired (D202). Rejudge each open thesis linked to affected statements once. A retelling-reference change alone does not trigger rejudgement. Keep old judgement records unchanged.
- On failure, record the item's completed step and retry from the failed step, replacing only that attempt's own result. Repeated failures go to human review.
- Trace both directions: a thesis to all information that affected it; an item to theses it created and all evidence it sourced, including repeats.

# Closing at the due day

> Status: decided and built (D54, D356). Corrected to D198 on 2026-11-05 (D367). English edition: November 4, 2026.
> This page replaces the final-verdict draft (D29), which D356 retired; Git keeps the earlier text.
> Design page: `docs/system-design.html` → New → Closing at the due day → Details. Recorded decisions take precedence.

## What it does

At the end of each day, pull mode closes every open question whose due day has come. The question's state freezes where it stands, and that rung is its conclusion. There is no separate final verdict and no separate result.

- The closing record names the rung the question froze at. A question that was never judged closes with "no data to verify", neither a pass nor a fail.
- Decisive information, such as an annual report or an official decision, arrives as evidence by the end of the due day and moves the state like any other evidence. Nothing waits for it.
- A question that depends on a closed one reads its frozen state and reasons in its next judgement.
- A closed question is not closed again and is not judged again. Further tracking uses a successor question under the library's rules.

```
Closing at the due day | Has this open question's due day come? (Pull mode, at the end of each day, with the day passed in)
├─ No → Stay open (Evidence keeps moving its state)
├─ Yes, and it has been judged → Freeze at its rung and close (The closing record names the rung; dependent questions read the frozen state and reasons)
└─ Yes, and it was never judged → Close with no data to verify (Neither a pass nor a fail)
```
<!-- /generated: closing -->

## Rules

<!-- generated: rules -->
1. A question has one state and no separate result: the rung it stands on at the end of its due day is its conclusion (D54).
2. There is no separate final review: each day's judgement already reads the evidence in view, D198's window (D356).
3. Nothing waits. Decisive information arrives as evidence by the end of the due day; a question whose result is still unknown closes at its state (D54).
4. Due days are set when a question is opened, on or after the day its result is published (D356).
5. The closing record is shown, never a gate; a question never judged shows “no data to verify” (D356).
6. The day is passed in: real time in production, the replayed day in tests. The system owns no clock (D31).

A closing is dated on the day it happens, also on a day without items: pull mode then records the day under a sequence number of its own. Tracking never gates a judgement.
