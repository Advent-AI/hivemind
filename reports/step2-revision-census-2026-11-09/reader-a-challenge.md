## Cases where both readers chose different

There are **14 such pairs** in the supplied comparison. Thirteen involve the Kyber report and related NVIDIA response: C002, C005, C007, C013, C019, C036, C046, C055, C065, C069, C070, C072 and C075. A shared, source-visible relationship is that a brief response to a reported Kyber delay is paired with a separate statement from the same broader product story. That is a pattern in these pairs, not proof of what produced any model's decision.

The stored statements are not all alike. C005, C069 and C072 concern NVL576's possible delay or low volume; C007, C046 and C070 concern CPO-NVSwitch's Feynman timing; C013 and C036 concern an alternative rack's cancellation; C065 concerns the separate four-die chip design; C019 and C075 concern an Oberon sales plan. In C005, Investing.com places “Our roadmap is intact” immediately after the Kyber NVL144 delay claim, while DCD separately describes NVL576. In C065, the same Investing.com article lists the four-die cancellation after the denial of Kyber's delay. Those articles do not explicitly say the response addresses each separate statement. C002 and C055 are different again: Tom's Hardware distinguishes 2026 Rubin GPUs from the *reported* Kyber delay; a denial that Kyber is delayed does not remove that distinction about the report's scope.

The comparison inputs can conceal the distinction. In C005, C007, C013 and C069, the incoming passage is the broad response while the surrounding source identifies Kyber as its target. The source context is absent from the text the comparison saw. Conversely, the record summaries in some cases already say Kyber; therefore missing context is not established as a single cause across all pairs. The underlying sources sometimes themselves leave the response's scope unclear, as C049 and C051 show.

C057 is the counterexample to a single Kyber-specific mechanism: the stored record describes a GPU-capacity backstop and revenue share, while the incoming record identifies initial partners and Sharon AI's planned GPUs. Both sources concern the same financing program, but the incoming main statement adds participant and deployment information rather than restating the backstop mechanics. The broader relationship across all 14 is **same subject or program but a distinct statement**; that is a description of the pairings, not proof of one common process failure.

A further boundary is C035: unlike a generic response to Kyber, NVIDIA explicitly says NVL576 will scale in 2027, addressing the stored NVL576 schedule. C068 shows why even an explicit statement can leave ambiguity: future planned scaling and a presently proven solution are not identical claims.
