# Saved Jev revision relations: full source census

2026-11-09. Alex authorized source review of the 56 saved D392 clean d1 Jev candidate answers that chose `supersedes`. These are 56 candidate judgments for 34 incoming records, not 56 independent stories and not a random sample of all Jev decisions. The actual library consolidates them to **30 distinct group-to-group links**. All links are present in the saved library; 30 retain the particular record that first wrote them, and the remaining 26 answers point to an already existing link between the same groups. See `METHOD.md` for archive, source, matching and blind packet checks. Every one of 76 selected source pairs matched a unique full archived request and exact saved answer; 236 cited passages matched their source article after whitespace normalization.

## What the two independent readers found

Two separate AI readers saw both complete saved articles, the compared records, and the existing PRD comparison definition, but neither saw the saved Jev decision or the other reader's result. The 56 cases were mixed with 20 sampled other-choice controls. Each independently reviewed every pair and cited source text. Their judgments are diagnostic interpretations, not a new gold or proof the articles' claims are true. They agreed independently on 47/56 of the revision answers; the nine remaining cases were separately re-read against both source texts and each other's reasoning (`reader-a-challenge.md`, `reader-b-challenge.md`). These post-challenge counts are a transparent description of their jointly held readings, not statistical confidence or a replacement ruler:

| What the readers concluded after challenge | 56 candidate answers | 30 actual saved group links |
|---|---:|---:|
| Later statement changes or directly responds to the prior main statement | 16 | 5 |
| Main statements differ; the alleged revision is not established | 17 | 9 |
| Later statement repeats the earlier main statement | 7 | 5 |
| Unclear from source or scope | 16 | 11 |

The 30-link column uses the actual `by` record for each saved group link, not another record that later chose the same link. A repeated old statement is not necessarily a substantive new revision; retained extra detail is not itself a change. Some cases classified different are related or may be included in a broader program; their source does not support the particular revision link. `comparison.json` preserves pre-challenge labels, and `comparison-after-challenge.json` carries the nine explicitly documented subsequent changes. The two readers classified the sampled controls as 10 repeats and 10 different, but these controls were deliberately selected and are not a representative accuracy test.

**Do not interpret 24/56 or 14/30 as general error rates.** The population is selected by Jev's own answer, the stories and group links repeat heavily, 16 answers / 11 links are unclear, and the model-reader interpretations have not been signed by Alex as a new reference. A source later reporting an official response is not proof the response happened after the earlier source: the old article may already contain that response.

## Concrete examples and source-level checks

- **A target not addressed:** C013, saved answer `70:e4 → 45:e3`: the stored CNBC record reports a proposed backup rack was cancelled. The new Investing.com article's surrounding sentences identify the spokesperson's response as addressing a separate report of a Kyber delay. It separately mentions the backup cancellation without saying that this was denied. Two blinded readers initially said different, and I inspected the source article and actual archived question. The group link already existed and is consolidated; other directly saved links illustrate the same issue (C005, C019, C046, C065).
- **Different information in one program:** C057, an actual saved link `108:e2 → 8:e2`: the old article's main claim describes a GPU-capacity financial backstop; the new record identifies a participant and its proposed GPU purchases. Both readers distinguish participation from the backstop's terms. This contradicts a theory that the entire error class is about one chip-rack event.
- **A true source-backed later change suitable as a bounded control:** C073, saved link `100:e1 → 24:e10`: a July 2 article describes China as having locked NVIDIA out of its market to date; a July 8 article reports, citing The Information and unnamed people, that selected companies would be permitted limited H200 purchases. Both readers judge this a relevant subsequent policy change. The sources report a planned, conditional easing, not proof of completed purchases, and this is not a direct official rule. C074 reports actual shipments later but its source does not specify the timing of the testimony or when shipments started.
- **A current-topic response, not an explicit denial:** C059, saved link `203:e1 → 119:e1`: an earlier article suspects Shield TV was discontinued but also allows ordinary stockout. The later article explicitly says NVIDIA confirmed a shortage while neither confirming nor denying discontinuation. One reader initially chose different and the other uncertain; after source challenge both call the specific discontinuation relationship different, while keeping the device's future unresolved. A past independent source reader offered a revision interpretation, so the product boundary remains legitimately contestable rather than an absolute gold.
- **A real denial with a time caveat:** C021, saved link `54:e3 → 45:e1`: an NVIDIA denial addresses the stored main claim of a Kyber delay and both readers call it a revision. The source of the earlier record already includes NVIDIA's broad rejection, which the extracted earlier record omitted. Thus the later article reports a response to that claim, but it is not evidence that the response was issued later than the original article. It is a useful target-recognition control, not a clean chronological update control.
- **Ambiguous wording:** C051, saved link `54:e3 → 45:e3`: the later article describes a delay and a backup-rack cancellation, then says NVIDIA “rejected the claims”. Both source readers agree after challenge that the plural reference could include both, but the exact statement says only that the roadmap remains intact. The scope is unresolved; neither a certain wrong edge nor a certain denial is justified.

## What can and cannot be attributed

Among the 14 of 56 cases independently labeled different by both readers **before** challenge, 13 concern the Kyber story and compare a denial of an alleged delay with another statement in the same broader story. One more (C057) concerns the cloud-financing program. Following challenge, C030, C052 and C059 also were judged different, yielding 17 total; their mechanisms differ. Many of the 13 share the same incoming article and original incident, so the count is not 13 independent events. The common observable error is linking a response to a related statement without sufficient support that it is the response's target. This is a description of the output, not an established internal cause.

Input context sometimes matters: C007's Jev incoming summary says only “SemiAnalysis delay report,” and the quotation says the roadmap is intact; its complete source specifies Kyber. However C036's actual Jev summary expressly says Kyber NVL144 delay while the stored record explicitly concerns a different backup plan, and C059's actual summary expressly says the company neither confirmed nor denied discontinuation. These counterexamples show a missing antecedent alone cannot explain the class. Current `clean-arch/step2/compare.py` already instructs Jev that another statement on the same topic is not a revision. A new hardcoded denial rule, a score cutoff, adding the whole article or splitting the question has not been shown to fix the class without losing valid revisions. Jev's typed output contains no model-authored explanation, so its causal reasoning cannot be read from confidence scores.

The 11 links with source ambiguity include unavailable time, vague targets, bundled main statements and articles whose own time metadata conflicts with their content; they must not silently become new facts. In particular, C047's incoming article is tagged July 12 but speaks of September and October 2026 events as past, making source-time sequence unreliable.

## Next decision

The proper next diagnosis is to separate which unsupported links had the specific target and chronology available in Jev's actual saved input from those where context was missing or ambiguous, across the full class, and contrast the same input dimensions against the source-supported C035/C073 controls. Trace the actual full request, candidate set, outcome and library writer; preserve all historic outputs. Only after this identifies one general mechanism and its share, use the cheapest controlled proof on stored data. Any change in what the model is asked requires the repository's full prompt-to-standard audit and independent review; the production configuration, source text and frozen reference remain unchanged. No new Jev request or code change to the product was made by this source census.
