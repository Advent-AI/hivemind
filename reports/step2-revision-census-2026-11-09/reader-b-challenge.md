# Independent challenge of reader disagreements

Scope: the nine disagreements in `comparison.json`, followed by the 14 cases in `revision_answers` where both readers said `different`. I re-examined each pair's supplied comparison text and source articles, including the other reader's reason. Source publication dates below are the supplied dates, not proof of when an underlying statement was made. The comparison received the record summary, passages, tier, reporting speaker, original party and stated date; it did not receive `evidence_period` or the full article. Labels describe the relationship between the two supplied main statements, not the truth of either source.

## Cases both readers called different

There are **14 of 56 comparison.json `revision_answers`**, of which **6 also appear in `saved_edges`**. They share a common comparison-level pattern: the incoming and stored records can concern the same company and broader event while their main statements describe distinct pieces of it. The evidence does not establish one internal cause in the model, search, or grouping code; this file contains the selected pairs and outcomes, not a trace of the decision that produced them. In particular, a later source's denial of one specific report does not automatically identify or answer every other statement in that report.

- **13 concern the Kyber/Rubin reporting cluster.** In C005, C069 and C072 the stored claim is about NVL576 delay or low volume; the source context of the incoming response identifies the Kyber NVL144 delay. In C007, C046 and C070 the stored claim is the CPO-NVSwitch/Feynman timing and associated scale-up inference. In C013 and C036 it is cancellation of a two-rack backup. In C065 it is cancellation of a four-die chip design. In C019 and C075 it is the plan to sell more existing-form Oberon-Rubin racks. In C002 and C055 the stored main statement explains which products a reported delay excludes. None of those is simply the incoming Kyber-delay denial.
- **C057 is a counterexample to a Kyber-only cause.** The stored statement describes the cloud-capacity backstop and revenue share; the incoming statement identifies a participating provider and its planned GPU purchase. They are related to the same financing program but describe different information. A claim that all 14 errors arise from confusion about Kyber would not fit this case.
- **Missing target context can make some comparisons harder, but does not explain every pair.** In C005 and C007, the source identifies the Kyber delay immediately before the spokesperson's short response, while the supplied incoming passage contains only “Our roadmap is intact.” The summary provides broader “Kyber/Rubin Ultra delays” in C005, but not the full source's exact referent. By contrast, C036's incoming summary itself specifies “Kyber NVL144 delay,” while its stored summary explicitly describes a backup-plan cancellation; C057's supplied summaries also distinguish program mechanics from Sharon's deployment. The latter examples show that omission of a preceding sentence cannot be asserted as the sole mechanism across all 14.
- **A specific counterexample to treating every response as unrelated:** C035, outside the both-different class, pairs an NVL576 delay/low-volume claim with NVIDIA's explicit statement that NVL576 will scale in 2027. That response names the same system and contests its timeline, although it leaves volume unresolved. C049 and C051 also show that a broad plural reference to claims can make a target genuinely ambiguous. These boundaries matter more than whether the same article or broad event is involved.

The six saved-edge cases are C005, C019, C046, C055, C057 and C065. Their inclusion indicates these selected comparisons have a saved grouping relationship in the supplied comparison file; it does not by itself establish which earlier comparison created the final group or the internal reason for the grouping. No code, prompt, source, or model call was changed for this review.
