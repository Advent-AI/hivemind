# Step 1: date and link source-support review

## Date fields in the original 49-article replay

Two independent readers examined all 73 non-null `statement_date_text` values in the original 49 completed articles, against the article input and the clean Step 1 date rule. One reviewed the first 20 complete records and the other the next 29; the failed attempt at article 21 is excluded. Their combined initial assessment is 18 fields that appear to use an unsupported original statement date or a period/event/place rather than a statement date, 48 supported, and seven ambiguous. These are fields, not independent events: seven flagged fields occur in the same Robot Report article and use its publication date for underlying NVIDIA or Hugging Face statements without an explicitly dated company statement. Some records combine multiple sources or claims; their dates need to be interpreted at that level. A second reviewer checked the actual Robot Report input and a selection of other flagged outputs. That review moved one SpaceX filing date from wrong to ambiguous: the filing month may itself be the date of an original public filing, but the available text does not establish the timing. The classifications remain an audited lead, not independent external validation of each source.

Examples of the period/date distinction include “since 2023” for the duration of a CoreWeave agreement, “early 2026” for an export authorization, “2028 time frame” for a forecast, and “on the sidelines of the RAISE Summit in Paris” for an interview setting. Correct controls include an explicitly stated “said in May,” a company's own dated announcement, and an author's own dated analysis. Three additional complete `statement_date` values in the first article may incorrectly use an NVIDIA post's date for third-party actions even though `statement_date_text` is null; the remaining null fields were not exhaustively reviewed and no error rate for them is asserted. The supporting review lists are in the [reproducible field inventory](../poc/data/clean-step1-date-accounts-2026-11-07/original-inventory.json) and [council conversation](STEP1-SOURCE-HANDOFF-COUNCIL.md); historical source and results remain in the original trace databases.

The first-segment reader flagged article 10 evidence-3 and evidence-7, article 18 e1–e7, and article 20 e7. The second-segment reader flagged sequence 10 e1, 20 e2, 21 e1/e6/e9, 22 e3/e7, and 28 e2; segment-two sequence 22 e8 was moved to ambiguous on cross-review. The inventory identifies each record and preserves its actual source passages. The field-level classification is preliminary where a record combines different speakers or statements.

An independent prompt-and-standard audit found that the existing system prompt already distinguishes the original statement date from the period covered, and instructs the model not to replace an unknown date with a later report date. The schema's shorter date descriptions are more abstract; that observation does not prove that wording caused the errors. A dated original release or an author's own analysis can legitimately use its own publication date, while a later article's publication date does not automatically date a third party's statement. Multiple source statements in a single record may have different dates, which the current prompt already tells the model to separate when a single set of fields would blur them. Unknown dates must not cause useful evidence to be discarded.

The later six-source comparison is a separate repeated sample. Its [independent review](STEP1-ADDITIONAL-SOURCES-RESULTS.md) found 13 apparent period/event-as-statement-date fields across eight answers, with ambiguous values reported separately. Three later continuations correctly distinguished the cases when asked directly; they were leading and cannot establish the cause of the original answers.

## Source support for link explanations in three additional-source articles

Three independent readers examined all 76 links in articles 2, 5 and 6 of the additional-source comparison, with condition labels hidden. They compared each attached passage, full sent text, captured page, summary and link explanation. This is a selected subset, not a frequency estimate for all links.

- **Article 2, Bloomberg:** three link explanations refer to competitor context found in the adjacent source paragraph but not in that evidence item's attached quotation. The links use the context to interpret market share; this is not proof of an invented fact. One summary goes further by implying a time overlap between customer custom-chip deployment and historic market shares that the source does not date.
- **Article 5, LG:** two explanations connect Vera Rubin details from another paragraph to the current evidence; one links the reported Koo/Huang discussion to a specific prototype although the article does not establish that the discussion concerned that prototype. These are explicitly framed as possibilities. A link to the existing Vera CPU performance question remains a relevance concern even when its literal rack facts are sourced.
- **Article 6, Kyber:** multiple explanations use the reported Kyber delay or the competitive opportunity from an adjacent paragraph without attaching that paragraph to the same evidence. Some are explicitly qualified inferences. In one answer, the summary's AMD opening requires the article's sentence naming both AMD and Alphabet, but the quotations include an Alphabet-specific sentence and separate AMD details, not that connecting sentence. The missing sentence is present in the sent text. Exact source location does not establish complete support.

The current Step 1 prompt and schema explicitly ask passages to support the **summary**. Link explanations must distinguish source claims from inference, but the passage description does not explicitly require support for source facts stated only in an explanation. This is a contract observation, not proof that the wording caused any particular error. Existing source-first, citation-order and follow-up-review experiments were mixed or failed; no new citation or date rule has been adopted. The full sources and all answers can be inspected in the [replay](../poc/data/clean-step1-reasoning-new-sources-2026-11-07/review.html).
