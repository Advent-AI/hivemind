# Step 1: citation-support trace audit

**Status: diagnosis, not a prompt change or a validated fix.** This audit examines why some returned evidence items do not carry all source passages needed for their own summaries. It uses the [five captured-source controls](STEP1-CAPTURE-CONTROLS-RESULTS.md) and an earlier selected citation-order comparison. The same articles and repeated answers are not independent population samples. A second reader checked the source, raw answers, request and standard; Claude Code's separate review is in progress.

| Earlier article 11, citation-first comparison, `18-article-11-candidate-1.json`, `e1` | The supplied article says an AI chip startup raised $1 billion. | The summary includes the $1 billion financing; the passages describe an $11 billion valuation and a prior financing amount. | The opening sentence establishing the $1 billion is not attached. These are incomplete citations for individual returned items, not proof that the source lacks the underlying facts or that their summaries necessarily invent those facts. Source roles, attribution, and unrelated links require separate review. The CNBC captured page postdates the article by months; its original publication-time completeness is not established.

## Request and standard

The actual system request already requires a standalone evidence summary and all source passages needed to support it. It allows related information to be combined from different parts of the item and asks the model to copy each passage separately and contiguously. The structured schema's `source_passages` is an array and says the passages jointly support the summary. Thus the request can express the missing headline or paragraph; the raw answers omitted those passages before code validated them. `runner.validate` checks each supplied passage's location and schema, not whether the attached passages jointly establish every summary claim.

The final check asks for unsupported claims and missing conditions, but does not explicitly say whether to compare each summary against its attached passages or the complete source. That ambiguity is a possible explanation, not an established cause. The signed extraction standard requires the original actor, claim, amount, period and conditions to survive and the record to be understandable; it does not require a particular multi-passage schema or prohibit a source-supported combination across the article. A located quote does not prove the summary or link is semantically correct.

## Prior attempts and open question

Moving the passage field before the summary did not resolve the article 11 omission in the selected three-draw comparison. Earlier final-check wording and same-article citation-review probes produced mixed results, and a per-record review missed a reversed relation and a connectivity condition. They are not adopted; see [citation-order results](STEP1-CITATION-ORDER-RESULTS.md) and the [fidelity council](STEP1-FIDELITY-COUNCIL.md). The current trace does not prove whether the model overlooked the instruction, interpreted the final review differently, or encountered another cause. Any proposal must be audited against the entire request, schema, signed standard and prior experiments before it is tested, and must retain useful source-supported combinations and indirect links.

One earlier 84-call placement experiment did explicitly ask, in a second user message after the article, whether source-attributed facts or conditions were jointly supported by the cited passages and source metadata. On selected cases its Firmus citation improved in some draws, while the FEV no-internet condition and a named SambaNova customer were omitted more often in some arms; no variant was adopted. In one FEV answer to that explicit citation-check arm, the summary still said connectivity could be unavailable while the attached passages omitted the source's explicit limited-or-no-internet sentence. This is not identical to changing the system message's final check, but it is a direct counterexample to assuming that merely adding a citation-check sentence resolves the issue. [Prior design and results](STEP1-DESIGN.md).

An independent reader suggested an untested representation hypothesis: generate ordered parts of an evidence summary, each associated with its supporting source passages, and assemble the displayed summary and passage union from those parts. A part could combine multiple passages, and the same passage could support multiple parts; this need not split one research matter into several evidence records. This differs from the earlier unsent structured-claims draft, which added claims beside an unchanged free-form summary and did not associate claims with passages. It also changes the output contract and could increase response size, lose qualifiers or antecedents, or lead the model to assign irrelevant quotes. A part may express a source-grounded inference or caution; a quotation need not literally state an absence. Structural references cannot prove semantic support: the model could put the whole article 11 summary into one part and link it to the same incomplete passages. A new shape would need an isolated projection into the original pair contract and explicit handling for unresolved references. It cannot by itself fix an incorrect link explanation or recover an omitted event. This is a hypothesis under council review, not an approved schema or validated fix.
