# Step 1 source loss: cleaner investigation

## Two traced cases

The saved source-to-input path loses real article text in two different ways. This is a review of the historical corpus cleaner and the isolated clean Step 1 input, not a change to the source database or the production configuration.

- **Article 2 — a reported outlook assumption.** The captured page says that NVIDIA did not include China revenue from the relevant market in its outlook, according to its CFO. The text was absent from the clean input. The current HTML extractor returns that line, but the first cleaning pass removes it before the extractor match is considered. After earlier filters, this line is followed by two related-story headlines; all three are at most 110 characters, lack an accepted ending character, contain no digit, and form a headline run. The first-pass rule removes the run. This is not a Luna omission: Luna did not receive the line. The raw page also contains an AI-generated summary label and related stories; a correct financial sentence is not proof that the whole surrounding page is article body.
- **Article 16 — financing.** The captured page reports the first close of a $1 billion Series F at an $11 billion valuation. This 282-character line survives the first pass, but is not matched by the current HTML extractor. The fallback keeps long unmatched lines only if they end with accepted punctuation; this one ends in `®` and is removed. The saved cleaner output is reproduced. This diagnoses a source loss, not the model's later decision: in a separate prepared-text comparison the financing was restored but omitted from all three answers. Another article had previously supplied related financing, so novelty in the stateful run is not established.

The inputs are the original saved page database and cleaned database: `gnews:c8c39f971721fd820aab5c1c` and `gnews:b2ff1fae25f82fd3111134de`. The cleaner is [`clean_pages.py`](../poc/corpus/scripts/clean_pages.py), including `clean_lines`, `without_headline_runs`, and `clean_article`. The saved HTML is the current archive, not an independently signed copy of every original historical HTML byte.

## Bounded corpus observations

The [$0 census](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/results.json) read all 34,084 original source rows and considered the 6,463 rows that were originally fed articles. It reconstructed publisher templates from the original database. Two known case controls were checked. Counts below describe rule conditions and exact cleaned-line presence, **not counts of material investment facts or model errors**:

| Observed cleaner condition | Pages | Line values |
|---|---:|---:|
| Removed by the headline-run rule | 2,129 | 12,101 |
| Survived the first pass, at least 120 characters, no accepted ending | 2,335 | 5,770 |
| Of the latter, absent as an exact line from the saved clean text | — | 3,703 |

The [extractor comparison](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/extractor-summary.json) then examined the 3,567 pages with at least one of these candidates using the current stored HTML and trafilatura 2.3.1. It recorded nonempty extractor output for 3,538, no HTML for nine and empty output for 20. Among classified headline-removed lines, 3,287 on 965 pages match the cleaner's normalized extractor lookup and are absent from the saved output. Among classified long lines without an accepted ending, 3,703 lack an extractor match and are absent from the saved output. Exact rule inputs and outputs, input hashes, a current HTML index hash, decoded HTML hashes and full candidate lines are preserved in the [work area](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/). A normalized extractor match uses the cleaner's own prefix rule and does not establish full-line identity, relevance, source role or correct article boundaries.

An independent audit found an additional matching limitation. The cleaner's `_letters` function removes non-ASCII letters; 11 of the 3,287 headline matches and 11 other long-line matches in this selected census have an empty normalized key. An empty key can match an empty extractor line even when the source sentence is absent from the extractor output. Two Chinese lines in `gnews:fdc4e0fba86169e7cc72754a` demonstrate that failure. The 22 are empty-key matches, not necessarily 22 false matches; this count covers only the candidates above, not all cleaned lines. A general repair remains unvalidated.

A separate [all-fed-article empty-key census](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/empty-key-summary.json) examined rule-surviving lines rather than headline-removed values: seven of 6,463 source articles contain 24 such line values. On three pages, the extractor's short-line set contains an empty normalized key; 15 values on those pages are in the saved cleaned text. Three of those 15 values, all from one page, are not verbatim substrings of the current extractor output and do not independently meet the long-line fallback. They are a Thai disclaimer and interface instructions, not established lost investment evidence. Exact nonoccurrence does not rule out reflow or a semantic equivalent; current extractor output is not independently guaranteed to reproduce historical HTML. This census does not measure partially normalized multilingual lines. A first attempt used a control removed by the preceding headline rule and failed its own precondition; the control was corrected to a rule-surviving Thai line, and the completed census was rerun.

An offline identity probe replaced the ASCII-only lookup key with Unicode `isalnum()` characters while keeping every other cleaner operation the same. The existing cleaner reproduced all 6,463 saved fed-article texts with current saved HTML. The candidate changed eight pages, removing ten lines and adding none: seven English titles with a Cyrillic publisher suffix and three Thai page-disclosure or interface lines. The [full changed lines](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/unicode-identity-corpus.jsonl) are preserved; this is not a judgment that all removals are safe or unimportant. The candidate is **not adopted**: `isalnum()` drops combining marks, so distinct Thai strings such as `กา` and `ก่า` have the same candidate key, as do some distinct Devanagari strings. It therefore does not supply reliable multilingual identity. A repair must preserve meaningful marks and examine both complete and partial-key collisions, existing quotations and all changed outputs before any integration. [Full-corpus probe](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/unicode-identity-corpus-summary.json).

A second offline key retained Unicode letters, numbers and marks after NFKC normalization. On the same 6,463 articles it changed 11 pages: two lines added and 11 removed, with the existing cleaner again reproducing all saved texts. The two additions are Thai gambling-page titles on pages whose stored source titles describe oil markets; this illustrates that improved text matching cannot establish that the captured page is the intended article. Another removal was a phrase inside a generated-analysis component. The [complete changes](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/unicode-marks-corpus.jsonl) require source-role and downstream review. This candidate has not been adopted; preserving marks does not by itself prove a complete Unicode identity contract.

A reproducible [sample of surrounding page text](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/sample-contexts.txt) shows why restoring every matched line would be unsafe. In one page, extractor-matched lines are links to other articles; other examples include code, lists and a section heading. Among long unmatched lines are advertisements, page titles and later-story teasers. These are selected structural examples; they do not quantify relevance or contamination across the full corpus. Nineteen long unmatched lines ending in `®` occur on 19 pages from one publisher, including article 16; this does not justify a publisher- or symbol-specific production exception.

Both missing sentences appear inside an HTML `article` element in their currently saved pages, but article 2's element also contains related-story links and article 16's page has twelve `article` elements. On the previously frozen 363-page lineage cohort, 362 saved pages were parseable: 196 had no `article` element, 105 had one and 61 had multiple. This cohort deliberately includes 163 pages from known affected structures and is not a representative population sample. A normalized title appeared in exactly one such element on 118 pages; title containment alone does not prove the right source or body. The [DOM observation](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/dom-summary.json) describes HTML structure only; selecting an element is not a validated cleaner.

## What remains to solve

The two failures are established; a general repair is not. Simply accepting all extractor matches after headline filtering would expose some source text and some unrelated material. Restoring every long unmatched line would likewise mix body text with navigation, advertising and other stories. The earlier source-selection and adjacent-line experiments also had tradeoffs; see the [source provenance census](STEP1-SOURCE-PROVENANCE-CENSUS.md), [source context observations](STEP1-SOURCE-CONTEXT-OBSERVATIONS.md), and [source-text comparison](STEP1-SOURCE-TEXT-RESULTS.md). Any proposal needs a complete source-preservation contract, source-role and ambiguity handling, tests against both recovered body text and contamination, and replay through the actual Step 1 path. Exact source location alone does not establish meaning. The frozen historical source and production settings remain unchanged.

Claude proposed testing whether stripping trailing Unicode symbols, formatting and separators before the existing punctuation check would recover the article 16 paragraph. The [$0 full-corpus comparison](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/symbol-tail-two-sites-corpus-summary.json) applies that idea only at the two proposed headline-run and long-line-fallback checks, leaving the short-fragment check unchanged. The baseline reproduces all 6,463 saved fed-article texts. The candidate changes 80 pages, adding 124 lines and removing none, for 68,452 added characters. It recovers article 16 but does not recover article 2. On one EY page it adds 27 lines and 23,837 characters, including serialized HTML and page-navigation material. This contradicts the initial claim that only 24 lines would be affected and one promotional line added. The experiment is not adopted; its input change is not a count of useful facts or model errors. A preliminary broader probe also changed the short-fragment check and returned different numbers; the two-site result above is the relevant comparison of Claude's stated proposal. The full council is reviewing the result.

Claude withdrew the symbol proposal in its [second response](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-council-2026-11-07/round2-response.md) and suggested examining the existing HTML paragraph/aside diagnostic. The [all-fed-article replay](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/html-v2-corpus-summary.json) reproduces all 49 previously saved diagnostic diffs and applies its same line-matching and page-tag logic across 6,463 originally fed articles. Thirty-two lack HTML; the candidate changes 1,054 pages, adding 2,816 line values and removing 296. It is a list of proposed changes, not constructed model inputs or a measure of accuracy. A sampled addition on `gnews:085fc8af40b0bd255bb43131` is a film story on a page whose source title concerns a BRICS summit, and other additions include promotional links. Some removals are bylines for related stories, but all removed lines require review in their source context before any claim of safety. The candidate also relies on the existing lossy ASCII matcher. The complete [per-page changes](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/html-v2-corpus.jsonl) are saved. The 49-article positive cases alone do not validate this approach.

An independent reader checked a fixed sample of additions and removals against their pages. Codex confirmed the key examples: a report about AI video startup Higgsfield would gain several unrelated Bloomberg story headlines; a report on an NVIDIA investment would gain a free-report promotion; and a Cisco security article would gain a page tag and a separate article's introduction. Some sampled removals are bylines belonging to related articles. These examples establish both contamination and useful cleanup opportunities, not the overall precision of the candidate. The reader also noted that the control-count code would need revision if a future archive contained error rows; the current 49-row archive has no error rows.

In a further source-backed counterexample, the candidate removes seven lines from `gnews:fb89ac7131700c42fdbec0ce`, an article about changes in AI training. They belong to an article-related timeline, including a reported Anthropic research-and-development measure and one accompanying sources line. Investment relevance remains a judgment, but the example shows that an `aside` can hold substantive article context; its HTML role alone does not establish that its contents are unrelated. Conversely, unrelated recommendation cards can be nested inside `main` and `article` elements, as in the BRICS page above. These counterexamples preclude adopting V2 based on tag ancestry alone.

In its [third response](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-council-2026-11-07/round3-response.md), Claude agreed that HTML V2 cannot be used and proposed another structural selection using page tags and title matching. Its proposed exclusion of link-only lines, repeated publisher text and nested articles without a title match still treats structural hints as source boundaries. These are unvalidated judgments: links can be source disclosures, repeated text can be a current statement, and a nested article or aside can contain source context. The lossy ASCII lookup and multiple occurrences further weaken identity claims. Codex sent these objections and the full-page alternative for [another council challenge](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-council-2026-11-07/round4-handoff.txt). No structural selector has been adopted.

In the [fourth response](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-council-2026-11-07/round4-response.md), Claude withdrew that structural selection after checking counterexamples, including article headers containing the author's name and publication date and linked text giving a source. The council favors testing full captured-page text as an isolated, one-call diagnostic without changing the current prompt in the first comparison; the existing clean text is the control. This is an untested hypothesis, not agreement that Luna can reliably identify the target article. The response proposes several known cases; its suggested BRICS source has only a `supply-chain-shift` theme tag, not the NVDA ticker, so it is not an eligible NVDA test item and must not be forced into that entity's context. Source selection and full evaluation details remain under review. The [diagnostic draft](STEP1-FULL-PAGE-DIAGNOSTIC.md) also records the independent review's cautions about fixed thesis states, incomplete captures, condition leakage and comparable citation checks.

In the [fifth](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-council-2026-11-07/round5-response.md) and [sixth](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-council-2026-11-07/round6-response.md) rounds, Claude accepted the separate roles of known and previously selected samples and the exclusion of the non-NVDA BRICS item. It identified the months between the July article dates and the October page captures as an important limitation. The council agreed that later page material must be reported by source role and date where supported, without assuming that every later-dated passage is false or that an arm difference proves an internal model mechanism. The proposed 17-article comparison, including the longest page from the original 49, remains exploratory. Its execution design and source-first notes are being reviewed; no model comparison or production change has yet occurred.

For a possible full-page comparison, the [source-only token census](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/page-budget-summary.json) measures the saved pages and cleaned text of all 6,463 originally fed articles using `o200k_base`. Median captured-page text is 2,015 tokens versus 770 cleaned; the 99th percentile is 7,573 versus 4,535; maxima are 28,516 versus 14,651. The 49-article replay has median 1,838 versus 690 and maximum 7,997 versus 5,506. These are source-field counts only, not complete model requests, model limits, costs or latency. A whole page can contain unrelated content, generated summaries, wrong captures or incomplete source; size alone cannot establish a safe input strategy.

An offline [saved-answer check](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-loss-census-2026-11-07/full-page-saved-answer-replay.json) passed all 49 completed responses through the current isolated `runner.validate` using the saved clean text and then the full captured page. The same 248 of 250 passage instances located in both, the same two remained unlocated, and no located source string changed. This compares only location behavior for already returned quotations; it does not show what Luna would extract when sent the full page, whether a cited passage belongs to the target story, or whether a quote supports its summary. No forward run or state change occurred.
