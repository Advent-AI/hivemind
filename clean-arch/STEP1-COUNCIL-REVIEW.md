# Step 1 source-faithfulness council

## Scope and record

On 2026-11-06, Codex and Claude Code (`claude-fable-5-1`) independently reviewed the clean Step 1 request, schema, code, source packets and saved answers, then challenged each other over four rounds. Claude had access to prior experiment reviews during its first investigation, so its review is not blind. The initial and challenge requests, streamed responses, final replies and Codex's independent notes are retained in [`poc/data/clean-step1-council-2026-11-06`](../poc/data/clean-step1-council-2026-11-06/). No extraction prompt, schema, configuration, source database or recorded answer was changed by this council.

## What the evidence establishes

1. **Source content and selection are a separate concern.** Previously, the Firmus input included unrelated article recommendations, while other cleaned inputs omitted useful text. For the specific prepared cases below, the relevant wording was present in the material Luna saw. This does not establish that every prepared page is complete or that model-selected source roles are correct.
2. **A quotation can be present but incomplete.** Firmus evidence included the US$2 billion fundraising figure while citing only a different paragraph. FEV evidence described operation with limited or no internet but did not attach the paragraph giving that condition. A reviewer found the Firmus paragraph in 3/3 draws and missed the FEV paragraph in 3/3. These are selected known examples, not a general rate.
3. **A correct quotation can accompany a wrong description.** The FEV page-time answer reverses which system is the backup in its link explanation, although its summary describes the direction correctly. In another saved answer the summary assigns US dollars to a $15.5 billion valuation where the supplied article does not explicitly name its currency. Additional citations alone do not repair wrong wording. Whether the FEV evidence belongs with that particular open thesis is debatable: its broad description and narrower outcome support different readings.
4. **Location is not meaning.** The clean runner checks that a passage is located in the selected input and, for prepared sources, the captured page. It does not decide whether the passage supports the complete summary or whether a link informs the thesis. That separation avoids giving code an investment-importance judgment. The tested additional-review call also left six of 69 expected evidence reviews unaddressed due to identifier mismatch; requiring a supplied identifier can protect identity, but does not prove correct association or source interpretation.
5. **Existing and proposed downstream paths differ.** Existing production Jev reads an evidence `one_line` and `quote`, so source passages can matter downstream. The clean architecture's multi-passage mapping, Step 2 and later consumers are not fully implemented; neither their behavior nor compatibility with the old writer is established. The old writer's lack of a destination for per-link relevance does not determine what the clean path will read.

## What is not established

The source-backed examples identify output errors, not the model's internal cause. In a few selected cases, the correct wording appears inside the citation even though the summary or explanation changes it. The current schema and recorded output place `summary` before `source_passages`; that does not show why the model made the mistake. A quote-first response is a hypothesis, not a fix. It could also change which theses and evidence the model returns.

A previous selected wrong-link comparison found 6 of 15 low-setting answers and 0 of 15 medium-setting answers with at least one specific wrong-product or unrelated-question link. It did not establish a general improvement; other fidelity issues remained, and medium used substantially more completion tokens. The prompt-placement and same-article citation-completion experiments also did not establish a safe semantic fix. They remain unadopted. A differently designed or independent review has not been ruled out, but there is no evidence to add another review call to the runtime now.

## Agreed next investigation

Review the 49-item historical forward run per article, including its 160 returned evidence records, against the material actually supplied and the original captured page where available. Flag contaminated or incomplete source input separately from model output. For each record, allow overlapping, evidence-backed flags for missing cited support, altered or unsupported claims, source attribution, mistaken relationship direction and thesis-link relevance. Identify the source passage, the record's own citation and the relevant thesis; distinguish clear errors from debatable indirect relevance and cases the available page cannot resolve. Review article-level omissions separately because an omitted fact has no evidence record to flag. Count records and article-level misses separately; do not add overlapping flags as though they were unique errors.

Use a second reader to adjudicate difficult findings and inspect controls, and compare the two seven-item replays separately by article and state. All seven of their source IDs occur in the original 49-item manifest; they are not fourteen additional independent articles. Their source preparation and order also differ. Findings apply to these recorded runs, not the unseen 34,084-item corpus.

Before the full review, exercise the review sheet on known examples, including a record with multiple simultaneous problems and a source-contamination example, plus independently checked ordinary records. Check that reviewers can point to the input, the cited passage and any relevant missing text without confusing an inferred connection with a source assertion. A disagreement is investigated and recorded; it is not automatically a defect in the sheet. This is a review of existing records, with no model calls or changes to the running system.

After that review, choose one intervention and one controlled test based on the evidence. Candidates include a reasoning-setting comparison for question links or quote-first output for fidelity, but neither is approved or presumed effective. Evaluate useful evidence retained, missed conditions, source attribution, thesis selection, link correctness and token cost as well as the targeted defect. Do not use a numeric threshold from this discussion as automatic proof of the cause or as a mandate to choose a particular intervention.
