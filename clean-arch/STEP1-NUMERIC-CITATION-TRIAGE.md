# Step 1: numeric citation triage and source review

**Status: investigative review, not a quality score or a runtime rule.** The [script](../poc/data/clean-step1-capture-controls-2026-11-07/numeric-triage.py) lists digit tokens in an evidence summary that do not appear in that item's supplied passages. It preserves the individual [records](../poc/data/clean-step1-capture-controls-2026-11-07/numeric-triage.json) for source review. A match does not establish support, correct actor, units or period. An absence can reflect formatting, relative dates, calculations or contextual inference. The scan cannot detect nonnumeric conditions, changed meaning, source-role errors, bad links, or a fact omitted entirely.

The three cohorts overlap and are not pooled. The 49-article forward run has 160 evidence items and 250 passage instances; one earlier failed attempt is excluded. The 102-answer comparison has 327 evidence items and 597 passage instances across repeated draws on 17 previously selected articles. The five source-control calls have 15 evidence items and 31 passage instances. None is a random sample or a production acceptance.

These are reader classifications of the **flagged records**, not error rates or estimates of all missed citations. The unflagged controls do not establish the scanner's sensitivity. Equivalent representations such as `$US500`, `2.5x`, or words for numbers can escape or create flags; a supplied page can itself be incomplete or contaminated. The actual sent input and each item's passages were read before classifying a finding.

## Concrete cross-checked examples

In full-page comparison attempt 18, one Quartz answer summarizes four different chip developments. Its `e3` reports Trainium2's growth and Trainium4's expected performance, but its only quote stops at a Trainium3 paragraph; the other details are in the following supplied paragraph. Its `e5` omits the source paragraph for a 6,144-chip cluster, `e6` omits the paragraph naming MTIA 300/450/500 and the 2027 schedule, and `e9` omits the paragraph giving Tesla's performance comparison and mid-2027 target. These details are in the actual supplied article; the gaps are in the attached citations. The one answer and its four records are not four independent articles.

The same cross-check found that attempt 36's `e2` includes a ten-minute tutorial and limitations not in its attached passage; attempt 74's `e1` includes toolkit model names outside its quoted passages; and attempt 91's `e1` includes model size found elsewhere in the sent page. At least some link and source-role questions remain distinct: an unflagged answer quotes a GPU product but links it to a CPU-performance question, and another cites an unlabelled generated summary alongside body text. A matching digit cannot validate either.

The reader's 14 flagged observations in the 102-answer cohort are attempts 8/e2, 16/e1 and e2, 18/e3, e5, e6 and e9, 36/e2, 38/e3, 72/e3, 74/e1, 76/e5, 91/e1 and 102/e3. They are not interchangeable: fiscal periods, model names and article references may need surrounding context, and repeated attempts on the same article must remain separate. The five-control findings and CNBC cross-check are detailed in [the source-control results](STEP1-CAPTURE-CONTROLS-RESULTS.md). The earlier forward-run case is article 11/e1, in [the request audit](STEP1-CITATION-SUPPORT-AUDIT.md).

## Interpretation

The observable class is real: several raw answers used source information without attaching all passages needed for that evidence item's summary. The current request already asks for those passages, and the schema supports multiple excerpts. This triage does not establish why the omissions occur, whether one input or prompt change will fix them, or how often they affect the product after writing and reading. It is not used to reject records, auto-add quotations or decide investment importance. Any next change must also preserve correct paraphrases, relative dates, source-supported multi-paragraph combinations, and legitimate indirect thesis links.
