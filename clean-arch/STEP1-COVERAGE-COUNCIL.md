# Step 1 whole-article coverage: council review

**Status: research and an untested candidate, not an adopted prompt or architecture.** Codex and Claude Code reviewed prior work and challenged one another on 2026-11-06. The requested outcome is a reliable source-backed `{thesis, evidence}` handoff for each article, using its actual open-thesis state. This review concerns Step 1; cross-article synthesis, Jev implementation, production writes and the relationship graph are outside its scope.

## What is known

In the recorded article 34 request, the text included a reported possible Kyber delay and a company denial, and the open-thesis list included the relevant Kyber question. The original answer and six subsequent comparison answers did not return those facts. The missing information was absent from the raw model answer: it was not removed by the runner's checks. A separate comparison returned the report and denial in all three answers when only the original 408-character paragraph was supplied and in none of three full-article answers. The excerpt also changed source length, position and surrounding content, and its outputs blurred the attribution between the report and denial. This is a selected-case observation, not a production remedy or an identified model mechanism. [Trace and original requests](../poc/data/clean-step1-kyber-trace-2026-11-06/TRACE.md).

Another Kyber article and a historical production-request trace retained relevant material under different conditions. The other article, open-thesis state, prompts, schema and output limits differ; the production request's entire text was not established byte-identical during the council. These observations do not isolate length, position, thesis visibility or prompt ordering. A separate show/hide comparison failed to recover the article 34 facts in either condition, three draws each, but does not prove that thesis visibility never matters.

## Council's proposed next candidate

**Historical proposal, subsequently tested:** the order-only candidate below was tested in the [order results](STEP1-COVERAGE-ORDER-RESULTS.md). Its mixed outcome does not establish a fix. Do not treat this section as a current recommendation to rerun an untested candidate.

Test a **single, isolated request-order change**: move the existing evidence-reading block in the clean Step 1 system prompt ahead of the thesis-definition and opening examples. Keep the article, open-thesis list, structured output, examples and other instructions unchanged. The model still returns only evidence linked to existing or newly proposed theses; a new thesis still needs evidence. It receives the entire article and does not receive historical evidence. This is an internal reading order, not a new stored evidence layer or a change to Jev.

The rationale is a hypothesis: the current prompt starts with research ideas and opening questions even though its existing evidence-reading instruction later asks the model to read the complete article. The historical production prompt and the signed standard describe reading relevant statements before filing them. Other request differences and the old block-swap result prevent attributing the Kyber omission to this ordering. Moving text does not establish that Luna actually follows a different reasoning sequence.

Before sending any changed prompt, audit the assembled system prompt, user payload and schema against the signed analyst standard and Alex's clean-pair decisions, with an independent read-only reviewer. Verify the exact text-only move, character and token counts, and that one realistic answer matching the request survives the actual clean contract. Record any unresolved conflict rather than editing the frozen standard. The candidate has not yet been written to the active prompt or run.

A subsequent isolated comparison should rebuild the actual saved clean requests and fixed pre-article states, with only the intended move changed. Compare repeated independent control and candidate answers on the same articles; do not combine or vote across answers. Inspect each article and each raw answer for important omissions, wrong or duplicate theses, wrong links, noise, source attribution, uncertainty and conditions. Include the known Kyber case and other relevant omissions alongside articles with valid indirect links, counterevidence and known noisy or unrelated links. Preserve the exact source, request, before-state and output so Alex can read the replay. Any case inventory is a diagnostic reading of the source, not replacement gold; keep the existing frozen ruler unchanged.

If the candidate still misses the Kyber report and denial in the selected comparisons, that weakens the proposal on this case; it does not prove the model's internal cause or the result on the wider population. If it recovers those statements while introducing wrong links, loss of other useful evidence, unsupported attribution or noisy duplicate theses, report those regressions alongside the recovery and do not present the candidate as a validated fix. A later decision on promotion needs broader evidence appropriate to its scope.

## Alternatives and unresolved design work

- **Source preparation:** Separately address captured-page contamination and source identity, while checking that cleaning does not discard article text. It is a different problem from failing to return a statement already present in the model's input.
- **A second full-article pass:** Existing self-review tests missed conditions and meaning errors. A future design would need to specify article-level reconciliation, source identity, thesis identity, partial failures and effects on the existing trace. Do not merge evidence simply because quotations overlap or match: one passage can contain different statements or speakers. No such design is validated here.
- **Multiple article windows:** Potentially useful as a future hypothesis, but cross-window conditions and attribution, duplicated new-thesis proposals, shared source context, state and latency must first be designed. Do not use digits, dates or paragraph length to decide investment importance. Preserve the full article and the final pair contract. The 408-character diagnostic is not a proposal to truncate articles.

## Continuing review: historical request comparisons

A later council found seven older raw production-era answers that received the same 6,418-character article 34 text and returned both the reported 2028 Kyber delay and NVIDIA's denial. An independent reader verified the original archive rows, source-text hash, response and model alias. The [reproducible archive check](../poc/data/clean-step1-coverage-continued-2026-11-07/historical-archive-check.json) retains the seven archive hashes, exact call IDs and returned evidence. The older calls were saved on October 28 or 31; the clean comparisons ran on November 6–7. The old and clean requests differ in open-thesis context, prompt, response schema, output cap and message arrangement, and the same model alias across dates does not establish an unchanged backend. These are raw answers, not a check that the old ledger retained them. The result suggests a request-level comparison worth studying; it does not establish the effect of any one difference or rule out an interaction with source length, location or surrounding text.

An independent audit of 34 selected full-article clean answers found 19 with the same order-preserving serialized request, of which two returned both the report and denial. The other 15 answers used five different modified requests, three per variant; one evidence-order answer returned both. The modified cases combine a link clarification, hidden thesis, evidence-order change, schema-property order and prepared source text. The prepared text differs from the original input. The two positive unchanged-request responses came from the same-day source-text comparison, which therefore must not be hidden behind the earlier sixteen omissions. Every one of the 34 archived responses has a distinct provider completion ID. The [reproducible selected-call census](../poc/data/clean-step1-coverage-continued-2026-11-07/clean-request-census.json) records source lengths, ordered request hashes, raw response IDs and summaries; its lexical match is not a semantic-quality score. These are selected observations, not a representative rate or a controlled comparison of a single change. The 408-character paragraph-only arm is separate and changed several input properties at once.

Claude's first interpretation attributed the omission to the clean request and dismissed length and position. Codex and two independent readers challenged that inference: the evidence does not identify the cause, and the clean prompt already asks the model to read from the first paragraph to the last and return all relevant matters. The reported zero-reasoning-token count does not show that a short answer is incomplete or that token usage caused an omission. A proposed prompt candidate bundled several distinct changes; a seven-article trial with predetermined outcomes would not isolate the responsible clause and would risk treating uncertain article 12 relevance as an unreviewed gold. The [initial Claude response](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/council-coverage-response.md), [challenge](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/council-coverage-round2-handoff.txt), source audit and prior experiments remain available. No prompt or model configuration was changed on this basis.

In the [third round](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/council-coverage-round3-response.md), Claude withdrew its claim that the full clean request lacks positive evidence examples: the full prompt includes examples involving bank credit, supplier capacity, a regulatory filing, geopolitics, debt exposure and competitor pricing. The number and position of returned items cannot show whether the model read a sentence or considered it irrelevant. A company's relevant denial may inform an existing thesis under the already accepted support-or-challenge rule; the denial and the report it disputes retain their respective attribution and uncertainty. The reviewer proposed more repeated unchanged requests; Codex challenged whether this alone would identify a remedy or whether an appropriately controlled comparison would provide more useful information. The extra article-level counts in Claude's third-round response have not yet been independently reproduced and are not established findings.

### Fourth round: agreement and next investigation

Claude accepted that repeatedly sending one unchanged article measures variation in that selected setting; it does not identify a remedy. The [fourth response](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/council-coverage-round4-response.md) also corrected its claim that the full prompt lacked company-figure examples: it includes loan delinquencies, filings, debt exposure and sales forecasts. Claude identified a possible gap in an explicit example of a named party's claim or estimate. The signed standard covers that class, while the existing prompt already mentions other parties' estimates. A proposed example is a general prompt-audit hypothesis, not a demonstrated cause of the Kyber or financing omissions, and has not been adopted or tested. It still needs the required complete-request audit and independent review before any prompt edit or send.

The shared next direction is to investigate source preparation separately from model omissions, and to evaluate any justified prompt candidate with fixed article text and pre-item thesis state, a same-session interleaved control, varied articles selected and read independently in advance, and blind review of the complete answers. Review omissions, noise, mistaken links, source attribution and qualifications together; preserve the frozen standard and the existing one-call pair contract. Such a comparison can reveal how the candidate behaves on the selected inputs, but cannot reveal the model's internal reasoning or establish production readiness by itself. Exact sample selection, repetitions, analysis and operational constraints remain to be designed; no new acceptance threshold or golden set is adopted here.

Source losses in articles 2 and 16 are established by comparing the captured pages and the actual model inputs. The clean-text preparation code and the population affected must be traced before attributing both losses to a specific mechanism or proposing a repair; alternate source selection has already produced mixed results. Semantic fidelity and incorrect thesis links are separate outstanding issues. The source quote-location change is verified only for its recorded stored-answer scope; locating text does not establish that its meaning or attribution is correct. Claude's additional numerical inventory and its claim about particular cleaner mechanisms have not been independently verified and are not used as established counts or causes.
