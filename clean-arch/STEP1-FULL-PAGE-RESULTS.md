# Step 1 full-page diagnostic: source-backed results

**Status: selected-case diagnostic, not an adopted source strategy or production acceptance.** The [design](STEP1-FULL-PAGE-DIAGNOSTIC.md) compares the original cleaned text with the captured page under the same saved prompt, schema and fixed thesis state. All 102 calls completed: 17 articles, three answers per input. Nine articles use recorded exploratory before-states and eight use a shared hypothetical state. The saved pages were retrieved in October, after the July article dates. The [interactive replay](../poc/data/clean-step1-full-page-diagnostic-2026-11-07/review.html) shows both inputs, all answers, thesis links and quotes.

Before condition labels were opened, three independent readers examined all 102 answers and 327 evidence records against the captured pages, cleaned text and thesis contexts. Their [original reviews](../poc/data/clean-step1-full-page-diagnostic-2026-11-07/review-01-06.md), [second review](../poc/data/clean-step1-full-page-diagnostic-2026-11-07/review-07-12.md) and [third review](../poc/data/clean-step1-full-page-diagnostic-2026-11-07/review-13-17.md) preserve every answer label. Source-first notes were recorded before the calls. Condition hiding was imperfect: a reviewer could infer one answer's input from a page-only quotation. The [revealed review](../poc/data/clean-step1-full-page-diagnostic-2026-11-07/review-revealed.md) adds the actual input labels without changing the original reports. Claude Code independently checked selected source-backed findings; the follow-up council is in progress.

## What changed in concrete cases

| Source | Clean text: three answers | Captured page: three answers | What the observation supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| China H200 outlook, case 1 | None included the CFO's statement that the outlook excluded China revenue; that sentence was absent from the clean input. | All three included it. | The full page exposed a previously removed statement and these three answers used it. This does not prove the statement would always be kept or establish population recall. |
| Firmus, case 2 | All three treated a teaser about **DayOne's** possible listing delay as an update about Firmus. The clean input contains the teaser but strips its later-story headline and date. | None returned that unrelated teaser as Firmus evidence. One page answer had an unresolved new-thesis target. | This particular clean input obscures article boundaries; the page answers avoided this specific contamination in three draws, but still had other errors. The captured target article itself is incomplete. |
| Nemotron tutorial, case 3 | Three answers quoted the AI-summary line; the clean input has the summary but omits its label and the body table. | None of the three quoted those summary lines; all three quoted the body table. | The page provided a usable labelled source and table in these answers. The result does not isolate whether the label, table, position or other context caused the difference. |
| SambaNova, case 5 | None returned the financing paragraph, which was absent from the clean input. | One returned the page's $1 billion funding-round first-close report; two did not. | The content became available, but availability did not guarantee extraction. Whether that financing informs the specific NVIDIA partnership thesis is a relevance judgment on which readers disagree. |
| Kyber, case 8 | None returned the reported delay and company denial. | One returned them; two did not. | The relevant passage was present in both inputs. These samples demonstrate variation, not a proven reason for the omission. |

On five selected NVIDIA technical pages with an explicitly bounded `AI-Generated Summary` component, an [exact quote-overlap count](../poc/data/clean-step1-full-page-diagnostic-2026-11-07/summary-quote-census.json) found 11 of 15 clean-text answers and none of 15 page answers quoted at least one summary line. The page body independently supports some of the cited claims. This is a count on five selected sources, not an error rate; it does not prove the label alone caused the difference or that every such claim is false.

## Problems that remain with both inputs

- **Meaning:** in case 6, the FEV release describes small language models *such as* Phi-4-mini. Five of six answers state that the proposed system uses Phi-4 as a definite model; a sixth says it focuses on that model, which is narrower than the source's wording. Both inputs contain the qualification. An exact quotation does not repair a summary that changes its meaning.
- **Thesis links:** one Firmus answer points evidence to a new thesis ID absent from its own output. Other cases connect automotive, inference or scientific-computing information to thesis questions whose specified subject or outcome may not follow; legitimate indirect relevance must be judged individually. The clean runner recorded the unresolved ID but this diagnostic did not exercise the production writer.
- **Source and quotation:** some answers attribute a reporter's cost characterization to NVIDIA, and some submitted passages are not verbatim. Across all 597 returned passage instances, three did not locate in the text sent and five did not locate in the captured page. The two additional instances are the same passage in two clean-input answers: the cleaner joined lines that were not contiguous in the captured page. Location alone is not semantic or source-role validation.
- **Coverage and noise:** some answers omit concrete material that appears in both inputs; others split one matter into several records or retain weakly connected technical details. The user's rule allows meaningful observable topics without requiring a proven monetary impact; whether a particular topic is noisy is a research judgment, not a fixed size threshold.

These outcomes support further work on both source handoff and source-faithful extraction. They do not justify replacing production input with full pages: the sample is selected, historical snapshots contain later material, three draws per input cannot establish a stable general effect, and the production writer's multi-passage handoff remains unresolved. The current prompt, production configuration and original source database remain unchanged.

The source population also depends on the cleaning decision. A read-only join by `input_id` finds 6,463 articles marked for processing in the raw-page snapshot: 5,848 remain marked for processing in the cleaned snapshot and 615 were switched off. All 5,848 still-fed cleaned articles have a nonempty saved page. This count does not establish how many of the 615 are useful articles; source completeness and article identity need direct inspection before changing eligibility. Among the 5,848, 189 have saved retrieval and publication calendar dates within 0–1 days; 171 of these additionally have an NVDA ticker, nonempty cleaned text and page, and no copy or supersedes marker. This date calculation uses each stored date as represented, not elapsed hours, and does not establish an unexposed, publication-time capture. The raw and cleaned snapshots must remain distinguishable when testing a new source preparation.

The exclusion is not only theoretical: CNBC Investing Club article `gnews:c213dae1f10fafab0a1e4c49` has 9,874 characters of retained article text in one line, including the reported $105 billion Ohio backstop and other investment initiatives. The cleaner marks it `feed=0` because its body check requires three long lines. Conversely, excluded `gnews:daf6b678f1ee122792ea0b89` contains a weather-site shell and `gnews:e720ccf5225621f356d92e9a` lacks an accessible story body; another long excluded page, `gnews:7347610568a6c70f7b821e6c`, contains serialized challenge code. These counterexamples rule out reinstating the entire excluded group or judging a body by length alone. Of the 615, 383 have zero retained lines of at least 120 characters, 170 have one and 62 have two; these are structural counts, not a count of useful articles or false exclusions. A [read-only replay](../poc/data/clean-step1-full-page-diagnostic-2026-11-07/excluded-article-replay.py) using all 34,084 raw rows for template construction and the currently saved HTML reproduced the cleaned text and failing body check for all 615. The saved HTML is not independently verified as unchanged since the historical run.

Within those 615 records, 271 have no cleaned text, all have a nonempty saved page matching the raw snapshot's text, and two have copy markers. Re-enabling them while keeping the old cleaned text would therefore leave empty inputs for 271; the saved page itself still requires source identification. Any isolated candidate needs to preserve both old and upstream processing flags and coverage values rather than treating either as a guarantee of a complete target article.

An offline [sentence-count probe](../poc/data/clean-step1-full-page-diagnostic-2026-11-07/sentence-body-probe.py) tested one proposal to replace the long-line check: count at least three sentences of 120 characters each, regardless of line breaks. Across the 6,463 stored cleaned texts, it would enable 89 previously excluded records and disable 49 previously enabled records. The known one-line CNBC article is recovered, but a readable Apple/NVIDIA market-value article (`gnews:08f40562679463490f9f047d`) would be excluded. In a systematic 12-of-89 review, the newly enabled records included a stock quote page and an unrelated news homepage (`gnews:ca3d4ef80eb5a1acf771ec08`), as well as substantive and partial target-article text. These findings reject this specific replacement; the sample is not a population accuracy estimate. The [full difference list](../poc/data/clean-step1-full-page-diagnostic-2026-11-07/sentence-body-probe.json) is retained for further review. Combining it with the old rule would avoid the 49 new exclusions but would not establish source identity or resolve the remaining exclusions; that alternative is not adopted.

A further systematic review of 14 of the 526 records excluded by both checks found one captured target passage that cleaning shortened, two paywalled leads and eleven records without usable target-article prose. For example, `gnews:160dfe91f4c37eef35330509` names an NVIDIA gaming article but captured a 1Password article; `gnews:2767876dec97a809eafe9591` retains one genuine market-movement sentence while the page contains additional target figures. This is a diagnostic sample, not a population estimate. It demonstrates that preserving the raw page is useful for investigation but does not by itself prove the capture is the intended article.
