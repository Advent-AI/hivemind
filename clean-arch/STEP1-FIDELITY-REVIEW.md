# Step 1: source-by-source fidelity review

## Scope

The original isolated forward run processed **49 articles and returned 160 evidence records with 199 thesis links**. This review compares every returned record with the full text actually supplied to Luna, its own source passages, original speaker/date fields and the open thesis state. Readers also checked article-level omissions and the captured page where available. A second reader independently checked the concrete findings and selected controls in each range; the second pass was focused, not a second full annotation of all 160 records. The original packets, raw provider answers, before/after thesis states and the exact completed trace rows were checked for identity and continuity.

The [interactive review](../poc/data/clean-step1-fidelity-review-2026-11-07/review.html) shows each article, the source sent, captured page, thesis state before and after, every evidence item, citation, link and reviewer observation. The detailed original reviews and cross-checks are linked below. This is one previously examined, NVIDIA-tagged exploratory run: it is not an unseen set, a general accuracy estimate, or proof that the captured pages are complete.

The adjudicated review marked 34 records with at least one definite finding, 74 with concerns only, and 52 without an identified finding. These buckets are a **review inventory, not correctness scores**: the first bucket includes different things such as a known source-contamination effect, misplaced citations, unrelated links and benchmark detail. Some flags reflect the signed keep/reject standard and others reflect semantics; a record can have several categories. “No finding” does not certify a record as correct. Article-level omissions and source-preparation losses are assessed separately and are not included in those record buckets.

The categories above overlap and must not be added as a total number of errors. Some interpretations require judgment: article 29 e4 may paraphrase an interviewee rather than incorrectly attributing every reporter-written sentence; article 28's “independent of a permanent Internet connection” may imply intermittent availability, even though the more explicit limited/no-internet sentence is outside one item’s attached citation. These were downgraded to concerns after cross-check. A mixed fact and attributed view, such as article 17 e1, is not automatically wrong.

## What the model could not see

Article 2's captured page includes a CFO statement about the outlook excluding China data-center revenue, but that line is absent from the cleaned text sent to Luna. Article 16's captured page includes financing and valuation information after the supplied excerpt ends. These are **source-preparation losses**; they are not counted as failures to extract text the model received. Article 40 is a short saved excerpt, so the review cannot score content outside it. Article 3 shows the opposite: an unrelated page recommendation entered the model input and affected an answer.

Article-level raw-output omissions are separate. For example, article 34 contains a reported Kyber delay, NVIDIA's denial and Q2 guidance, with relevant questions already open, but its raw answer contains only DeepSeek evidence. Article 36's raw answer omits management's roughly $1tn revenue-visibility statement; that is attributed visibility over 2026–27, not booked revenue. The review cannot decide whether a later Jev comparison would classify those items as already represented in history.

## What this means for a fix

The errors do not have one demonstrated cause. The raw model sometimes changes the meaning or omits a supporting passage even though the full text is present. The source-preparation path sometimes loses real article text or includes another article. `runner.validate` identifies whether a quotation can be located and preserves unverified text; it does not decide whether a summary, actor or link is semantically supported. That is its observed boundary, not proof of a defect in the check.

The earlier prompt already tells Luna to keep source passages for all summary claims, preserve conditions, distinguish plans from outcomes, and link only to informed theses. Prior prompt-placement, source-first and same-article review experiments had mixed results. We cannot honestly claim that any particular sentence or extra validation gate explains all the failures.

The subsequent isolated diagnostic tested whether **placing the existing `source_passages` field before `summary` in the JSON Schema** changes source support or fidelity. The [completed comparison](STEP1-CITATION-ORDER-RESULTS.md) did not establish a fix, and the change is not adopted. The complete article, thesis state, system text, schema field definitions, field set, validator and paired output remain the same; only property order changes. This does not assert that the model reasons in output order. The frozen production schema already puts a quote before its summary and has had source errors, so the change is a hypothesis, not a known repair. The comparison examined provider field order, important omissions, useful links and whether an exact quote still accompanies a wrong meaning. No new semantic code gate, automatic source-name match or forced split of mixed matters is introduced.

The existing clean trace retains multiple passages and link explanations. The clean Step 3 mapping to the older one-quote evidence table has not been implemented. In the older path the writer stores a missing original date as empty, while the Jev input and revision-order path can use the publication date as fallback; these are different readers and do not prove that this clean run wrote or lost those fields.
