# Step 1: source-backed thesis-link review

Two independent readers examined every link in the eight preregistered additional-source articles: 214 links across 48 answers. They read the full target question and outcome, the evidence, source and link explanation with condition labels hidden. The sample is intentionally stratified and contains repeated answers to eight articles; link counts are not independent events or a population error rate. Readers distinguished a relevant but incomplete signal from a link whose subject or explanation does not connect to the target.

## Findings with source-backed controls

- **Emerald AI and an AI-cloud financial-backstop question:** one answer linked all four financing, demonstration and Aurora energy-management records to the existing question about NVIDIA cloud financial guarantees and revenue sharing; another linked the planned software deployment while explicitly noting the article did not discuss those arrangements. The source does not establish a backstop or revenue-sharing bridge. Four other answers proposed an Aurora energy-management question and linked the actual project and demonstrations; those are source-supported connections, while whether the new question is worth tracking remains an analyst judgement.
- **nanousd and Nemotron/NemoClaw:** three answers linked nanousd/OpenUSD tools to the existing question about adoption of the specifically named agent stack, but the article does not describe their connection. Some other answers proposed a nanousd-specific question. A possible relationship to the existing LeRobot robotics integration question is broader ecosystem context; the article does not provide a specific bridge, so it is recorded as uncertain rather than automatically rejected.
- **Vera Rubin and the Vera CPU question:** two answers connected an article's general planned adoption of Vera Rubin chips to a question specifically about Vera Arm CPU performance and adoption. The article did not say those customers planned to adopt that CPU. A disclaimer that adoption is not confirmed does not establish the missing product connection.
- **Investor trading versus customer demand:** a CNBC article reports investors shifting attention among GPU and memory stocks. One answer linked that to a question about customers' accelerator spending and competitive alternatives; the source did not establish a customer purchasing shift. Other links to market sentiment and infrastructure spending can be relevant context when they do not claim the article proved spending or revenue changed.
- **Self-denying explanations:** an estimate of NVIDIA's valuation was linked to the AI-spending growth question while the explanation itself said it did not materially inform that question. Another link explicitly acknowledged the source did not discuss the named financial arrangements. These differ from a useful caveat such as “this planned integration does not yet prove adoption,” where the evidence can still inform a prospective adoption question.
- **Positive controls:** the DGX Station article actually discusses Nemotron/NemoClaw availability and implementation, which can inform the named adoption question without proving uptake. An article's customer AI-spending plans and NVIDIA growth forecasts can inform an AI-infrastructure growth question without proving that the forecasts will be achieved. Six Ising answers opened a question about prospective adoption and linked capability and availability to it; relevance of the new topic to an investor is a separate judgement from whether its evidence matches its own question.

The earlier [date and link review](STEP1-DATE-AND-LINK-CENSUS.md) examined another 76 links, including cases where a link used a fact from another paragraph without attaching that paragraph and cases of explicitly qualified inference. Neither review uses a hardcoded product-name filter or treats every indirect link as wrong. The sources, targets, answers and citations are available in the [article replay](../poc/data/clean-step1-date-unseen-2026-11-07/review.html).

The existing Step 1 prompt asks the model to match the underlying question, subject and scope, and to explain direct or indirect relevance. Some answers do not follow that instruction. Whether prompt emphasis, model sampling, inadequate thesis descriptions, or another mechanism caused a particular decision remains unresolved; leading retrospective accounts are not proof of the original reasoning. No prompt or relevance gate has been adopted from this review.
