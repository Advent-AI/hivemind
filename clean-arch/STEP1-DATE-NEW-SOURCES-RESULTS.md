# Step 1 date-text diagnostic: preregistered additional sources

## Result

The unchanged candidate date-text description is **not adopted**. On one NVIDIA-authored technical article, all three candidate answers left the original full statement date empty while all three controls recorded the article's supported 15 July 2026 publication date. On another NVIDIA-authored article, all six answers preserved its full date. This is a clear regression in one sampled article, not a measurement of how frequently the issue occurs across the feed or proof of its cause.

All 48 calls completed. Three independent readers inspected all outputs with condition labels hidden, then the key was opened. The [replay](../poc/data/clean-step1-date-unseen-2026-11-07/review.html) shows all eight articles, the thesis context, every answer and citation, and source-contamination notes. Both arms for each article saw the same source and fixed 22-thesis exploratory context; this state is not a production state or verified historical-as-of reconstruction.

The eight source IDs were selected before their titles or bodies were read, using a fixed seed and source-type strata after a structured exclusion of locally recognized clean-Step-1 inputs. The inventory cannot prove that a source was absent from all historical work or detect every syndicated copy under a different ID. The sample intentionally overrepresents company and analyst sources relative to the eligible window and is not a population sample. [Selection and design](../poc/data/clean-step1-date-unseen-2026-11-07/design.md).

## What the readers found

- **NVIDIA's nanousd technical article:** three controls retained the full 15 July date for the company's own published analysis; three candidate answers left it empty. The date was supplied as publication metadata and visible on the page, but its ISO form was not in the cleaned body. This supports the full date under the existing own-analysis rule; whether `statement_date_text` should contain metadata rather than source wording is a separate ambiguity. Candidate answers linked the article to the existing Nemotron/NemoClaw agent-stack and Hugging Face robotics questions even though the article does not name those products; two control answers proposed a nanousd-specific question, and controls also used some broad robotics links. The relative link outcomes are observed in a small sample; they do not establish that the date instruction caused routing differences.
- **NVIDIA's Ising article:** all six answers retained the full 27 July date for the company's own analysis. The three candidate answers left date text empty; two controls placed normalized publication metadata there and one left it empty. Those date-text choices are not automatically wrong. All six cited at least one opening paragraph that the captured page labels as AI-generated, but the cleaned input omitted that label. One quote was contiguous in the cleaned input because checkpoint names had been removed, but not in the captured page; another rewrote “VLM” as “model” and remained unverified. The generated-label and stitched-input problems originate in the supplied source representation, not proof that Luna knew those labels.
- **Emerald AI:** all six preserved the month-only “announced … in October,” without inventing a full date; two control answers also retained an April announcement. One control called Axios's source anonymous although the sent article does not establish that. Several answers linked Emerald material to an existing question about NVIDIA's AI-cloud financial backstop and revenue sharing, although the article does not describe that arrangement. Whether the alternative proposed Aurora energy-management topic is worth opening is an analyst judgement.
- **Motley Fool growth articles:** the answers generally did not invent original dates for Huang's undated statements, analyst forecasts or future reporting periods. Some separately attributed author analyses lacked the available author-publication date. One answer quoted a sentence with words missing; it was retained as unverified. Certain links explained that their own evidence did not materially inform the target question, which deserves review rather than being treated as a correct match.
- **DGX Station article:** some answers attributed the reporter's description of hardware being the only cost to NVIDIA, although the source did not say NVIDIA made that claim. Both conditions produced such attributions. The article's discussion of current availability and integration does not itself establish customer adoption.
- **Ropedia article:** all six responses returned no pairs. Its indirect connections to NVIDIA are a matter of judgement; the article describes investors associated with companies including NVIDIA, not a confirmed NVIDIA investment. The cleaned text includes unrelated recommended stories, one dated after the article; none of the six answers treated that snippet as Ropedia evidence.
- **Apple/NVIDIA market report:** some answers put stock-move day or year-to-date periods into statement-date text. When a record expresses the reporter's own contemporary analysis, its publication day may separately be a supported statement date; a named investor's statement date is not supplied. One answer attributed an inferred Apple preference to Apple itself. These require record-by-record attribution, not a blanket date rule.

These findings distinguish source representation, claim meaning, dates and relevance. The comparison does not establish why a particular answer changed, and a valid contextual inference is not an independently sourced fact. No source was removed after selection because its date opportunities or cleaned text were inconvenient.

## Citation and resource checks

Of 296 passage instances, 293 had located text that also appeared in the captured page, two were unverified, and one was located in the cleaned input but not as a continuous passage in the captured page. This [reproducible location count](../poc/data/clean-step1-date-unseen-2026-11-07/citation_census.py) does not measure whether the passage completely supports the summary or link. No literal string `"null"` appeared in the three date fields of the 158 evidence records in this cohort; the expanded selected-run census is [recorded separately](STEP1-DATE-DESCRIPTION-RESULTS.md).

The 24 control calls used 148,980 prompt tokens and 33,980 completion tokens, including 7,026 reasoning tokens. The 24 candidate calls used 150,684 prompt tokens and 31,252 completion tokens, including 5,963 reasoning tokens. They produced 81 and 77 evidence records respectively; record counts do not establish quality. No production ledger, frozen prompt, configuration or ruler was changed.
