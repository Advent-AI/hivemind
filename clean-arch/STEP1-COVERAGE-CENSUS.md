# Step 1: source-first review of the 49-article replay

## Scope and method

Two independent readers examined articles 1–24 and 25–49 respectively, comparing the saved text sent to Luna, the open theses and the raw answer. They checked captured pages selectively for suspected source issues; the first reader screened long omitted page lines and inspected selected shorter provenance cases, not every removed short line. Part of article 33’s long sent text was truncated in the second reader’s view; the first reader subsequently read all 25,861 characters in separate ranges and found no additional clear omission. Codex checked the raw requests and answers for articles 10, 12, 34, 36 and 42, and independently checked the captured-page differences for articles 2, 7, 8, 16, 32 and 33. The findings below are source-backed review leads, not a frozen gold set, a complete correctness certification, a population recall score or proof of why the model made a choice. Several observations require analyst judgment about whether a detail is worth keeping as an independent matter. Step 2 was not run, so prior evidence novelty is not established.

## Input loss and source identity are separate

- Article 2's captured page contains Kress's statement that the outlook included no China revenue from the relevant market; that line was absent from the text sent to Luna. Its answer captures a different no-revenue statement. This is a source-preparation loss, not an omission of text the model saw.
- Article 16's captured page contains SambaNova's reported first close of a $1bn Series F at an $11bn valuation; the original sent text omitted that paragraph. A separate earlier article had already supplied related financing, so incremental novelty is unknown. In a later source-preparation comparison the paragraph was restored, but none of six answers mentioned the financing; the two observations have different inputs and do not prove a single cause.
- Articles 7, 8, 32 and 33 have an `AI-Generated Summary` label in the captured page that was removed from the sent text while summary content remained. Some answers cited those passages. Source identity is therefore missing even where corresponding numerical details also appear elsewhere in the body; that alone does not establish that those numbers are false.
- Article 3's input includes later related-story snippets, and the answer treats a different company's teaser as possible Firmus listing risk. Its captured article is also incomplete, so content not captured cannot be assessed.

## Previous attempts and next question

On article 34, showing or hiding its Kyber thesis produced zero Kyber outputs in three draws each. Showing only the relevant paragraph produced three of three, while the full article produced zero of three; length, location and surrounding context changed together, so the cause was not isolated. Changing evidence order produced a Kyber output once in three candidate draws but also yielded link and attribution concerns. Recorded source-role selection removed some contamination, but did not reliably improve extraction or preserve the “such as” qualification. In a later same-day comparison for article 34, the Kyber report and denial appeared in two of three original-text answers and none of three prepared-text answers, after sixteen earlier saved answers had omitted them; this shows variation and does not prove the cause of the difference. Higher reasoning and a summary-description change also produced mixed results. These known-case experiments do not establish a general fix. [Trace](../poc/data/clean-step1-kyber-trace-2026-11-06/TRACE.md), [order comparison](STEP1-COVERAGE-ORDER-RESULTS.md), [source-text comparison](STEP1-SOURCE-TEXT-RESULTS.md), [reasoning comparison](STEP1-REASONING-FIDELITY-RESULTS.md), and [summary comparison](STEP1-SUMMARY-FIDELITY-RESULTS.md).

The next investigation should isolate why material already in the supplied article is missed while preserving the full article, source qualifications and the user's thesis–evidence pairing rule. It must examine the complete request and schema against the signed standard and the actual source/answer traces before proposing a change. It must also include source-preparation losses as a separate problem, because a prompt cannot recover text that was not supplied.

A further read-only archive check found seven older production-era raw answers that received the identical 6,418-character article 34 text and each returned both the Kyber report and company denial. These were saved on October 28 or 31; the clean comparisons ran later. The old requests also differ in displayed thesis context, prompt, response schema, output limit and message arrangement. The recorded model alias is the same, but that does not prove the backend model snapshot was unchanged. This is a useful comparison across historical configurations, not a same-day controlled test or proof that any particular prompt sentence, article length or position caused the clean omissions. The presence of raw output does not prove it reached the old evidence ledger. A Codex–Claude council is investigating these differences.
