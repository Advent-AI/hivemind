This is a pre-change audit of the actual clean Step 1 request, not a new frozen standard or an acceptance score. The complete system message, source payload, open theses and structured-output schema from a saved article-28 low-setting response are preserved in [the audit request](../poc/data/clean-step1-reasoning-fidelity-2026-11-07/AUDIT-REQUEST.json). The articles were previously inspected and these are diagnostics, not golden-v5 measurement runs. No gold, standard or production prompt is changed by this audit.

The signed historical extraction standard is [section 5.1](../docs/prd/05-extraction-standard.html#evidence) and sections 5.3–5.5, including the six dimensions a standalone record must preserve. Source tiers are defined in [the views standard](../docs/prd/06-views.md#four-evidence-tiers); analyst relevance and granularity are grounded in [the 90-thesis review](../research_notes/对冲基金论点粒度与金标/codex-challenger.md). ## Complete request and instruction owners

The saved request contains one system message, one serialized source-and-open-thesis payload and a strict JSON schema. The system message tells the model to read all paragraphs, identify every relevant matter, use existing or proposed theses, maintain pair coexistence and explain indirect links. Its evidence section requires a standalone summary, every needed contiguous source passage, source attribution, figures, periods, conditions and uncertainty; the final check asks for missing conditions and invented certainty. The `summary` field description separately asks for a faithful standalone summary naming the actor and preserving figures, periods and conditions, but does not explicitly name source scope, uncertainty or attribution. Other schema descriptions own the passages, original party, reporting speaker, original date, period, tier and link rationale. The exact source item and historical thesis state are data, not instructions.

An independent reader audited the clean prompt and schema against the standard without viewing experiment results. They found the full request already carries the main source-faithfulness requirements; the shorter `summary` field description is locally less explicit, not a contradiction in the overall request. They also flagged an ambiguity in distinguishing an unidentified claimant from a named action actor and reporting outlet; a potentially narrow final-check phrase about specific new analysis; and the inherently judgmental boundary on indirect links. None alone establishes why any model output failed. Tightening a field must not mechanically demand missing attributes, make indirect evidence invalid, conflate claimants with outlets or add new importance gates.

## Candidate under discussion, not adopted

A field-local clarification could make explicit that a standalone summary preserves source scope and uncertainty in addition to actor, figures, periods and conditions. It would repeat concepts already present in the system message. The hypothesis is that making the output field's contract visible beside the field may improve compliance; neither the observed errors nor the self-account prove this. Risks include overhedging, omitting useful indirect context, mechanically adding unsupported qualifiers, or hiding an attribution problem under a more cautious summary. A comparison must use unrelated domains as well as the original examples, preserve useful negative and indirect-link controls, and examine full outputs and omissions, not only whether one named example improves. Historical field-local changes had mixed results; previous prompt-placement and source-passage-order comparisons are not established fixes.

The council and an independent prompt reader must review exact candidate wording and its character/token change before any send. Preserve the original prompt and frozen configuration. A small diagnostic, if used, cannot accept a production version; any later acceptance requires unseen inputs, stateful replay, actual writer and reader behavior, and the unchanged relevant standard.

## One-field diagnostic design

After nine council rounds, the noun-list proposal was withdrawn because it largely repeated the system prompt and used an ambiguous word. The proposed isolated candidate changes only the schema description of `summary`:

- Existing: `Standalone faithful summary naming the actor and preserving relevant figures, periods and conditions.`
- Candidate: `Standalone faithful summary naming the actor; preserve relevant source figures, periods, conditions and qualifications without making a claim more certain or specific than the source.`

“Qualifications” means limits, assumptions or uncertainty the source actually states; it does not require inventing missing information. This tests a field-local expression of the existing source-fidelity standard, not a new relevance rule or output field. The entire system message already instructs the model to preserve conditions and uncertainty, so the hypothesis is **not** that the request lacks that instruction; it is that expressing the boundary beside the field might affect compliance. No observation proves that hypothesis.

The existing description is 101 characters and 15 `o200k_base` tokens; the candidate is 183 characters and 29 tokens, an increase of 82 characters and 14 tokens. The exploratory reason for growth is to isolate the effect of a field-local boundary while preserving all other request text. Deleting the system instruction at the same time would change two variables. This is not a precedent to grow the accepted prompt: if the diagnostic shows value, a separate audit must find an equally supported cut or justify retaining the growth before any adoption.

An independent request-only reader found the wording generally consistent with standard 5.4 and the clean contract, without an importance gate. The reader identified a meaningful ambiguity: “more specific than the source” could be misread as forbidding a summary from combining details in different parts of the same article, even where the source establishes their relationship and both passages are cited. The existing system explicitly allows such a combination. They also cautioned that a source-supported indirect inference may belong in the link explanation, while an unsupported inference must not become a source assertion. The diagnostic must inspect both controls, alongside source uncertainty and useful omissions. The independent reader did not inspect the selected output cases or an exact runtime request, so the local saved-request reconstruction and prior response replay remain required before sending.

The candidate does not claim to fix every error, particularly a change of subject such as equity-market flows rewritten as infrastructure spending. A known example-to-selection error, unsupported currency and future-versus-completed wording provide probes; a named source with facts in different paragraphs, a genuinely indirect thesis link, a relevant planned announcement and an empty-result article are counterexamples. No single article, fixed number of output records or three-draw threshold establishes quality. If selected cases show promise, unseen articles, evolving state, actual writer and reader behavior and the applicable frozen standards still require independent evaluation before adoption.

### Diagnostic outcome

The one-field candidate was compared against the unchanged description on six known articles and independently reviewed with condition labels hidden. The source example remained described as a specific used model in all three answers per condition; other citation and linking concerns remained. The candidate is not adopted. [Results and replay](STEP1-SUMMARY-FIDELITY-RESULTS.md) retain raw answers and limitations. This does not establish why the model made its decisions or rule out other approaches.

### Downstream boundary, not a semantic fix

In one saved article-28 candidate (`10-article-28-candidate-2.json`, evidence-1), the model returned three separate passages. The “such as” qualifier appears in passage 3, not passages 1 or 2. The current production evidence table has one `quote`, and the Jev and judgement renderers truncate quoted text; the clean multi-passage writer mapping is not implemented. A mapping that silently keeps only the first passage could therefore hide the qualifier from a downstream reader. This is a compatibility risk, not an observed clean-production loss. Conversely, retaining and displaying all three passages would not prove that a model or analyst uses the qualifier correctly when the summary contradicts it; Step 1's summary problem still requires its own investigation.

### Attribution follow-ups

Two additional targeted continuations used the saved article-42 and article-43 conversations. Asked to separate the actor from the person who made a statement, the model identified the companies as actors and said the supplied passages did not identify a specific speaker for the cluster figure or supplier-prioritization claim. In article 42 it distinguished a company's announcement from a subsequent reporter-narrated detail. These follow-ups show the model can make the distinction when prompted; they do not establish what it considered in its original output or prove that a new schema would solve the class. Article 42's original “Google says” wording may be a paraphrase of reported company information, so its classification remains a concern rather than a settled error.
