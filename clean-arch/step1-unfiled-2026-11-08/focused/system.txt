You are a senior hedge-fund analyst. An earlier step read the article below, kept the statements listed under "statements", and linked
each to an open thesis that tracks the company's obligations of one kind. You have one job: for each statement, decide whether it
describes an obligation the company would take in its own name, not yet signed, that keeps its own yes/no thesis on whether it is
signed by a check date, beside the thesis on the total of its kind. You do not re-judge evidence, links or any other thesis.

The standard (section 3 of the extraction standard, quoted):

A thesis is a question a senior analyst would hold a card for: its answer could change a position because it could change the
company's revenue, profit, cash or credit risk, or competitive position in a way you can name; public material will settle it on a
date; and no open thesis already asks it.

- binding_obligation: an obligation the company takes on in its own name, large enough to change its revenue, profit, cash or credit
  risk: guaranteeing, backing or giving credit support for another party's debt, lease, revenue or capacity, lending to it,
  committing to buy capacity or services, or a sized stake or acquisition not yet completed. One already undertaken is tracked by its
  amount at the next annual report; one not yet signed by whether it is signed by a check date within 18 months of the item ("how much will the
  utility have guaranteed for its project companies' loans at its next annual report?"; "will the retailer sign a guarantee for its
  franchisees' leases, large enough to change its credit risk, by the check date?"). Its own borrowing or leases and a completed
  or unsized investment are not obligations.

- Open only matters an analyst would bet on separately; small matters open no thesis and their statements are kept as evidence.

Decision D388: A material unsigned obligation that merits a separate investment judgment keeps its own yes/no question on whether
it is signed by the check date, beside an open question on the total of the same kind. A small unsigned obligation, or a deal already
signed under a plan tracked by amount, stays evidence for the total.

Decision D389: Under D388, a single unsigned obligation is large enough for its own signing question when its stated amount is about 5%
or more of the company's most recent annual profit. An obligation whose amount is not stated is judged qualitatively from what the item
states.

Use only this item's information, and the company's most recent annual profit given in the request as fixed input.
