**Both arms are NOT READY:** the final read-back can preserve a significant unsigned obligation as its own question, but earlier instructions can already have rejected it. The exception’s precedence is not clear.

For example, an open question asks for the total guarantees a company has given. A proposed, significant guarantee raises a separate yes/no question: will the company sign it? D388 says to keep that question beside the total if it merits a separate investment judgment. Both requests also tell the model to reject questions whose answers cannot move apart, without expressly exempting this case.

The two system prompts are identical. These verdicts therefore apply to both:

- **Role and linking instructions:** open important questions not already covered, require supporting evidence, and do not open merely to house evidence — **consistent** with D383–D384, but “not covered” can be read too broadly against D388.
- **Section 3 opening test:** importance, a dated public check, one outcome, and the four permitted reasons — **consistent** with §§3–4 as amended by D382 and D384.
- **Product milestone:** revenue-producing step, with separately judged milestones kept apart — **consistent** with D248(3).
- **Obligation:** significant obligations in the company’s own name; signed amounts versus unsigned signing questions; exclusions and dates — **consistent** with §3 and D248(6). It does not itself say the unsigned question survives beside the total.
- **Shared quantity and policy stage:** defined shared quantities and identifiable policy steps — **consistent** with §3.
- **Small matters and customer deployment:** reject small questions; treat customer deployment as evidence unless the company’s own obligation qualifies — **consistent** with D248(6) and §4.
- **Dates and “do not open” list:** the date rules and listed exclusions are **consistent**, except “another estimate, source or wording of what an open thesis tracks” **keeps narrower** if interpreted to include the distinct signing question.
- **Separate-bet instruction and schema:** “If the two cannot move apart” and `why_a_separate_bet` **keep narrower** when applied to D388 without an exception. The schema requires that explanation even for the signing question.
- **Closing duplicate, evidence and public-check tests:** **consistent** when applied to the same question. The unconditional “drop … if … its answer cannot move apart” **contradicts D388** in the exception case.
- **Final read-back:** preserving an unsigned signing question beside the total is **consistent** with D388, but scale alone **keeps wider**: it omits D388’s requirement that the obligation merit a separate investment judgment. It also says signed or small items go to the total *only*, which is too broad if they inform other open questions.

The requests also omit D248(2)’s rule for later deadlines while the earlier question is open, and D248(4)’s fiscal-year versus calendar-year treatment. D248(5)’s identity check for event and policy questions is not stated.

For **c10b**, `unsigned_obligation` describes the same unsigned population as the read-back’s trigger, but includes unsized investments, which §3 excludes, and does not itself test materiality or a separate investment judgment. Moreover, the read-back’s “already signed” branch cannot be reached through a field that must be null for signed obligations.

**c10a: NOT READY** — the unconditional separate-movement rejection conflicts with the unsigned-obligation read-back.

**c10b: NOT READY** — the same conflict, plus the signed-obligation branch is unreachable through its field trigger.
