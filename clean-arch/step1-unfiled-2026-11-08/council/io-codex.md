**hold — replace “naming the company, the thing asked and any amount in full” with “naming the thing asked, any company relevant to that question, and any amount it includes in full”.**

For example, a question about an export rule should name the rule and the authority, without being forced to name NVIDIA when its business is not what is being judged.

1. **The three changes**
   - **Standalone question:** The aim matches D404, but “naming the company” is too broad for a theme question. It can pull a company into a question about the world, contrary to the distinction in D38. The replacement keeps the requirement to make the question understandable on its own.
   - **Theme labels:** The change matches D38 and D404: name each outcome, without bullish or bearish labels. The schema and closing check agree.
   - **Reference words:** The change matches D405: copy one unbroken passage from the article. It is consistent with the strict check; requiring exact copying is narrower than the check’s permitted normalization of spacing, case and punctuation, but does not ask for anything the check refuses. It does not fix treating a present amount as an expectation; the existing sentence already says to use null when there is no expectation.

2. **Fit to the articles:** None of the added wording names NVIDIA or a test article. The unconditional “naming the company” is a general wording problem, not a company-specific example.

3. **The three previously correct cases:** The yes/no shipment question already names the company and can retain its two answers. The Ohio question already names the company; “any amount in full” could tempt the model to put the unjudged amount into the question, so the replacement limits it to amounts the question actually includes. The no-main-thing case should still be declined: the new sentence applies to a question being written, and does not override the explicit refusal rule.

The change also cannot supply facts absent from the source: for the four-hyperscaler question, the article and topic do not name the four companies. The new wording alone cannot make that question fully identifiable without invention.
