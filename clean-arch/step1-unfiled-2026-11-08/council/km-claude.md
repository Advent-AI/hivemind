What I think it means:
1. The baseline is high and concentrated: three figure questions whose evidence has no amount carry every fault, before a
   sized comparable arrives. Yes/no questions are clean.
2. For Stage B the risk is in the ladder step's figure questions with no reference; the per-shape comparison (figures
   against 8%) is the one that tells, not the total against 3%. The total depends on the mix of shapes.
3. Line 5 as proposed (last-judgement SUPPORTED within 10 points of 100%) asks at least 90% of judged theses to end
   supported. With 7 to 12 laddered theses a draw, one unsupported last judgement in a draw of 10 is 90%: the line has
   almost no slack, and a single draw can miss it by chance.
4. The reading agreed with itself too well to measure the readers' threshold: 155 of 159 alike, so "the readers move by
   one in fifteen" (the second-look test) does not show here; it may be the form (the judge's rules quoted in full) or
   the fact that most judgements are easy yes/no repeats.

One recommendation for Alex: set Stage B's lines with the per-shape figure line made explicit (Stage B's figure-question
UNSUPPORTED share compared with the baseline's 8%), and open Stage B's paid send (about $0.3 a draw, three draws, an
estimate) on the step as it ran in the fourth Stage A, read in the same shape as the baseline (each judgement alone). One
test: Stage B itself, read with the same form, controls and count.

Unsure: whether line 5 should be relative to 100% at all with so few theses; whether the readers' near-total agreement
is a sign the form is clear or that both families share a lenient threshold on judgements that merely repeat the
previous conclusion.
