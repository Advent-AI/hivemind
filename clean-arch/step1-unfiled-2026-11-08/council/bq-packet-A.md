# The standard (verbatim)

3. WHEN TO PROPOSE A NEW THESIS

A thesis is a question a senior analyst would hold a card for: its answer could change a position because it could change the
company's revenue, profit, cash or credit risk, or competitive position in a way you can name; public material will settle it on a
date; and no open thesis already asks it. A thesis asks one quantity for one period, or one event. It opens for one of four reasons, a closed set; a matter that fits none of them is
evidence, kept and linked to the theses it informs or left unlinked, never a thesis:
- product_node: when the generation of the main product that carries the company's value reaches the step that brings revenue,
  usually first shipments; announcements and samples are its evidence, and only milestones an analyst bets on separately (a drug's
  trial result and its approval) are separate theses ("will the carmaker's new platform start deliveries in 2026?";
  "will the drugmaker's next-generation inhaler, the product line that carries its value, reach pharmacies by mid-2027?").
- binding_obligation: an obligation the company takes on in its own name, large enough to change its revenue, profit, cash or credit
  risk: guaranteeing, backing or giving credit support for another party's debt, lease, revenue or capacity, lending to it,
  committing to buy capacity or services, or a sized stake or acquisition not yet completed. One already undertaken is tracked by its
  amount at the next annual report; one not yet signed by whether it is signed by a check date within 18 months of the item ("how much will the
  utility have guaranteed for its project companies' loans at its next annual report?"; "will the retailer sign a guarantee for its
  franchisees' leases, large enough to change its credit risk, by the check date?"). Its own borrowing or leases and a completed
  or unsized investment are not obligations.
- shared_quantity: a quantity or price outside any one company that several companies depend on, for this or next calendar year:
  several companies' combined output or spending, a contract price, an interest rate ("the largest container lines' combined capacity
  added in 2026"; "next year's average contract price of a raw material"). Another population or wording of the same aggregate is
  evidence for it.
- policy_stage: the step a policy or regulatory process reaches, naming who decides ("will the regulator approve the drug for a second
  indication?"; "will the government require licences for the product's exports by a date?").

- Open only matters an analyst would bet on separately; small matters open no thesis and their statements are kept as evidence. A
  customer's deployment plan is evidence for the company's demand and product theses, unless it changes the company's own cash or
  credit risk, which is judged as an obligation.
- Give the date the item supports. When it gives none, use the date the reason fixes and say how it was set: an obligation already
  undertaken, its next annual report; an unsigned obligation, a check date within 18 months of the item; a pending regulatory
  decision, the current fiscal year-end. A shared quantity takes its release date and a product event the date its outcome can be
  checked, only when the item supports one. A question no date can settle is rewritten or not opened.
- Do not open a thesis about: an executive appointment or organisational change; the company's own borrowing or leases as an
  obligation; a buyback authorisation or dividend declaration; a financial measure's level, growth, margin or guidance for any period,
  on its own; an undated moat or strategy; a feature, content or software update, price point, regional service or variant of a
  product already on sale; another estimate, source or wording of what an open thesis tracks; an annual quantity after next year (an
  obligation excepted); shareholder votes, bylaws or routine litigation procedure. Their statements are evidence for the theses they
  inform, or left unlinked.
- Before proposing a new thesis, name its reason (opens_as), the public material that will settle it (settled_by), and the closest
  open thesis with why an analyst would bet on the two separately because their answers can move apart; with no open thesis, say so.

Decision D388: A material unsigned obligation that merits a separate investment judgment keeps its own yes/no question on whether
  it is signed by the check date, beside an open question on the total of the same kind. This confirms D248 boundary 1's exception,
  which had not been put to him. A small unsigned obligation, or a deal already signed under a plan tracked by amount, stays evidence
  for the total.

According to the report, the initiative is internally referred to by some NVIDIA personnel as the 'AI Compute Partnership.' A NVIDIA spokesperson also confirmed the existence of the program. This move marks a significant strategic shift for NVIDIA:

Business Model Shift: From Selling Chips to Sharing Cloud Revenue
According to NVIDIA’s official press release, the company will earn not only standard product revenue but also an additional share of cloud service revenue, creating a usage-based, recurring revenue stream. The core objective of this model is to overcome the long-standing financing barrier that has constrained startups from accessing large-scale computing power.

The promise of this new model is to realign economic structures so these groups can rapidly access full-stack accelerated computing capabilities without waiting for the traditional infrastructure build-out cycle to complete.

Taken together, NVIDIA is constructing a multi-layered mechanism to align interests: equity investments, capacity leasebacks, lease guarantees, and now revenue-sharing arrangements. Each layer deepens the financial ties between NVIDIA and downstream cloud service providers, enabling NVIDIA to directly share in the incremental returns from the commercialization of AI computing power beyond chip sales alone.

Historically, September has been a difficult month for Wall Street. The S&P 500 has fallen by an average of about 1.1% during the month from 1926 through 2024. Yet Nvidia ( NVDA +2.55% ) stock has gained ground in seven of the past 10 Septembers, with a median return of about 1.5%. Hence, while the September Effect may offer a reason for some caution around the overall stock market, it hasn't been such a negative indicator for Nvidia's stock.
And recently, CEO Jensen Huang also gave investors a potentially more important signal. He said Nvidia expects revenue to grow about 70% in its fiscal 2028, which begins Jan 31, 2027. But management says even that forecast doesn't reflect the full scope of demand for its offerings; it's constrained by the limited supply of components required to build its artificial intelligence (AI) platforms.
Nvidia's visibility into future demand is sharpening
In its fiscal 2027 second quarter (which ended July 26), Nvidia's revenue soared by 106% year over year to $96.2 billion, including $89 billion in sales from the data center segment. However, management's long-term outlook was even more impressive.
Wall Street analysts had previous expected the company to deliver fiscal 2028 revenue of about $570 billion, translating to growth of roughly 44%. Nvidia's guidance, however, implies revenue of close to $700 billion.
CEO Jensen Huang said that Nvidia has never offered revenue guidance for a full year in advance before. However, this time, the company has much better visibility into memory and manufacturing capacity, as well as the land, power, and data-center infrastructure needed to deploy its products. Some of this infrastructure needs to be secured two to three years in advance.
Supply constraints could limit Nvidia's fiscal 2028 growth
CFO Colette Kress highlighted that customer forecasts currently indicate Nvidia's revenue may double next year. Huang also said Nvidia's revenue growth could be "a lot higher" if the company were not supply-constrained.
Nvidia exited the fiscal second quarter with $279 billion of supply and capacity commitments, up sequentially from $119 billion. These commitments are primarily related to memory purchases and manufacturing capacity. Of that total, $92 billion is scheduled for the rest of fiscal 2027, $87 billion for fiscal 2028, and $88 billion for fiscal 2029.
Nvidia's largest customers are also continuing to spend heavily on AI infrastructure. Amazon ( AMZN +0.56% ) Web Services plans to deploy another 2 million Nvidia GPUs in calendar years 2027 and 2028. Nvidia also expects the combined capital expenditures of the top five hyperscalers to approach $800 billion in 2026 and $1.3 trillion in 2027.
The demand trends look impressive, even when the company is not factoring any data center compute revenue from China into its current outlook. Hence, a meaningful recovery of its position in the Chinese market is not currently part of Nvidia's growth expectations.
Nvidia's revenue opportunity per AI data center is expanding
Nvidia is also expanding its revenue opportunity from each gigawatt (power capacity) of AI infrastructure. Management estimates that this opportunity has increased from roughly $18 billion with Hopper GPUs to $25 billion with Blackwell systems and $40 billion with Vera Rubin systems. These increases are partly a result of the fact that Nvidia is selling more components of the overall AI system, including CPUs, GPUs, NVLink (Nvidia's high-speed technology for connecting GPUs and other processors), and other networking products. Hence, the company benefits not only from the construction of more AI data centers, but also because it is generating more revenue per gigawatt of new capacity deployed.
Increasing adoption of agentic AI could further drive demand for compute capacity. Huang said AI agents can require roughly 15 to 100 times more compute than direct human use of AI, depending on the task. These agents can also run continuously and interact with other agents. Hence, future demand for Nvidia's wares may increasingly depend on the actual use of AI applications rather than on the computing power required to train AI models.
Nvidia's growth outlook also comes with risks
Nvidia, however, has warned that customer demand forecasts can prove inaccurate. Its customers may also delay purchases because of constraints related to data center infrastructure or capital availability. In such a scenario, Nvidia's large commitments to its own suppliers could result in higher costs.
Nvidia is also providing support for some large AI infrastructure projects. In August, the company agreed to provide guarantees of up to $105 billion for a data center project in Ohio. That campus will exclusively host Nvidia computing systems under 20-year leases to OpenAI . Huang also said AI labs for which Nvidia expects to use its balance sheet could account for roughly one-quarter of the company's business in fiscal 2028.
Rising memory costs could also pressure profitability. Nvidia expects its gross margins to fall from 75% in the fiscal second quarter to around 71% to 72% in the fiscal fourth quarter, before improving to around 72% to 73% in fiscal 2028. Management attributed much of this pressure to sharp increases in memory prices.
Nvidia's stock also faces near-term pressure. On Sept. 1, rising Treasury yields and oil prices pushed the Nasdaq Composite down by 1%. Besides these marketwide risks, Nvidia is also facing concerns about some of its AI financing arrangements. Its stock slipped by 1.5% during the session.
September could still bring volatility for Nvidia investors. However, it is obvious that Nvidia now has much greater visibility into demand several years ahead. If that visibility holds, short-term weaknesses could matter far less than the scale of the growth opportunity the company is preparing for.

Rising memory costs could pressure profitability. Nvidia expects gross margins to fall from 75% in the fiscal second quarter to around 71% to 72% in the fiscal fourth quarter, before improving to around 72% to 73% in fiscal 2028. Management attributed much of this pressure to sharp increases in memory prices.
Nvidia is also deploying its balance sheet in unprecedented ways. In August, the company agreed to provide guarantees of up to $105 billion for a data center project in Ohio, which will exclusively host Nvidia computing systems under 20-year leases to OpenAI. Huang said AI labs for which Nvidia expects to use its balance sheet could account for roughly one-quarter of the company's business in fiscal 2028.
The adoption of agentic AI could further accelerate demand. Huang said AI agents can require roughly 15 to 100 times more compute than direct human use of AI, depending on the task. These agents can run continuously and interact with other agents, meaning future demand may increasingly depend on the actual use of AI applications rather than the computing power required to train AI models.
The next test comes with Q3 results, where management has already committed to $108 billion in revenue. If Vera Rubin ramps as promised and hyperscaler capex holds, the record book for corporate profitability may indeed need a new binding.
