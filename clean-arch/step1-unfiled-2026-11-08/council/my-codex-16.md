**The proof is not ready to build yet.** The proposed harness can test part of the matching and writing path, but its recovery test and its description of the fake comparisons would let a pass claim more than it exercises.

For example, adding `v14` changes the **whole Jev request**, which contains the candidate and a set of relation questions. Add explicit cases through dispatch and replay for a failure before extraction, after the handover is committed, during matching or writing, and after the item is completed. Check that the same sequence and accepted handover are used where required, later articles do not pass unfinished earlier work, and no question or evidence is written twice. 4. **Correct the list assertions.** D375 requires questions that were open and committed before the item, for that item’s objects and the applicable themes. It does not require the per-item list to contain every day-start question: one may have closed or been superseded earlier that day. Check both an earlier same-day opening and an earlier same-day closure. The expected list must be derived independently, including the relevant objects, open/closed state, groups, aliases and parents—not by calling the function under test twice.

5. **Strengthen the control and state checks.** The feasibility script compared selected columns of five kinds of record; it did not prove the complete stores identical. In particular, it did not compare all question keys, groups, aliases, dependencies, handovers or match decisions. Its deliberately refused partial-day judgement also writes failure records to the scratch store. Account for those records and verify the state needed by the tested entry point before calling it equivalent. 6. **Define what may differ in the fake-“different” arm.** Even when `v15` still opens, the changed comparison request and match record necessarily differ; group membership or links could also differ. Compare the product state field by field and declare the allowed differences in advance, rather than require “no other change to the store.”

The proof can still be useful after these changes. Its conclusion should remain narrow: **given specified fake service answers, the proposed visibility path passes the existing checks and writes the expected records**. It cannot show that real comparisons return those answers, that the reader benefits, or that D375 is ready for acceptance.

Council position: Revise the proof design before building it: make the synthetic request boundaries explicit, add real interruption-and-resume cases, correct the as-of list invariant, and verify the state and allowed differences. Keep the stored-answer injection only as a clearly labelled matching-and-writing stand-in.
