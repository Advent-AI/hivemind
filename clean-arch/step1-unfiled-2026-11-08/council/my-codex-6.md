I would change the proposed falsifier. **Variation across three July draws as large as the observed difference would not prove that comparison is impossible at an affordable size.** Three draws give a noisy estimate; the July questions and frozen-reference outcomes also do not measure all the fresh-news readings. Nor would a large observed gap prove three new draws can reliably separate the systems.

The $0 step should therefore produce a decision-ready design with:

- The **actual per-draw counts** available for each reading, their denominators, reader disagreements, and the difference between systems. Mark readings that have only one draw or were not performed; do not manufacture comparable results.
- A predeclared **difference the comparison is intended to detect**, and a sizing calculation that shows its assumptions and uncertainty. Use the stored draws as limited inputs and stress-test plausible variation. Report a result as inconclusive when the evidence cannot support the calculation.
- A delivery check that can detect both an omission and a non-omission. The 9-of-9 custom-chip example is a **group-of-facts** positive control; it does not calibrate a claim that one fact would change an answer.
- A source and exposure check for the chosen news. If the 40 previously read articles are excluded, check how that changes the news sequence and coverage; taking another checked window may be cleaner.
- Equal draws for both systems, the cited-statement check, explicit empty cases, and a cost and ceiling for the actual article list. D505 remains in force, and D489 is not rescored.
