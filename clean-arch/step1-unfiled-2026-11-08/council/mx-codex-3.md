**The test is worth doing, but its current pass line can call an incomplete or misleading record usable.** For example, if both readers say “not in the material,” the design does not say whether that counts as a matching answer—even though a record that answers nothing must not pass.

The following changes are needed before reading the entries:

- **Selection and reference:** The record covers the whole 235-article week, but the reference covers only 40 articles. The record reader could correctly cite a figure from outside the 40 and be marked different. Fix the article population for each question *before* building the reference: either restrict both sides to the same articles or give the reference reader the full week. Record the population and article IDs. The seeded 20-plus-20 fidelity sample must also name the actual eligible populations and preserve their sizes; it cannot be reported as a representative 40-entry error rate.
- **The six questions:** Q1’s “now,” Q4’s “last” and Q6’s “differ” need an explicit cutoff and comparison rules. Distinguish a changed estimate from two sources reporting different estimates at the same time. Q4 asks *why* margin changes, but the view selects financial figures; a cause in an accompanying statement could be missing even if the figure fields are correct. Keep that question, and count the missing explanation as a usability limitation rather than guessing. Fix in advance which parts of each multi-part question are required.
- **Fidelity and grouping:** Wrong speaker or date can make a financial record misleading, yet neither currently makes an entry wrong. Include both in the fidelity outcome; distinguish an accurately stored unknown from a missing value the article states. Check that collapsing a Step 2 group does not erase different figures, periods, qualifications, sources or dates; retain source-level access and flag conflicts. The keyword filter must report relevant revenue or margin figures it excludes, and the fiscal-year normalization must not equate different fiscal calendars or unspecified years.
- **Lines:** Define “same” as answering every required part supported by the common source population, with correct attribution and no unsupported claim. “Both lack it” is **unexercised**, not a successful answer. Require a predeclared minimum of answerable questions and count wrong answers, omissions and reader disagreements separately. Do not let “wrong by both” turn an unresolved single-reader error into a pass. Show the individual marks and settle verdict-changing disagreements against the article.
- **Who reads:** The reference must be written from the articles without seeing the record or its answers. The record reader sees only the frozen view. Comparers should be blind to which answer came from which source, inspect citations, and report unresolved disagreements. Clarify how the two reader families operate at $0; no model call is authorized in this read-only round.
- **Claim after a pass:** A pass would show that this particular stored week’s selected financial information can be assembled and read faithfully under the fixed questions and lines. It would not establish comprehensive extraction, a production-ready record, an effect on judgements, or that financial questions are unnecessary. A failure identifies a problem to trace; it does not automatically prove that standing questions are the remedy.

Council position: First run the $0 reconstruction test after fixing the common source population, required question parts, attribution and missing-data rules, and the treatment of reader disagreement. Judge the resulting record against the original articles; reserve any product choice about standing questions or presentation until the evidence is read.
