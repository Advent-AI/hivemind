## What I think it means

1. **The generic second look is not a fix.** It mends what one rule and one field show (the three wrong refusals; a ceiling
   as a reference; a missing unit) and, told to check every rule, also acts on right answers: two correct references
   removed, a correct refusal turned into a ladder, one fault traded for another twice.
2. **The pattern of four runs holds for a fifth kind of change.** Each run mended its target class and showed a new small
   one. The second look does the same inside one test. AGENTS §7.3 (D391): "A fix that keeps moving its error to
   neighbouring cases stops being tuned on that set."
3. **Most of the fourth run's agreed faults are real**: 14 of 18 agreed again by fresh readers, 13 on the same mark. So the
   step, as it ran in run 4, writes an answer with a fault two readers agree on about 18 times in 131 (the lines count the
   worst kinds: 0 to 1 wrong questions, 1 to 2 rulings out, 2 to 3 wrong refusals).
4. **The readers move by about as much as what is left**: 2 of 30 clean answers draw an agreed fault on a re-read; the third
   readers rule alike on 22 of 54 points. A line of "none in 131" read this way can be missed by a step with no fault of
   that kind. I proposed those lines (D400) without estimating that; AGENTS §7.1 asks for it ("the criterion's null
   estimated").
5. **What nobody has measured:** what these faults cost the judgement. A wrong refusal leaves a thesis unjudged; a wide
   ruling out touches a rung rarely reached; a question on 2027 due at the end of 2028 reads "still open" for a year. Only
   the frozen judge on these ladders shows it (Stage B, design §8, an outline; its first part is a $0 reading of 159 stored
   judgements).
