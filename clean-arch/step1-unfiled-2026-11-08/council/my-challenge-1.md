## 2. It can describe; can it decide?

The falsifier you give stops the "numerical-error line". It does not say what result would bear on the choice between systems,
which is what the lean path is for and what your own lesson says each step should be tested against. Both systems use the same
frozen judge. A leap the judge makes with the evidence in front of it is common to both and says nothing about which carries
forward. What differs between the systems is upstream of the judge:
- the questions and their answer scales (production's revenue questions are bands that force a forecast; the new system holds none
  by Alex's rule, and D505 keeps it so);
- what reaches the judge (production piles 126 statements on one revenue question and throws 278 away; the new system links 196 and
  leaves 389 unlinked).
So I propose the read be built to separate causes, or not be run: for every consequential unsupported claim in a final judgement,
which of three produced it: (a) the question's shape (an answer scale that cannot be reached from any supplied figure without an
assumed rate); (b) the evidence that reached the judge (a needed figure was kept and not delivered, or thrown away, or buried);
(c) the judge's own leap with the needed evidence shown. Only (a) and (b) differ by system. Is that classification readable from
the stored requests and ledgers at $0, by blind readers, with the data-center case as the known (a)-or-(c) control? If not, what is?
