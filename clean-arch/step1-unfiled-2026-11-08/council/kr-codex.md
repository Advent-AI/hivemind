## 2. What is weaker than it looks?

**The baseline’s apparent precision is misleading.** There are 159 daily judgments but only 16 question paths. Repeated judgments on the same question tend to share evidence and errors. All four unsupported decisions sit in three paths. Zero unsupported final judgments out of 16 is compatible with a much worse underlying rate; the project’s own calculation gives an upper one-sided bound of about 17%. [Baseline result](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step4-2026-11-10/stage_b/RESULT.md>), [chance calculation](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step4-2026-11-10/stage_b/result/line-chance.txt>).

**Agreement is not independent proof of correctness.** The two reader families agreed on 155 of 159 baseline judgments, but they could share a mistake. The six planted errors were selected after both families caught them in a trial; they test whether readers catch those errors, not whether readers recognize a sound but debatable judgment. No control is known to be sound. One of three repeated readings changed from supported to insufficient. [Baseline plan](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step4-2026-11-10/stage_b/BASELINE.md:41>), [result](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step4-2026-11-10/stage_b/RESULT.md>).

**One calculation cannot fail on the issue it purports to model.** Resampling the baseline to estimate the chance of failing the final-judgment line produces zero failures because the baseline contains zero unsupported final judgments. The report says this explicitly and adds hypothetical rates, but those hypothetical rates are assumptions, not measurements of the current judge. [Chance calculation](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step4-2026-11-10/stage_b/result/line-chance.txt>).

**Stage B can pass while leaving important work unjudged.** Its last-judgment rate excludes insufficient judgments and reports them alongside; it also operates on the 26 questions the step produced, while eight theses lack questions in the dry runs. Its other coverage and control lines matter, but a favorable correctness percentage alone cannot establish that the user received answers to the important questions. [Reading plan](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step4-2026-11-10/stage_b/STAGE_B_READING.md:32>).

Finally, the seeded gain and the opening gap are each based on three draws over one historical company-and-article window. The direction is useful; the size should not be treated as a reliable forecast across companies.

## 3. Lessons about why progress has been slow—and what worked

The largest waste was **reviewing imagined behavior for too long before observing the model**. The log says roughly 30 design versions and over 60 reviewer sessions preceded a six-topic probe; that probe found three classes of model behavior the reviews had not found. The reviews were not useless, but many polished cases that the model might never produce. A small, authorized probe earlier would have changed what was worth reviewing. [Round log](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step1-unfiled-2026-11-08/LOG.md:3345>).

What worked was **isolating a mechanism and testing it before a larger measurement**. For example, the model repeatedly estimated different publication dates for the same annual figure. Moving the date calculation into code made dates consistent: from 18 of 34 topics in run 2 to 34 of 35 in run 3. That shows consistency, however, not that the chosen date is when the information actually appears. [Run 3](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step4-2026-11-10/stage_a/result-3/RESULT.md>).

Review also caught issues that would have invalidated or made the next test unsafe: identifiable planted answers, a spending cap that concurrent launches could exceed, and a question that could be judged after its due date. The second build review still found serious issues; the present launcher now contains an exclusive lock, but the summary correctly says another review is needed. I have not independently run its tests. [Baseline review history](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step1-unfiled-2026-11-08/LOG.md:4655>), [second build review](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step1-unfiled-2026-11-08/council/kp-codex.md>), [launcher](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step4-2026-11-10/stage_b/run_stage_b.py:39>).

My assessment is that the loop should use **small tests to decide which risks are real, then review the parts whose failure would change the result or exceed an agreed boundary**. The baseline’s chance analysis is a good example: it changed the decision from a single overall percentage to separate figure and yes/no results, with uncertainty disclosed. Repeated review over speculative corners, by contrast, had diminishing returns. This is a recommendation about future effort, not a request to bypass Alex’s existing review condition.

## 4. Is Stage B the highest-impact next step?

**The largest demonstrated product gap is opening the right questions. The best immediate next measurement is still Stage B, provided the required clean review is completed first.**

The distinction matters. On the seven selected important questions, the system opened and correctly answered an average of four without seeds, versus 6.33 when given the questions. For example, it never opened the specific memory-price question in any of the three draws, although it opened broader related questions. An accurate answer cannot reach a user when the relevant question does not exist. [Unseeded results](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/unseeded-2026-11-10/result.txt>), [adjudication](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/unseeded-2026-11-10/adjudication-runs.md>).

The eight of 34 theses without questions are a related but **different** loss: Step 1 did open a topic, but the next step did not turn it into a judgeable question. The 200 stranded links show potential downstream exposure; they do not show how many of those refusals were wrong or how many final answers would change. Stage B should report that loss prominently, not bury it behind the accuracy of the surviving 26.

The capital-spending problem is also real, but its measured scope is narrower: the system failed to write the fact needed to infer NVIDIA’s capital spending, and two attempted changes failed their registered tests. It is one known high-value question, not evidence that all capital-spending questions fail. [D393–D394](</Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/DECISIONS-log.md:4258>).

I would finish Stage B next because Alex has already authorized it after a review finds no serious issue, it is prepared, and it answers a separate unresolved question: **when a question is produced, does the real judge make defensible answers on it?** If that fails, improving opening alone would send more questions into an unreliable judge. If it passes, I would prioritize a source-by-source diagnosis of the opening gap and the eight refusals before choosing a change. I would not infer that either gap is fixed by a Stage B pass.
