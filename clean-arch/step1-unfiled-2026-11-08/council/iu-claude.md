# Council iu: my own reflection on Stage A's result, written before any council read (2026-11-10)

**What is solid.** Three lines are missed whoever reads: a gap or an overlap in the rungs (7 ladders failed by both reader
families), an unjustified refusal (1 of 17 by both), and the three sends of a thesis not agreeing (the favourable end in 2 of 8
theses, the due day in 11 of 32, both counted by code). The acceptance failed.

**What is not solid.** Lines 1, 2 and 5 turn on the reader. The strict family holds 47 of 111 ladders sound, the other 84; the
third reader is of the strict family and ruled with it on 54 of 73 differing marks. "21 of 44 theses right" is the strict
reading and "36 of 44" the other. I chose the stricter third reader on purpose (I wrote the step and am of the other family),
and I knew the trap: a family's adjudicator sides with its family. So the count as ruled is a floor, not a measurement of how
many ladders are wrong. The 25 ladders both families fault are the firm set.

**Where the faults are.** Not spread evenly: five states for the company are unsound in 10 of 17 by both families (15 of 17 as
ruled); yes/no in 2 of 24; figures in 12 of 67. A thesis that is an event with more than two outcomes is where the step fails,
on three marks at once: the states (R), the labels and the Neutral baseline (F), and whether a regulator's decision is the
company's question or a theme's (O). The practice readings said this before the send ("five states that cover every outcome
are hard to write even by hand").

**What the model is being asked to do in one call:** pick the main thing, pick the object, pick the shape, write the states or
the ranges, order them by what is good for the business, find the reference and quote it exactly, set a day. The faults fall on
the parts that are judgement with no anchor in the article (states, order, day of publication) and on one part where the field
cannot hold what the article says (a range as the expectation).

**What code could own (identity, not importance):** a figure's due day, from the year the figure covers; refusing a company's
quarterly figure, as production's sift does; the reference's kind (an expectation, or the last reported amount) as a typed field
rather than one meaning stretched over both. What stays the model's: which thing is the main one, the states of an event, the
direction.

**What I do not know.** Whether the faults readers mark change what the judge concludes. A gap for "shipped and not yet
confirmed" may never be met; a flipped direction turns the stance round. Stage A's lines count them alike. Stage B would show
it, and Stage B is not open (D401; AGENTS §7.3: a failed step is fixed and re-run at that step).

**My lean for the next step, before the council:** stay at this step. Take the classes both families agree on, largest first,
and close each at its owner rather than with sentences: (1) an event is asked as yes/no, one event and its complement, and five
states are dropped for events (this reverses D399 and is Alex's); (2) a figure's due day is set by code; (3) a company's
quarterly figure is refused by code; (4) the reference carries its kind and may be a range. Prove each on the stored answers at
$0, then run this same Stage A once. And put to Alex the one reading of the form the families split on: a group named and not
listed.

**What would change my mind:** if the yes/no ladders of the same event theses are no sounder than their five-state ones (the
stored run has seven theses written both ways: read them side by side first); if readers of both families fault the code-set
due day as often as the model's.
