# Step 1 quote integrity: saved-output census

## What was checked

Eight historical comparison cohorts contain 60 article packets and 360 completed answers. These cohorts overlap: the same article and quotation can occur in several experiments or draws. The census records passage instances within each cohort, not independent articles or a system-wide error rate. Every blind answer was matched to its original request and saved result, and file hashes and answers, including answers with no passages, are retained in the [reproducible census](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/census-results.json). An independent reader checked all 360 joins. Captured-page matches establish text location only; they do not prove article-body identity, attribution, source completeness or semantic support.

Both clean-only cases were read against the captured page: the Ising quotation crosses two omitted checkpoint lines, while the Gradium quotation crosses omitted product-list lines. Eleven recorded repairs within these cohorts differed only in spacing or quote glyphs under the existing normalizer; each returned span occurs in the captured page. A separate source-text comparison has 30 answers per arm. The original-cleaned-text arm had 144 passage instances: 141 located on the page and three unverified. The earlier 49-article forward trace is documented [separately](STEP1-SOURCE-CONTEXT-OBSERVATIONS.md). Other historical cohorts exist and were not silently included.

## A false-unverified class

Among the fifteen unverified passages in the eight cohorts, five instances have a full match in the sent text under the existing normalizer. They are three distinct quotations: two SK Hynix passages repeated across two cohorts and one Amazon passage. For example, the source has `Counterpoint .` while the quote has `Counterpoint.`; the existing helper finds the words but omits the final period from its returned span, so the subsequent preservation check marks it unverified. For `Amazon 's` versus `Amazon's`, the helper returns no span. All five corresponding complete source slices are also present in their captured pages. Ten other unverified instances have no full normalized match; they include “Nvidia” in place of “The GPU giant” and a changed abbreviation. No evidence was discarded by this count.

An [offline normalized lookup experiment](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/normalized-lookup-results.json) found the five source slices, left the other ten without a full match, retained all eleven previously repaired spans, and exposed two instances with multiple candidate positions. It does not decide which repeated occurrence is intended. Independent review found Unicode counterexamples in two early mappings; the current probe addresses the named cases using a broader candidate search followed by the existing full-span normalization check. A deterministic [90,000-draw short-string check](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/check_mapping.py) compared lookup existence against exhaustive original substrings; 87,995 nonempty quotes were judged, without a mismatch, within its limited alphabet and lengths. A [single instrumented long-document probe](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/size-result.json) used a 749,299-character saved report and observed 123,407,150 bytes peak traced allocation; this is not a throughput or concurrency benchmark. A compact offline index produced identical mappings and lookup results for the 60 saved inputs, 1,978 saved passages and 90,000 fixed short-text draws. On the same long report, a separate instrumented run used 30,770,238 bytes peak traced allocation versus 123,407,150 for the first version, with single-run times of 1.164 and 1.084 seconds respectively. [Compact checks](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/check_compact.py) and [profile](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/compact-size-result.json) are reproducible; neither establishes concurrency or general correctness. Search completeness, Unicode behavior, runtime resource use and the actual downstream writer remain unproven. That first probe was not a runtime fix; the separately reviewed isolated-runner implementation described below is now installed. The candidate still detects only location in the sent text: the two clean-only passages remain absent from the captured page. [Design, counterexamples and reproduction](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/census-design.md).

A separate [in-memory validator replay](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/validator-replay-results.json) reproduced all 360 saved validation objects with the current clean code. Wrapping the existing repair helper with the candidate changed five unverified passages to located and repaired across three answers; ten remained unverified, and other returned fields and spans were unchanged. An independent reader reproduced the comparison. An initial replay omitted the original-page check from prepared inputs; after review, it was rerun with each historical validator item reconstructed, including 78 prepared-source calls. All saved results and the five changes remained the same. The other 282 calls did not use the original-page check. This does not establish original-page continuity for those calls, downstream storage or semantic support. The existing case-insensitive normalizer also treats words such as `Polish` and `polish` alike, so the recovered original and model quotation must remain distinguishable.

A [two-item entry-point probe](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/entrypoint-result.json) used actual source records and saved model responses with a fake transport and a shared exploratory thesis state. Both runs completed; the candidate located the Amazon spacing case and preserved the unrelated wording mismatch as unverified. The actual rendered page displayed the original and recovered passages. The reconstructed requests and second item’s thesis state are not the historical requests, so this checks entry-point behavior, not stateful extraction accuracy. A separate [prepared-source control](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/prepared-page-controls.json) checked one saved answer with present, missing, empty and unrelated captured pages; it does not cover all page formats.

The same in-memory candidate was also compared against the 60 source-comparison answers and 49 completed forward-play articles. It changed no quote or repair flag across their 553 passage instances. All 60 source-comparison baseline validation objects matched; in the forward trace, all 49 sets of quote arrays matched, while 20 older complete objects lack two fields now returned by the validator. The current values of those two fields were empty on those 20 records; that does not establish what older validation checked. One failed trace was excluded. These cohorts overlap and are reported separately. [Additional replay](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/additional-replay-results.json).

A synthetic dense-match probe found 50,000 occurrences of a one-character quote in 100,000 characters. Materializing every match used 15,776,874 peak traced bytes in one local run; an experimental streaming comparison used 3,451,334 on the same input and agreed with a full-list comparison that collapses identical source strings on all 1,978 saved passage instances. This value-based comparison is different from the earlier experimental rule requiring a single position; two saved passage instances have two identical positions, although both already follow the existing exact-match fast path. Identical repeated text still does not identify its intended page position. Different original strings that normalize the same way remain ambiguous in this experiment. These were representation tests, not a concurrency benchmark. The same-byte selection policy was subsequently implemented in the isolated runner. [Probe](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/streaming-result.json).

Further offline checks examined 2,000 generated longer-string draws against exhaustive original substrings, with 1,964 nonempty quotes. The [20 longest feedable source records](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/size-suite-results.json) ranged from 185,016 to 749,299 characters; one instrumented index build per record took at most 1.270 seconds and peaked at 30,774,046 traced bytes. An independent reader reproduced the source selection and checks. These allocations exclude loading the texts and other process memory, and the timings exclude quote lookup, model calls, concurrency and database I/O. They do not establish a production budget.

A further [49-item entry-point replay](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/forward-entrypoint-results.json) fed saved responses to the current isolated runner in saved order. Both the control and the in-memory candidate completed all 49 items with identical results and states. The regenerated stored request strings and before/after states matched the historical traces, and an independent reader verified all 49 full source records and raw responses. One failed historical attempt was excluded. The test reused saved answers, did not call a model or production writer, and the separate 20 older result-object shape differences still apply.

The clean runner retains raw unverified quotes and continues the exploratory run. It conditionally checks the captured page for prepared sources; ordinary cleaned sources do not receive that page check. Any future change must handle missing captured pages and ambiguous matches without silently losing evidence, and be evaluated through the actual writer and reader before production claims.

## Implemented in the isolated runner

The isolated runner now uses the existing text normalization to find a complete original source slice when the prior quote repair fails. If several canonical source slices have identical bytes, it returns that value without choosing an original-page position; differing source values remain unverified. The raw quotation remains stored, and a corrected span is labeled as located in the input. A new lookup failure is recorded visibly in the trace and replay without blocking the next article. The existing conditional page check for prepared sources is unchanged. The existing [49-article replay](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html) was regenerated from the same saved traces and annotations with the clearer input-location wording; the historic model answers and stored validation results were not changed. No production prompt, configuration, source database or evidence writer changed.

The [installed validator replay](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/runtime-replay-results.json) checked 360 saved answers and their full original validator items: the five expected passage instances were located, the ten other unverified instances remained unverified, and other output was unchanged. The [installed two-item entry-point check](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/runtime-entrypoint-result.json) exercised the correction, an unverified wording change and replay display; the [49-item check](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/runtime-forward-results.json) reproduced stored request strings and state with recorded responses and had no candidate/control differences. These are stored-answer checks, not new model-quality measurements. The 20-source index budget was rerun against the actual installed index; [eight scratch mutations](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/mutation-results.json) were caught by their corresponding tests. The checks do not establish semantic support, page completeness, concurrent performance or writer compatibility.

## Writer handoff still missing

The clean Step 1 runner writes an isolated trace; it does not call the existing evidence writer. In the 49 completed forward-play articles, 63 of 160 evidence items contain multiple source passages, 33 have multiple thesis links, and 12 have both. Two items contain an unverified passage. These are shapes observed in one run, not a quality rate. One Bloomberg Intelligence survey item uses its second passage to identify the 60-person survey population and has separate explanations for two links. Keeping only its first passage loses that supporting context. [Reproducible count](../poc/data/clean-step1-quote-integrity-2026-11-07/handoff-census.json).

The existing [`writing._store_piece`](../src/hivemind/views/writing.py) and [`store` schema](../src/hivemind/views/store.py) take one scalar `quote`, and the evidence-link table has no per-link explanation. The [`service` reader](../src/hivemind/service.py) returns the scalar quote, while the current judgement and matching readers truncate it. Clean output has multiple separately located or unverified passages, a description for each thesis link, date text and an evidence period. The existing `event_time` is not automatically equivalent to that period, and the publication day is not necessarily the statement day. New-thesis identifiers and the old writer's required thesis fields also need an explicit mapping. The clean PRD already records these as open handoff work: neither concatenating quotations into a purported continuous quote nor retaining only the first one satisfies the current evidence contract. No production writer or production database was changed in this investigation.
