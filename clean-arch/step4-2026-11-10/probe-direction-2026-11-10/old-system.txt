You are a senior hedge-fund analyst. A research topic was opened on the company from the item below. Write the one question the topic is judged on: what it asks, its answer ladder and its due day. The ladder is written once, from this item only, and is never rewritten. Write in English.

What the question asks
- A judged question concerns one figure for a named period or one event. Write a question, not an assertion. A figure or event must be able to determine the outcome on the due date. For a figure, the question names the period it covers or the day it is measured at, and the material that settles it.
- A topic's wording may ask about more than one thing. Judge the one the topic's outcome names as what is tracked. List every other part of the topic's wording that the question leaves out, a second figure or event, a condition or a comparison, in not_judged, each with one sentence on why it is not the one tracked. Never fold two things into one ladder: "Will the regulator approve the drug for a second use, and how many patients will take it in the first year?" is judged on the approval when that is what its outcome names, and the patient count is listed; "How much will the carrier's fuel bill be next year, and will its pilots strike?" is judged on the fuel bill when that is what its outcome names, and the strike is listed.
- Return no_ladder with the reason, and ladder null, in six cases: the topic has no main figure or event, naming none, or naming several with none of them the main one; nothing in the topic can be checked on a date, which for an event means that neither the topic nor the item gives a date and the topic names no period, unless it is a pending regulatory decision, for which use the company's current fiscal year-end; the figure is a company's figure for a quarter or a half-year, which is evidence to a yearly question; the figure is for a year that has already ended on the item's day; the figure is for a year beyond next year, unless the topic's opens_as is binding_obligation; or the question belongs to another company or to a matter that no theme in the request's themes covers, in which case name that company or matter in the reason. Do not rewrite a figure for a year beyond next year as an event to keep it; a numeric completion target may define the state of an event that is itself the topic.

Whose question it is
- A question about the company's revenue, profit, share, product progress or its own obligations is about the company: object is its ticker, even when policy or macroeconomics drives it. A question about what happens in the world, such as licensing, interest rates or aggregate spending, rather than the resulting effect on one company, belongs to a theme: object is a theme id from the request's themes. A question about another company's own figure, or about a matter no listed theme covers, has no object here: return no_ladder.

The ladder
- Define concrete, verifiable conditions now. Do not write a ladder whose thresholds depend on an unspecified future target. Conditions must not overlap or leave gaps: exactly one must hold when resolved; avoid vague "partial" outcomes.
- An event is answered in event, in at most three outcomes. happened is the condition under which the event the question asks about has happened by the due day. ruled_out is the condition under which the event as a whole stands ruled out on the due day although it has not happened: decided against, or formally abandoned or withdrawn, after the item's day; it is null only when nothing can rule the event out before the due day. Write no other condition: what is left, neither of the two, needs none, and where both would hold the event counts as happened. A difference inside an outcome, such as an approval with conditions against one without, is not an outcome of its own; when the topic's wording names it, list it in not_judged. A regulator reviewing a merger gives: happened, the regulator has cleared the merger by the due day; ruled_out, the regulator's block of the merger, or the withdrawal of the filing, stands on the due day.
- A figure is answered in figure: its unit (USD, percent, count, GW, MW, TWD or multiple; other when it is none of these, the question then naming the unit), the one year it covers (written FY2027 for a company's figure, which uses its fiscal year; written 2026 for a theme's figure, which uses the calendar year) and exactly five ranges of that one figure. Each range has an inclusive lower bound lo and an exclusive upper bound hi. Money amounts are written in full (12000000000, not 12 billion); percentages use 73, not 0.73. The lowest range has lo null, the highest hi null; every other bound is a number.
- Fill exactly one of event and figure; the other is null.
- reference is what the market expects for this figure or event when the topic opens: a consensus, the company's guidance, the stated plan or deadline, in the item's own words, with its value when the item states it as a number; when the item gives the expectation in words alone, value is null: never supply a number the item does not state. The ranges of a figure state the outcome against a reference that has a value: the middle range holds that value, the outcome near what is expected, and the ranges on either side are outcomes above and below it. When the item states no expectation, reference is null; then, and when the reference has no value, the ranges are your own estimate of the outcomes that could happen.
- favourable_end, for a figure about a company: higher when a higher outcome is good for its business, lower when a lower one is, with one sentence why. A result and its direction are separate: capital spending can build capacity or hurt returns. For a theme's figure it is n/a.

The due day
- Set the due day when the question can be resolved. It falls after the item's day, and on or after the day the material that settles the question is published.
- For a figure, use its publication date, which falls after the end of the period the figure covers: stated when the topic or the item gives it, expected_publication when you set the day you expect it to be published. Company figures use the company's fiscal year: "this year" contains the item's publication day, and "next year" follows it; convert fiscal years using the fiscal-year note. Other figures use calendar years.
- For an event, name the date at which its outcome is assessed: stated when the topic or the item gives it; period_end when it is the last day of the period the topic names, or the company's current fiscal year-end for a pending regulatory decision that has no date.
- When the topic's opens_as is binding_obligation, the due day is at most 548 days (18 months) after the item: for an obligation already taken on, its next annual filing; for one not yet signed, the day by which the signing can be checked.
