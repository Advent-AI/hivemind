# Step 1 source context: offline observations

## Result

A simple “restore source lines next to a long line retained by the cleaner’s fallback” approach is **not a complete fix** for missing source labels. On the 163 previously identified pages, considering only the immediate original-page neighbors would expose the known label on 144 pages; considering the full adjacent unmatched runs would expose it on 149. Neither covers the 12 TradingKey pages, where the retained summary matched the extractor rather than the long-line retention fallback. Other cases are blocked by an intervening retained line. These are counts for three known page structures, not a corpus-wide estimate.

This was an offline observation: no source database, model input, prompt or evidence was changed. The [source-context viewer](../poc/data/clean-step1-context-restoration-2026-11-07/review.html) shows all 363 pages, prospective original-page positions and existing cleaned text. The [lineage replay](../poc/data/clean-step1-source-lineage-2026-11-07/review.html) shows all candidate source positions. Page adjacency does not establish whether a label applies to a particular paragraph, and the observations do not automatically classify content.

The full-run figures include the immediate-neighbor positions; they are not additional to them. One of the 200 additional pages had no saved HTML and could not be assessed for this extractor-dependent anchor. Multiple possible source positions are treated as conservative stopping points, not confirmed retained occurrences. The [raw observations](../poc/data/clean-step1-context-restoration-2026-11-07/observations.json) retain page IDs, source positions, ambiguous stops, extraction status and the exact selection.

Thirty pages were selected by a fixed hash before their proposed lines were reviewed. Three independent readers inspected every proposed line in those pages against the original page and its context. Nineteen pages had proposals; eleven had none. Across the thirty, full runs contained 1,102 positions, including 109 also selected by the immediate-neighbor approach. These are observations of a fixed review sample, not a population rate.

The recovered text includes real provenance and useful context, including generated-summary labels, an editor disclosure, authors and dates, source links, technical resources, and sponsorship labels. It also includes menus, social controls, advertising, unrelated recommendations and, on at least one page, stories dated after the original article. Some headings have ambiguous scope. For example, NVIDIA's “AI-Generated Summary” label is associated with a summary component; adjacent “Next Steps,” author information and the later article body must not automatically inherit that label. A sponsored label is useful provenance but does not establish that a particular claim is false.

An Ising quote previously appeared contiguous in the cleaned text while two checkpoint-name lines intervened in the captured page. Neither observed approach proposed those two lines. An offline replay constructed hypothetical texts for three overlapping articles whose cleaned lines have unique, ordered page matches. It rechecked 18 saved answers containing 153 passages with the current clean Step 1 validator. For each approach, all 151 previously exact passages remained exact and both previously unverified passages remained unverified; no returned quote text changed. The Ising passage stayed falsely exact because neither approach restored the intervening checkpoint lines. As a separate control, checking the same saved passages against the captured full page left 150 exact and two unverified unchanged; the Ising passage became unverified. This detects the missing intervening text in that example, but neither confirms quotation meaning nor tests what the model would extract from the full page. This is a selected three-article compatibility observation, not a safety guarantee: exact matching proves presence in the supplied text, not faithful continuity in the original page. The reconstructed texts are temporary diagnostic inputs with old metadata, not production records. The [replay and passage results](../poc/data/clean-step1-context-restoration-2026-11-07/quote-replay.json) are saved alongside the [script](../poc/data/clean-step1-context-restoration-2026-11-07/replay_quotes.py).

No model quality claim follows from these observations. The next design must preserve useful context and source identity without blindly adding all page material, guessing a label's scope, deleting evidence or adding a relevance gate. Existing ambiguous or missing original positions must remain visible.

## Historical saved quotation census

An independent read-only count of the two original forward-play traces found 49 completed articles containing 250 passage instances: 248 stored verified passages also occurred contiguously in the saved captured page, and two passages were already unverified in the cleaned input. One failed trace is excluded. All 49 completed items had captured page text. An independent reader checked the source items, requests, both non-locating examples and file hashes. This is a location check of saved instances, not an estimate for all articles or proof of source role, completeness or meaning. The separate eight-article comparison contained 296 passage instances, including one clean-only continuous match and two unverified passages.
