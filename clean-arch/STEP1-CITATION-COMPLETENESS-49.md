# Step 1: citation support in the 49-article forward run

**Status: source-backed review of one selected historical run, not a population score or a new golden set.** Two readers examined all 160 returned evidence records from the 49 completed articles. They compared each standalone summary with its own attached passages and the article text actually sent to Luna. A separate reader cross-checked the nine preliminary citation-context findings against the raw answers and sources. One failed attempt was excluded; the resumed answer is included. Articles with no returned evidence remain in the article count but have no citation record to examine.

The first readers marked nine records in nine articles where a detail in the summary is supported elsewhere in the sent article but is absent from that item's own passages. The cross-check agreed with the underlying source observations, while distinguishing missing substantive conditions from identity or contextual antecedents. These are not nine fabricated facts: the relevant text exists in the supplied article. Nor do they count all extraction omissions or establish a production loss. The sent source itself may be incomplete or contaminated.

| Article and evidence | Source-visible detail outside the item's own passages | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 8 `e2` | Eight active nodes and the 10K/30K scale configuration for a reported 64% runtime reduction. | The quoted passages give the reduction and optimization, but the configuration appears in the preceding figure context. |
| 10 `evidence-1` | The prior sentence names Colette Kress and Raj Mirpuri as the joint announcement speakers. | Their quoted announcement is attached; names and attribution are available in the sent source and fields, but not in the attached quotations. This is a contextual-support gap, not an invented announcement. |
| 11 `e1` | The startup's $1 billion financing. | The quote begins with the valuation and earlier funding; the $1 billion is in the opening sentence of the supplied article. |
| 15 `evidence-3` | The preceding context identifies the company and Bank of America attribution for a 115% sales figure. | The quote contains the 115% figure but not all the antecedent and reporting context. |
| 28 `evidence-1` | The later source says central functions remain available with limited or no internet. | The quoted “independent of a permanent Internet connection” does not explicitly establish no connectivity; the later sentence does not itself establish that every multimodal function works offline. |

The full article and separate speaker field identify the director; the missing subject is more substantive than requiring the full name in every quotation. |

The primary readers also identified other types of concerns that must not be counted as citation omissions. In article 28, a source says a model **such as** Phi-4 can be used; the summary makes that example a definite selection, even though the quotation retains “such as.” In article 29, a source discusses money flowing into infrastructure **stocks**; the summary describes infrastructure spending. Article 3 specifies a US-dollar denomination for a valuation whose bare-dollar denomination the supplied sentence does not explicitly establish. Some speaker attribution, source contamination and planned-versus-completed wording remain concerns rather than confirmed errors. A complete quote alone would not fix these meaning or attribution problems.

The [first-half review](../poc/data/clean-step1-citation-completeness-2026-11-07/review-01-25.md) covers 93 returned records; the [second-half review](../poc/data/clean-step1-citation-completeness-2026-11-07/review-26-49.md) covers 67. “No material gap identified” in those first readings is not proof that a record is correct or relevant. The readers' primary labels are focused on returned records; article-level omissions such as Kyber and unreturned risk analysis are a different unit and must not be ranked against record counts as if they were the same measurement.

The [numeric triage](STEP1-NUMERIC-CITATION-TRIAGE.md) flagged only the article 11 financing example among these nine source-context findings. It can help find cases but cannot be used as the quality ruler or an automatic repair. The current prompt already asks for every supporting passage. The next design must examine the full request and prior failed attempts and measure both citation completeness and whether summaries, attribution, useful indirect links and article-level coverage remain correct.
