# Step 1: deep review of the 49-article forward run

## What this review covers

This is a review of one exploratory run, not an acceptance result or a score against a golden dataset. The inputs are the 49 locally stored NVIDIA-tagged records selected in their stored order, starting from an empty isolated thesis library. The first 20 completed articles are in the first trace; the remaining 29, including a new attempt of article 21, are in the continuation trace. Every adjacent committed before/after thesis state was checked. The final state has 22 theses, and the answers contain 160 evidence items and 199 thesis links. Two source passages are unverified. Neither the thesis nor evidence counts measure correctness.

Three independent read-only reviewers examined the recorded answers across all 49 articles, the applicable thesis context, and the source material. The author checked the key examples in the original source and output. The reviewer of articles 21–35 read the two long technical items in full; the separate earlier scan of the continuation was not a complete recall audit. A precise recall or precision rate is not available. Some analyst-judgment calls are flagged for review rather than presented as settled errors. No prompts, model output or historical source records were changed as part of this review.

The [article replay](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html) preserves the source text, model proposal, references, and before/after state. Each article number below links to the corresponding replay entry. The [run log](STEP1-RUN-LOG.md) explains the two interruptions and continuation.

## Findings that matter most

### 1. Evidence can be linked to a thesis even when the model says it is unrelated

This is a semantic error, not a missing database ID. It puts a statement in a dossier whose question it does not answer.

- [Article 4](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-4): company-wide valuation and revenue figures were linked to a thesis specifically about Nemotron's agent performance. The explanations for two links explicitly say that the evidence does not inform the available theses. The third link is only broad market context. A revenue figure can matter without answering the Nemotron question.
- [Article 27](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-27): a GeForce NOW cloud-gaming server expansion was linked to the Shield TV product-line thesis. The explanation explicitly says the server expansion does not inform that thesis, yet returns the link.
- [Article 18](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-18): seven items on Isaac, Cosmos and Hugging Face LeRobot were linked to a Nemotron/NemoClaw adoption thesis, although the article names neither product. [Article 20](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-20) subsequently opens a separate robotics thesis. In the recorded Step 1 outputs the seven earlier items remain attached to the Nemotron thesis; no Step 3 linking or later reader was executed in this run.
- [Article 41](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-41): optical networking and copper-capacity statements are connected to specific inference-chip partnership and Kyber manufacturing-setback questions, without the source reporting that Kyber setback. Some links to the broader custom-accelerator competition thesis are more defensible; the whole article is not irrelevant.
- [Article 32](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-32): a technical demonstration using Nemotron Nano is linked to the distinct agent-stack and competitive-performance questions, even though the explanations acknowledge that the demonstration is not evidence of adoption of that stack or a comparison against closed models.

A narrow text search of the **199 returned links** found three explanations explicitly stating that their evidence did not inform the referenced or supplied theses: the two article-4 links and the article-27 link. This is a lower-bound diagnostic, not the total number of wrong links. Whether indirect relationships such as a competitor's expansion inform a narrower partnership question requires reading each thesis and the source; shared subject matter alone is not enough.

**Possible mechanism, not proven:** The request requires every evidence item to be paired with a thesis and tells the model to use the open list. In these examples the model appears to retain an item and choose a nearby topic even while stating that the specific relationship is absent. The trace does not prove that the pairing requirement caused the choice. A fix must preserve Alex's thesis–evidence pairing rule while helping the model choose a supported existing thesis, a justified new one, or no pair; it must not invent a hard-coded list of subjects.

### 2. A correct quotation can accompany a wrong summary or attribution

- [Article 29, evidence e2](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-29): the source says money is flowing into AI-infrastructure **stocks**, mainly memory stocks. The answer describes AI-infrastructure **investment and spending** going to memory companies. Buying shares is not the same event as companies spending on infrastructure. Its e4 also combines a journalist's named-company explanation with an executive's direct remarks, so who made each claim needs review.
- [Article 17](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-17): one `fact` item combines actual company revenue with analysts' interpretation of market sentiment and AI spending. A later reader could treat the interpretation as if it were an actual company result.
- [Article 3](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-3): the model summarizes the reported investment as a commitment, while the source says NVIDIA had “put up its hand.” The source does not establish a completed transaction. The same answer also misattributes a separate later related-news snippet, discussed below.
- [Article 1](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-1): items naming LangChain as the original party use the NVIDIA blog's publication day as the statement day. The supplied article does not separately date the underlying benchmark or platform-download statements. This is an attribution/date concern, not proof of the correct original date.

The quoted passages for these examples are present in the supplied text, but location matching cannot prove the summary means the same thing, identify the correct speaker, or establish when the original party spoke.

### 3. Useful statements are sometimes omitted even when an appropriate thesis is open

- [Article 34](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-34) returns DeepSeek chip claims but omits NVIDIA's stated FY27 Q2 guidance of **$91 billion ±2%**, approximately **75% non-GAAP gross margin**, and the assumption of **no China Data Center compute revenue**. The source also reports a possible Kyber delay to 2028 and NVIDIA's denial. Open growth, margin, China and Kyber theses were already available. The report must remain qualified as reported guidance or allegations, not actual results. Whether a statement repeats prior evidence is for Step 2.
- [Article 36](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-36) includes management's reported roughly **$1 trillion of Blackwell/Rubin revenue visibility for 2026–27**, but the answer returns only general data-center revenue and next-quarter guidance. Revenue visibility is management's stated outlook, not booked orders or realized revenue.
- [Article 12](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-12) returns nothing despite named contractors' figures and comments about data-center demand and construction constraints. Those are plausible indirect signals for the open AI-infrastructure thesis; exactly which are useful is an analyst judgment, not a mechanical rule to keep every contractor figure.

These are source-backed omissions in the specific article outputs. This run did not execute evidence deduplication, so their novelty against a production evidence history is not established.

### 4. Some answers include low-signal or overly specific material

- [Article 8](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-8) creates an enterprise Presto-adoption thesis and returns six pieces of evidence. Four are detailed NVIDIA-authored technical benchmark results, including a single query's 50-second-to-2-second change. The sent prompt's final check excludes company technical benchmarks. The IBM production-workload testing announcement is a separate potentially relevant development.
- [Article 7](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-7) also returns an NVIDIA technical evaluation score and individual test results. An announcement that the profile is available is a different question from a vendor's benchmark.
- [Articles 37 and 38](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-37) retain generic commentator statements about AI demand and possible growth deceleration. These should be assessed separately from specific sourced numbers, such as the DeepSeek efficiency claim in article 37.
- Individual theses on Shield TV, a regional confidential-computing partnership and a scientific-computing optimization may be too narrow for an analyst's dossier. Their inclusion is a significance judgment, not proof that all small projects or products should be rejected. The user explicitly allowed trackable topics without requiring demonstrated financial impact.

The trace establishes that the model returned these items. It does not establish why it missed or misapplied a prompt instruction, and a single draw does not show whether a prompt edit will fix the class.

### 5. The source passed to Luna is not always the July article

[Article 3](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-3)'s stored cleaned text appends two October related-news summaries to the July Firmus article. The page identifies one as a separate story about another data-centre builder. Luna turns that snippet into a possible Firmus listing delay. The snippet is a real substring of the supplied text, so the source-location check correctly finds the text but cannot know it belongs to a later, different article. The page's timestamp is July 9 in Sydney and July 8 in New York; this is the same instant, not a date error by itself. The Google listing timestamp is a separate value. A later timestamp audit found that the fixed run order is not necessarily page-publication order.

[Article 28](../poc/data/clean-step1-2026-07-08-09-continuing/review.html#article-28) also contains later related-news snippets; its returned evidence did not cite them. Other records contain recommendation text or reader comments, but this review did not establish a full contamination census. The run's source order is the stored order, not proof of real-world publication order. This issue exists before the model reads the item; a prompt-only change cannot reliably turn contaminated text into a clean historical source.

## What worked in this run

- Article 2 preserves the reported H200 permissions, purchase conditions and uncertainty; article 40 keeps a similar later report within the limits of its short stored excerpt.
- Article 10 captures the announced AI-cloud financing arrangement, the CoreWeave exposure report, the rack-setback report and NVIDIA's counterstatement as distinct matters.
- Articles 23, 25 and 49 reuse existing Shield, inference-partnership or Gradium theses. Repeated evidence is expected at Step 1 and needs the later evidence-comparison stage; it is not automatically an extraction defect.
- Article 42 retains concrete named competitors' chip plans and an attributed market-share projection. Article 45's trading-card item and article 46's old GPU-feature list yield no pairs, with no obvious significant omission in their stored text.
- The run now retains unmatched citation text and can continue, with unverified passages visibly identified. Two such passages occurred; this does not mean the other citations' summaries have been semantically verified.
