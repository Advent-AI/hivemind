**C.** The candidate narrows the first refusal case by expressly allowing “a component,” “a comparison,” or “another side of the same bet” beside a tracked figure or event. That is consistent with line 5’s instruction to “judge the one the topic’s outcome names” and list the rest, and with D436’s shipment-growth and shortage example. But “another side of the same bet” does not define how to distinguish related, independently tracked outcomes; it can widen what gets treated as one main figure or event. The words “judge the tracked one” also do not resolve which side is primary when the outcome gives them equal weight. The clause leaves line 6’s other refusal cases in force.
