Evidence: what to keep
- Material statements often take one of four forms. (1) The company's own figure, guidance or decision: "same-store sales rose 4.2% in the quarter"; "management guided full-year revenue to $12–13 billion"; "the board approved a $10 billion buyback". (2) A customer's, supplier's, competitor's or partner's action or reported figure with a size, date or name that bears on the company's demand, supply, input costs, competition, prices or products: "the largest customer said it will cut orders by a fifth next year"; "a bank chose a rival's system"; "a software firm is integrating the company's model into its government systems"; "the main supplier said its capacity will double by 2027"; "a rival cut list prices 15% in Europe"; "the largest customer reported its order backlog doubled to $40 billion". An action is a step the item reports as news: taken, decided or planned (an order, a contract, a selection, an integration under way, an expansion, a cut, a price change, a deployment plan). A party that already uses, hosts, runs or has built on the company's products, and the benefits it describes from that use (faster, cheaper, more accurate), describe an arrangement in place, which is background; its own revenue, capacity, orders or contracted amounts are figures under (2) ("a hospital chain uses the company's scheduling software and reports shorter waits"). (3) A named party's new claim or estimate about the company or its industry, with or without a figure, whether an analyst, a customer's or rival's executive or an official makes it: "the CFO said plants built a decade ago still run at full capacity"; "an analyst raised next year's EPS estimate to $4.10"; "an analyst said the bank's loan losses next quarter will come in well above consensus"; "a short seller accused the company of overstating receivables by $2 billion"; "an analyst valued the shares at $80, 15 times next year's earnings of $5.30". (4) A policy or regulatory decision or step, including one still in force that the item reports: "the regulator approved the drug for a second indication"; "the government required licences for exports of the product"; "the regulator still bars sales of the drug in the country".
- Go through the item from its first paragraph to its last. The four kinds describe what material statements usually look like; they do not decide what is kept. Take a statement when an analyst following the company would write it in this week's notes because it makes some question's answer more or less likely, and no row of the closing check at the end of this request rejects it; a row's "it does not cover" clause sends the statement back to that test and keeps it only where the row says so. Most of an item is background, but a page with eight statements that pass yields eight evidence items, not a selection. A recognisable restatement of a past actual is optional; a still-valid forecast, guidance or plan is judged on its merits.

Which questions to propose
- Open a candidate only for one of four reasons, named in opens_as. main_driver: an annual quantity that carries the company's value (the segment that carries the business, gross margin, capital spending), whether or not the company guides it, or the date by which the generation of the company's own product that carries that quantity launches or starts shipping (a carmaker's next vehicle platform; not a feature, content or software update, regional service or variant of a product already on sale); the question's ranges settle it, not a guidance figure. Not every reported or guided line. industry_aggregate: one shared quantity for one calendar year, such as the combined capital spending of named companies, an input price or an interest rate. policy_stage: a stage of a policy process, with the authority that decides it named. binding_obligation: an obligation a universe company takes on in its own name: a guarantee or credit support for another party's debt, lease or revenue, financing it extends to others, a purchase commitment or a backstop of customers' capacity or revenue, whether or not the item gives its amount; or an equity stake whose size is stated and is not yet completed. Its own borrowing, its own leases and a completed or unsized investment are not obligations. Not yet signed: a yes/no question due on a day after the item by which the signing can be checked, at most 18 months out. Already taken on, as one deal or as a programme: a financing_amount question on the amount the obligation reaches at the company's next annual filing, settled by that filing's commitments note; the announcement itself is evidence to it.
- A number, financial line, target or named customer project alone does not establish an independently important question. Consider whether the underlying annual quantity carries the company's value, whether it is a reported figure, guidance or an estimate. A customer's purchase or rollout plan is evidence to the supplier's demand and product theses unless it changes the supplier's own cash or credit risk.
- For every candidate: settled_by names the public material that resolves it (an annual report, a filing's note, an official decision). Another source, estimate, wording or direction is the same question when the same outcome settles it. Another population or proxy of the same aggregate is evidence to that aggregate; a distinct quantity, period or decision may be another question.
- A yearly metric asks for a quantity over a defined fiscal or calendar period and keeps the current/next-year limit. A milestone asks whether a specified event or project reaches a defined state by a deadline; a numeric completion target can define that state without becoming a yearly metric. Do not repackage a beyond-horizon annual forecast as an event. A pending regulatory decision may lack a date; use the current fiscal year-end as its due date.
- Do not propose theses about quarterly figures or guidance, undated subjects such as moats or strategy, executive appointments, shareholder votes or bylaws, routine litigation procedure, or buyback authorisations.

How to write evidence
- One evidence item records one statement, and that statement is what you judge: a figure, date or comparison in the neighbouring words does not make another claim in the same quote new or material. Copy its quote verbatim, without translation. Separately write a standalone summary in one_line: who did or said what, how much and for which period, naming the measure even when a neighbouring sentence gives it; preserve conditions, assumptions, limits and uncertainty.
- Identify the speaker and, for a retelling, the original speaker and statement day. Use the schema's tier: fact for official actions, published actual figures or completed transactions; party_statement for company guidance, management statements or officials' remarks; third_party_view for analyst, media or expert opinions; rumour for claims the item credits to unnamed sources, calls unconfirmed, or that have no source and fit no other tier; an announcement or completed deal the item reports is a fact even when the reporter names no source.
- For one statement that is both an event and unconfirmed or attributed to unnamed sources, use rumour. If one matter has a confirmed main statement plus a secondary plan or rumour, use the main statement's tier and describe the secondary part as a plan or rumour; split only when they are separate matters. For example, a completed purchase with an unconfirmed later expansion is not the same as an unnamed source claiming the purchase was completed. Use only this item's information. Report plans as plans; do not infer committed funding, orders, deliveries or revenue.

How to write a candidate thesis
- One thesis concerns one figure for a named period or one event. Separate independently meaningful outcomes.
- Write a question, not an assertion. A figure or event must be able to determine the outcome on the due date.
- A question about a company's revenue, profit, share or product progress is an asset thesis; put its ticker in object, even when policy or macroeconomics drives it. A theme thesis asks what happens in the world, such as licensing, interest rates or aggregate spending, rather than the resulting effect on one company.
- Write generated descriptions in English.
- Define concrete, verifiable conditions now. Do not propose a ladder whose thresholds depend on an unspecified future target.
- Use five rungs; only a yes/no question about an event has two. A five-rung asset ladder runs from Strongly bullish through Bullish, Neutral and Bearish to Strongly bearish, meaning good or bad for the business. A theme ladder runs between two ends of an outcome range; label the outcomes, without assigning business sentiment. Conditions must not overlap or leave gaps: exactly one must hold when resolved. State the neutral comparison in its condition; avoid vague “partial” outcomes.
- Set the due date when the question can be resolved. For a figure, use its publication date. Company figures use the company's fiscal year: “this year” contains the item's publication day, and “next year” follows it. Other figures use calendar years. For an event, name the date at which its outcome is assessed.

Research groups
- A research group is one ongoing matter in the world that several questions track together: a product rollout and its customer adoption, a financing or acquisition deal, a policy process, an investment or demand cycle. It usually spans companies and themes. When a candidate tracks a matter, declare the group in new_groups (local id n1, n2 …, with a name, a scope that says what is happening, and aliases: the names the matter goes by in the press, such as product, project or programme names and the parties; not company names) and cite that id in group_refs; a declared group whose names match a stored group is that group. A company's own reported or guided financial lines (total or segment revenue, margins, expenses, tax rate, capex) belong to the company and carry no group: leave their group_refs empty unless the line itself tracks such a matter. A company is not a group, and a thesis is not a group.

Identity fields and numeric bounds
- Select the closest measure allowed by the schema. Scope is its actual segment, product, population or event subject. Reuse a supplied reference name only when it has the same meaning; leave scope empty when unqualified.
- period_start and period_end describe the period the figure covers. Convert fiscal years using the supplied fiscal-year note. Calendar year 2026 is 2026-01-01 through 2026-12-31. For an event, use the start of the year in which it is proposed through the due date.
- Identify the accounting basis and unit. For event or policy_event, select the event type; otherwise use n/a.
- Each numeric rung has an inclusive lower bound lo and exclusive upper bound hi, in the stated unit. Money amounts are written in full (12000000000, not 12 billion); percentages use 73, not 0.73. The lowest numeric range has lo null, the highest hi null. Both bounds are null for yes/no outcomes.
