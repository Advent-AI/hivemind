The financial-equivalence problems from round 1 are resolved. One material issue remains: the fifth variant restores a filing restriction that later decisions explicitly removed.

For example, a supplier’s announced price increase can inform a retailer’s gross-margin question without stating or estimating the retailer’s margin. The fifth variant would exclude it from that question even when no narrower question applies.

1. definition_no_fallback_d207 is consistent with D207’s historical restriction for revenue and margin, but contradicts the later filing decision.

Proposed:
“a company-wide revenue or margin question takes only figures, guidance, estimates and management statements of that quantity”

D207:
“company-wide figure questions take only their own figures, guidance and management statements.”

Standard B7:
“以 D207 为准”

For revenue and margin questions, the proposed sentence faithfully expresses that historical restriction. Allowing estimates is also supported: D254 quotes the earlier placement instruction as including “estimates of that figure”. “Of that quantity” appropriately concerns what the statement describes, rather than requiring the company itself to say it.

However, D254 explicitly says:
“D207’s ‘only their own’ sentence goes”.

D259 subsequently records Alex’s instruction:
“先放款take 吧，辅助反正就是个中间地带我们给点leeway”

Its resulting rule admits:
“what makes that figure more or less likely: the demand, supply, competition and pricing behind it”.

D259 expressly acknowledges the consequence:
“The sink D207 guarded against is accepted as the price of the leeway, bounded by the narrowest-question rule and the driver questions.”

Verdict: keeps narrower than the later recorded decision. This is a substantive reversal, not merely clarification of the existing rule. The standard’s frozen B7 reference and the later decisions point in different directions; citing B7 alone does not resolve that conflict.

Correspondingly, definition_no_fallback remains wider than historical D207/B7, but that breadth has a source in D259. My round-1 report did not adequately distinguish those two comparisons.

2. The revised financial explanation resolves the round-1 objections.

Proposed:
“a statement need not be the question’s own figure to bear on it”

And:
“what suppliers sell to the buyers a question counts”

These replace the unsupported equivalence claim with relevance, while explicitly identifying the relevant buyers.

Standard §5.1:
“因为它让某个问题的答案更可能或更不可能”

Standard §5.3:
“要说得出它通过需求、供给、竞争、定价这类路径动了在研公司的哪个问题”

Verdict: consistent. The revised sentence explains an allowed connection; it does not authorize treating a seller’s revenue from all customers as purchases by one particular customer group. The retained “another … population” allowance still concerns “a question’s quantity”, not any population reporting a similarly named number.

The three examples now say “bear on”:
“a drug wholesaler’s sales to pharmacies”; “a lender’s new loans to small firms”; “an airline’s growing forward bookings”.

They are financially defensible as evidence of corresponding purchases, borrowing, and demand that can influence aircraft spending. They no longer claim equality, guaranteed conversion, or matching timing. Their wording is general. Independent origin from test articles remains unverified, but I found no substantive example defect.

Removing the fallback also leaves no internal contradiction in definition_no_fallback: a genuinely relevant indirect statement can still reach a revenue question, while “empty when no question and no candidate is connected” handles statements without a home. Removal eliminates the automatic destination; it does not restore D207’s narrower admission rule.
