# Research A: the anatomy of an investment thesis at hedge-fund granularity

Date: 2026-10-22. Web research only; no repository files touched. ~50 distinct sources. Each bullet gives the
finding, a quote or close paraphrase, and the URL. Where a page could not be fetched in full and the quote comes from
a search-engine excerpt, the bullet says "(excerpt)". The closing synthesis labels what is well-evidenced versus my
inference.

Design context this answers: one view = one question about one object, a fixed 5-rung ladder of mutually exclusive
checkable outcomes, a due date 6–12 months out. Open question: how big one view should be, what to combine or carve
out, and how many views per company a serious fundamental analyst tracks.

## 1. How practitioners define and write a thesis; what unit they track day to day

### 1a. The classic four-part unit: idea, consensus, variant perception, trigger

- Steinhardt's definition, from his memoir: "I defined variant perception as holding a well-founded view that was
  meaningfully different from market consensus." Pitch format: "Analysts pitching an idea must state the idea, explain
  the consensus view, describe the variant perception, and identify the trigger event." Exit rule: "When the market
  begins to reflect your view, the variant edge is gone, and it is time to exit the trade."
  https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-michael-steinhardt.md
- The two-minute rule, same source lineage: Steinhardt told a summer intern he should be able to state in two minutes
  "1) the idea; 2) the consensus view; 3) his variant perception; and 4) a trigger event", because "complexity is your
  enemy". https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2007/09/21/your-plan-for-successful-investing.aspx
- A current practitioner essay makes the unit operational as four questions: "What future appears consistent with the
  current price? What do I expect instead? Why should my expectation be more accurate? Which observable developments
  would show that I am right or wrong?" And on size: a variant perception needs "one overarching disagreement with the
  future implied by the price"; "you have to express the market's view in language precise enough to disagree with";
  "The price embeds a forecast, but that forecast is encrypted. The analytic act of variant perception is decryption
  first and disagreement second." It must be grounded in "repeatable drivers" and name the "turbo triggers that will
  test it". https://thefinancialpen.substack.com/p/variant-perception-the-mechanics
- CFA Institute's "five dimensions of variant perception" (fundamental, history, policy, agency costs, behavioural) is
  a checklist of where an edge can come from, not of how many views to hold; its Ambac case turned on one macro
  input (housing prices) with a 12-month lag before the stock reflected it.
  https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/blogs/enterprising-investor/2015/the-five-dimensions-of-variant-perception

### 1b. Sonkin & Johnson, "Pitch the Perfect Investment": four questions, one mispricing, one catalyst

- The PM's four questions, verbatim from a detailed summary: "What is the upside? ... What is the downside? ... Why
  this opportunity? This addresses the unease that an idea is too good to be true, requiring absolute proof that a
  tenet of market efficiency has been compromised. What closes the gap, and when? This demands a clear, actionable
  catalyst." Catalyst: "any specific precipitating event that will force the gap between the market price and the
  intrinsic value estimate to close by increasing information dissemination, restoring crowd diversity, or
  alleviating trading constraints." Structure: a 30-second hook, then a 2-minute drill that "tells a coherent story,
  lays out the main arguments, and directly answers the four key questions."
  https://www.antoinebuteau.com/pitch-the-perfect-investment-book-summary/
- (excerpt) "if you spend 90% of your time explaining what the market is missing and why the stock is mispriced,
  rather than justifying your valuation, the portfolio manager will listen intently."
  https://medium.com/graham-and-doddsville/pitch-the-perfect-investment-1a4e8d0c077b
- The Ivey (spring 2025) teaching deck built on the book frames "Stocks as narratives": "Estimate of intrinsic value
  (IV) = a narrative (can be modeled as a DCF). Price = consensus of all narratives." "Stock price changes because
  consensus expectations change ... Consensus expectations change because new narratives emerge." The catalyst slide
  asks "how the next person will figure it out?" and the method rule is "Keep any debate centered on the conjectures –
  which is where it belongs." https://www.ivey.uwo.ca/media/xuap5lsi/pitching-spring-2025-ivey.pdf

### 1c. Mauboussin & Rappaport, "Expectations Investing": solve for what's priced, then find the one driver that matters

- Three steps: "Estimate market expectations that justify current stock price; identify potential revisions to those
  expectations using competitive strategy analysis; establish decision standards for buy, sell, or hold."
  https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/blogs/enterprising-investor/2022/book-review-expectations-investing
- The granularity claim, from a close reading of the book: "differences in expectations between you and the market are
  only likely to be meaningful on a few metrics. Overwhelmingly the key metric is sales." The value triggers are
  sales (volume or price/mix), operating costs, investments.
  https://cowboycomputer.substack.com/p/book-review-expectations-investing
- (excerpt) The "turbo trigger" is "the key value driver likely to significantly revise expectations ... For most
  businesses, increasing sales is that trigger, because larger shifts in sales generally have a greater overall impact
  on value than do changes in expenses or spending."
  https://www.shortform.com/pdf/expectations-investing-pdf-michael-j-mauboussin-and-alfred-rappaport
- Applied example of "what's priced in" as one number with a horizon: for Adobe "the market is pricing in roughly 11%
  annual free cash flow growth" (reverse DCF, 10% discount rate, 2.5% terminal growth). Rule: "When expectations are
  too high, we walk away. When they're too low, we buy."
  https://summitstocks.substack.com/p/whats-priced-in-the-question-every

### 1d. Valentine, "Best Practices for Equity Research Analysts": two to four critical factors per stock

- Verbatim: analysts should "identify the two to four critical factors that impact every one of your stocks", because
  "at least eight out of ten, even nine out ten of those pieces of information have no impact on moving a stock";
  doing "offense" (differentiating from consensus) is incompatible with "doing defense and trying to read the news
  tape" all day. https://tearsheet.co/?p=1603
- The book's own chapter list makes the unit explicit: Ch. 8 "Identify and Monitor a Stock's Critical Factors",
  Ch. 18 "Forecast Scenarios for the Most Important Critical Factors"; and the warning that sometimes there is no
  call because "the market has reasonably assessed the critical factors that drive a particular stock."
  https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/-/media/documents/support/research-challenge/challenge/best-practices-equity-sample.pdf
- Reviewer's reading: analysts should "focus much of their time in identifying, tracking and monitoring stock's
  critical factors" and keep "a short-list of critical factors"; the FaVeS frame (Forecast, Valuation, Sentiment)
  says a call needs an edge in at least one. https://cfasocietyindia.org/book-review-best-practices-for-equity-research-analyst/
- (excerpt) The three criteria for a critical factor: material impact on earnings/cash flow (e.g. 5% on EPS), an
  associated catalyst, and not yet fully in consensus. https://sobrief.com/books/best-practices-for-equity-research-pb
- Valentine's current workshop sells exactly this: identifying "just the few critical factors per stock" and
  separating "potential critical factors from the typical 'noise'". https://analystsolutions.com/kr7imscfeag-fp/

### 1e. What a pod-shop analyst actually tracks: debates, bogeys, "the key #"

- An ex-pod analyst's seven-part idea template: "thesis, numbers/valuation, buy-side consensus/bogeys, catalyst and
  path to getting paid, sentiment and positioning, risks, and portfolio fit"; the bar is "a differentiated and
  meaningful view supported by evidence with a well-defined catalyst". (excerpt from the sequel post) the analyst
  must "figure out a company's key narratives and points of debate—what outcomes will drive the share price up or
  down". https://phoenixlearning.substack.com/p/7-steps-top-pod-shop-investors-use and
  https://phoenixlearning.substack.com/p/new-top-hf-analyst-financial-model
- Ex-Citadel/Millennium PM Doug Garber's earnings-preview checklist names one number per print: "This is the key #
  that will move the stock if they guide or if Analysts triangulate on it during the call." For VSTS the key # is the
  4Q FY guidance range; the debate is EBITDA "$63MM vs. $70MM expectations".
  https://pitchthepm.substack.com/p/buy-side-insights-no-14
- A trader-run preview service describes the same unit: "identify the KPIs and metrics that will actually move the
  stock, separate formal guidance from consensus and the real buyside bogey". (excerpt) The preview asks "whether the
  debate is about revenue, margins, capex, bookings, supply constraints or what management needs to say about the
  next six months." https://streetsignal.substack.com/p/our-eps-previews
- Fundamental Edge (Brett Caughran, ex-Maverick/D.E. Shaw/Citadel): "I often view the buy-side bar as a continuation
  of the current biz momentum. If it's growing 5%, start there." "the big moves come with a SURPRISE FACTOR. If the
  news is known, it's baked into the price." https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1564408743533547521.html ; his
  training is described as "programmatic frameworks for isolating the key drivers of a stock and developing
  investment theses on differentiated key drivers" (excerpt)
  https://marketing.tegus.com/webinars/the-key-driver-mindset-from-fundamental-edge
- Druckenmiller on the horizon and the unit: "you have to visualize the situation 18 months from now, and whatever
  that is, that's where the price will be, not where it is today"; and the mentor's rebuke that shaped him: "This is
  useless. What makes the stock go up and down?" https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-stanley-druckenmiller.md
  (excerpt on 18 months) https://chitchatstocks.substack.com/p/stanley-druckenmiller-the-investor

### 1f. Scenario/probability methods (Alpha Theory, Morgan Stanley study, superforecasting in a fund)

- Alpha Theory: "Scenarios ... Potential future paths for an investment ... combined by assigning probabilities to
  calculate the Probability-Weighted Return." Price targets are "more predictive of future returns than manager
  position sizes." https://www.alphatheory.com/glossary
- Worked example of scenario granularity: "the stock is trading at $100, and the analyst has three positive scenarios
  of $120, $150, and $200 with probabilities of 40%, 30%, and 5%" (i.e. a ladder of price outcomes with weights).
  https://www.alphatheory.com/blog/new-probability-of-success-calculation-improving-batting-average-analysis
- Morgan Stanley's internal study of "around 70,000 forecasts over 10 years" found "a demonstrable benefit in using a
  scenario-analysis instead of a single price target"; strategies built on scenario Downside/Tilt/Uncertainty
  "consistently created positive alpha" with hit rates in the "mid-50s".
  https://alphatheory.com/blog/capturing-alpha-in-risk-rewards-morgan-stanley
- Superforecasting inside a $26B fund (Mark Steed, CIO, Arizona PSPRS): every decision carries "a very explicit
  probabilistic forecast" with four parts: probability, "clear success definition with specific metrics", time
  horizon, confidence. Example: "I believe there's a 55% chance this ... manager will outperform the MSCI healthcare
  index by 400 basis points over the next five years." Scored with Brier scores; calibration tracked per person
  ("here are all your 80% confident forecasts ... 8 out of 10").
  https://pod.wave.co/podcast/how-i-invest-with-david-weisburd/e347-the-26b-cio-who-turned-superforecasting-into-alpha
- Tetlock/Good Judgment on question size, the "rigor-relevance trade-off": a broad question like "What will market
  conditions be like after the pandemic?" is unforecastable; a narrow one like "On 31 January 2022, what will be the
  average US tariff rate on imports from China?" is resolvable. The fix is a cluster: "sets of questions about
  discrete events that, in combination, shed light on a broader topic"; "Aggregating the probabilities ... generates
  a comprehensive forecast about the strategic question, as well as early warnings". Their Taiwan cluster has six
  questions. https://goodjudgment.com/question_clusters/ ; method rule: "Break the big question into sub-questions.
  Estimate each one independently. Recombine them." https://goodjudgment.substack.com/p/inside-a-superforecasters-toolbox-1c8

### 1g. Kill criteria / pre-mortem / falsifiable theses

- Annie Duke via T. Rowe Price (Oct 2025): define "kill criteria—explicit, ex ante conditions that, if met, trigger an
  exit ... based on the fundamentals of your thesis, not arbitrary price points", set "as you initiate a position ...
  when you are less emotionally invested". The named failure mode is "thesis creep—e.g., 'I bought this stock as a
  diversifier, but it persistently correlates with other risk assets or has serially missed earnings, yet it's too
  cheap to sell.'" https://www.troweprice.com/en/us/investment-institute/insights/ten-investing-lessons-from-a-master-of-probabilities
- A worked falsification clause: "Company X's revenue will grow 20%+ annually over the next three years, driven by its
  new product. I will consider this hypothesis invalid if quarterly revenue growth falls below 10% or if a major
  competitor captures more than 15% market share." Once written, "you've made a pact with yourself not to ignore it."
  https://www.polymathinvestor.com/p/what-would-prove-you-wrong-the-most
- "A thesis that exists primarily as prose can absorb new information almost indefinitely because the explanation can
  be rewritten faster than the investment can be falsified." "conviction should be decomposable. If no one can
  identify the assumptions whose failure would reduce conviction, 'high conviction' is describing an attitude, not a
  research conclusion." Signposts should be read in patterns, not single prints: "one weak monthly observation may be
  noise; deterioration across multiple cohorts ... may be evidence against the mechanism."
  https://enquire.ai/blog/the-thesis-is-a-hypothesis-not-a-narrative
- (excerpt) a pre-mortem is when "the investor assumes that at a predetermined future date, an investment will have
  become a failure" and lists why. https://fidelity.ca/en/insights/articles/lessons-from-three-decades-of-investing

## 2. Sell-side "key debates": how the street carves a stock, how many, how phrased

The pattern across firms is stable: two to four named debates per mega-cap at any time, each phrased as a question or
a worry, each tied to one metric that will settle it on a dated print, with the firm's stance on each. Concrete
2024–2026 examples:

### Nvidia (Morgan Stanley, Joseph Moore)
- 18 May 2026, PT $285: "three investor debates" the earnings are expected to address: (1) "market share versus ASIC
  alternatives" (the firm's TCO view "difficult to disprove" while compute is supply constrained); (2) "gross margin
  trajectory" (above 75% now, modelled to 72.7% by FY2028 on the Rubin ramp, "manageable compression rather than
  structural deterioration"); (3) "Vera Rubin readiness" (on schedule, rack deliveries may slip "a few weeks").
  https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/morgan-stanley-resets-nvidia-stock-154922400.html
- 20 Dec 2024, PT $166: three worries, each a supply/timing metric: "Prior generation Hopper builds continue to slow";
  "Not every version of new Blackwell products is ready to ship at the same time"; Marvell/Broadcom "projecting strong
  longer term growth in Application-Specific Integrated Circuits".
  https://www.tipranks.com/news/the-fly/morgan-stanley-says-strong-demand-for-blackwell-to-prevail-over-nvidia-worries-2
- 29 May 2025, PT $170: supply constraints ("massive backlog"), China/H20 ("$2.5B in lost revenue"; "$8B expected Q2
  impact"; "Without H20 ban, the company would have beaten guidance by over $3 billion"), and unmet demand.
  https://sherwood.news/markets/morgan-stanley-on-nvidia-everything-should-get-better-from-here
- Note how the same name's debate set rotates over 18 months (Hopper/Blackwell timing → China → ASIC share/margin/
  Rubin) while the count stays at three.

### Amazon (Goldman Sachs, Eric Sheridan)
- 4 Oct 2025, PT $275: the debate is AWS share: "AWS share of public cloud revenue declined to ~45% from 50%+"; the
  firm's answer is a path to "more than 20% revenue growth" and "low-to-mid-30% operating margins through late 2025
  and 2026"; advertising "about 16% [CAGR] through 2028"; "roughly 3-to-1 upside skew".
  https://finance.yahoo.com/news/goldman-says-aws-strength-underappreciated-193426761.html
- 9 Jul 2026, PT $335: debates named as "concerns about the overall health of the consumer as well as AI capital
  spending and ROIC"; the numbers attached: AWS to "grow about 33% year-over-year in 2026, nearly 35% in 2027";
  capex "about $827 billion between 2026 and 2028".
  https://www.tipranks.com/news/heres-why-goldman-raised-amazon-amzn-stock-price-target-ahead-of-q2-earnings
- (excerpt) an earlier Sheridan preview: "Three central investor debates are heading into earnings, and ... they are
  likely to drive incremental share price performance." https://benzinga.com/z/40048770

### Tesla (Morgan Stanley vs Barclays, 2026)
- 19 Jul 2026: the debate set is auto gross margin ("18.1%, broadly matching expectations"), deliveries (1.67M/1.86M
  for 2026/27), robotaxi fleet ("1,500 supervised and unsupervised vehicles by year-end"), capex "$26.8 billion" with
  "free-cash-flow burn of $11.4 billion"; and the valuation split that tells you which debates carry the stock: only
  "$47 attributed to the automotive business" of a $417 target.
  https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/tesla-stock-much-does-improved-234420878.html
- 23 Apr 2026, Barclays (Dan Levy), PT $360: capex "to $25 billion from $20 billion"; "proof points on Robotaxi
  scaling before turning constructive"; FSD "1.28 million subscriptions" but "outdated hardware may be a headwind to

  https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/04/23/barclays-holds-the-line-on-tesla-after-capex-shock-is-the-robotaxi-story-worth-the-spend/

### Apple (Bernstein, Toni Sacconaghi): two questions, and "the one number"
- Feb 2024: "(1) Even if Apple's AI functionality is not unique, will the novelty of AI + Apple's powerful marketing be

  https://www.tipranks.com/news/apple-stock-is-ai-iphone-coming-bernstein-weighs-in

### JPMorgan: the debate is one line (NII) and its macro inputs

## 4. What goes wrong when the unit is too coarse or too fine, and the guards

### Too coarse (thesis = "revenue grows", or prose that cannot be falsified)
- "A thesis that exists primarily as prose can absorb new information almost indefinitely because the explanation can
  be rewritten faster than the investment can be falsified." https://enquire.ai/blog/the-thesis-is-a-hypothesis-not-a-narrative
- Thesis drift, defined with a case: Seadrill 2014, original thesis "the youngest, highest-specification fleet of rigs
  on the water" winning premium contracts; when oil fell the company "was receiving early contract termination
  notices"; instead of selling the author invented a new thesis about "cash load and ... insider ownership". The
  guard adopted: "I build an easy-to-measure criteria into a thesis" and avoid theses "that require catalysts" with
  multiple "if" conditions. https://www.fool.com/investing/2018/04/21/the-fatal-flaw-in-my-investing-approach.aspx
- "Thesis creep causes investment performance to suffer, says Cypress Funds's Michael Wang" (Institutional Investor,
  10 Sep 2025; body paywalled). https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsy5guo0uzwqfg6sozy8/corner-office/what-makes-a-world-class-investor
- Duke's kill-criteria discipline exists precisely to stop the coarse thesis from mutating (section 1g).
- Narrative without numbers: "creativity can carry you to the outer realms of reason and into fantasy" and "there are
  no yard sticks to use to see whether you are on track" (Damodaran).
  https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/numbers-and-narrative-modeling-story.html

### Too fine (thesis = every line item; data without a thesis)
- Damodaran's three dangers of numbers without narrative: "valuations become plug-and-point exercises"; businesses
  "that can exist only in spreadsheet nirvana"; "discussions and debates about inputs become shallow exercises in
  quibbling about the 'right' values to use." Same URL as above.
- "a model with four hundred inputs is not a model but a second set of books." Good assumptions are "Few ... Sourced
  ... Owned". https://www.dissent.one/case/financial-modelling
- Valentine: 8–9 of 10 datapoints do not move the stock; reading the tape all day is "defense". https://tearsheet.co/?p=1603
- Fundamental Edge: only surprise moves the stock; "If the news is known, it's baked into the price."
  https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1564408743533547521.html
- Over-quantifying is its own failure: "Forcing a numeric probability onto every qualitative judgment can produce the
  appearance of rigor without additional information." https://enquire.ai/blog/the-thesis-is-a-hypothesis-not-a-narrative

### The guard pros use: pre-registered print tests, and patterns rather than single prints
- "if this number prints, I'm wrong" in practice: Bernstein's "December quarter revenue guidance implying 70 (million)
  iPhones or fewer ... would likely be viewed worrisomely" (a threshold on one guided line, on one date).
  https://www.fortune.com/2016/08/16/the-one-number-apple
- Garber's "key #" per print, with the bogey written beside consensus ("$63MM vs. $70MM").
  https://pitchthepm.substack.com/p/buy-side-insights-no-14
- Falsification clause with metric, threshold and horizon (20%+ growth; invalid below 10% quarterly or >15% competitor
  share). https://www.polymathinvestor.com/p/what-would-prove-you-wrong-the-most
- But judge on patterns: "one weak monthly observation may be noise; deterioration across multiple cohorts ... may be
  evidence against the mechanism." https://enquire.ai/blog/the-thesis-is-a-hypothesis-not-a-narrative
- Steinhardt's exit rule closes a view when the market has caught up, not when a number prints: "When the market
  begins to reflect your view, the variant edge is gone." https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-michael-steinhardt.md

### How macro/industry debates are phrased
- As questions with a sector map (Morgan Stanley 2026): "which industries actually see meaningful gains, and which
  simply absorb higher costs"; "will power constraints, pricing pressure, and uneven demand limit upside?"
  https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/here-is-what-morgan-stanley-thinks-will-likely-to-shape-stocks-in-2026-4413449
- As a resolvable ladder when traded: Kalshi's Fed market is a set of thresholds on one object and one date, "Will the
  upper bound of the federal funds rate be above 4.25% following the Fed's Dec 9, 2026 meeting?", offered at every
  25bp from 2.75% to 5.25% (prices today: >3.75% 98c, >4.00% 80c, >4.25% 15c, >4.50% 6c). This is the market's own
  version of a rung ladder with a due date. https://coinstats.app/prediction-markets/odds/economy/kalshi-kxfed-26dec/
- As a cluster (Good Judgment): a strategic question is covered by six dated, resolvable sub-questions spanning
  "political, economic, financial, security, and informational" angles. https://goodjudgment.com/question_clusters/

## Synthesis (15 lines)

What one view looks like at hedge-fund granularity
1. Well-evidenced: the unit is one disagreement with what the price implies, on one driver, with a trigger and a date
   (Steinhardt's four parts; Sonkin & Johnson's four questions; The Financial Pen's "one overarching disagreement").
2. Well-evidenced: the driver is a business KPI or segment line that carries value, not a statement line: AWS growth
   %, NVDA gross margin %, China DC revenue $, iPhone units guided, JPM NII $, Tesla auto GM ex-credits, FSD attach.
3. Well-evidenced: each debate has a named settling print and a bogey beside consensus ("the key #"; "70 million
   iPhones or fewer"; "$63MM vs $70MM"), which is exactly a rung boundary on a due date.
4. Well-evidenced: pros write the invalidation in advance as metric + threshold + horizon (Duke's kill criteria;
   "invalid if quarterly growth falls below 10%"); your ladder does this by construction.
5. Well-evidenced: the market itself ladders macro objects (Kalshi Fed Dec-2026 thresholds every 25bp; Alpha Theory's
   $120/$150/$200 scenarios), so a fixed rung set per object is standard practice, not an invention.
6. Inference: your NVDA example ("FY27 China revenue vs FY26", five rungs, FY print as due date) matches the street's
   unit; the one adjustment practitioners would make is to also hold the ASIC-share and gross-margin debates as
   separate views, because the three move on different evidence.
7. Inference: a view should be split when its rungs would be settled by different prints or different evidence streams
   (margin vs share vs timing), and merged when two candidate questions would always resolve together.
8. Inference: a view is too coarse when its question cannot be put on a dated print ("AI wins"), and too fine when it
   is a line nobody prices (Visible Alpha's 156th line item); the test is whether a sell-side note or a PM would name
   it as "the debate".

The typical count per company
9. Well-evidenced: two to four live views per name. Valentine: "two to four critical factors"; Morgan Stanley NVDA:
   three debates in each of three notes 18 months apart; Goldman AMZN: "three central investor debates"; Bernstein
   AAPL: two questions; Barclays TSLA: four with one carrying the value.
10. Well-evidenced: the set rotates (NVDA: Hopper/Blackwell timing → China → ASIC/margin/Rubin) while the count holds;
    a views database should expect retirements and replacements, not growth.
11. Well-evidenced: analysts carry 20–50 names, so 2–4 views each is 60–150 live views per analyst, the practical
    ceiling the quotes imply ("hard ... on more than a small number of stocks"; 80 modelled names "absolutely hell").
12. Well-evidenced: vendors hold ~156 consensus lines per company; the debate sits where the estimate range is widest
    (Blackwell $2.5B–$24.4B from 11 sources), not on the mean; dispersion is a good signal for carving a view.
13. Inference: themes should be one view each at the theme level (a dated ladder on the macro object) plus a stated
    link to each company view it feeds, with direction, line and magnitude (JPM: ±$2.2B NII per 100bp; NVDA:
    ~$0.25 EPS per $10B China revenue), rather than being folded into the company view.
14. Inference: for a theme, the Good Judgment cluster is the right size test: if one macro question needs six dated
    sub-questions to be resolvable, those six are the views and the macro question is their parent.
15. Strongest single pieces of evidence: Valentine's "two to four" (tearsheet.co), Morgan Stanley's repeated "three
    debates" on NVDA (Yahoo, May 2026; TipRanks, Dec 2024), Sacconaghi's "70 million iPhones" threshold (Fortune),
    and Kalshi's Fed-rate ladder (coinstats).
