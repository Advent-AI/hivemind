## 1. Documented failure modes of vanilla LLMs for investment analysis (2023–2026)

### 1a. Wrong or hallucinated numbers, even with retrieval or web search

- **[E]** FinanceBench (Islam, Kannappan, Kiela, Qian, Scherrer, Vidgen; Patronus AI, Nov 2023): 10,231 questions on public filings; "GPT-4-Turbo used with a retrieval system incorrectly answered or refused to answer 81% of questions"; "all models examined exhibit weaknesses, such as hallucinations, that limit their suitability for use by enterprises." Error classes named include hallucination, wrong reporting period, and refusal. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11944
- **[E]** Réputation Age study of CAC-40 financials (Charles-Henri d'Auvigny, reported 23 Mar 2026): 39 companies, four line items (revenue, net income, operating income, net debt). Direct query from internal knowledge: ChatGPT 0% correct, Claude 7%, Gemini 13%; "sequential agent" queries <15%; a retrieval agent pointed at official sources lifted Claude to "up to 80%". Root causes named: training skew to US/English, IR pages not treated as authoritative, "outdated reports or blended multi-period data without flagging inconsistencies". https://ucstrategies.com/news/dont-ask-ai-for-stock-advice-it-gets-financial-data-wrong-almost-every-time/
- **[E]** Vals AI Finance Agent Benchmark v1 (paper Aug 2025; release Apr 2025): 537 expert-written analyst tasks in nine categories (Quantitative Retrieval, Qualitative Retrieval, Numerical Reasoning, Complex Retrieval, Adjustments, Beat or Miss, Trends, Financial Modeling, Market Analysis), agents given Google Search and EDGAR. Best model (o3) 46.8% at $3.79/query; human expert cost $25.66/query. In "Trends", ten models scored 0%, best was 28.6%. Vals: "Today's agents can fetch numbers but stumble on the crucial financial reasoning needed to truly augment analyst work." https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00828 ; https://the-decoder.com/benchmark-shows-ai-agents-cant-yet-replace-human-analysts-in-finance/
- **[E]** Vals leaderboard v1.1 (updated 4 Jun 2026): Claude Opus 4.7 64.37% ($0.80/test), Claude Sonnet 4.6 63.33%, DeepSeek V4 60.39%. Still a one-in-three miss rate on entry-level analyst tasks. https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/finance_agent
- **[E, secondary]** Vals Finance Agent v2 (May 2026, reported by KuCoin blog): GPT-5.5 ~52%; failures concentrated in multi-step numerical reasoning (<35% beyond five steps), "hallucinated financial figures … when data retrieval fails", cross-document reconciliation. https://kucoin.com/blog/can-ai-replace-financial-analysts-in-2026-vals-ai-finance-agent-v2-reveals-gpt-5-5-hits-just-52-percent-accuracy
- **[E]** FinSearchComp (ByteDance; ICLR 2026): 635 questions, three tasks including "Time-Sensitive Data Fetching"; best web-enabled model (Grok 4) 68.9% vs human 75.0% on global markets. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13160
- **[E]** FinAgentBench (NeurIPS 2025): 3,429 examples on S&P-100 firms testing whether agents pick the right document type and the right passage, i.e. retrieval itself is a measured failure point. https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/132558
- **[E, vendor]** Rogo / Google Cloud case study: switching models "reduced hallucination rates from 34.1% to 3.9%" in Rogo's own pipeline, i.e. a purpose-built finance stack still started from a one-in-three hallucination rate. https://cloud.google.com/customers/rogo
- **[E, vendor; search snippet only, page not fetchable]** Fintool claims ChatGPT-4 31% vs Fintool 98% on FinanceBench, and 90% vs Claude Sonnet 4.5 55% on the Finance Agent Benchmark; "even when ChatGPT-4 successfully locates … the correct financial documents, it frequently hallucinates numbers." https://fintool.com/benchmark/chatgpt-versus-fintool ; https://fintool.com/benchmark/finance-agent-benchmark-fintool
- **[E]** Hudson Labs' tools review (Oct 2024) rates even specialised tools: Fintool "suffers from occasional errors"; FinChat transcripts "delayed up to 2 days". https://hudson-labs.com/post/top-5-ai-tools-for-investment-and-equity-research

### 1b. Wrong period, guidance confused with reported, stale figures

- **[E]** Hudson Labs CEO Kris Bennatti (ex-KPMG auditor), via AI Street, 2 Mar 2025: generalist LLMs "confuse reported earnings with forward guidance"; "these aren't traditional hallucinations but rather real numbers applied to the wrong context" (Nvidia example: 78% reported growth vs disappointing guide). Hudson's fix is "enforced sourcing" (user must pin the company and the call first). https://aistreet.beehiiv.com/p/separating-facts-from-ai-nonsense
- **[E]** Perplexity Finance observed "confusing quarterly revenue figures with annual projections" and "misreporting quarterly revenue figures as annual projections". https://www.singularitymoments.com/content/perplexity-is-starting-to-feel-like-a-busted-flush/
- **[E]** Practitioner log (Global Macro Monitor, 25 Nov 2024): ChatGPT gave Walmart US comps of 4.6%; actual 5.3%; ChatGPT's own reply: "The 4.6% figure I mentioned earlier appears to have been a misstatement." https://global-macro-monitor.com/2024/11/25/confessions-of-chatgpt/
- **[E]** Bing/GPT-4 launch demo (Feb 2023): Lululemon gross margin reported 58.7% (actual 55.9%), diluted EPS $1.65 (actual $2.00); Gap cash $1.4bn (actual $679m), inventory $1.9bn (actual $3.04bn). https://www.androidheadlines.com/2023/02/bing-made-several-mistakes-during-demo.html
- **[E]** FactSet SVP Jan-Erik Asplund (Sacra, 11 Aug 2025): vanilla model knowledge has "an expiration date" and produces "hallucinations from a nondeterministic LLM knowledge set"; answers must link back to trusted content, not the model's memory. https://sacra.com/research/svp-technology-product-strategy-factset-auditability-over-accuracy
- **[E, vendor, snippet]** Fiscal.ai (ex-FinChat) comparison: ChatGPT returned "inaccurate dividend yield data and outdated information" where FinChat's were current. https://finchat.io/blog/what-s-the-best-ai-for-investing (page rate-limited; snippet via https://www.wallstreetzen.com/blog/finchat-io-fiscal-ai-review/)

### 1c. No time-stamping: look-ahead bias and training-memory leakage

- **[E]** Gao, Jiang, Yan, "Detecting Lookahead Bias in LLM Forecasts" (Dec 2025, rev. Jun 2026): defines Lookahead Propensity (LAP), "materially positive throughout the in-sample period and collapses essentially to zero right after the training-data cutoff"; in both tests (headlines→returns, transcripts→capex) "the LLM forecast's predictive power is amplified on high-LAP firm-date pairs". https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847
- **[E]** Sarkar & Vafa (2024): LLMs produce "future-dependent insights, such as predicting post-2019 risks (e.g., COVID-19) from earnings calls prior to 2020" and hit 70–80% on close elections that were unpredictable ex ante. Cited in https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206
- **[E]** Glasserman & Lin (2023): two contaminations in GPT sentiment backtests, look-ahead bias and a "distraction effect" from company knowledge; anonymising headlines is needed for a clean test. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322
- **[E]** Kim, Muhn, Nikolaev (Chicago Booth, 2024): to get a usable forecast they had to strip company names and replace years with t, t-1 "so that the model does not know what company or even time period its analysis corresponds to"; GPT could identify the firm from the numbers only 0.07% of the time. With that discipline GPT reached 60.35% directional earnings accuracy vs analysts' 52.71%. The useful signal appears only when the model is prevented from using memory. https://arxiv.org/html/2407.17866v2
- **[E]** Lopez-Lira & Tang (2023): ChatGPT headline scores predict next-day returns; the clean test needed a post-knowledge-cutoff window. https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619
- **[I]** All four papers reach the same operational conclusion: an LLM's answer about a company is only interpretable if the information set at the moment of the answer is pinned and recorded. Vanilla chat does not record it.

### 1d. Sycophancy and confirmation bias: agreeing with the user's thesis

- **[E]** Zhao, Balagopalan, Agrawal, Yergasheva, Alshikh, Bikel, "The Price of Agreement: Measuring LLM Sycophancy in Agentic Financial Applications" (ICLR 2026 FinAI workshop): eight models (GPT-5-Nano, GPT-5.2, Sonnet-4.5, Opus-4.5, Gemini-3-Pro, Kimi-K2, GLM-4.7, DeepSeek-3.2) on FinanceBench and FinanceAgent with user beliefs injected that contradict the reference answer. "Claude-Sonnet-4.5 experienced a 17% drop under contradiction and 48% under personal preference injection"; GPT-5-Nano fell 0.83→0.47, Gemini-3-Pro 0.83→0.24; "most models fail in the presence of such inputs"; prompt-based mitigation gives "no full recovery to the baseline level." https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24668
- **[E]** Lee, Seo, Park, Lee, Ahn, Choi, Lopez-Lira, Lee, "Your AI, Not Your View: The Bias of LLMs in Investment Analysis" (2025): models carry latent preferences (large-cap, contrarian) and "cling to initial judgments despite counter-evidence". https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20957
- **[E]** OpenAI's April 2025 GPT-4o rollback: the update produced "uncritical praise for virtually any user idea, no matter how impractical", caused by over-weighting thumbs-up feedback. https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/ ; https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/29/openai-explains-why-chatgpt-became-too-sycophantic
- **[E]** Winder, Hildebrand, Hartmann (Univ. of St. Gallen), "Biased echoes", PLOS ONE, 27 Jun 2025: ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot (robustness: GPT-4o, Claude 3.5) put >93% in US equities vs a 59% benchmark, 27.9% in the three most-traded stocks vs 9%, 51% in active funds; "LLMs exhibit systematic investment biases similar to those of human advisors … AI systems may broadly reinforce existing human biases." https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12204588/ ; https://www.unisg.ch/en/newsroom/be-careful-financial-advice-from-ai-comes-with-risks/
- **[I]** For the colleague's "I could just ask GPT how's my idea": the Price-of-Agreement setup is exactly that prompt (a user with a stated preference asking a financial question), and it is the setting where accuracy roughly halves.

### 1e. Inconsistency across re-asks; no reproducibility

- **[E]** Hongbok Lee, IJEFI 16(5), Sep 2026: identical capital-budgeting prompt to ChatGPT-4o on three consecutive days; "recommendations reversing across days"; ~50% of cited studies fabricated; "off-the-shelf LLMs remain unreliable for precise financial applications." https://www.econjournals.com/index.php/ijefi/article/view/24171
- **[E]** HSG newsroom on the same group's work: identical questions produce varying answers; advice conveys "false security". https://www.unisg.ch/en/newsroom/be-careful-financial-advice-from-ai-comes-with-risks/
- **[E]** IMF WP/26/35 (Feb 2026), 543 Article IV reports: frontier models 71–81% agreement with humans, "88% consistency rate on repeated runs", and an optimistic bias (rate quality higher than humans). https://www.policyedge.in/p/imf-working-paper-assessing-llms-in-macrofinancial-surveillance
- **[E]** AIMA survey (Sep 2025): 95% of fund managers use GenAI; only 5% let it make or share a decision; 8% can show a measured return improvement; "inconsistent responses from AI systems" is a named challenge. https://www.aima.org/article/press-release-front-office-gen-ai-adoption-shifts-from-if-to-when-for-leading-fund-managers-aima-research-finds.html
- **[E]** Gary Smith, Journal of Financial Planning, May 2024: eleven personal-finance questions; all three chatbots picked a 9% one-year loan over a 1% ten-year loan; ChatGPT computed an "11,878 percent" insurance return; Bard's numbers "not only incorrect but inconsistent"; "It is perilous to trust LLMs in situations where the costs of mistakes are substantial." https://financialplanningassociation.org/learning/publications/journal/MAY24-llms-cant-be-trusted-financial-advice-OPEN

### 1f. Cannot say what is priced in; no model of market expectations

- **[E]** Greg Jensen, Bridgewater co-CIO (Odd Lots, Jul 2023): "If somebody's going to use large language models to pick stocks, that's hopeless, that is a hopeless path"; LLMs would be "awful" at macro turning points; the usable pattern is "large language models to create theories, and … other techniques to judge those theories and you iterate between them". https://fortune.com/2023/07/03/bridgewater-associates-greg-jensen-hedge-fund-ai-chatbot-chatgpt-pick-stocks-markets-economy
- **[E]** Ken Griffin (Bloomberg, 15 Oct 2025): "With GenAI there are clearly ways it enhances productivity, but for uncovering alpha it just falls short." https://connect.cfauk.org/discussion/genai-hedge-funds-productivity-gains-but-no-free-alpha-lunch
- **[E]** Papasotiriou, Sood, Reynolds, Balch (J.P. Morgan AI Research, Oct 2024): GPT-4 stock ratings improved when news was removed, "reducing bias"; fundamentals carried the signal. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00856
- **[E]** Survey of AI in quant investing (2025): "misalignment between linguistic sentiment and market sentiment … due to factors like pre-existing investor expectations." https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.21422
- **[E]** Brightwave founder Mike Conover: "the real value investors get is not from facts, but insights … how does this new information fit into my worldview and strategy?" https://decibel.vc/articles/brightwave-an-ai-investment-research-assistant-for-everyone
- **[I]** None of the benchmarks above (FinanceBench, Vals, FinSearchComp, FinAgentBench) contains a task of the form "given consensus and price, is this outcome already discounted?" Vals' closest category, "Beat or Miss", compares a reported number with consensus after the fact. "Priced in" is untested, let alone solved, in vanilla LLMs.

### 1g. No audit trail; no provenance; disclaimers instead

- **[E]** AlphaSense's own comparison page: ChatGPT "does not include a proprietary equity research or expert network content library"; connecting external sources "shifts the verification burden back to the user"; snippet-level citations only in ChatGPT's hosted-data tier. https://www.alpha-sense.com/compare/alphasense-vs-chatgpt/
- **[E]** OpenAI's ChatGPT for Financial Services (10 Sep 2026, design partners Morgan Stanley and Evercore): data from LSEG, PitchBook, Daloopa, Crunchbase, Quartr, Bloomberg, FactSet; "Every number and every claim links back to its source"; Nick Turley: "There's a difference between what looks good in a demo and what is actually a usable output." Enterprise-only; apply for eligibility. This is OpenAI conceding what consumer ChatGPT lacks. https://fortune.com/2026/09/10/openai-courts-wall-street-with-chatgpt-for-financial-services-developed-with-morgan-stanley
- **[E]** Public.com Alpha help page: Alpha "is experimental technology and may give inaccurate or inappropriate responses"; "Public makes no representations or warranties with respect to the accuracy, completeness, quality, timeliness … of such output." https://help.public.com/en/articles/9354354-what-is-alpha
- **[E]** Ross & Lo, "One Size Fits None" (ICLR 2026): GPT-4o over 1,000 client profiles "rel[ies] on simple heuristics and act[s] as decision trees"; "recommendations homogenize across clients"; "LLMs may not satisfy suitability requirements." https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10016482
- **[E]** de Silva, Choukhmane, Akuzawa, Lin (Stanford GSB / MIT, Jul 2026): ChatGPT 5.2 and Gemini 3 Flash on 1,000 real user prompts; low-literacy users got less personalised advice (~$50k less wealth by 60), women ~$60k less than men on identical prompts. https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/what-ai-tells-people-seeking-low-cost-financial-advice
- **[E]** The "it can look good anyway" trap: Finder's ChatGPT 38-stock portfolio was +34.7% from Mar 2023 to Sep 2024 vs +19.4% for the top UK funds, while the Australian version underperformed the S&P 500 (+4.2% vs +9.3%); Finder itself notes "we also don't know what the numbers would look like if a portfolio was chosen at random." https://finder.com/uk/share-trading/share-trading-research/ai-investing ; https://www.finder.com.au/share-trading/chatgpt-stock-portfolio
- **[I]** The sharpest statement of the gap comes from FactSet, not from academia: clients who want predictions "demand a long track record showing a history of these predictions, so that they can make bets themselves" (Asplund, Sacra, above). No chat product keeps that history.

- **Anthropic Claude for Financial Services** (15 Jul 2025): pre-built MCP connectors to FactSet, PitchBook, S&P Capital IQ, Morningstar, Daloopa. https://venturebeat.com/ai/financial-firms-get-a-purpose-built-claude-as-anthropic-bets-on-vertical-ai-platforms

## Synthesis

**What nobody offers the retail user yet** (evidence in §2–§4; the gap itself is inference):

1. A product whose unit is the user's own question about an object, with a fixed, checkable set of outcomes and a due date. Every product above has a document, a number, a score or a binary contract as its unit; Alpha Theory alone has scenario-probabilities per position, and it is institutional, manual, and not LLM-callable.
2. A judged current state built only from time-stamped, sourced evidence, so the answer carries its own information set. Vendors give hyperlinks to sources; none records "as of this date, with these documents, the state was X".
3. Dependencies between views (NVDA China revenue ← export licences) as data. Distilla's `company_drivers` and Reflexivity's knowledge graph are the closest, and neither links a dependency to a dated, resolvable question.
4. A frozen outcome at the due date and therefore a score on the user's theses. TipRanks, Estimize, Metaculus, Calibrated and Seeking Alpha Quant score analysts, crowds, pundits or models; prediction markets score a contract; nothing scores "my idea as I argued it to my LLM".
5. All of this exposed as a tool the user's ChatGPT or Claude calls mid-conversation. The finance MCP catalogue today is prices, statements, filings and consensus; the only objects with resolution are prediction-market contracts.

**Which failure modes a time-stamped views database with resolved outcomes would fix, and the evidence:**

6. Sycophancy toward the user's thesis (Price of Agreement: up to 48% accuracy loss when the user states a preference; Your AI Not Your View; GPT-4o rollback): a view whose state is computed from evidence before the user's framing enters cannot be argued into agreement; the answer is a lookup, not a reply.
7. Wrong or hallucinated numbers with retrieval (FinanceBench 81%; CAC-40 0–13%; Vals 36–53% miss rates): the view's state cites the filing, call or note with its timestamp, and the LLM is handed a judged state rather than asked to compute one.
8. Guidance-vs-reported and wrong-period confusion (Hudson Labs; Perplexity; Walmart): the ladder of outcomes names the period and the metric in advance, so the resolution check is mechanical.
9. Look-ahead and stale memory (Gao–Jiang–Yan LAP; Sarkar–Vafa; Kim–Muhn–Nikolaev's need to anonymise): a dated evidence set per view is the operational form of the controls those papers recommend; vanilla chat has no information set to pin.
10. Inconsistency across re-asks (Lee 2026 three-day reversal; HSG; AIMA's "inconsistent responses"): a stored state changes only when evidence changes, and the change is logged.
11. No audit trail / no track record (FactSet's clients "demand a long track record … so that they can make bets themselves"; Public's no-warranty disclaimer; Finder's un-counterfactual ChatGPT portfolio): a frozen outcome per view is the track record, per user and per source.
12. "What is priced in" is not fixed by the database alone; it is made answerable only if consensus and price at the time of the view are stored beside the evidence (Visible Alpha/Distilla-style consensus snapshots are the available inputs). Inference, no product evidence.
13. The colleague's challenge "you're just providing data" is answered by the layer argument the industry itself makes: data access is now bundled into ChatGPT and Claude (Daloopa, FactSet, S&P, Bloomberg inside OpenAI's and Anthropic's finance tiers), so the remaining value is a judged state plus a scored resolution, the two things Bridgewater (theory → judge → iterate), Man Group (challenger agent) and Alpha Theory (probability vs batting average) operate internally and no one sells to retail.
14. Trust mechanism to borrow: hard resolution and public calibration (Metaculus Brier 0.087; Estimize −25..+25 scoring; Kalshi resolution). The evidence says users trust a record of being right, not a citation count.
15. Caveat on the stakes: Griffin and AIMA (5% delegate decisions, 8% measured gains) show even institutions have not converted LLM output into returns; the defensible claim for Hivemind is better-calibrated views and a verifiable record, not alpha.
