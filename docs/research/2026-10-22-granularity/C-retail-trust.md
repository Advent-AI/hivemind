## Q1. What retail investors demonstrably get wrong, and whether structure helps

### Overtrading, overconfidence, attention, disposition (the canon)

- **[E] Trading is hazardous.** Barber & Odean (JF 2000), 66,465 discount-broker households 1991–96: "those that trade most earn an annual return of 11.4 percent, while the market returns 17.9 percent"; average household turns over 75% of its portfolio a year; "the stocks investors buy subsequently underperform the stocks they sell" by more than 2% over a year. Mechanism: overconfidence. https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/Papers%20current%20versions/Individual_Investor_Performance_Final.pdf
- **[E] Disposition effect.** Odean (JF 1998), 10,000 accounts 1987–93: outside December, proportion of gains realised 14.8% vs losses realised 9.8%; a winner was "almost 60% more likely to be sold than a stock that was down"; not explained by rebalancing or costs, and "nor was it justified by subsequent portfolio performance". https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/disposition/disposition.html
- **[E] Attention-driven buying.** Barber & Odean, "All That Glitters" (RFS 2008): individuals are net buyers of stocks in the news, with abnormal volume or extreme one-day returns, because of the search problem across thousands of stocks; stocks bought on high-attention days subsequently underperform those sold. https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v21y2008i2p785-818.html
- **[E] Day traders do not learn.** Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean (Taiwan 1992–2006): "97% of day traders are likely to lose money in future day trading"; "74% of day trading volume is generated by traders with a history of losses"; losers quit more often but many persist. https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf

### Robinhood era (2020–2026): herding, options, meme stocks, finfluencers

- **[E] Robinhood herding reverses.** Barber, Huang, Odean, Schwarz (JF 2022): "the top stocks bought by Robinhood users fall by five percent over the next month while the most extreme herding events see reversals of nine percent"; Robinhood users are more attention-driven than other retail because the app surfaces top-mover lists in a low-information environment. https://www.ucdavis.edu/curiosity/blog/what-effect-brokerage-robinhood-having-stock-market ; paper: https://iimb.ac.in/sites/default/files/inline-files/attention-induced-trading.pdf
- **[E] 0DTE options.** Beckmeyer, Branger, Gayda, "Retail Traders Love 0DTE Options… But Should They?": "more than 75% of retail investors' trades in S&P 500 options involve 0DTE contracts"; retail "lost more than $125 million in aggregate on 0DTE options, with more than $90 million of these losses being the result of transaction costs". https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf
- **[E] Anatomy of retail option trading.** Bogousslavsky & Muravyev: retail "prefer cheaper, weekly options with average bid-ask spread of 12.6%, and lose money on average"; retail option flow raises underlying volatility via dealer hedging. https://www.lsu.edu/business/files/event-files/2025-finance-mardi-gras/retail_option_trading_v2.pdf
- **[E] Copy trading raises risk.** Apesteguia, Oechssler, Weidenholzer (Management Science 2020): showing others' success "leads to a significant increase in risk taking", larger still with a one-click copy option; "copy trading leads to excessive risk taking". https://bse.eu/research/publications/copy-trading
- **[E] Finfluencers are mostly anti-skilled, and the anti-skilled have more followers.** Kakhbod, Kazempour, Livdan, Schürhoff (SFI 23-30): "28% of finfluencers are skilled generating 2.6% monthly abnormal returns, 16% are unskilled, and 56% have negative skill ('antiskill') generating -2.3% monthly abnormal returns"; "antiskilled finfluencers have more followers and more influence on retail trading than skilled finfluencers"; a contrarian strategy yields 1.2%/month. https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/n-23-30-finfluencers
- **[E] Meme stocks.** Swedish FSA analysis of winners and losers in meme-stock trading (FI Analysis 40, 2023) and Mills et al. (prospect-theory account of the COVID/meme boom as wealth-destroying for late entrants). https://fi.se/en/published/reports/fi-analysis/2023/fi-analysis-no.-40-winners-and-losers-in-the-trading-of-meme-stocks ; https://alphaarchitect.com/covid-trading/

### Retail use of ChatGPT/AI for investing (2024–2026)

- **[E, survey] Investing.com, March 2026, n=938 US retail investors:** 62% use AI to inform investment decisions (23.6% regularly, 27.4% occasionally); 54% use ChatGPT-type chatbots; uses: 60%+ research stocks, ~33% understand news, ~33% generate trade ideas. Trust: "54% indicate they trust AI only somewhat … mostly (20%) or completely (4%)". Concerns: 39% wrong/misleading recommendations, 24% "market herding", 21% over-reliance, 8% lack of transparency. 65% *say* AI improved performance (self-report, no outcome measure). https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/survey-nearly-twothirds-of-retail-investors-use-ai-to-inform-market-decisions-4598846
- **[E, survey] CFP Board, July 2023, n=1,153:** "31% of investors report feeling comfortable implementing AI financial advice without verification"; comfort rises to 52% once a planner verifies it; 51% distrust AI advice vs 71% distrust social-media advice. https://www.cfp.net/news/2023/08/cfp-board-survey-investors-trust-ai-more-than-social-media--but-advice-still
- **[E, survey] CFA Institute 2025 Graduate Outlook (as reported):** 91% of young graduates trust human advisors, 83% trust AI assistants like ChatGPT, 74% trust robo-advisors; other 2025–26 surveys report "eight in 10 Gen Z investors" using AI for investment-related advice. https://www.outlookmoney.com/magazine/what-gen-z-should-ask-before-using-ai ; https://techradar.com/pro/gen-z-investors-are-regularly-turning-to-ai-for-advice-and-that-could-soon-be-a-huge-problem
- **[E] Measured behaviour change.** Martin, Chang, Dong, Zhou, "AI (ChatGPT) Democratization, Return Predictability, and Trading Inequality": retail trades began aligning with ChatGPT-derived earnings-call sentiment immediately after November 2022, as short sellers already did; "During ChatGPT outages … that alignment fell away". The gap between retail and institutions around earnings narrowed. https://olin.washu.edu/about/news-and-media/news/2025/04/chatgpt-level-playing-field-retail-investors.php
- **[E] What retail uses it for, and where it hurts.** PLS-SEM study of 121 French investors: ChatGPT use for data analysis (β=0.209) and risk management (β=0.287) improved self-assessed decisions; use for **market forecasting had a strongly negative effect (β=-0.511)**; sentiment-analysis use turned negative among the financially literate. https://mf-journal.com/article/view/539
- **[E] LLMs carry retail's biases.** AEA 2026 paper "What Does ChatGPT Make of Historical Stock Returns?" (as excerpted): GPT-4o return forecasts are over-optimistic and LLMs "exhibit the same behavioral biases documented in humans (optimism, overconfidence, extrapolation, and framing effects), despite demonstrably 'knowing' the behavioral finance concepts". https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2026/program/paper/zNrQ4Yn6
- **[E] Ungrounded LLM = biased LLM; grounding fixes it.** HBS (Cao, Wang, Xiang), ~5,000 Chinese listed firms, ChatGPT 4.1 vs DeepSeek R1 six-month price forecasts: ChatGPT "projected stock prices would be about 12.5% higher", had "13% larger absolute forecast errors"; when fed Chinese news, "ChatGPT's 'excess optimism' disappeared and its predictions fell in line with DeepSeek". https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/chatgpt-vs-deepseek-what-chinese-stocks-reveal-about-ais-limits
- **[E] LLM augmentation may push retail toward complexity.** MDPI IJFS 2025 "Strategic Complexity and Behavioral Distortion: Retail Investing Under LLM Augmentation" studies "a shift in retail investors' behavior—from low-complexity strategies to cognitively demanding, risk-intensive structures" (page blocked; abstract via search). https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7072/13/4/210

### Does structure (pre-commitment, checklists, forecasting training, pre-mortems) help?

- **[E] Pre-commitment beats reminders for the disposition effect.** Fischbacher, Hoffmann, Schudy (RFS 2017), lab experiment: access to automatic selling devices "significantly" reduced the disposition effect, "driven by a significant increase in realized losses"; "the automatic selling device treatment significantly reduces the disposition effect, but the reminder treatment does not"; it is "the opportunity to ex ante commit" that works. https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/353526
- **[E] Listing reasons against your answer fixes overconfidence.** Koriat, Lichtenstein, Fischhoff (1980): listing reasons for and against before answering "produced a marked improvement in the appropriateness of confidence judgments"; in Exp. 2 "only the listing of contradicting reasons improved the appropriateness of confidence". Lord, Lepper, Preston (1984): "consider the opposite" corrects more than instructions to "be fair and unbiased". https://newiipdm.haifa.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/1980-Koriat-Lichtenstein-Fischhoff-JEPHLM.pdf ; https://causeweb.org/cause/node/7638
- **[E] Pre-mortem.** Mitchell, Russo, Pennington (1989): prospective hindsight raised the *number* of reasons generated by ~30% (quantity, not quality); Veinott et al. (2010, n=178): imagining the plan has failed cut confidence about twice as much as pros/cons. https://corporate.jasoncollins.blog/premortem
- **[E] Forecasting training and scoring.** Good Judgment Project (Mellers et al. 2014): a <1-hour probability/base-rate training "consistently improved accuracy (Brier scores) by 6 to 11% over the control condition"; teams beat the crowd by ~10%; the project "beat a prediction market inside the intelligence community … by 25-30%"; "deliberation time and number of predictions made per question ('belief updating') both improved accuracy"; year-to-year skill correlation 0.65. https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/W94KjunX3hXAtZvXJ/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good ; https://aiimpacts.org/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good-judgment-project/
- **[E] Feedback is what calibrates experts.** Weather forecasters are "quite well calibrated" (Murphy & Winkler 1977), attributed to "the prompt and continuous feedback as well as by long enduring practice"; calibration improves with "training, tracking, timely and accurate feedback (Murphy and Daan, 1984)". https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.06006
- **[E, correlational] Decision journaling for professionals.** Essentia Analytics, 75 portfolios, 15,000 nudges 2018–21: PMs who engaged with nudges (including a journaling "brain dump") outperformed non-engagers by 1.6 pp annualized; "every monthly response correlated to an additional 18 basis points of alpha per year". https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1rt09vth6qhw0/Keeping-Active-Managers-From-Underperforming-May-Be-This-Simple
- **[V/E] Conviction made explicit.** Alpha Theory 2025 review, 200+ managers, >1m price targets over 14 years: sizing to stated probability-weighted targets would have beaten actual sizing by "+3.9% annualized", in "13 of 14 years"; positions *with* a written price target earned 5.7% (long) / 6.4% (short) higher ROIC than those without; refreshing stale (90+ day) targets added 1.6% / 2.9%. Vendor data on its own clients. https://www.alphatheory.com/blog/alpha-theory-2025-year-in-review-position-sizing-as-a-persistent-edge

**Which retail mistakes a "dissect into debates, pre-commit outcomes, resolve at a due date" workflow maps onto:** attention/narrative buying (the unit becomes a checkable claim, not a ticker in the news), overconfidence (a ladder of outcomes forces the "reasons against" step Koriat showed works), disposition effect (an outcome frozen at a due date is an ex-ante commitment of the kind Fischbacher showed works where reminders fail), no-feedback trading (resolved outcomes supply the feedback loop that makes weather forecasters calibrated and that day traders never get), and using the LLM for the one job it is measurably bad at (free-form forecasting, β=-0.511) rather than for data analysis and risk framing.

## Q2. Trust mechanisms with evidence: do measurable track records change behaviour?

### Ratings and ranks move money; their predictive value is modest

- **[E] Morningstar stars move flows.** Del Guercio & Tkac (Atlanta Fed WP 2001-15), 3,388 funds 1996–99: "An initial five-star rating results in average six-month abnormal flow of $26 million, or 53 percent above normal expected flow"; upgrades/downgrades produce immediate abnormal flow "suggesting that some investors view rating changes as 'new' information". https://www.atlantafed.org/research/publications/wp/2001/15
- **[E] …but are weakly predictive.** WSJ "The Morningstar Mirage" (2017): five-star funds average three stars ten years later; Morningstar's reply: "the Star Rating is moderately predictive … what we'd expect of a backward-looking, entirely quantitative measure", a "starting point". https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2017/10/29/morningstar-versus-the-wall-street-journal-who-won ; https://www.fa-mag.com/news/morningstar-responds-to-paper-s-scathing-criticism-of-its-fund-ratings-35442.html
- **[E] Value Line Timeliness: a track record that decayed.** Group 1 minus Group 5 spread "trending toward zero" (1965–2008); "predictive power of the ranking system seems to be missing from about 2000 onward"; once post-earnings-announcement drift is controlled, rank differences "are no longer significant". https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/performance-trend-for-value-lines-timeliness-ranking/ ; https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/358281
- **[V] Zacks Rank: hypothetical, frictionless.** Returns are "from hypothetical portfolios … rebalanced monthly through 12/31/13 and weekly thereafter with zero transaction costs"; critics: high turnover frictions and data-snooping "may offset gross returns", and "Zacks does not provide enough detail". https://www.zacks.com/p/p/7203 ; https://www.cxoadvisory.com/fundamental-valuation/are-zacks-rankings-exploitable/
- **[V] Seeking Alpha Quant.** Backtest since 2010: Strong Buy 26.97% annualized vs S&P 13.53%; SA itself: "The backtest and model is not an investable product". https://seekingalpha.com/article/4683185-university-of-kentucky-study-finds-seeking-alpha-quant-ratings-beat-the-market-sa-quant ; https://www.moneysmylife.com/seeking-alpha-quant-rating-review/
- **[E] Seeking Alpha *articles* did carry information.** Chen, De, Hu, Hwang (RFS 2014), ~100,000 articles 2005–12: article tone "predicted stock returns, as well as earnings surprises, above and beyond" analyst reports and news. https://thuifr.pbcsf.tsinghua.edu.cn/english/info/1013/1611.htm
- **[E] Sell-side rankings are largely popularity contests.** Emery & Li (JFQA 2009), II and WSJ rankings 1993–2005: "factors with a primary component of recognition are the most important determinants"; WSJ stars' next-year recommendations "significantly worse than those of nonstars". But skill persists when measured on outcomes: Mikhail, Walther, Willis (2004) find analysts whose past revisions earned the highest returns "continue to outperform". https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/are-the-wall-street-analyst-rankings-popularity-contests/2D0547EDA75D309D33A675E13518D363 ; https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2004/do-security-analysts-exhibit-persistent-differences-in-stock-picking
- **[V] TipRanks / StarMine.** TipRanks: 625,000+ recommendations, top analysts' "success rate … 67.6%" and +21% vs S&P over a decade while "an average analyst underperformed the S&P500 by 1.27%" (vendor). StarMine: most-accurate analysts "roughly four times as likely to remain among the most accurate" (vendor). https://www.tipranks.com/news/labs/tipranks-identifies-the-top-25-analysts-of-the-decade ; https://lipperalpha.refinitiv.com/2021/03/20-years-of-starmine-smartestimates-20-years-of-outperformance/

### Crowds scored against outcomes

- **[E] Estimize.** Jame, Johnston, Markov, Wolfe (JAR 2016), 51,012 forecasts: Estimize is "incrementally useful in forecasting earnings and measuring the market's expectations", stronger with more contributors; consensus revisions earn significant two-day returns. https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v54y2016i4p1077-1110.html
- **[E] Blind beats visible.** Da & Huang (Management Science): when users could see others' estimates the crowd beat Wall Street ~57% of the time; blind, 64%; Estimize went blind in Nov 2015. Independence is a design lever. https://olin.washu.edu/about/news-and-media/news/2019/03/olin-research-on-the-wisdom-of-crowds-making-the-bandwagon-better.php
- **[E] Motley Fool CAPS.** Avery, Chevalier, Zeckhauser (NBER 17298; Rev. Finance 2016), ~2.5m picks: long-top/short-bottom "produces a return of over 9 percent per annum", but "negative picks on the site strongly predict future stock price declines, while positive picks … produce returns that are statistically indistinguishable from the market". https://www.nber.org/digest/dec11/w17298.html
- **[E] Estimize's fate.** Acquired by ExtractAlpha in 2021 and sold as an institutional data feed; the consumer-facing crowd product did not become a retail business. https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/estimize-part-one-the-wisdom-of-crowdsourcing/

### Forecasting platforms and prediction-market calibration

- **[E] Metaculus:** community Brier ~0.111 across resolved questions; both community and Metaculus predictions "robustly outperform naive baselines"; accuracy varies by domain. https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/hqkyaHLQhzuREcXSX/data-on-forecasting-accuracy-across-different-time-horizons ; https://x.com/metaculus/status/1653417560216354818
- **[E, platform-published] Kalshi calibration:** page not fetchable (429); search excerpt of https://kalshi.com/research/publications/calibration: "Across 2,243,741 resolved Kalshi markets from 2021 to mid-2026 … Brier scores fall from about 0.09 at three months to below 0.02 at close"; calibration improves "near-monotonically with both event-level trading volume and the number of unique traders".
- **[E] Independent calibration study (arXiv 2602.19520):** Kalshi 64.7m trades / 210,608 contracts; Polymarket 288.7m trades / 218,000 contracts. "At long time horizons, prices in every domain move toward the favorite–longshot pattern"; politics "persistently compressed toward 50%"; sports near-calibrated inside 48h; "roughly half of the raw slope variation reflects estimation noise". https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19520v2
- **[V/E] Polymarket's "90% accurate" is a hit-rate, not calibration.** Polymarket's own write-up: 90.4% one month out, 88.5% one day out, and "Polymarket tends to overestimate event probabilities, but just by a couple of points"; critics note a 99% contract that misses counts the same as a 51% miss, and that e.g. contracts priced 67% resolved ~63% of the time (search excerpt of predictionnews.com). https://news.polymarket.com/p/how-accurate-is-polymarket ; https://predictionnews.com/news/study-reveals-nuance-behind-polymarket-90-percent-accuracy-rate/
- **[E] Macro prints: Kalshi vs consensus.** Fed Board FEDS paper "Kalshi and the Rise of Macro Markets" (Diercks, Katz, Wright, Feb 2026): Kalshi provides "a high-frequency, continuously updated, distributionally rich benchmark"; press coverage of the results: "a statistically significant edge over Bloomberg Consensus estimates for headline CPI" and improvements over fed funds futures for FOMC decisions. https://federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/kalshi-and-the-rise-of-macro-markets.htm ; https://www.yogonet.com/international/news/2026/03/20/118189-federal-reserve-study-highlights-kalshi-as-emerging-tool-for-macroeconomic-forecasting

### What users actually trust: score, reasoning, or citations?

- **[E] Reputation first, information quality last (robo-advice).** FPA Journal 2024 mixed-methods (n=86+10): "reputation of the firm" was the most significant trust driver; service quality second; "Information Quality (Lesser Impact)", though "participants expressed desire for more detailed output transparency". https://www.financialplanningassociation.org/learning/publications/journal/AUG24-customer-trust-and-satisfaction-robo-adviser-technology-OPEN
- **[E] Behaviour, not stated trust: OSC/BIT RCT (n=7,771 Canadians).** No material difference in adherence to AI vs human suggestions; people *did* discriminate quality: "participants who received a 'sound' suggestion deviated 74% less from the suggested allocation than those who received an 'unsound' suggestion". The experiment also notes prior work that "increased explanation of AI algorithms (e.g., what data is being used) was found to increase adoption". https://www.bi.team/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Artificial-Intelligence-and-Retail-Investing-Use-Cases-and-Experimental-Research-Final-Report.pdf
- **[E] Track records change behaviour, but by recognition as much as by skill** (Del Guercio & Tkac; Emery & Li above), and the mechanism that keeps a track record honest is *scoring against resolved outcomes* (CAPS, Estimize, GJP), not a displayed rating.

## Q3. "Show your work": citations, time-stamps, confidence, resolved histories and reliance

- **[E] Citations raise trust even when random; checking them lowers it.** Ding et al. (AAAI 2025): "a significant increase in trust when citations were present", holding "even when the citations were random"; and "a significant decrease in trust when participants checked the citations". https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01303
- **[E] Users mostly do not open references.** Study of reference presentation in ChatGPT/Bing/Google-style assistants: "users frequently do not engage with or verify the sources provided"; "the mere presence of references does not guarantee users will critically evaluate source reliability". https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.15326
- **[E] Review of hallucination detection in organization-backed AI advisors (Blanchard, Garvey, O'Laughlin, June 2026):** across product-search, medical and chatbot studies, "the cues used to prompt scrutiny of the AI response are predominantly related to the AI output, such as source citations"; "general warnings about the risk of hallucinations … show the weakest and most mixed effects"; "more targeted cues about where AI systems tend to fail appear promising"; skepticism and verification are rarely measured separately from reliance. (Search excerpt of the same paper: explanations alone raised reliance on both right and wrong answers; citations and visible inconsistencies reduced over-reliance.) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23491
- **[E] Explanations do not fix over-reliance by themselves; friction does.** Buçinca, Malaya, Gajos (CSCW 2021): cognitive forcing functions (decide first, then see the AI; delayed access) "significantly reduced overreliance on AI compared to … presenting explanations", with a trade-off: users prefer the less-effective designs. https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml
- **[E] Explanations help when they make verification cheap.** Vasconcelos et al. (CSCW 2023), five studies: "explanations can reduce over-reliance when they are easier to understand" and when performance is incentivised: a cost-benefit account of whether people bother to check. https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-overreliance-problem-are-explanations-solution
- **[E] The calibration gap.** Steyvers et al. (Nature Machine Intelligence 2025): "Users tend to overestimate the accuracy of LLM responses when provided with default explanations"; "Longer explanations increased user confidence, even when the extra length did not improve answer accuracy"; aligning explanations to the model's actual confidence narrowed both the calibration and discrimination gaps. https://www.socsci.uci.edu/newsevents/news/2025/2025-01-22-steyvers-uci-study-finds-mismatch-between-human-perception-and-reliability-of-ai-assisted-language-tools.php
- **[E] Uncertainty expressed in the first person reduces over-reliance and raises accuracy.** Kim et al. (FAccT 2024): "I'm not sure, but…" "decrease[s] participants' confidence in the system and tendency to agree with the system's answers, while increasing participants' accuracy". https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1030965
- **[E] Overreliance is under-measured.** Passi et al. (arXiv 2509.08010): overreliance is "relying on LLMs beyond their warranted capabilities"; remedies: calibrated uncertainty, friction "like an extra click", targeted education; they concede "the current lack of empirical evidence" that overreliance is being measured in deployed products. https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08010v2
- **[E] Finance-specific.** OSC/BIT RCT above (adherence equal across AI/human; quality discriminated). Narrative synthesis of consumer FinTech transparency studies: "Transparency cues are generally associated with more positive trust-related outcomes", but evidence is concentrated in robo-advisory. https://doi.org/10.3390/fintech5020041
- **[E] Resolved-forecast histories as a trust signal: no controlled consumer study found.** The closest evidence is indirect: calibration improves where forecasters receive prompt outcome feedback (Q1), and scored platforms (CAPS, Estimize, GJP) produce measurable skill. No 2023–26 study was found that shows *displaying* a resolved history to a retail user changes reliance appropriately. This is a gap, not a negative result.

## Q4. Prediction markets and tournaments as a product model for "views with due dates"

### Scale and composition (2025–26)

- **[E/V] Kalshi.** Sacra: "active traders growing 5x from 240K to 1.2M during 2025"; monthly volume $226m (Dec 2024) → $6.6bn (Dec 2025) → ~$30bn (Jun 2026); annualized revenue ~$4bn (Jul 2026); "Sports now represents 75% of Kalshi's volume" and ~80% of fee-generating volume; a liquidity bootstrap problem: "users avoid low-liquidity markets, which prevents those markets from developing sufficient liquidity". Kalshi distributed through Robinhood to >1m customers in 2025. https://sacra.com/c/kalshi/
- **[E] Combined volumes.** Kalshi + Polymarket ~$45bn in June 2026; Kalshi ~$19bn in the week of 5–11 Oct 2026. https://sccgmanagement.com/sccg-articles/2026/07/03/kalshi-polymarket-combined-volume-surges-75-45-billion-june/ ; https://defirate.com/news/kalshi-hits-record-19-billion-week-as-polymarket-us-triples-fall-trade-volume/

### Company and macro markets, and how they are phrased

- **[E] Polymarket earnings markets** launched 15 Sep 2025 with Stocktwits; contracts take the form "Will [Company] beat earnings for [Quarter] [Fiscal Year]?" relative to analyst consensus as of contract creation (e.g. BLSH at ~65% to beat EPS). https://www.blocmates.com/news-posts/stock-picks-or-side-bets-polymarket-launches-company-earnings-prediction-markets ; https://www.theblock.co/post/370819/polymarket-adds-company-earnings-forecasts
- **[E] Do they beat analysts?** Rabetti, Shao, Zhang (Apr 2026), 469 firm-quarter beat contracts Sep 2025–Feb 2026, ask "whether and, if so, why" Polymarket outperforms analysts (results paywalled on the summary site); Wolfe Research (via Bloomberg): when Polymarket priced a miss, firms missed 44% of the time "more than double the historic benchmark of 18%". https://www.cxoadvisory.com/fundamental-valuation/prediction-markets-are-better-than-humans-as-earnings-analysts/ ; https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-16/wall-street-quants-see-an-edge-in-polymarket-earnings-forecasts
- **[E] Kalshi earnings-call "mention" markets** (e.g. will Nvidia say "Hyperscaler", 99%; "Cosmos", 74%) show how far the product goes toward micro-resolvable, low-stakes questions. https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-earnings-prediction-market-preview-135133016.html
- **[E] Resolution rules in regulated form.** Kalshi's CFTC filings: CPI contracts settle on BLS CPI-U at the scheduled 8:30 ET release; "Revisions to the underlying data made after expiration will not be accounted for"; Rule 7.2 lets Kalshi amend specs when "an event affects the reliability of the source agency" (e.g. a shutdown delays the print). https://www.cftc.gov/filings/ptc/ptc03042412286.pdf ; https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules08202618974.pdf

### Question-writing guidelines (Metaculus, Good Judgment, tournament practice)

## Q5. Attempts to democratise hedge-fund research for retail

- **[E/V] Motley Fool Stock Advisor.** Track record is "a simple arithmetic average of these individual returns" since 2002 with each pick on its own clock; four early picks (Nvidia +128,583%, Netflix, Amazon, Disney) dominate; critics: "zero evidence and little methodology" behind the "beat the market by 4X" claim. Unit the user buys: a ticker recommendation. https://azat.tv/en/motley-fool-stock-advisor-performance-analysis/ ; https://liberatedstocktrader.com/motley-fool-review
- **[E] Motley Fool CAPS** (scored player calls) was informative, and only on the short side (Q2). Unit: a resolved directional call with a public score. https://www.nber.org/papers/w17298
- **[E/V] Seeking Alpha.** Articles carried information (Chen et al. 2014); the business then shifted to Quant ratings and "Alpha Picks" subscriptions whose figures are backtests ("not an investable product"). Unit: a thesis write-up, now sold as a rating. https://thuifr.pbcsf.tsinghua.edu.cn/english/info/1013/1611.htm ; https://seekingalpha.com/article/4683185-university-of-kentucky-study-finds-seeking-alpha-quant-ratings-beat-the-market-sa-quant
- **[E] Substack analysts.** Top 100 investing Substacks ≈$30m/yr; ≥52 newsletters earning >$0.5m/yr (2025); SemiAnalysis grew into a $100m institutional shop; "no barriers to entry and marketing skill may count for as much as content quality". No accountability mechanism beyond reputation. https://behindthebalancesheet.substack.com/p/the-substack-gold-rush-whos-winning ; https://pressgazette.co.uk/newsletters/biggest-substack-newsletters-2025/
- **[E] Unusual Whales / Quiver Quant (congress trackers).** Eggers & Hainmueller: "no evidence of either informed trading or above-market portfolio returns"; Congress "underperformed the market by 2-3% annually" 2004–08. NANC beat the S&P since Feb 2023 (≈30% vs 24% by early 2024) via mega-cap tech weightings, KRUZ lagged. Unit: someone else's disclosed trade; the signal is mostly a tech tilt. https://www.mit.edu/~jhainm/Paper/Eggmueller_CapitolLosses.pdf ; https://www.etf.com/sections/etf-basics/nanc-vs-kruz-battle-congress-stock-trackers
- **[V] Robinhood Cortex Digests** ("why is it moving" summaries from news, analyst notes, technicals, proprietary data): "95% of those surveyed in the US told us they love using the product … sources were relevant". Unit: a stock-level explanation of today's move; no claim is scored. https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/digests-by-robinhood-cortex-uk
- **[V] Public.com Alpha** (GPT-4, May 2023): screens, movers, news retrieval, earnings-call summaries "as soon as 2 hours after they air"; free. Unit: a chat answer. https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/96889/public-taps-gpt-4-for-investment-research-tool
- **[E] Fey** (Bloomberg-like terminal for retail, explicitly "does not offer investment recommendations"): acquired by Wealthsimple in 2025 and wound down as a standalone by 30 Sep 2025. Data-first research terminals for retail did not sustain a business. https://www.fintech.ca/2025/08/27/wealthsimple-acquires-fey-launch-features/
- **[E] eToro copy trading:** raises risk-taking (Apesteguia et al. 2020, Q1). Unit: another person's P&L.
- **[V/E] Alpha Theory (pros):** the unit is a *probability-weighted price target per position*; having one and refreshing it is associated with higher ROIC (Q1). https://www.alphatheory.com/blog/alpha-theory-2025-year-in-review-position-sizing-as-a-persistent-edge
- **[E] Estimize:** scored crowd estimates were measurably useful (JAR 2016) and improved when made blind; the product survived as institutional data, not as a retail habit.

**Pattern across Q5 [I]:** products whose unit was *scored against an outcome* (CAPS, Estimize, GJP, prediction markets) produced measurable information; products whose unit was a *rating or a story* (Fool averages, Zacks/SA backtests, Digests, finfluencer posts) produced engagement and marketing claims, and the evidence that they help the user is weak or vendor-supplied. The scored products also failed commercially as retail businesses (Estimize sold, CAPS marginal) while the story products scaled, which is the honest warning for Hivemind's distribution.

## Synthesis (15 lines)

1. **(a) Mistakes addressed, with evidence.** Attention/narrative buying: retail buys what is in the news and Robinhood's top lists, and it reverses 5–9% within a month [E: Barber & Odean 2008; Barber et al. 2022]; a workflow that converts a ticker-in-the-news into "which debate moves this stock, by when, at what number" replaces the attention trigger with a checkable claim.
2. Overconfidence: the one debiasing step with replicated lab evidence is forcing reasons *against* and considering the opposite [E: Koriat 1980; Lord 1984; pre-mortem Veinott 2010]; a ladder of outcomes with the bear rungs written down is that step, built in.
3. Disposition effect / no exit rule: ex-ante commitment reduces it, reminders do not [E: Fischbacher 2017]; a due date with a frozen outcome is the commitment, and the stream's refusal to re-litigate after the date is what makes it bite [I].
4. No feedback: day traders lose for years without learning [E: Barber et al.]; experts who get prompt outcome feedback become calibrated [E: Murphy & Winkler; GJP]; resolved outcomes are the missing feedback loop.
5. Using the LLM for the wrong job: retail who use ChatGPT for forecasting do worse (β=-0.511), those who use it for data analysis and risk framing do better [E: mf-journal 2025]; ungrounded LLMs are over-optimistic and lose the bias when grounded in local data [E: HBS 2026]. Hivemind's grounding layer is the measured fix; its job is to stop the model free-forecasting.
6. **(b) Best-evidenced trust mechanism: a scored track record on resolved outcomes, kept honest by design.** Ratings and ranks demonstrably move behaviour [E: Del Guercio & Tkac; TipRanks/StarMine persistence claims] but decay or become popularity contests when the score is detached from outcomes [E: Value Line; Emery & Li; Fool averaging]. Crowd platforms that scored every call against the print produced real information [E: CAPS; Estimize; GJP; Kalshi/Polymarket macro and earnings markets].
7. Citations alone are a weak and even perverse signal: they raise trust when random and lower it when checked, and most users never click them [E: Ding 2025; arXiv 2604.15326]. Time-stamped evidence is necessary for resolution but is not itself what earns trust.
8. Reasoning/explanations raise reliance on wrong answers unless they make verification cheap; friction (decide first, then see) and honest first-person uncertainty are what reduce over-reliance [E: Buçinca 2021; Vasconcelos 2023; Kim 2024; Steyvers 2025].
9. Calibration displays work only if the number is real: users over-trust confident, long explanations, and aligning stated confidence to actual accuracy narrows the gap [E: Steyvers 2025]. A resolved-history page is the one calibration display that cannot be faked after the fact [I].
10. The answer to "why trust it over GPT": GPT has no resolved record, no due date and no frozen outcome; it is a sampler that inherits retail's own biases [E: AEA 2026; HBS 2026]. The product's defensible claim is not "better data" but "every view is scored, and you can see the score before you lean on it" [I].
11. Users still weight firm reputation and convenience above information quality [E: FPA 2024], and adhere to AI and human suggestions equally while discriminating sound from unsound advice [E: OSC/BIT RCT n=7,771]; so the record must be visible at the moment of decision, not in a settings page [I].
12. **(c) Honest limits.** No controlled study shows that *showing* a resolved history to a retail user improves their trades; the evidence is indirect (feedback calibrates forecasters; scored platforms carry information). This claim must be measured on Hivemind's own data, not asserted.
13. Retail will still size badly, trade too often and chase the next meme in the 75–80% of attention that goes to sports-like action [E: Kalshi mix; 0DTE losses]; a views database changes the quality of a decision it is consulted on, not the decision to consult it. Copy and social features would make this worse [E: Apesteguia 2020].
14. Resolution is the product's liability: misaligned or ambiguous questions blow up even on public events [E: Zelenskyy suit; Venezuela "invasion"; NK missile]; the checkable ladder must name the source, the exact field, the absolute date and the fallback, in code, before a view is accepted.
15. Commercial risk: the scored-unit products (Estimize, CAPS, Fey-style terminals) did not survive as retail businesses while story-and-rating products scaled; the record must be the hook, delivered inside the chat the user already has, or it will be correct and unused [I].
