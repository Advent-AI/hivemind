**The design is not ready.** It keeps the frozen files intact, but it chooses a different source of evidence from the one stated in D658, and some answer choices cannot safely be scored by its proposed yes/no rule.

For example, a question can have “Yes,” “Ruled out,” and “Still open” as three different outcomes. Treating either of the last two as the same “No” can count an answer as right even when the evidence supports only one of them.

1. **Alex’s choice and the frozen pieces.** D658 opens a new answer reference for the 15 questions the new system actually asked, with independent readers, source-based resolution, Alex’s signature, and one subsequent scoring. The design stays within that population and proposes separate files, so it does not itself edit the frozen gold, packet, or scorer. However, D658 also says the new reference is written from the **complete material the judge is shown**. The design instead gives readers the frozen 309-item packet and withholds the judge’s evidence. Those instructions are in direct conflict and need resolution before readers start. [D658](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/DECISIONS-log.md:9208), [design](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/research/2026-11-14-use-acceptance/stage-b-controls/NEW-REFERENCE-DESIGN.md:38).

   There is also a potential weakening in the scoring rule: the design calls the existing mapping of both “Ruled out” and “Still open” to “No” unchanged, but that mapping can collapse distinct outcomes in the new questions. Keeping the files untouched does not by itself keep the comparison meaningful. [Design](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/research/2026-11-14-use-acceptance/stage-b-controls/NEW-REFERENCE-DESIGN.md:22), [existing comparison](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step4-2026-11-10/stage_b/control_theses.py:101).

2. **The material.** The frozen packet is appropriate for asking: *given the same fixed 14 days of analyst material, does the system’s final answer cover an independent analyst’s view?* It keeps the reference stable across runs and can reveal a disagreement when the judge received the decisive material. It cannot establish that the judge was wrong based only on what it actually saw unless that material-shown condition is met. The frozen ruler expressly chose the packet rather than the changing system ledger. [Frozen ruler](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/research/2026-11-01-e2e-loop/judgement-gold/ruler-v1.md:13).

   Using the **complete evidence shown to the judge** asks a different question: *was its answer sound given its own inputs?* It cannot detect the effect of relevant material omitted before judging, and the reference can change with the inputs in each draw. D658’s recorded decision specifies this choice, while its option also says “the same material”; the design should surface that conflict for resolution instead of silently selecting the packet. [D658](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/DECISIONS-log.md:9210), [D658](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/DECISIONS-log.md:9221).

3. **The reference rule.** Independent A and B readings, source-based rulings on every disagreement, and Alex resolving splits fit the new-gold requirement. But automatically replacing B with A whenever both third readers prefer A is **not** the D262 precedent: Alex chose B’s answers verbatim and retained A as the comparison record. D658 requires disagreements to be ruled; it does not specify this automatic replacement. The design should identify that as a new choice for Alex to sign, rather than attribute it to D262. [AGENTS §7.1](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/AGENTS.md:282), [D262](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/DECISIONS-log.md:3063), [design](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/research/2026-11-14-use-acceptance/stage-b-controls/NEW-REFERENCE-DESIGN.md:47).

   I would retain B as the proposed reference and present **all** disagreements and the third readers’ reasons to Alex for the final choice. This preserves his earlier choice while satisfying the requirement to examine disagreements. Taking the overlap of two ranges would create a narrower third answer neither reader necessarily endorsed; a third reading of every agreement could add review, but does not resolve the authority question.

4. **Fitting risk.** Blinding the two readers is necessary but insufficient. The questions and stored answers already exist; the builder, signing material, third readers, material-shown readers, and scorer could leak or use the stored answers when selecting or revising a reference. The design only explicitly restricts the two initial readers and says Claude does not see judgments until scoring; it does not establish the same boundary for every participant or Alex’s signing packet. [Design](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/research/2026-11-14-use-acceptance/stage-b-controls/NEW-REFERENCE-DESIGN.md:40), [design](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/research/2026-11-14-use-acceptance/stage-b-controls/NEW-REFERENCE-DESIGN.md:84).

   Fix the question set and source material mechanically, keep stored answer and chosen-rung data outside every reference-building, reconciliation, and signing packet, and record that access boundary. Freeze the signed reference and scoring rules before exposing answers. The material-shown readers can see the relevant evidence and reference items, but should not see the chosen answer; the existing brief already uses that separation. Preserve all revisions and apply corrections only as separately printed records. [Existing brief](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/step4-2026-11-10/stage_b/control-reader-brief.md:6), [D261](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/DECISIONS-log.md:3056).
