### Conflicts and tensions inside the request

- **Role paragraph:** No direct contradiction. A main annual question can be an important question raised by an item, supported by evidence. “Never open a thesis only to give a statement a home” still rules out opening one merely because a figure appears.
- **CONDITION:** No direct contradiction. A financial figure can be recorded as a condition and inform a revenue or earnings question; the fifth reason can also allow a qualifying annual question. The wording about an **open** thesis does not prohibit a new one: the next sentence explicitly says “open or new.”
- **One quantity, one period:** No direct contradiction, but the growth example exposes an ambiguity. A level and its growth may be two ways to ask about the same result, rather than two separately bettable outcomes.
- **Date rule:** There is a real instruction gap. The new reason says the figure’s release date settles it, but the general date paragraph does not list `main_driver` among the reasons whose date it fixes. It starts “Give the date the item supports” and ends by rejecting a question no date can settle. An item may identify FY revenue without giving its publication day; the schema does not supply that day.
- **Another estimate, source or wording:** Compatible in principle. It prevents a revised forecast or another phrasing of an already tracked result from becoming another question. It does not explicitly show that **level versus growth** can be the same underlying result.
- **Annual quantity after next year:** Compatible with “current or next fiscal year.” Both reject an annual main-driver question beyond that window. The request should not reinterpret a later-year forecast as a current-year forecast.
- **`closest_open_thesis` and `why_a_separate_bet`:** Compatible and useful for an existing question. They require a reason the answers can move apart. They do not explicitly require the same independent-bet comparison **between two new questions**: the closing check checks same underlying question among new proposals, but its “cannot move apart” test names only an open thesis.
- **Closing check on figures and conditions:** The instruction not to drop a reported figure merely because it was retold concerns *evidence*, not automatic question opening. The same-fact row combines a reported measure and its growth in one evidence item; it does not decide whether two research questions are independently meaningful. The final cash-flow check applies only to **unlinked company figures**; a figure linked to a newly opened question does not receive that particular check. The earlier general evidence test still applies. This makes the decision to open a question consequential for what survives.
- **Closing check on reasons:** Changing four to five is internally consistent. It does not say that the new fifth reason replaces the other four.

### Examples

The new examples use generic bank, retailer and airline descriptions. They name no covered company, product or number, and draw on several industries. Their measures are supported by row A, with the qualification that the retailer example changes “same-store sales” to “same-store sales **growth**.”

They fit the schema’s ability to express a question, outcome, period, reason and settling material, but **the examples themselves do not supply a release date or identify the public document**. They are measure examples, not complete valid output examples. The airline example agrees with the standard but exceeds the clearly stated revenue-and-margin test.

### Specific risks and smallest wording changes

These are risks in how the request can be read, not claims that the model will make these mistakes.

**(a) Every statement line, including EPS, net income, cash flow or a small segment.** The limiting words are “the annual quantity that carries the company’s value,” “not every line” and “only matters an analyst would bet on separately.” They make indiscriminate opening contrary to the text, but “annual quantity” leaves the model to decide whether any of those measures carries value. The fuel-cost example makes the category visibly broader than revenue and margin.

For the D490 test, the smallest scope correction is to replace **“the annual quantity”** with **“the company’s annual revenue or margin”**, and remove or replace examples outside that scope. Retain the existing importance and separate-bet tests. This is a restriction to the approved test, not a claim that the signed standard excludes other main drivers.

**(b) A quarter or a year after next fiscal year.** The explicit “current or next fiscal year,” quarter exclusion and “annual quantity after next year” oppose both. No additional prohibition is needed. A missing fiscal year is a separate problem: the model should not infer one from a quarterly figure or a distant forecast.

**(c) Two questions for level and growth, or total and main segment.** “Same-store sales growth” alongside “revenue” and “gross margin,” combined with “one quantity,” can make the same result look like two different measures. A main segment is explicitly eligible, while the separate-bet check against **new** questions is less explicit than against open ones.

The smallest sourced clarification is: **“Compare each proposed question with open and other proposed questions: if the same result settles both, keep one; separate them only if their answers can move apart and an analyst would bet on them separately.”** This follows §4’s “同一个结果能判掉两题，就是同一题” and its separate-bet test. It does **not** categorically merge total and segment: §4 says to judge the actual material, and a segment can have independent drivers.

**(d) Another company’s figures.** For this new reason, “the company” is defined as `current_entity`, and “the company’s value” points back to it. The condition field also excludes other companies’ figures. Those words constrain the new category; an inadvertent opening remains possible because evidence can describe another party, but the candidate does not instruct it. A minimal clarification, if needed, is **“the current entity’s own annual revenue or margin.”** Note that the signed standard separately permits proposing another company’s own question subject to its tests; this candidate’s current-entity scope is narrower, so do not generalize this restriction beyond the requested test.

**(e) The other four reasons stop opening.** There is no explicit instruction to stop them: the five reasons are alternatives and all four remain in the schema. The broad financial prohibition applies to a financial measure **on its own**, while the other categories have their own criteria. A model could nevertheless overapply that row to an obligation or shared quantity. The smallest clarification is **“This financial-figure row does not exclude a matter that independently meets another reason.”** That restates the existing closed-set structure rather than adding a new opening reason.

### What is missing to write the question

- **Measure and scope:** The schema has `description` and `outcome`, but neither explicitly asks for the exact measure and scope—such as total revenue versus a named segment, or which margin. Section 4 requires “算谁、不算谁、算什么写清楚.” The answer can express this, but the new instruction does not make the distinction explicit.
- **Fiscal year:** The new reason requires current or next fiscal year, and `period` can carry it. It does not tell the model what to do if the source does not identify a supported fiscal year. Section 4 requires the subject, measure and time to be supported by the source; it should not invent one.
- **Settling material:** Present: `settled_by` requires public material, and the new sentence says the publication of the figure, usually the annual report. The document should actually publish the specified measure and scope.
- **Date:** The *kind* of date is present—the figure’s release date—but a supported actual day may be absent. The schema’s `period` allows null and combines period with check date; `settled_by` is only a string. The request does not resolve how to supply an unknown future release date without inventing it. Section 4 requires a date and public material, and permits an analyst-set date only in the specified cases; it does not provide that fallback for the annual main driver.
