[Item] id gnews:2e603d9182199771803bce9b, category T7, author ca.finance.yahoo.com (media), published 2026-09-27, title: China chipmaking stocks tumble as Beijing reportedly mulls allowing Nvidia sales

Investing.com-- Chinese chipmaking stocks fell sharply on Monday after a report said Beijing was considering allowing some local firms to buy NVIDIA Corp's (NASDAQ:NVDA) advanced chips.
The Information reported that China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology asked domestic firms, including Alibaba and ByteDance, to report how many Nvidia RTX Pro 5500 units they planned to purchase and what they would use them for.
The questioning signaled an intent to eventually approve the sales, The Information reported.
Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corp (HK:0981), China's biggest chipmaker by volume, slid 3.7%, while rival Hua Hong Semiconductor Ltd (HK:1347) shed nearly 5%.
Moore Threads Technology Co Ltd (SS:688795), regarded by many as China's answer to Nvidia, slid 6.3%, while Cambricon Technologies Corp Ltd (SS:688256) fell 5.7%.
Chipmaking equipment maker NAURA Technology Group Co Ltd (SZ:002371) lost 3.4%, while memory chip major CXMT Corp (SS:688825) fell nearly 4%. Chipmakers lagged broader gains in Chinese and Hong Kong markets.
The RTX Pro 5500 is a Blackwell-generation professional workstation GPU released by Nvidia earlier this month. The chip, while capable of heavy AI computing tasks, still sits outside the AI accelerator category of chips the U.S. has largely blocked from being sold to China.
The Information report comes after the U.S. and China held a summit in Washington and agreed to better trade relations, including a $30 billion exemption on tariffs both ways.
But the two did not discuss AI chip sales.
The approval of Nvidia chip sales in China presents heightened competition for local chipmakers, who are racing to produce comparable processors amid a storm of regulatory headwinds. China's homegrown chipmaking processes are still seen as years behind the advanced technology found in the U.S. and Taiwan.
Beijing was seen leaning heavily into developing more homegrown AI chips, with several major and upcoming players– including CXMT and Moore Threads– logging bumper stock market debuts over the past year.
