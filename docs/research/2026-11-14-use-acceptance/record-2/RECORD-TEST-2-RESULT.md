## Council (thread mx, round 13; `clean-arch/step1-unfiled-2026-11-08/council/mx-challenge-12.md`, `mx-codex-13.md`): consensus

- The formal outcomes stay on record as the rule gives them (c8 met, c22 undecided) and are **not a ranking**: the fresh Codex
  comparer marked the same two readings wrong in c8's reader too, and the rule used the fresh marks only where the first pair
  differed. The comparison is reported as **not separated**; neither an advantage nor equivalence is established.
- **c22 is not adopted, and no further wording or field arm follows.** c8 stays the adopted request.
- The $411 billion and the 91.2% lines: treating "this year" (2026) as fiscal 2027, or "Q3 2026" as the fiscal third quarter of
  2027, is an inference the articles do not state; a record must leave such a period as written.
- What the test supports: on these forty articles the adopted request kept figures from which both readers could state the tested
  guide, quarter, margin path and market estimate. It does not show the kept figures are complete, or that a table gives a reader
  what a tracked question with a judgement gives.
- The council's correction to Claude's proposed next step, accepted: a grouping step may join only what the stored evidence
  establishes (the same source under another spelling, a period the article writes out), keeps the article's wording and source, and
  leaves an uncertain period unresolved; **a quarter is never derived from the article's day**, since an article may speak of a past,
  current or coming quarter or of a calendar year.
- To Alex, one choice: whether to test a financial record as something the product shows. If yes, the first test is $0, on the stored
  answers of these forty articles, read by fresh readers: does it reduce the reader's work without merging different claims. If no,
  the lean path returns to its choice of system with D489 unresolved.

## Lessons

1. **A field built for a fault found on chosen cases is tested first where a reader would meet the fault.** c21 and c22 were probed
   on cases picked for wrong parties (8.2% against 2.1%); on articles drawn by seed the fault did not reach a reader of c8's record.
   Three arms (c20, c21, c22) cost $2.56 before this $1.07 test asked the reader-level question.
2. **Settle a disputed part for every set, not only the set whose first marks differed.** A stricter fresh reader then weighs on one
   record and not the other for the same reading. The fresh pair re-marks the whole part, and the rule uses those marks for all sets.
3. **A question that asks for "every" item needs its answer key fixed before the readers** (the comparers listed four, five, eight
   and nine pairs for Q6): otherwise it measures the comparers.
4. **What made the record hard to use is the same fact under many names**, in both arms: an identity matter for code on what the
   evidence establishes, not for more words in the reading request, and never by guessing a period from the article's day.
