**My verdict: both the product and the measuring method are holding the project back.** The records show real cases where important information never reached an answer, and where an answer asked the wrong question. They also show that some gates are sensitive to a particular question’s wording, unresolved reader disagreements, or an empty starting library. Those gates cannot yet tell Alex which whole system better serves an analyst.

For example, when stored facts about custom chips were added to the judgement request, the answer moved off its lowest range in **9 of 9 sends**; without those facts it stayed there in **9 of 9**. That is direct evidence that missing information can affect the product’s answer. It is not proof that either complete system is ready for production. The use-oriented trial exposed further gaps. Its readers shared only **7 of 12** listed matters; its planted wrong answers repeated requests in the same packet; and its missing-fact check had **no known positive control**. Both readers said no missing fact would change an answer, but the test could not distinguish that finding from readers who always say “no.” In the fresh week, one reader rejected **two of four** independently confirmed supported controls. These are measured limitations, not reasons to assume every reader disagreement is an error. A useful system could fail the matching gate because it tracks the same business matter in a different shape or period. Conversely, an answer could overlap a reference range by luck, or be supported by the incomplete facts it was shown while missing a decisive fact elsewhere. The scorer’s own definition makes that last distinction necessary. The use-oriented reading is closest to the product’s purpose because it asks what an analyst received, but its controls and coverage are not yet sufficient to certify reliability. **Conditions matter.** The July end-to-end census compared three draws per system on the same July 1–14 news, unseeded and from empty libraries; its seven graded questions are a subset, not the product’s full research universe. The fresh September 22–28 comparison also started empty and had only one draw per system. The October 6–12 run processed 272 articles and three answer-choice attempts per topic, but did not run matching or judging. The current frame records that a product-oriented test should start with a library established from real earlier material; the empty-start results cannot establish performance in that condition. **A sound next measurement:** build a compact, dated “research record” from existing stored articles, library snapshots, links, exact judgement requests and answers. For a fixed sample of important matters, show Alex, on one page each: the question as it stood that day; the material that arrived; the answer and its reasons; source passages; and material that was available but did not reach the answer. Carry the same matter forward on subsequent days and preserve prior readings, so a later run adds evidence instead of resetting the assessment. Include cases where a question was never opened.

Before using it for a pass line, mix in independently checked sound and wrong answers, a known consequential missing-fact **group** such as the custom-chip case, and irrelevant or already-delivered facts. Have two readers assess them independently; repeat a sample and report disagreement, missed planted errors and false alarms separately. This would establish **observed** error rates on those controls, not a universal error rate. A reasonable first packet would be 10–15 matters for Alex to read in under an hour, but that timing is a proposal requiring a timed trial; the existing 25-minute figure was for model readers, not Alex. Stored-data preparation can avoid model charges; human review time and any new-reader charges have not been priced. It would not establish coverage of all news, future investment returns, or general performance across companies. **Alex’s three questions:**

1. **What are we trying to reach?** A research record an analyst can trust and follow as new information arrives, with an understandable, supported answer and its sources. I would change that description if Alex’s current product decision narrows the intended use or excludes an accumulating question library. [Clean PRD]( /Users/lchingen/hivemind/clean-arch/PRD.md:7)

2. **Is the measuring method part of the problem?** Yes: reader disagreement, shape matching, weak positive controls and empty starting libraries limit what the current results can decide. I would revise that conclusion if independently checked controls and repeated real-library readings showed low error at the decision thresholds.

3. **Why are we stuck, and how do we break through?** Real delivery and question-writing failures coexist with tests that often cannot say how much those failures hurt the final reader. The next useful step is the small accumulating record above, tested for reader error before it is used to choose a system; a contrary result would be a controlled product-path comparison that already demonstrates a clear winner on those conditions.
