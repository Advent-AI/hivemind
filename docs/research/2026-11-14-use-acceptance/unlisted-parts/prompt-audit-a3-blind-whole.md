The request has two clear conflicts that can cause the reported errors: it overstates what belongs in `not_judged`, and it forces company figures into fiscal years even when the topic names a calendar year.

- **H — contradiction.** The system says, “List every other part of the topic’s description and outcome that the question leaves out,” and the **closing check** repeats “every other part.” The **`not_judged` schema** says the list is empty only when the question leaves nothing out. Those instructions can treat delay and termination as omitted even though the event’s outcomes already account for them. The acquisition’s **payload `outcome`** names both, while `settled_by` names the documents used to check them. D397 and D436 require omitted parts and a second side of the same bet to be shown; D409 already judges the event’s alternatives. The request does not distinguish those from omitted parts or checking material.

- **Y — contradiction.** The system’s **“What the question asks”** says a figure “names the period it covers,” but **“The ladder”** requires “FY2027 for a company’s figure,” and the **`figure.year` schema** says a company figure uses “its fiscal year” and that “Code refuses the other form.” Thus a topic asking for calendar 2027 is pulled toward FY2028. D437 concerns separating years in topic opening; it does not authorize replacing the period the topic names.

- **XR — abstract.** The system’s **`reference` instruction** says to carry “what the market expects: a consensus, the company’s guidance, the stated plan or deadline.” The **`reference.kind` schema** compresses that to “what the market expects.” Neither illustrates an approximate, spoken range as an expectation or plan. The **closing check** checks the value *if a reference exists*, but does not check whether a stated reference was omitted. Whether the CEO’s statement qualifies as an expectation depends on its surrounding text, which is not in this request; the quote alone does not establish why the model omitted it.

- **W — missing.** The system’s **“The due day”** says the due day is “on or after the day the material that settles the question is published,” and the **closing check** says it “follows the rules above.” There is no example showing that a fiscal-year annual report arrives *after* fiscal year-end. This is a plausible gap, not proof of the cause in the reported answers: their actual question and due-day text were not supplied.

- **N — abstract.** The system says to “decline only when no part is the main one,” and the **`no_ladder` schema** says “several with none the main one.” The examples show cases with a main part but do not define how to select it when several parts compete. D397 declines only when there is no main part; D436 keeps two sides of one bet together. The declined topics and reasons are not supplied, so this cannot establish whether a particular refusal was a misreading or came from another refusal rule.

One further conflict: the system says a figure’s due day must follow publication, but also says a binding obligation’s due day is **at most 548 days** after the item. If the next annual filing falls later, both cannot be met. The **payload’s `fiscal_year_note`** also directs “use that day as the due date” for FY2027, which can conflict with a topic tracking a different period.
