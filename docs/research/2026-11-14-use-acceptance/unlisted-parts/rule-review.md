The decisions give a **limited distinction**, but not a general procedure for choosing a main part whenever an outcome names several things. D397 says to judge “the part its `outcome` names” and says a topic with “no main part gets no judged question.” It does not say that the first-mentioned part, a consequence, a comparison, or the description always wins.

D436 says “a second side of the same bet … is one question” and that it “stays as context … and is shown as not judged.” Its examples include “the shortage beside the shipments.” That supports keeping a second side as context when it is already identifiable as such; it does **not** provide a general test for deciding which of two equally stated parts is main. D417 supplies a separate refusal rule for certain non-annual figures without a publication day; it does not choose between competing parts of an outcome.
