# The boundary: is an announced "not by the due day" a ruling out? Worked cases (council round 108)

## The mechanism, from the model's own account (`taiwan-account.json`; $0.0007; a lead, not proof)

- On Taiwan p3 the step followed the request's instruction: "decision is one full sentence that names them and says what they decide
  against the event as a whole, or that they formally abandon or withdraw it".
- It set aside "formally abandons or withdraws the proposed tightening": "the topic and item do not identify a proposal or plan that
  Taiwan could abandon or withdraw".
- It then wrote the question's own deadline into the decision ("will not adopt … by January 31, 2027"). It names the clearer wording
  as "formally decides not to tighten the controls".
- The 37 company-delay answers of the frozen step have the same shape: the question's deadline is carried into the decision.
  The account does not show that the same mechanism produced them (council round 109).

## The rules that bear on it

- **D409:** an event has three outcomes: it happened by the due day, it is ruled out, or neither.
- **D416 (Alex's question):** "把“到期还没发生”写成“明确落空”". The fix is a typed decision: who decides, what they decide.
- **D420, for an authority's proceeding that seeks a published conclusion:** "its still going on is the wait".
- **The brief's R row:** a decision "against that event as a whole … and not the event's merely being still awaited".
- **D54:** at the due day a question freezes at the rung it stands on and closes.

## What each reading costs on the card (a reader's terms)

- **Reading 1:**
  - The card says "ruled out" for a product that slipped past the date and for one that was dropped alike. A reader cannot tell a
    delay from a cancellation without opening the evidence.
  - A deadline bet lost by a slip shows as lost, which is how an analyst who held "ships by 2026" would book it.
- **Reading 2:**
  - The card says "still open" at its close for a slip the company announced.
  - A reader sees no decision, although one was made, and the question still closes without having happened.
  - It matches the brief's words "not the event's merely being still awaited" and D420. It turns most of the frozen step's 37
    company-delay rungs into line-3 failures.

## What the readers do today

- They split by kind: mostly reading 1 for a company's product, reading 2 for a deal's closing and an authority's rule-making.
- No rule in the records makes that distinction. So the same sentence shape passes or fails depending on the decider.
- Neither reading is settled by a decision of Alex's.

## For the council

**My recommendation: change nothing now, record both readings, and put the question on Alex's page with these cases.**

- Writing reading 1 into the brief would turn the frozen step's one fresh line-3 failure into a pass. That is the trap of relaxing the
  form on reader judgment, and the brief's present words lean the other way.
- Writing reading 2 into the brief would make about 37 stored answers fail line 3, and would call for a repair of the ladder step,
  on a meaning no decision settles.
- Both are product choices about what the card says. The cheaper, reversible course is the present form, with the split shown in
  the two countings.
- Gate reports list these 38 answers (37 company delays and Taiwan p3) as **candidates affected by an unsettled boundary**,
  beside their original marks. They are not 38 unresolved rulings: the readers agreed on many of them, so the two countings
  alone do not show the whole disagreement (round 109).
- **My lean for Alex's page is reading 1.**
  - It books a deadline bet as lost when the decider announces a slip past the date.
  - It matches D416's typed decision and the readers' majority.
  - Its cost (a delay and a cancellation look alike on the card) is stated beside it.
