# Topics declined for "several figures, none the main one": the boundary, read again with the new week (D555, under D439), 2026-11-15, $0

The council's question: among the topics the answer-choice step declines for having no main part, is there a general,
evidence-backed change, consistent with Alex's rule (D397) and with D436, that recovers a question on at least two distinct
worthwhile matters while keeping the topics that should stay declined? Read from stored material only: the twelve cases of the
earlier boundary check (D469, D470; `clean-arch/e2e-comparison-2026-11-13/ladder-refusal/`, two readers, one of them blind), the
earlier source check of July's refusals (D476, D477; `reachability/RESULT.md`), this week's reading (`RESULT.md`) and the stored
attempts of every run. No model was asked anything; no file of a frozen piece was touched.

## The answer

**No candidate meets the condition, among those considered; that does not prove no future design exists.** On the topics
two readers call "none main" the step declines every time; on the topics they call "one main" it answers four attempts of
five, so on that side the line is not applied steadily. What is lost on
worthwhile matters is lost by the rule itself (a topic that really asks two separately tracked things gets no question), and
the two ways to recover it both break a guard: taking one of the two things discards a bet an analyst holds apart (the
ordering rule D469 refused), and writing a question for each thing also writes questions for the topics that should never
have been opened.

## 2. What the table shows

- **On the agreed no-main cases the step applies the rule as the readers do; on the agreed one-main cases, not steadily.**
  The five topics both earlier readers call "none main" were declined in
  every stored attempt (27 of 27: three attempts each for the four July topics, fifteen over five runs for the one of
  Alex's); the four they both call "one main" were declined in 9 of 48 attempts over
  five runs. This week adds two topics of the first kind (declined 6 of 6) and one of the second (declined 2 of 3).
- **A topic that names two years was declined every time.** Over all 104 stored topics three name two years in their own
  wording (this week's two, and one of Alex's): 9 of 9 attempts declined. On Alex's one the two earlier readers split on
  whether it has a main part, so "two years" is not by itself "none main" in the readers' eyes.
- **Which of the always-declined topics lose something worth having:** the cloud companies' spending (this week) and the
  2026 memory price (July); the federal obligations are disputed; global AI spending is doubtful; the Intel stake and the
  Firmus investment are the two the readers said should never have been opened. So in these cases two worthwhile matters are lost to the
  rule outright, and a further one or two topics a run are decided by chance. That describes the stored cases; it is not a
  rate: the 104 topics include repeated runs of the same news and populations read in different ways.

## 4. What follows from it

- The refusals of true no-main topics are the rule working, with the cost Alex was told of when he chose it. On the news read so far it has fallen on
  two worthwhile matters, one of them central (the cloud companies' spending this week).
- The guards are kept today by accident: the two unworthy topics happen to ask two things. Any change that makes the system
  answer more two-thing topics has to face first that the first step opens topics an analyst would not hold a card for. That
  is the proposed line "at most one in five questions trivia or duplicate" of the use-oriented acceptance, not a matter of
  this step.
- Nothing here is a candidate to send. What remains is a product choice no decision yet makes: when one matter carries two
  figures an analyst tracks apart (two years of the same spending), is that one topic with no judged question (today), two
  topics, or one topic with two judged questions. Alex named the third as the gap when he chose the first.
