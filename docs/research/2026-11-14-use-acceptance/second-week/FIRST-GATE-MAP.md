## 4. What the record supports as a direction, and what it does not

- **The count by written choices has one class on the record that is this step's alone and large in one reading:** an event's
  reference left empty. Without it, and with nothing else changed, July's 22 would be at most 27 of 40 against 26: a paper
  upper bound, not a predicted result. It is two matters in one population, one attempt this week and two in Alex's topics;
  and the field it concerns is one the judge is never shown for an event, so removing the fault would move the count and
  nothing a reader of the product sees. It has never been put to a prompt audit. Reference marks on figures are a separate
  matter (a baseline amount, not an expectation in words) and are kept apart.
- **Unlisted parts** are the whole shortfall of the fresh week (three topics) and part of this week's; the audit of D547
  stands as it was left.
- **The refusals by rule** (no main part, a period that is not a year, no listed theme) account for 8 or 9 of the 13 short
  topics among Alex's 44, 10 of July's 18 and 2 or 3 of this week's 6. Every one has a decision behind it or a stopped line, and a
  good part of them are topics that should not be answered at all: the survey (D417), the two stakes, the background sentence.
  An answerable but unworthy question is not a recovery.
- **The zero-fault lines have no bounded direction on this record.** Each reading carries 6 to 8 agreed faults on them (on 5% to
  29% of its attempts), of different kinds each time, on a step that was tuned through three readings and then frozen. Nothing
  stored shows a set of changes that would bring all four to zero at once. This is the part of the gate that the record does
  not show within reach.
- **The form itself leaves many marks unsettled:** 12 to 39 open points a reading; the due day is the largest kind in three of
  the four (in July it is whether a refusal was justified). Until that is settled, every reading has two answers, and the one
  with the open points against has never passed.
