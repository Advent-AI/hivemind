# Can a same-news product comparison from established libraries decide anything? (D610's review), 2026-11-16, $0

**Answer: not within what is left.** It closes by D610's own condition.

1. **Power.** D517's sizing (`comparison/COMPARISON-SIZING.md`) stands. A week ends with about 7 questions on the new system and 15 on
   the old.
   - The shapes about $9 buys detect a difference of about 31 points or more in answers supported, under assumptions the stored
     readings cannot check.
   - Big matters covered cannot be sized this way: a week gives two to seven.
   - D439 has about $10 left.
   - A starting library changes where questions come from, not how many a week produces, so it does not change this arithmetic.
2. **Comparable libraries.** The old system's library ends at 2026-08-28 (the sealed three-month run, D206). The new system has
   carried libraries to 10-05, 10-12 and 10-19. A same-day start needs the old system replayed from 08-29 for about seven weeks.
   - That is about $3.50 at D517's estimate of $0.50 a week, unpriced by token count here.
   - It would leave about $6 for the comparison itself.
3. **Unread news.** By the stored manifests, every week from 2026-09-21 to 10-26 has been read by at least one run of the new system
   or by Step 1's development runs. The week of 10-19 appears in the takeover, unfiled, capture-control and full-page diagnostic
   runs. So no exposure-free week remains for a fresh comparison, and the earlier assumption that three later weeks were unread
   (D517) does not hold.

**So:** a comparison now would be underpowered, and partly built on news the new system's development has seen. Nothing is run. This
does not show the systems equal. What would change it:
- more unread news than the database holds;
- a reading line that does not depend on the handful of questions a week produces;
- or Alex's own marks as the reference (Deck 1).
