# Claude's own reflection, written before the cold read was opened: what is the core problem? (2026-11-15)

Alex, after the week's result: "没过。然后你需要run council 到底核心问题是什么".

## What I think it is

**The system has no owner and no tight definition for the one decision everything else stands on: what exactly is the
question.** That decision is made in pieces. The reading call, while it extracts statements from one article, also proposes a
topic in free words taken from that article. A second call must then press that topic into "one figure for one year, or one
event", and may only accept it or decline it. The standard leaves the largest choices of shape open (which year, which
deadline, the part or the whole, one question or two). Readers then referee the outcome with a form whose own boundaries they
read differently. So each run asks different questions of the same news, each reading fails in a different place, and each
local fix moves the failure.

## The evidence I rest it on (all on the record)

1. **Given the questions, the system answers; asked to form them, it loses.** With the reference's own questions the new path
   agreed on 6.33 of 7 graded questions; opening its own, 4.0 (D395), and 2, 1, 0 in the three July runs, with "every loss
   before judging" in both systems (D445, D454). D483 limits that sentence to the seven graded questions.
2. **The same news gives different questions.** The same fourteen July days opened 15, 10 and 15 topics. One matter, the
   cloud companies' spending in 2026, came out as a figure in one run, a figure with a threshold in another and a yes-or-no in
   the third; the rental price as a yearly average, and twice as "will it keep falling"; memory prices as a rolling twelve
   months, as two things at once, as a yes-or-no. This week it came out as two years in one topic.
3. **One topic in five never becomes a question it can be judged on.** In the four stored readings 21 of 104 topics were
   declined in at least two of three attempts (9 of 44, 9 of 40, 0 of 7, 3 of 13), most for the shape the first step gave
   them or because readers say they should not have been opened.
4. **Wording fixes do not hold.** Since 2026-11-13 at least a dozen changes to the reading call or the answer-choice call
   were measured (c14 to c22, the period rule, the unsigned-deal rule, outcomes at opening, the price rule in production,
   the refusal clause): none adopted, several moved the error (D441, D465, D473, D491). D483 already named why: "one call
   must decide what to ask, its scope, its years and where facts go, so local rules interact."
5. **The referee is unsteady at exactly this joint.** This week the third readers left 41 of 48 points open; in July 41; the
   same sentence was failed in one reading and held in another. D458: the largest classes of opening losses "are left open by
   the standard".
6. **Every run starts from a blank page.** Each measured run opens its questions from an empty library, so each run measures
   the invention of the whole question list again, the hardest and least defined act, and never the state a research desk
   lives in, where the list exists and changes slowly.

## What I had wrong, or what is weaker than it looks

- For two days I treated the first gate as a property of the answer-choice step. The map says more than half of the short
  topics come from what that step is handed. I ranked a gate by its position in Alex's order, not by where the loss is.
- "Opening is the problem" was already written on 2026-11-13 (D445, D454, D466) and its mechanism on 2026-11-14 (D483). I did
  not carry it into the plan after D544. This reflection must not present it as new.
- "Given the questions it answers 6.33 of 7" is one comparison on seven questions of one window, read once.
- A separate opening call is my favourite remedy, not a finding: the old system has a separate admission step and loses at
  opening about as much; c19 (the reading call writes the outcomes at opening) failed; the answer-choice call itself gives a
  refusal and a written answer for the same topic.
- The blank-page point is an argument, not a measurement: no run has been made on a library carried over.

## Candidates I considered and rank below it

- The answer-choice step's own faults: real, small, changing; the map and the audit found no bounded, product-relevant repair.
- The gate's zero tolerance read by unsteady readers: true, and it means the gate cannot tell us which part to fix; but
  loosening it would not give the product better questions.
- Delivery of facts to questions (the phase's own blocker, PRD 10): the phase's stated goal; the last measurements found
  small numbers in the windows read (D457, D508, D513); it stays the goal, and it too presupposes that the questions exist.

## What I would put to Alex, if the council agrees

One sentence: the system does not yet know how to turn news into the right questions, and we have been measuring what comes
after that. One choice that is his, because it changes what the product is: how the question list comes to exist. (a) The
system invents it unaided from news, from nothing, as every run so far; (b) the system proposes each new question in a fixed
form and an analyst confirms or edits it before it is tracked; (c) coverage starts from a list an analyst wrote. He removed
the 22 "artificial" preset questions (D370) and said extraction should read the existing research library; D466 put the
blank-page choice to him and it was not answered. My lean is (b), with one test at $0 first: take the questions the stored
runs already opened for one window, have them put into one fixed form (subject, measure or event, one year or one deadline)
by a single reviewer pass, and count how many of the stored failures (declined topics, two-thing topics, the same matter in
several shapes, unworthy topics) that one pass removes or leaves. If it removes few, the core problem is not where I put it.
