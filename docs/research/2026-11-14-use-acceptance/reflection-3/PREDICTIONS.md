# Predictions, written before any reader's answer of this review is opened (2026-11-15)

The statement under test: the system forms a steady, answerable question when the news hands it one thing with its date or
its yearly report, and does not when someone has to choose the yardstick (what is measured, the period or deadline, which
part, whose matter).

Readings: task S (did the runs ask the same question; two families) and task K (is each topic's yardstick stated or chosen;
two families who do not see the judged questions). A topic counts as "stated" or "chosen" only where both families say so;
the others are "split" and reported apart. A matter is "stated" when all its topics are, "chosen" when any of its topics is
chosen by both families.

- **P1, the three July runs, by matter.** Among the matters that two or three runs opened, the share that task S calls
  `same` (both families) is at least 50 points higher for stated matters than for chosen ones. A gap under 25 points, or
  fewer than three matters on either side, and the pattern is not shown.
- **P2, the three July runs, by topic.** Of the topics declined in at least two of three attempts (9 by code), at least 8 are
  chosen; of the stated topics, at most one in ten is declined in at least two attempts.
- **P3, held out: the two later weeks (20 topics the July sorting never saw).** The three topics declined in at least two of
  three attempts are all chosen; no stated topic is declined in two attempts. With three declined topics this is a weak
  test; it can refute, it cannot establish.
- **What refutes the statement:** P1's gap under 25 points; or more than two of the nine declined July topics stated; or a
  stated topic declined twice in the later weeks.
- **What it cannot show even if all three hold:** that a rule for the yardstick would fix it; that the questions lost matter
  to a final answer; a rate.
