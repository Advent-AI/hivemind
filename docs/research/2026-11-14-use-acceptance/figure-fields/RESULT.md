## The undecided guard, and the boundary behind it

The article behind most of the differing marks is an outlet's piece that states figures in its own voice: "Gross margins are expected to
bottom in the 71% to 72% range in Q4 …, recovering to 72% to 73% in fiscal 2028"; "fiscal 2028 growth pegged at approximately 70%". The
arm writes them as the company's outlook (kind guidance, party the company, or the CEO for the growth figure). One reader family marks
that wrong, since the article does not say whose expectation it is; the other marks it right, since it is the company's outlook in fact.
A fresh reader of each family read the same answers and split the same way on most of them.

The six settled wrong in the arm: two are a guarantee total stored as an estimate (the adopted request's two settled wrong are the same
figure), four are on the outlet's own-voice article, where the arm also kept more figures than the adopted request did.

So the guard's line (kind wrong in no more than one in twenty, and not more than the adopted request's rate plus one in twenty) is met on
the marks the readers settle and missed if the disputed marks on that article are wrong. By the design that is undecided. The council's
reading (round 8): this is not a new rule for Alex; the signed standard already says an unknown speaker stays unknown and an inference is
not written as a source's statement, and the field's own words say "null when the item does not say whose figure it is". By those, the
strict family is right, the arm's party on those figures is wrong (4.7% in all, inside the line) and its kind is wrong (5.1% or more,
outside it). How often the arm should have left a party empty is not known from "2 empty of 234": most figures have a stated source.
