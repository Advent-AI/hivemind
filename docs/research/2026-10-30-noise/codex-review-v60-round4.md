## Required keep rules missing from the keep list

These omissions do not prove the model will drop the evidence: the opening analyst test still covers it. They leave less explicit protection against the drop list.

1. **Partners’ actions and published operating figures generally.** The list protects “a partner's or customer's adoption or integration,” but its broader action-and-figure clause names only customers, suppliers and competitors. §5.1 explicitly includes “客户、供应商、竞争者、伙伴的……动作，以及它们公布的数字.” A partner’s relevant revenue or backlog could be caught as background or a long-standing situation.

2. **Other companies’ own operating figures beyond the named relationships.** §6 explicitly makes “别家公司自己的经营数字（收入、利润率、积压订单）” supporting evidence. D238 also says “别家公司的动作和经营数字不能用这一行删掉，它们留不留按 5.1 的规则判.” The keep list names figures for customers, suppliers and competitors, but does not state that wider boundary.

3. **The company’s own figures, guidance and decisions as a general class.** §5.1 names “公司自己的数字、指引或决定.” The list protects some specific product changes and the opening paragraph protects guidance, but it does not explicitly protect the full class against the background or repeated-fact wording.

4. **A named party’s specific claim or estimate.** §5.1 includes “具名一方的主张或估计,” and protects “一个新的具体判断.” The list protects named actions and forecasts, but does not state the wider class of specific claims or estimates; this omission matters especially beside “an unargued view.”

5. **Relevant shared market quantities and input prices.** The glossary includes “原料和零部件价格、燃料、矿石品位,” and §6 includes “云巨头 2026 年合计支出 6500 亿美元” and “关键投入的供给和价格.” The list protects product cost changes but does not explicitly cover relevant aggregate spending or shared input prices, which could be mistaken for general demand or supply background.

## Internal contradictions

- **Forecasts and retelling:** “a forecast is never one” says a forecast cannot be a retelling, while “a forecast or a plan still in force, keep their place even when retold” explicitly describes a forecast being retold. The second formulation matches §5.3’s boundary.
- **Unargued views and protected evidence:** “an unargued view” is listed as noise, while the keep list protects forecasts, counter-examples and other evidence whenever they move a question. A new, specific forecast or counter-example can also be an unargued view, so the instructions can give opposite answers for the same item.

## Three findings most likely to cause real evidence to be dropped

1. **“an unargued view” is too broad.** It can suppress a new, specific named judgment that §5.1 says to keep when the analyst would note it.
2. **The keep list omits partners’ general actions and figures, and other companies’ relevant operating figures outside the named relationships.** Those are expressly covered by §5.1, §5.3 and §6, but can be mistaken for background.
3. **The keep list omits the company’s own figures, guidance and decisions as a complete class.** The general analyst test covers them, but an explicit class-level protection would resolve ambiguity where a relevant figure or decision resembles a retold arrangement or company history.

These are risks from the text and standard; this read-only audit does not establish how often the model actually drops each class.
