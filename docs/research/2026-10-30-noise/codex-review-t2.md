The current text carries much of the standard, but three places can steer the model to drop evidence the standard keeps: the carmaker example, retold quarterly guidance, and the treatment of existing arrangements. The carmaker example, for instance, labels a market-share figure as noise even though it gives no basis for deciding whether that figure matters to the parts supplier being studied.

This audit judges [review-core-t2.txt](/Users/lchingen/hivemind-extraction-quality-2026-10-28/docs/research/2026-10-30-noise/review-core-t2.txt) against [the version 4 standard](/Users/lchingen/hivemind-extraction-quality-2026-10-28/docs/prd/05-extraction-standard.html). I do **not** count the loss of §6’s optional items as a breach. “No source” means I could not find the instruction itself on the standard page; it does not by itself prove the instruction will cause a wrong drop.

### Keep rules missing from the exception list

- **Other companies’ operating figures.** §5.3 says “别家公司的动作和经营数字不能用这一行删掉，它们留不留按 5.1 的规则判”; §6 lists “别家公司自己的经营数字（收入、利润率、积压订单）” as supporting. The text names figures from customers, suppliers and competitors, but does not explicitly protect partners’ figures or relevant figures from other companies. The D238 clause itself is correctly limited, so this is an **omitted protection**, not an explicit ban on all other-company figures.

- **A new fact about an old situation.** §5.1 keeps a named party’s claim such as “CFO 说十年前建的工厂仍满负荷” and says “不是常识的，是新信息……分析员会记就留”. The exception list does not explicitly protect this from the broad examples “an arrangement already in place, described again” and “the company's history”; the separate qualification for a long-standing situation helps, but does not clearly qualify those two examples.

- **The retelling test.** §5.3 says “抽取看不到库，只能从文字和日期看” and “看不出是旧事的不算重提”. The text asks whether an item is new but does not state this limit, so it gives no explicit protection when the article does not establish that a statement is a retelling.

- **The general limit on every drop rule.** §5.2 says “下面的不留清单不能用来删掉分析员会记的陈述”. The text says the listed items “usually” fail and gives specific exceptions, but it does not explicitly state that **every** drop example is subordinate to the analyst’s keep judgment.

### Internal tensions

- **Existing arrangements versus still-effective plans.** “an arrangement already in place, described again” usually fails, while “a forecast or a plan still in force keeps its place even when retold”. An existing arrangement can also be a still-effective plan, and the text does not explain which instruction controls it.

- **Company history versus new information.** “the company's history” usually fails, while the opening asks whether the item is new and whether it changes an answer. A newly reported fact about company history can fit both descriptions.

- **Carmaker example versus the analyst test.** The example places the market-share figure in the usual-drop list, while the opening asks whether it changes an answer about the company or its customers. The example does not establish that the carmaker’s position is irrelevant to the supplier.

### Three findings most likely to drop real evidence

1. **The carmaker example labels a potentially relevant customer-market fact as noise without establishing irrelevance.** Unlike the standard’s Intel example, its stated facts do not show that the figure has little bearing on the company being studied.

2. **The retold-guidance clause can narrow the standard.** “still in force” can be read as applying to guidance, while §5.3 separately preserves retold quarterly guidance at its original weight.

3. **“an arrangement already in place, described again” is broader than the standard’s rejected background.** It can overlap with a still-effective plan or an analyst-worthy fact, and the text does not clearly resolve that overlap.
