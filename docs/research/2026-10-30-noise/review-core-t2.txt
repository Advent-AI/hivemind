Before you return the answer, check it once. Read each evidence item back as the analyst following the company and keep it when you would write it in this week's notes because it makes some question's answer more or less likely, about the company or its customers, suppliers, competitors, regulators or the economy. Ask first whether it is new: repeated common knowledge, a long-standing situation retold and an unargued view are noise; guidance, and a forecast or a plan still in force, keep their place even when retold.
These usually fail that check:
- background the analyst already knew: an arrangement already in place, described again; a long-standing situation retold, with no new figure or claim about it; the company's history ("the company was founded in 1998"; "the product line launched in 2020");
- a typical claim that is common knowledge, without a figure and a specific account: that a firm leads or competes, that demand is strong or supply tight, a general risk ("if demand slows it would hurt"); a figure alone does not lift it ("the leading soft-drink brand has held 40% of its market for years", in an article about a bank);
- a product, game, driver or benchmark detail with no business scale ("the card draws 415 watts"; "a benchmark score");
- an unargued view or a quote that only characterises ("the stock is a hold"; "a pivotal moment");
- a share-price move or an investor's position ("shares fell 13% this month"); another company's valuation, share price or deal that touches none of the company's markets;
- a second item for the same fact (keep the fuller one).
These are not by themselves reasons to drop; judge them by the check: a forecast, a rumour, a counter-example, a distant or conditional statement; a named party's action without an amount or a date; a customer's, supplier's or competitor's action or reported figure; a competitor's comparison against the company's product; a partner's or customer's adoption or integration; the launch, shipment, delay or cancellation of a product, and a change to the plan or configuration of the product that carries the company's value; a change to what a product costs; a policy or regulatory step; a valuation with its assumptions, derivation and date; the news behind a share-price move. Return only the items you keep.
