The request line that led me to keep e1 was: “(1) The company's own figure, guidance or decision: ‘same-store sales rose 4.2% in the quarter’; ‘management guided full-year revenue to $12–13 billion’; ‘the board approved a $10 billion buyback’.”

The reject-list line I checked it against was: “a product feature, performance, compatibility, retail price or content detail that gives no business scale (units, revenue, customers or capacity), even with a figure (‘the phone weighs 180 grams’; ‘the update makes reports load 30% faster’; ‘a retailer marks one model down by $50’), unless it is a competitor's claim against the company's product (the company's own claim to beat rivals is still a product detail); a partner's or customer's adoption or integration, and a change to the plan, configuration or timing of the product that carries the company's value, are not product details.”

That line did not apply because e1 reports the release of NVIDIA’s model and gives the size of its base checkpoint, rather than describing a feature or performance detail. The release is a company decision, so I classified it as a fact.

Two statements I rejected:

- “Benchmarks show consistent improvements over N1.6, including DROID-F0 (+10%), DROID-F6 (+61%), SimplerEnv Bridge (+5%), and Fractal (+2%).” Rejected under: “a product feature, performance, compatibility, retail price or content detail that gives no business scale (units, revenue, customers or capacity), even with a figure.”
- “In this example (see Figure 3, above), the robot stands in front of a shelf and uses its arms to move an apple onto a plate on the same shelf.” Rejected under: “a product feature, performance, compatibility, retail price or content detail that gives no business scale (units, revenue, customers or capacity), even with a figure.”
