The request that led me to keep e3 was: “(3) A named party's new claim or estimate about the company or its industry: ‘the CFO said plants built a decade ago still run at full capacity’; ‘an analyst raised next year's EPS estimate to $4.10’; ‘a short seller accused the company of overstating receivables by $2 billion’; ‘an analyst valued the shares at $80, 15 times next year's earnings of $5.30’.”

The reject-list line I checked it against was: “common knowledge an analyst following the company already has, unless it gives a figure and a specific account: framing or background, general risks or unargued verdicts, political or strategic views (an official's order, law, bill or ruling is kind (4), not a view), typical claims that a firm leads or competes or that demand is strong or supply tight (‘rivals have spent billions but still lag’; ‘the company was founded in 1998’; ‘if demand slows it would hurt’; ‘the stock is a hold’; ‘the airline expects travel to stay strong’).”

That line did not apply because Tan’s statement was a named expert’s specific claim about the implications of the reported Kyber delay for Nvidia’s next-generation architecture, not generic background or a typical general-risk claim.

Two rejected statements:

- “Japan’s Ibiden, a PCB maker that counts Nvidia as its largest client, dropped as much as 10 per cent.” Rejected under: “an investor's position or trade, a share-price move or the market value (‘he is short the stock’; ‘shares fell 9% this week’), though the news a move reacts to is kept.”
- “Samsung Electro-Mechanics has surged more than 600 per cent this year, while Kingboard Laminates is up more than 470 per cent.” Rejected under the same line about share-price moves.
