The request sentence that led me to keep e6 was: “a customer's, supplier's, competitor's or partner's action or reported figure with a size, date or name that bears on the company's demand, supply, input costs, competition, prices or products: ‘a hospital network signed a seven-year contract for the system’.”

The reject-list line I checked it against was: “a product feature, performance, compatibility, retail price or content detail that gives no business scale (units, revenue, customers or capacity), even with a figure (‘the phone weighs 180 grams’; ‘the update makes reports load 30% faster’; ‘a retailer marks one model down by $50’), unless it is a competitor's claim against the company's product (the company's own claim to beat rivals is still a product detail); a partner's or customer's adoption or integration, and a change to the plan, configuration or timing of the product that carries the company's value, are not product details.”

That exclusion did not apply because the sentence reports a named partner tie-up supporting development of complementary semiconductors, rather than merely describing a product feature or a partner’s product adoption or integration. In hindsight, though, the sentence gives no size, date, or business scale, so the inclusion was not justified by the evidence rule.

Two statements I rejected:

- “Nvidia's forward P/E ratio of 22 is just one-quarter that of Palantir's.” I rejected it under: “an investor's position or trade, a share-price move or the market value (‘he is short the stock’; ‘shares fell 9% this week’), though the news a move reacts to is kept.” (This was a mistaken rejection: the author’s named estimate about Nvidia’s valuation should have been retained as a third-party view.)
- “Nvidia has more favorable upside in the current environment.” I rejected it under: “common knowledge an analyst following the company already has, unless it gives a figure and a specific account: framing or background, general risks or unargued verdicts, political or strategic views … the stock is a hold.”
