The request that led me to keep e17 was:

> “(3) A named party's new claim or estimate about the company or its industry: "the CFO said plants built a decade ago still run at full capacity"; "an analyst raised next year's EPS estimate to $4.10"; a short seller accused the company of overstating receivables by $2 billion"; "an analyst valued the shares at $80, 15 times next year's earnings of $5.30".”

The reject-list line I checked it against was:

> “common knowledge an analyst following the company already has, unless it gives a figure and a specific account: framing or background, general risks or unargued verdicts, political or strategic views (an official's order, law, bill or ruling is kind (4), not a view), typical claims that a firm leads or competes or that demand is strong or supply tight ("rivals have spent billions but still lag"; "the company was founded in 1998"; "if demand slows it would hurt"; "the stock is a hold"; "the airline expects travel to stay strong").”

It did not apply because e17 gives specific figures and a modeled relationship: SemiAnalysis estimates how a change in funding cost affects Neocloud PBT margins. It is a named party’s estimate about the industry, not merely background or an unargued verdict.

Two statements I rejected:

- “Our GPU Rental Pricing Index tracks bilateral contract prices across the entire term structure, from on-demand to 5-year deals, across all major GPU SKUs, giving lenders and investors a transparent, transaction-validated benchmark where none previously existed.” Rejected under: “a product feature, performance, compatibility, retail price or content detail that gives no business scale (units, revenue, customers or capacity), even with a figure …”
- “The H100 1y index is freely available for public use!” Rejected under the same line: it is a product availability/pricing detail without business scale.
