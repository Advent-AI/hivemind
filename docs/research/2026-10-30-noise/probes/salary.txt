The request that led me to keep e3 was: “(1) The company's own figure, guidance or decision: ‘same-store sales rose 4.2% in the quarter’; ‘management guided full-year revenue to $12–13 billion’; ‘the board approved a $10 billion buyback’.”

The reject-list line I checked it against was: “a product feature, performance, compatibility, retail price or content detail that gives no business scale (units, revenue, customers or capacity), even with a figure (‘the phone weighs 180 grams’; ‘the update makes reports load 30% faster’; ‘a retailer marks one model down by $50’), unless it is a competitor's claim against the company's product (the company's own claim to beat rivals is still a product detail); a partner's or customer's adoption or integration, and a change to the plan, configuration or timing of the product that carries the company's value, are not product details.”

That line did not apply because e3 reports the company’s own compensation terms and grant values, not a product detail.

Two statements I rejected:

- “Mr. Parker will be eligible to participate in the Company's benefit programs available to similarly situated employees.” Rejected under: “common knowledge an analyst following the company already has, unless it gives a figure and a specific account: framing or background, general risks or unargued verdicts, political or strategic views (an official's order, law, bill or ruling is kind (4), not a view), typical claims that a firm leads or competes or that demand is strong or supply tight (‘rivals have spent billions but still lag’; ‘the company was founded in 1998’; ‘if demand slows it would hurt’; ‘the stock is a hold’; ‘the airline expects travel to stay strong’).” It is a general benefit-eligibility statement without a figure or specific account.
- “Mr. Parker will enter into the Company’s indemnity agreement, which would require the Company to indemnify Mr. Parker, under the circumstances and to the extent provided for therein, against certain expenses and other amounts incurred by Mr. Parker as a result of being made a party to certain actions, suits, proceedings and the like by reason of his position as an officer of the Company.” Rejected under: “common knowledge an analyst following the company already has, unless it gives a figure and a specific account: framing or background, general risks or unargued verdicts, political or strategic views (an official's order, law, bill or ruling is kind (4), not a view), typical claims that a firm leads or competes or that demand is strong or supply tight (‘rivals have spent billions but still lag’; ‘the company was founded in 1998’; ‘if demand slows it would hurt’; ‘the stock is a hold’; ‘the airline expects travel to stay strong’).” The filing describes a standard officer indemnity arrangement but gives no amount or specific quantified exposure.
