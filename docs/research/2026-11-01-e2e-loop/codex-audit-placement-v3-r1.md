- **Serious finding: the clause makes a broader rule than the documents support.** “A statement about the question’s own quantity or event **bears on it**” declares relevance based on subject alone. For example, a statement about a company’s revenue in a distant past period may concern the same quantity without making a particular future-year answer more or less likely. [Standard §5.1](docs/prd/05-extraction-standard.html) requires information to make an answer more or less likely; [D207](docs/DECISIONS.md) requires one narrowest home and limits company-wide figure questions to their own figures, guidance and management statements. D240 says missing details alone are not a reason to discard relevant evidence; it does not say every statement about the same quantity is relevant. D208, D209 and D248 do not supply that broader rule.

- **The same words can recreate a sink.** “A statement about the question’s own quantity or event bears on it” can be read as filing every statement on that subject, even when the statement does not help answer the particular question. The smallest fix is to replace the assertion with: **“a statement about the question’s own quantity or event is not disqualified solely because it states no period, basis or exact amount”**. Keep the existing test that it makes the answer more or less likely and that the rules below allow the home.

- **“The judge weighs that” is imprecise.** The placer still has to decide whether the statement belongs under the question; it cannot defer that decision. The later assessment can consider how strongly the statement supports an answer, but [standard §6](docs/prd/05-extraction-standard.html) says the formal core/supporting weight is assigned in evaluation, not stored as a judging output. Removing the parenthesis avoids implying otherwise.

- **Wording:** “Basis” is less specific than “period” or “exact amount.” If retained, it should mean a qualification needed to interpret the quantity, rather than any missing explanation.
