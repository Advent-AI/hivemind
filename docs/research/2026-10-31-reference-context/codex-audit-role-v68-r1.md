Serious issues remain: the draft can treat “new and connected to a question” as enough to keep a statement, and its one-item wording can split or trim a single matter incorrectly.

For example, a new product comparison might suggest an effect on demand, but §5.3 still rejects comparisons published by the company, a partner or a third party. The opening paragraph’s instruction to keep **“every new event, figure, forecast or specific judgement that moves an answer”** does not mention that boundary.

1. **The character paragraph risks a global keep rule.** [Line 1](docs/research/2026-10-31-reference-context/role-v68-draft.txt:1) says every such statement “goes into your notes.” [§5.1](docs/prd/05-extraction-standard.html:369) says the four kinds describe what an analyst might note; belonging to them does not itself require keeping a statement. Its boundaries are judged with §5.3. “Cautious and thorough at once” is fine; the unconditional “every” sentence is the problem.

2. **Removing the separate analyst judgment changes the test.** [Line 4](docs/research/2026-10-31-reference-context/role-v68-draft.txt:4) says: “A statement that passes all three is one you would write.” The three questions check whether it is new or an allowed retelling, identify a question and path, and apply the reject rows. They do not themselves ask the analyst whether the statement is worth recording. A new statement can have a plausible path and escape the listed reject rows while still being too minor for the notes. §5.1 makes “分析员会不会记” the decision, including for statements outside the list. Restore that judgment after the three questions.

3. **The final check repeats that gap in both directions.** [Line 41](docs/research/2026-10-31-reference-context/role-v68-draft.txt:41) requires “every statement that passes the three questions” to be in the list and every failure to be removed. Reading the article against the list as well as the list against the article is fine. But the test used for both directions must include whether the analyst would record it, with each §5.3 boundary applied on its own row.

4. **The item and quote wording needs narrowing.** [Line 8](docs/research/2026-10-31-reference-context/role-v68-draft.txt:8) says “a figure, an event, a forecast or a judgement is its own evidence item” and “never fold two matters into one quote.” That can pull against the same line’s correct example, “one deal with its terms is one”: a deal’s amount and other terms may themselves be figures. It also conflicts with [D250’s counting unit](docs/DECISIONS.md:2983), which permits not-kept words to ride inside the quote of a kept matter without making that record noise. Keep **one matter per item**, including its terms; do not prohibit incidental words from another matter in the verbatim quote. Eight *separate matters the analyst would note* yielding eight items is consistent with §5.1.

The “few words” fields are fine as a format choice if they still identify the question and its path. The example **“q4, supply”** names a question and a category, but may not explain *how* the statement moves the answer; a few words can do so without requiring a sentence.

This is a read-only text audit. It identifies risks in the instructions; it does not measure how often the model would follow either reading.
