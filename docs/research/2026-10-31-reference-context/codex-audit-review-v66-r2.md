The revised ending answers the round-1 finding: it sends a reaffirmed plan back to the same judgment as the plan itself, rather than automatically keeping it.

1. **Trace to the standard:** The clause follows the retelling boundary: guidance and plans still in force retain their original weight when retold. “Schedule” is an application of that rule to a plan’s timing, not a separate keep rule. A statement that merely describes a quote remains subject to the characterisation row. ([Standard §5.3](docs/prd/05-extraction-standard.html#), D243 items 6 and 10; D250 item 1.)

2. **Contradictions:** I found no direct contradiction with the named sentences. The background row excludes an existing arrangement with no new step while preserving guidance and plans at their original weight. The strategic-stance row and the role’s reject list can still reject a statement when the underlying “plan” is only a stance, boilerplate, a product detail or a characterisation. (Standard §5.3; D250 items 1 and 3.)

3. **Re-admission risk:** “We remain committed to our strategy” could be described loosely as a plan still standing. The revised ending directs it to the judgment of the underlying statement, and the existing strategic-stance and company-boilerplate exclusions still catch it. Likewise, an old plan is not automatically kept just because it is retold; its original weight governs. (Standard §5.3, strategic-stance and retelling rows; D243 item 6.)

4. **Example:** “The carmaker said the launch date has not moved” is general: it names no covered company, product or test-article number. The same example works with a bank, drug maker, retailer or chip maker and a relevant launch. (D252.)
