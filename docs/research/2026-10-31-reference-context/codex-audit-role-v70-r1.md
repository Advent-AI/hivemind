Serious issues remain: the closing sentence can drop useful evidence, and two examples reproduce details from the test material.

For example, a new policy step can matter even when it has no date or amount. The closing sentence could make the model look for the dated step in the example before keeping it.

1. **Delete the closing sentence.** [Line 19](docs/research/2026-10-31-reference-context/role-v70-draft.txt:19) says a statement shaped like a left side is “**kept only when the item adds what the right side has**.” That turns each example into a requirement. It can exclude a named party’s action without an amount or date, a new specific judgment, retold guidance or an in-force forecast, a policy step, or a competitor’s comparison. Those are judged under [§5.1–5.3](docs/prd/05-extraction-standard.html:368), with their own boundaries; they do not have to resemble a paired example. The earlier three questions already provide the test.

2. **Replace the two examples that reuse test-article details.** The draft’s “**2026 capital spending plan to $190 billion**” repeats the year and figure in the [test material](docs/research/2026-10-31-reference-context/blind-v68a/packet.json:4105). Its “**sensor maker cut its unit cost from $10,000 to $200**” closely follows another [test passage](docs/research/2026-10-31-reference-context/blind-v68a/packet.json:4050). Neither names the company, but D252 also excludes covered figures, and the prompt rules exclude examples taken from a test article. Replace both with invented examples from other domains and different figures.

3. **Make the valuation example complete.** [Line 11](docs/research/2026-10-31-reference-context/role-v70-draft.txt:11) gives “**$80, 15 times next year’s earnings of $5.30**” but does not give the valuation’s target date; 15 × $5.30 is also $79.50. The standard’s conditional-valuation boundary specifies assumptions, derivation and a date. Smallest fix: give a target date and make the arithmetic consistent, for example “valued the shares at **$79.50 by year-end**, using 15 times next year’s estimated earnings of $5.30.”

4. **Make the kept examples’ paths clear where they are currently assumed.** “**The airline took delivery of 12**” does not say what it took delivery of or tie it to the company. “**The ministry issued the import licence**” does not say what the licence concerns. “**The delay pushes first shipments**” does not identify whose product is delayed. These can be useful evidence, but the text alone does not establish that each passes the three questions as the heading claims. Name the aircraft or engines, the company’s product covered by the licence, and the company’s delayed product respectively. Likewise, “**was chosen**” in the sensor example should say **newly chosen** so it is a new adoption step rather than an arrangement already in place.

The other left sides fit their stated boundaries: existing arrangement, cheer, unargued view, common trend remark, product detail, stance without a decision, general risk, an unrelated company matter, an interpretation without a new fact or calculation, and background framing. The other right sides are sound **when their stated relationship to the covered company holds**: new order, guidance, customer spending plan, supplier’s output cut, and the new adoption step. The framing-only line is fine as a rejection of framing alone; it should not imply that a new specific judgment in the same sentence is discarded.
