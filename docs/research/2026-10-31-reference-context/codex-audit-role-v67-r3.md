Two serious gaps remain: the draft can reject a new judgment the standard allows, and it omits an explicit reject row for general risk lists.

For example, an expert may make a new, specific judgment about a supply shortage without explaining the calculation behind it. The standard says to judge that statement for the analyst’s notes; the draft can be read as requiring the explanation first.

1. **A new judgment is made narrower than the standard.** [Draft line 5](docs/research/2026-10-31-reference-context/role-v67-draft.txt:5) describes new information as “a new specific judgement **with its reason**.” But [§5.1](docs/prd/05-extraction-standard.html:369) includes a new specific judgment and gives examples without requiring a stated reason. The opening line of the draft also says “a new specific judgement” without that qualification.

   Smallest fix: remove “with its reason.” The analyst’s judgment and the relevant reject row still decide whether to keep it.

2. **The general-risk reject row is missing.** [Draft line 7](docs/research/2026-10-31-reference-context/role-v67-draft.txt:7) rejects “an unargued view,” but [§5.3](docs/prd/05-extraction-standard.html:400) also rejects an author’s **general risk list**, including a conditional statement such as “if an industry downturn occurs, the company will suffer.” A risk can name a plausible path and still be generic; the model could treat that path as an argument and retain it.

   Smallest fix: add “an author’s general risk list” to that row, while keeping the conditional valuation boundary attached to it.

The four round 2 changes are present. The revised procedure now asks for the analyst’s own judgment, permits the stated retellings, includes questions too small to open, and adds the independent-meaning test. The row-specific boundaries remain attached to their rows. The examples span industries and do not name a covered company. The two added string fields can express the requested explanations, although their actual schema descriptions were not supplied for this audit.

This is a read-only text audit; it does not measure how often either gap changes an answer.
