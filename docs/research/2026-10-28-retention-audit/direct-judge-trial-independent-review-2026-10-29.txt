The trial is a useful **development calibration**, but its current contract has serious gaps that should be resolved before implementation or sending. None requires a product change. For example, a control response that omits an entire article could otherwise appear to agree with Alex that the EPS prediction should be excluded.

**Serious findings**

1. **The control can turn missing output into a correct exclusion.** The trial requires nine unique IDs for the new task, but says the control keeps its old output shape and is mapped by hand. It does not explicitly require all six unique article IDs, valid article contents, and a successful call before treating an absent fact as “excluded.” The old recount maps a missing fact to `exclude`; that is meaningful only after the relevant article response is known to be complete. Define control completeness first, record partial or failed calls separately, and score exclusions only in valid article responses. Keep the three requested attempts and report their individual statuses; do not replace failed calls or count them as successful exclusions. [Trial design, lines 7–14 and 22–23](docs/research/2026-10-28-retention-audit/direct-judge-trial-design-2026-10-29.md#L7); [old recount, lines 40–55](docs/research/2026-10-28-retention-audit/user-judge-recheck-2026-10-29-v2.py#L40).

2. **The nine inputs and the scoring contract are not yet concrete enough to freeze.** The design names nine original passages but does not list their exact article IDs, source spans, labels, or the new task’s actual prompt and output schema. It also says to report source, meaning, quotation and page separately without specifying what is checked or how incomplete or incorrect content affects the result. For instance, a `core` label on the revenue-growth case could be counted correct even if the explanation loses “revenue,” as an earlier answer did. Before implementation, specify the nine ID-to-article-to-exact-span-to-label mappings, the complete prompt and closed output shape, and separate rules for label agreement and content correctness. A label score can remain narrow, but it must not imply that a wrong or unsupported explanation passed. [Trial design, lines 5–7 and 17–25](docs/research/2026-10-28-retention-audit/direct-judge-trial-design-2026-10-29.md#L5); [D214, lines 2718–2722](docs/DECISIONS.md#L2718).

3. **The free-reading control’s mapping remains subjective and unregistered.** A response may split one fact, combine it with another, describe only part of it, or omit it. The current contract says “map manually” but does not define when a returned fact corresponds to each of the eight decisions, how to handle multiple matches or contradictions, or how an uncertain match is reported. Freeze those rules and preserve the supporting output and rationale for every mapping. Mark ambiguous mappings as unresolved, rather than silently making them a match or omission. This matters especially because the previous Singapore answers contained different details and were correctly left out of the eight-item score. [Baseline, lines 5–18](docs/research/2026-10-28-retention-audit/user-baseline-and-routes-2026-10-29.md#L5); [trial design, lines 5 and 22](docs/research/2026-10-28-retention-audit/direct-judge-trial-design-2026-10-29.md#L5).

4. **The trial does not set an explicit spending or execution limit.** Three attempts per group and no automatic retries limit the planned sample, but the design does not state a maximum total spend, per-call limit, timeout, or what happens if a provider returns partial output or usage is unavailable. Register a cap no higher than the already authorized cap, model and execution settings, and a stop rule before sending. The repository instructions also require preflight before paid calls; D213 records that the existing preflight was defective, so its result cannot simply be assumed trustworthy. [Trial design, lines 5–15](docs/research/2026-10-28-retention-audit/direct-judge-trial-design-2026-10-29.md#L5); [D213, lines 2710–2714](docs/DECISIONS.md#L2710).

5. **The comparison cannot isolate the cause of any score difference.** The proposed structural change is clear: provide a candidate for each judgment instead of asking the judge to find facts itself. But it necessarily also changes the output task and makes every candidate salient. A higher score could reflect being shown the answer location, a different response format, or other task effects. The design already warns against claiming better completeness; make the interpretation equally explicit for economic judgment: the two scores describe **different tasks**, not a controlled estimate of improved judgment. Freeze the complete requests and settings so the scope of that change is inspectable. [Trial design, lines 3–5 and 25](docs/research/2026-10-28-retention-audit/direct-judge-trial-design-2026-10-29.md#L3).
