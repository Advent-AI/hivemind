The inclusion came from the broad material-statement rule for **a named party’s claim about the company or industry**:

> “(3) A named party's claim or estimate about the company or its industry”

But that rule is limited by the explicit exclusions. On review, I should not have kept these four: they are qualitative market judgments or a general risk claim, not material quantified claims under the keep rules.

1. **The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index being unusually extended versus its 200-day moving average**  
   I treated Burry’s chart-based statement as a claim about the semiconductor industry under rule (3). The exclusion that should have led me to reject it is:

   > “an expert's view on politics or strategy without a figure”

   The statement gives no measured degree of “extended”; “most … since the dot-com bubble” is a qualitative comparison.

2. **The SOXX ETF being unusually overvalued**  
   I treated Burry’s assessment as an industry claim under rule (3). The applicable rejection is:

   > “a quote that only characterizes (‘a pivotal moment’; ‘an industry-leading platform’)”

   “A pure form of overvaluation” characterizes the index; it gives no estimate or figure.

3. **Samsung and SK Hynix spending announcements lifting semiconductor-equipment stocks**  
   I treated Burry’s prediction as a claim about the industry under rule (3). The passage does name Samsung and SK Hynix, which initially seemed relevant to the rule about a competitor or partner action:

   > “a customer's, supplier's, competitor's or partner's action with a size, date or name that bears on the company's demand, supply, input costs, competition, prices or products”

   But it supplies no size or date for the spending announcements, and the claimed effect on equipment stocks is Burry’s qualitative prediction. The rejection that should have controlled is:

   > “an expert's view on politics or strategy without a figure”

4. **Big Tech overinvesting in Nvidia and peers’ chips that will quickly become outdated**  
   I treated this as Burry’s claim about Nvidia and its industry under rule (3). It should have been rejected under:

   > “an author's or investor's general risks or unargued verdicts (‘if demand slows it would hurt’; ‘the stock is a hold’)”

   The claim about chips becoming outdated is also a qualitative risk assertion without a figure.
