The instruction that led me to set aside the memory-spending estimate was:

> “Reject everything else: … another company's own figures that do not bear on the company, such as its valuation, profit or unrelated deals (‘a rival's price-to-earnings ratio’; ‘an unrelated merger in the same news digest’)”

I treated the estimate about memory’s share of data-center spending as a view about the broader data-center market rather than a figure specifically about NVIDIA. That was too narrow a reading: the item explicitly connects the estimate to a shift in the data-center trade away from NVIDIA.

The other instruction relevant to the supply statement was:

> “Report plans as plans; do not infer committed funding, orders, deliveries or revenue.”

I treated “NVIDIA has been smart about locking in its own memory supply” as an unspecific description, rather than a material statement about an action affecting NVIDIA’s supply. I should have considered it as evidence, while avoiding any inference about its amount.

I also set aside this passage:

> “NVIDIA shares are under pressure on reports of delays across their upcoming lineup of Vera Rubin server systems.”

I treated it as market framing rather than a sufficiently specified report about a named NVIDIA product. But it names the Vera Rubin lineup and reports delays, so I should have considered it as evidence of a reported product delay.
