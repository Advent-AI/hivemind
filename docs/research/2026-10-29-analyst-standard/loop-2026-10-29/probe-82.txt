No instruction required me to set aside a candidate about NVIDIA’s credit support. I incorrectly treated the reported framework as a description of a business model rather than considering it under the **binding_obligation** rule.

The relevant instruction is:

> “binding_obligation: an obligation a universe company takes on in its own name (a guarantee, financing it extends to others, a purchase commitment, a backstop of customers' capacity or revenue, or an equity stake whose size is stated and is not yet completed; its own borrowing is not one). Not yet signed: a yes/no question due on a day after the item by which the signing can be checked, at most 18 months out. Already taken on, as one deal or as a programme, whether or not the item gives an amount: a financing_amount question on the amount the obligation reaches at the company's next annual filing, settled by that filing's commitments note; the announcement itself is evidence to it.”

Statements I considered but set aside were:

- “NVIDIA’s new AI compute framework allows cloud providers to deploy advanced hardware using revenue-sharing and credit-support agreements.”
- “NVIDIA is partnering with AI clouds to deploy large‑scale, multi‑tenant AI factories through revenue-sharing and credit-support.”
- “The company earns revenue from hardware sales and from cloud usage generated by supported capacity.”

The first two describe NVIDIA’s programme and credit-support arrangements, which should have prompted consideration of a **financing_amount** candidate, with the next annual filing’s commitments note as the resolution source. I also wrongly made the third statement standalone evidence; it describes how the framework generates revenue, but it does not establish an amount or period.
