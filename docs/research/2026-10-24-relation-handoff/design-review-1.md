One serious gap remains: valid evidence can lose its ID before the proposed handoff resolves it.

For example, extraction returns `e1` and `e2` containing the same quote, and an association cites `e2`. The existing extraction check merges `e2` into `e1`, combines their viewpoint links, and rewrites candidates’ evidence references. However, it does **not return that alias mapping** ([extraction.py:173](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/src/hivemind/extraction.py:173), [extraction.py:185](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/src/hivemind/extraction.py:185), [extraction.py:193](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/src/hivemind/extraction.py:193)). The experiment’s adapter therefore cannot pass it onward ([ingestion_groups.py:80](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/poc/hierarchy_study/ingestion_groups.py:80)).

The proposal requires persistent evidence IDs and says to obtain them from the original writer ([design.md:29](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/research/2026-10-24-relation-handoff/design.md:29), [design.md:65](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/docs/research/2026-10-24-relation-handoff/design.md:65)). But `Written.evidence_ids` maps only surviving handover IDs; it cannot resolve the original `e2` ([writing.py:17](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/src/hivemind/views/writing.py:17), [writing.py:118](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/src/hivemind/views/writing.py:118), [writing.py:159](/Users/lchingen/hivemind/src/hivemind/views/writing.py:159)). The association could therefore be rejected or skipped even though its evidence was accepted and stored—another way to lose a valid extracted relationship.

The value/check tables need an explicit **original ID → merged ID → persistent ID** mapping, distinguishing merged evidence from genuinely rejected evidence. The free proof should include two identical quotes under different IDs, with the association citing the merged-away ID.
