The values came from treating the report itself as the source: the item gives no separate named speaker or original source for the investment announcement, and no separate date for it.

Relevant wording from the request:

- Item header: **“author finance.yahoo.com (media), published 2026-10-10”**
- System evidence instructions: **“Identify the speaker and, for a retelling, the original speaker and statement day.”**
- System evidence instructions: **“an announcement or completed deal the item reports is a fact even when the reporter names no source.”**
- Category guidance: **“Officially or company-confirmed events are facts; unnamed-source claims and unconfirmed reports are rumours; quoted company or official statements are party statements; the journalist's own judgements are third-party views.”**
- The reported statement: **“NVIDIA (NasdaqGS: NVDA) agreed to invest in AI lab Nous Research to support both open-weight and proprietary model development.”**

I used **“Yahoo Finance”** for both speaker and original_who as a readable form of the byline, **“finance.yahoo.com (media)”**. I used **2026-10-10** as original_day because that is the item’s publication date and the item gives no other statement date. In retrospect, the source field could have retained the exact author string **“finance.yahoo.com”** rather than normalizing it to “Yahoo Finance.”

Other values I considered and rejected:

- **NVIDIA** or **Nous Research** as original_who: the item reports that NVIDIA agreed to invest, but does not attribute the statement to either organization or quote an official from them.
- A named NVIDIA or Nous executive: no individual is named or quoted.
- An original_day other than **2026-10-10**: the item supplies no separate date for the agreement or for an original statement.
