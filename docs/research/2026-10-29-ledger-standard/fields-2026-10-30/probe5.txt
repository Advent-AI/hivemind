No words in the request justify assigning **rumour** to e2. I applied that tier incorrectly.

The relevant wording is:

- Category instruction: **“Officially or company-confirmed events are facts; unnamed-source claims and unconfirmed reports are rumours; quoted company or official statements are party statements; the journalist's own judgements are third-party views.”**
- Tier instruction: **“Use only this item's information. Report plans as plans; do not infer committed funding, orders, deliveries or revenue.”**
- Evidence tier definition: **“fact for official actions, published actual figures or completed transactions; party_statement for company guidance, management statements or officials' remarks; third_party_view for analyst, media or expert opinions; rumour for claims the item credits to unnamed sources or calls unconfirmed; an announcement or completed deal the item reports is a fact even when the reporter names no source.”**
- The article’s wording: **“Sharon AI plans to deploy up to 40,000 Grace Blackwell GB300 GPUs.”**

The article names Sharon AI and does not say the report is unconfirmed or attribute it to unnamed sources. The fact that it does not identify a source for the plan is not enough to make it a rumour.

I would reject **third_party_view** because this is presented as a report of Sharon AI’s plan, not the journalist’s judgement or an analyst’s opinion. **Party_statement** would require treating the sentence as Sharon AI’s statement; the article does not quote Sharon AI or say that it made the statement. **Fact** is the closest tier for the article’s reported plan, though it is a plan—not a completed deployment—and should not be described as one.
